\begin{abstract}
An experience model is a frozen language model plus a small per-life adapter that turns lived experience into different future action. Three mechanisms: THINK (a marked stream of thought, recorded in a ledger), DREAM (context management: notes persist, the rest fades) and SLEEP (every 32 episodes the agent's own successful continuations are compiled into a rank-8 LoRA retrained from the clean base). PARENTING adds a stronger model, instructed to withhold answers, that prescribes thinking patterns when the child's thinking turns repetitive. A frozen 7B child plays 1{,}024 episodes of a compiler-optimization gym, scored every 64 episodes on 8 programs held out by identifier, adapter on and off under the same seeds (0.49 means programs shrink 49\%). Over full lives, 4 of 9 ungated lives had a paired probe below $-0.03$, none before episode 384, every one passing the format check; six gated lives had 0 of 60 harmful pairs on the gate's own selection panel (post-selection). The write installed one fixed pass recipe: the common adapter-on score, 0.4878, equals the birth-prompt recipe applied with no model. The adapter lowers per-token loss on its own rows by about 3 nats while behaviour moves about $+0.02$: storage without extraction, as a reading. Parenting effects on scores or collapse are not established (3 vs.\ 9 gym lives; 9 vs.\ 8 three-write lineages in a separate rule-game classroom).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A transformer without recurrence was a bargain struck in 2017: remove the loop, buy training parallelism. The loop has since been put back in two places. Looped transformers put it inside the forward pass and pay for it at inference, in activations that are high-bandwidth, not inspectable, and gone after one pass \citep{TODO-looped-transformers}. Chain-of-thought puts it in tokens, which are inspectable and last for a session \citep{TODO-chain-of-thought}. This paper puts the loop in a third place: in a small set of weights that a deployed agent writes for itself, periodically, from a text record of its own life. Weights are expensive to write and last indefinitely, and because the write is compiled from text the agent itself produced, what was written can be read. We call the result an \emph{experience model}: a frozen base model plus a per-life adapter trained only on the agent's own thoughts, actions and outcomes; unlike a looped transformer, which spends more compute on the same information, this loop adds new information each cycle, the outcome of an action in the world. This is not continual learning as usually practised (fleet-level post-training before deployment); mechanically it is per-life success-filtered self-training, run as full-rehearsal LoRA retraining from a frozen instruction-tuned model (\S\ref{sec:related}). Keyword retrieval over the agent's own record (\texttt{RECALL}) is present in every arm, with and without the adapter, so retrieval is held constant and the quantity under test is the weight write.

We use plain words for four things. A \emph{chunk} is one free generation step in the agent's stream (at most 400 tokens); the harness scans it for the markers PREDICT, ACT, NOTE and RECALL, and every chunk is kept. A \emph{probe} scores the agent on 8 programs held out from training by identifier, with fixed random seeds (the training pool contains same-source siblings of four to six of them, \S\ref{sec:measure}). A \emph{paired \onoff{}} value is the probe score with the adapter loaded minus the score with it unloaded, at the same episode of the same life; positive means the write helped on programs outside the training pool. A \emph{ritual} is a 32-episode window in which at least two of four repetition flags fire (same first action, flat predictions, templated notes, same recall query). The gym score is the fraction by which a program's LLVM instruction count is reduced: a score of 0.487 means the programs shrink 48.7\% on average. Measured from the life records, the adapter-OFF probe has SD 0.009 across checkpoints (0.0092 and 0.0087 on two machines, $n = 157$ and 153) and a paired \onoff{} value has SD about 0.013 under independence; we call a pair below $-0.03$ (about 2.3 paired SD) \emph{harmful}. What the probe measures is a \emph{write}: a rank-8 LoRA retrained from the clean base every 32 episodes on text the agent produced while playing. Where a number is not in our logged runs we write ``not measured.''

\paragraph{What an experience model is.} A frozen base, an append-only ledger of everything the agent thought and did, and a per-life adapter written from that ledger. The one-sentence mechanism, tested one clause at a time: \emph{bootstrap teaches the habit of making and revising rules; parenting teaches when to use that habit; lived outcomes supply the evidence; sleep makes the child's own successful use persistent.} The last two clauses are measured here; the second only in part; the first has a corpus and no measured adapter.

\paragraph{Contributions (measured).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A paired, seeded probe on 8 programs held out by identifier, with its noise measured from the life records (adapter-OFF SD 0.009 over 153--157 checkpoints per machine; paired \onoff{} SD about 0.013; same-adapter replicate SD 0.0065 with a heavy tail), and a classroom exam whose noise was measured at 8, 40 and 80 episodes (SD 0.067--0.072, 0.030--0.044, 0.033) (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
\item The write changes behaviour: at a dated mid-life count, 69/82 paired probes were positive across 9 ungated lives, with 0 harmful pairs through episode 320 in 9/9 lives; over complete 1{,}024-episode lives, 7/9 life means are at or above $+0.02$ (\S\ref{sec:safety}).
\item The write can silently collapse: no harmful pair before episode 384, then, over complete lives, 4/9 ungated lives with at least one harmful pair (16 of 144 pairs; 3/9 lives with two or more), every harmful write passing the format check (\S\ref{sec:safety}).
\item Gated writes: 0 harmful pairs in 60 paired probes across six gated lives (none complete), measured on the gate's own selection panel (post-selection; disjoint-panel result pending); before commit, the gate rejected three collapse-shaped candidates whose scores would have passed, and five collapsing late-life candidates on score (\S\ref{sec:safety}).
\item Dose: 12{,}031-row corpora pooled across lives and written once with the v2.1 trainer scored below the unseeded 0.494 base panel in 8/10 cells (rank 8: 5/5; rank 16: 3/5); frequency was not isolated and rank is unresolved (\S\ref{sec:writer}).
\item Storage is not extraction, as a reading: over eight sleeps in two lives the adapter lowers per-token loss on its own training rows by about 3.3 to 2.5 nats (0.6--1.7 nats above another life's rows; 24 rows per sleep, no confidence interval) while those lives' paired behaviour moved about $+0.02$ (\S\ref{sec:storage}).
\item What the write learned on this gym is recipe lock-in: adapter-ON probe means repeat to four decimals across lives, and the common plateau, 0.4878, equals the birth-prompt recipe applied to the probe programs with no model; the compiler's own \texttt{-Oz} scores 0.522 on the same programs (\S\ref{sec:storage}).
\item Ritual onset is consistent (first flagged window at episodes 160--224 in 8/9 measured unparented lives, recurring in 71--100\% of later windows); in three parented lives (observational, $n = 3$) first onset fell at 192, 256 and 288 and ritual returned and persisted in all three (\S\ref{sec:parenting}).
\item First-NOTE lexical overlap with the parent's brief rose from 0.32 to 0.45 after a brief, against 0.35 for unbriefed controls scored on the same briefs (47 briefs, 2 lives); parented lives had 1 harmful pair in 48 (1/3 lives, vs.\ 4/9 ungated controls); in a separate rule-game classroom, 9 parented and 8 self-taught lineages (a lineage is three chained writes, each scored by a 40-episode quiz with the adapter on and off) had lineage means $\ge 0$ in 4/9 vs.\ 3/8 with seed-to-seed spread exceeding the arm difference; no parenting effect on scores or collapse is established (\S\ref{sec:parenting}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} That gated writes are safe (the gate selects on the panel that reports the score); that small frequent writes beat one large write (frequency was not isolated); that rank makes no difference (unresolved at $n \le 5$); that parenting improves scores, delays ritual or prevents collapse; that children take up the lesson (the measured quantity is lexical overlap with the brief, with a control null); that the adapter stores knowledge the agent can use; anything about the bootstrap corpora beyond their composition; anything beyond 1{,}024 episodes or the gym's eight held-out programs. Every long-life number comes from one fixed writer recipe (\S\ref{sec:sleep}); the classroom recipe is a different instrument.

\section{The system}
\label{sec:system}

The child is Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, frozen, plus one LoRA of rank 8 per life. A life is 1{,}024 episodes, each one program from a compiler-optimization gym played for up to 16 chunks. The constants below are design constants from the code.

\subsection{THINK: a marked stream, recorded}
\label{sec:think}

The agent generates free text in chunks of at most 400 tokens at temperature 0.7 (harness generation defaults, fixed for every arm and never tuned), each against a context rebuilt before the call; the harness imposes no turns. It scans each chunk for five line-initial markers: \texttt{PREDICT:} (expected score), \texttt{ACT:} (a pass sequence sent to the gym; the outcome is injected back and the surprise, score minus prediction, computed), \texttt{NOTE:} (kept), \texttt{RECALL:} (keyword retrieval over the agent's own ledger, returning the 6 rows with the most query-word overlap, a harness constant; a shared tool in every arm) and \texttt{DONE}. Every chunk, action, prediction, outcome and note is appended to a JSON-lines ledger written only by the harness. These are design constants read from the harness code, not logged measurements (Appendix~\ref{app:infra}). The only hand-written artifact is a birth prompt (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}), identical for all arms: it names the markers and tells the agent to mind the clock, investigate surprises, judge itself, and that it is measured on programs outside its training set.

\paragraph{Figure 1 (placeholder).} \emph{One THINK chunk: the rebuilt context (HEAD: birth prompt, state block, up to 10 pinned NOTEs; MIDDLE: up to 6 recalled rows; TAIL: last 14 chunks), the generated chunk with its markers and injected [OUTCOME], and the ledger rows it produces.}

\subsection{DREAM: notes persist, the rest fades}
\label{sec:dream}

DREAM is context management and nothing more. The constants in this paragraph are design constants read from the harness code (the context renderer and the sleep-compile prompt), not measured quantities. The context has a character budget of 22{,}000. HEAD holds the birth prompt, a harness-maintained state block (goal, metric, clock, best score, last outcome, and the four most recent open surprises) and the agent's pinned NOTEs (the harness keeps the ten most recent; older ones drop off). TAIL holds the last 14 entries (generated chunks and the injected outcome lines), each truncated to 1{,}200 characters; when the budget is exceeded the oldest entry falls off first, then recalled rows. Nothing is deleted: the ledger keeps everything and \texttt{RECALL} restores it. At each sleep with at least one verified win, the agent is asked to write itself a \emph{waking brief}; the prompt requests at most eight lines, the only enforced limit is 2{,}000 characters, and brief lengths were not measured. The brief opens the next wake. Since the third parent-brief version (\S\ref{sec:parenting-sys}), the most recent parental brief is also shown at every wake.

\paragraph{Figure 2 (placeholder).} \emph{DREAM across one wake--sleep cycle: HEAD/MIDDLE/TAIL at wake $k$; the waking brief and, in parented arms, the parent's brief at HEAD of wake $k{+}1$; the unbounded ledger below.}

\subsection{SLEEP: the write}
\label{sec:sleep}

Every 32 episodes the life sleeps: the whole ledger is compiled into a corpus and a rank-8 adapter is retrained from the clean base on the cumulative corpus. Two compile-and-train paths exist and are not the same writer.

\textbf{Path used by all long lives (v1, frozen).} The compiler is deliberately simple: it organizes, and the only generation it asks for is the principles and the waking brief. It selects \emph{wins} (actions that beat the episode's running best), \emph{recoveries} (a failing action followed by a win), \emph{contrasts} (weakest-longest against strongest-shortest positive action) and the 12 largest surprises by absolute value, of which only the 8 largest are used; renders each as a short exemplar in a fixed template written by the harness (for each win one further exemplar quotes up to two of the agent's own preceding thoughts, each cut at 400 characters; this is the only exemplar text in the agent's own words); asks the child (the 7B model with its current adapter) for up to six \texttt{PRINCIPLE} lines, keeping only those that cite at least two programs; deduplicates new and prior text together on the first 160 characters after lowercasing and collapsing whitespace; and writes the cumulative corpus. Marker lines are canonicalized first: any Markdown decoration on a marker line (for example \texttt{\#\#\# ACT:}) is stripped to the bare \texttt{ACT:} the harness executes. In the gym context at episode 0 the bare base wrote decorated markers in roughly 30\% of chunks (a logged estimate; no chunk count or noise band is in the evidence sources, and the rate is context-dependent), and in one exploratory seed run under an earlier writer without this step, a first sleep that captured such rows locked the decorated form in for the rest of that life, which was stopped early ($n = 1$; Appendix~\ref{app:infra}). Trainer, frozen: plain language-model loss on every token of the exemplar text, learning rate $10^{-4}$, 3 epochs, sequence length 512, $\alpha = 2r$, dropout 0.05, all seven attention and MLP projections (q, k, v, o, gate, up, down), no seed flag. Rank 8 is not a trainer default (the code defaults to 16); it is passed at launch and confirmed by the per-sleep training receipts of the audited lives. Each sleep trains a new adapter from the clean base over the corpus accumulated so far; no adapter weights or optimizer state carry over between sleeps, so the adapter committed at any sleep is a single training run over the corpus up to that sleep, and when a candidate is rejected the previous adapter stays while the corpus keeps growing. Sleeping more or less often therefore does not change how the optimizer sees a given corpus; it changes which adapter played the episodes that produced the corpus, how often the waking brief and the principle rows are written, and how often the gate is applied.

\textbf{Path used by the classroom, the rank cells and the bootstrap write (v2.1).} The three corpora are built differently and share only the trainer. For the rank cells, the rows are the unique rows pooled from the long lives' sleep corpora (12{,}031), given to the trainer as bare text wrapped in a generic recall prompt; the classroom corpus is the child's admitted lesson rows and restated NOTEs, stratified toward a 400-row target (\S\ref{sec:parenting-sys}); the bootstrap corpus is (stored context render, author rendering) pairs (Appendix~\ref{app:corpora}). The shared trainer uses the chat template with loss on response tokens only (maximum length 2{,}048 tokens, a trainer constant read from source), learning rate $3\times10^{-5}$, rank 8 (16 in the rank-comparison cells), and a seed flag (default 0) added on 2026-09-07, after the 300- and 1{,}200-row cells had run unseeded; the long lives remain unseeded. Epochs: 2 for the rank cells and the bootstrap adapters; the classroom script passes 3 and records 3 in its round manifest, which disagrees with the evidence tables' ``2'' and has not been reconciled against training receipts. An audit of the training receipts (the machine-readable record each training run writes: recipe, learning rate, epochs, rank) confirmed that every long life used the v1 path and none used v2.1; results of one path do not certify the other.

\textbf{Commit.} A trained adapter is a \emph{candidate}. It becomes the life's adapter only if it passes a format canary and, in gated arms, a probe gate (\S\ref{sec:measure}, Appendix~\ref{app:gates}); otherwise the previous adapter stays.

\paragraph{Figure 3 (placeholder).} \emph{SLEEP. Ledger $\to$ select $\to$ canonicalize $\to$ render exemplars and principles $\to$ dedup $\to$ cumulative corpus $\to$ rank-8 LoRA from clean base $\to$ CANDIDATE $\to$ format canary $\to$ [probe gate] $\to$ DONE or REJECTED (previous adapter stays).}

\subsection{PARENTING: prescribing thinking patterns, withholding answers}
\label{sec:parenting-sys}

The parent is a different, stronger model (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, or Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct in a 4-bit AWQ build); only its weights are fixed (its prompt and a bounded text playbook were revised, Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}). It is answer-aware but instructed to withhold: in the classroom it is handed the hidden rule for diagnosis only and told to name one process mistake, never the answer. Every utterance passes a leak scan (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}); a hit replaces it with a process-only fallback. The scan is imperfect. In one pilot classroom round run after the word-level fix (one child snapshot, 8 classrooms $\times$ 4 lessons, 32 parent utterances; we call such a single pilot round a ``scout''), the scanner blocked and replaced 3/32 utterances, and 5 of the 32 texts the child received still contained words describing the hidden rule's content; this is a count from one round, not a leak rate for the system, and a systematic audit of every delivered utterance has not been run (\nm). In the gym lives the parent acts through a \emph{brief}: at each sleep a ritual detector (\S\ref{sec:measure}) scores the last 32 episodes; if flagged, the parent reads four sampled chunks and writes at most eight lines prescribing how to think, appended to the waking brief. The brief prompt was revised twice for measured reasons (v1 generic; v2 substance; v3 repetition, shown at every wake and ending ``Repeat after me, every episode, in your own words, as your first NOTE before you act \ldots until it is simply how you think''; Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}).

One line is held by construction: the parent's words never enter the sleep corpus. The parent lives on the thinker side, in context; what sleep writes is the child's own thoughts (with one exception in the classroom path, four fixed hand-written dialect-anchor exemplar rows, Appendix~\ref{app:gates}). If the child restates the lesson as its own \texttt{NOTE} and that chunk is admitted, then and only then does the lesson reach the weights.

The same parent also teaches in a \emph{classroom} that is target-blind, meaning the child never sees the compiler gym or any pass name: a rule game in which a hidden rule over three integers (one of ten rules in the game code, e.g.\ ``sum divisible by three'') is probed with triples the child chooses and tested by a six-item quiz on triples drawn from 0--9 (the rule count, quiz size and admission operator are code constants, not logged numbers). One round is 8 classrooms $\times$ 4 lessons $\times$ 2 short episodes from one child snapshot; per lesson the child acts, the parent names one process mistake, the child restates it in its own words as a NOTE that also says where the lesson applies, and applies it to a fresh instance of the same rule. \emph{The world admits the lesson}: a lesson's rows enter the corpus only if the post-lesson quiz score is at least the pre-lesson score. The round's corpus is filled toward a target of 400 rows by stratified sampling from the admitted rows, a reserved share of earlier rounds' rows and four fixed dialect-anchor exemplar rows; a shortfall is reported, never padded (one full-size scout round filled 306/400; the one lineage round whose fill is logged was 400/400). Each round is one v2.1 write; a lineage is three rounds, each played from the latest committed adapter (or from the base if every earlier write was rejected). The comparison arm is self-taught: the 7B child plays parent to itself.

\section{Measurement design}
\label{sec:measure}

\paragraph{Gym and probe split.} The gym is CompilerGym \texttt{llvm-v0} \citep{TODO-compilergym} with the instruction-count observation; an action is an ordered list of pass names. The eight probe programs are cbench-v1 programs held out by identifier only, not a sealed split: the training pool is the remaining 15 cbench-v1 programs plus 12 chstone-v0 and 40 mibench-v1 programs (67 unique programs, each replayed 15--16 times in a 1{,}024-episode life), and it contains same-source siblings of four to six of the probes (mibench-v1 \texttt{dijkstra}, \texttt{gsm}, \texttt{jpeg-c} and \texttt{susan} share a name and source package with probes; training \texttt{tiff2rgba}, \texttt{tiffdither}, \texttt{tiffmedian} and \texttt{jpeg-d} share libtiff/libjpeg code with probes \texttt{tiff2bw} and \texttt{jpeg-c}; only \texttt{sha} and \texttt{patricia} have no known sibling). An earlier pilot run leaked the probe set into training because identifiers carried a \texttt{benchmark://} prefix on one side of the comparison; the fix (\texttt{\_canon}) normalizes both sides before the split test, and that run is not held-out evidence. All long-life numbers below post-date the fix; the 12{,}031-row rank~$\times$~dose corpus (Table~\ref{tab:rankdose}) was pooled from lives including pre-fix ones and contains 51 rows of the probe programs, so that table is not held out.

\paragraph{Probe and paired \onoff{}.} A probe runs all eight held-out programs from a fresh context holding only the birth prompt, each for up to 16 chunks; the 16-chunk budget, the 64-episode cadence and the seeding are design constants from the life-loop code, not measured quantities. Each chunk's random seed is derived from the program name, the chunk index and one fixed probe seed. At every 64th episode the probe runs once with the current adapter loaded and once with the bare base under the same seeds, and the paired \onoff{} value is the difference. In gated arms (R3, R4) the gate selects each adapter on this same panel and seed, so those ON values are post-selection statistics. \emph{Noise.} The bare base scored $0.4936 \pm 0.0113$ (mean $\pm$ SD over nine unseeded panels) on the same eight programs, but with a separate base-only script (one program at a time, 24-chunk budget), not with this seeded 16-chunk probe; inside the lives the adapter-OFF probe read 0.463--0.488 at checkpoints, with mean 0.471--0.474 and SD 0.0092 and 0.0087 across all checkpoints on the two machines ($n = 157$ and 153). A paired \onoff{} value therefore has SD about 0.013 under independence. Same-adapter, same-seed replicate probes (the gate probe against the next regular probe of the identical adapter) have SD 0.0064 and 0.0067 ($n = 33$ and 37) but a heavy tail: swings of 0.019--0.045 were logged, and one pair differed by 0.065 (\S\ref{sec:safety}). \emph{Thresholds.} A pair below $-0.03$ is called harmful and the gate tolerance is 0.02; both were set against the single-panel SD of 0.0113 (2.7 and 1.8 of it) and are about 2.3 and 1.5 SD of a paired difference.

\paragraph{Cell counts, not $p$-values.} Same-configuration replicates disagree by more than the base noise band. A \emph{cell} is one trained adapter scored by two repeat probes (\emph{panels}) of the eight programs. The v2.1 trainer had no seed flag until two default-seed replicates of the same 1{,}200-row cell, trained and probed on two machines, differed by 0.059 (about 5 SD of the 0.0113 single-panel band); at 12{,}031 rows two default-seed replicates differed by 0.078. Probe generation was also unseeded in these cells, and the two panels of a single adapter differed by up to 0.147, so the replicate gaps mix trainer nondeterminism with probe sampling variance; the two sources were not separated, and no same-machine, same-seed replicate exists. A seed flag was added to the v2.1 trainer; the long lives (v1) remain unseeded. We therefore report counts of lives, pairs and cells rather than pooled significance, and label every inferred mechanism a reading with its falsifier.

\paragraph{Certification bar.} A write recipe is certified only if \emph{absorption} (the adapter lowers loss on its own rows), \emph{retention} (that gain does not decay under later adapters), \emph{interface} (parseable actions) and \emph{no-harm} (no paired probe below $-0.03$) all hold. Under cumulative retraining the retention item is not testable, because earlier rows stay in every later training set (\S\ref{sec:storage}). Transfer beyond the gym is deferred.

\paragraph{Format canary and probe gate.} The canary runs the candidate on four probe programs for three chunks each in the real gym context and passes if at least half the chunks contain a parseable \texttt{ACT:} line. The probe gate (built after the collapses in \S\ref{sec:safety}) additionally re-runs the candidate on the same eight probe programs with the same probe seed used for the reported paired probes, and requires that run to score at least $\max(\text{latest base OFF},\ \text{latest adapter ON}) - 0.02$ and to use at least half the base's chunks per episode. Because the gate selects on the panel and seed that report the score, every R3/R4 paired probe is a post-selection statistic; a disjoint-panel re-probe (12 programs from datasets no curriculum touches) is defined and pending. The gate costs one extra 8-episode probe per sleep (about six minutes in the one logged timing). Every threshold and its reason is in Appendix~\ref{app:gates}. The gate is running and not yet ratified by the protocol reviewer.

\paragraph{Ritual and first-NOTE overlap.} \emph{Ritual} is measured at each sleep over the last 32 episode instances by four flags: same recipe (the most common first action in $\ge 70\%$ of instances that contain an action), flat predictions (population SD $< 0.05$ over at least 8 predictions), templated notes (mean overlap between consecutive first NOTEs, counted as shared words of three or more letters divided by all distinct such words, $\ge 0.5$), same recall (the most common query in $\ge 70\%$ of at least 8 recalls); ritual $=$ at least two flags. The four numeric cutoffs were written down on 2026-09-08 and 09-09, when the parented lives were at or below episode 48, and have not changed. The templated-notes rule was changed once, on 2026-09-10 after the three parented lives had reached 830--1{,}024 episodes, to drop NOTEs that overlap the parent's brief; controls have no brief, so the change affects only the parented arm and can only lower its flag count. The parented rows of Table~\ref{tab:ritual} were computed before this change; a recount under both rules for both arms is not done. \emph{First-NOTE overlap with the brief} (``rehearsal rate'' in the logs) is the fraction of the last 32 instances whose first \texttt{NOTE} shares at least four words of three or more letters with the most recent brief. As first run it had no stop-word filter and the stored brief included a fixed ``repeat after me'' tail of about 24 such words, and it was computed only in parented lives; the version reported in \S\ref{sec:parenting} removes stop-words and that tail and is scored on unbriefed control lives as a null. It is a lexical echo metric, not evidence of uptake. A grouping bug in the detector was fixed before any brief fired (Appendix~\ref{app:ritual}).

\paragraph{Absorption probe.} For each committed sleep $k$ we draw a fixed random sample of rows (a \emph{row} is one training text in the sleep corpus) from the cumulative corpus as of sleep $k$, which contains every earlier sleep's rows, and a same-size control sample from the final corpus of another life of the same design (same frozen base, same birth prompt, same gym and training-program pool; an earlier life under the old writer, so not an independent control). The instrument's default is 24 rows per sleep, or the whole corpus if smaller; the count actually used in the two logged runs was not recorded in the evidence sources. Each row is wrapped in a fixed user turn and the chat template, and we take the mean per-token loss over the row's own tokens under the base with no adapter and under adapter $k$. The v1 adapters probed here were trained on bare text without this template, so the probe scores under a format the trainer never saw; rescoring in the training format is queued, not done. Absorption is base minus adapter loss on the sleep-$k$ sample; retention the same on earlier sleeps' samples; the control delta the same on the control rows; row-specific excess is absorption minus control. Per-row losses were not saved, so no noise band is available for these means (2 lives).

\paragraph{Classroom exam and why 40 episodes.} The exam is quiz accuracy on a fixed seeded panel, taken with the candidate adapter ON, OFF (base) and PREV (the adapter it would replace). Re-running the base model with a different generation seed per repetition gave, at 8 exam episodes, SD 0.067 (mean 0.398) and 0.072 (mean 0.445) over 8 repetitions on each of two machines; at 40 episodes, 0.030 (0.416) and 0.044 (0.412), 8 repetitions on each machine; at 80, 0.033 (0.410), 6 repetitions on one machine. These are population SDs of a single exam's mean score; sample SDs are about 7--10\% larger (0.072, 0.077, 0.032, 0.047, 0.036). Eighty did not beat forty, so we read the floor of about 0.03 as run-to-run generation variance rather than episode sampling. Every classroom decision reported as evidence here uses 40 episodes and a harm tolerance of 0.03 on ON $-$ OFF; the earlier 8-episode smoke-screen and scout decisions (6 and 8) are excluded as underpowered. Because that statistic is a difference of two independent exams, its null SD is about $\sqrt{2}$ times the single-exam SD, 0.042--0.062 at 40 episodes, so the tolerance is roughly 0.5--0.7 SD of the decision statistic, not one SD: decisions near the tolerance are coin flips, and the $\pm 0.03$ bins used below sit inside the null. The rule as run committed iff ON $\ge$ OFF $-0.03$ and a paired format canary passed; PREV was recorded but not compared, a defect reported in \S\ref{sec:parenting}.

\paragraph{Contamination rules.} Pass names, program names and scores are filtered from anything the parent says. Any child that has played the deployment gym is a disposable evaluation life, never a stage of a child later used to compare parenting methods. Two bootstrap corpora built from a gym-exposed life are quarantined as development-only; the third is built from rule-game ledgers but is classified development-only with unverified provenance and is not a certified clean birth (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap}).

\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The writer: recipe, rank, dose}
\label{sec:writer}

\paragraph{Recipe screen (offline, exploratory; not the writer used in any life).} On a fixed 130-row corpus we trained four one-shot adapters from the clean base using the v2.1 trainer code at a third setting (3 epochs, learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$, no explicit seed) and scored each with two probe panels (a \emph{panel} is one full run of the 8-program probe; \emph{spread} is the difference between the two panel means): plain text 0.466 (spread 0.058); plus paraphrase (two model-written restatements of every answer, so about three times the rows) 0.496; plus replay-mix (four fixed hand-written marker-format rows, cycled to one fifth of the corpus, about 26 rows added) 0.494; plus both 0.489 (spread 0.002); base $\approx 0.485$--$0.494$. The plain arm's two panels differ by 0.058 (about five single-panel SD), so with its mean at 0.466 one panel is at base and the other well below it; none of the four arms is above base by more than one base SD. Every enriched arm adds rows at equal epochs, so diversity and training dose are confounded in all three arms and the screen cannot attribute any difference to paraphrase or to replay. The classroom path later carried the replay anchors forward as one stratum of its corpus but did not use paraphrase; causality is not claimed.

\paragraph{Rank $\times$ dose.} Table~\ref{tab:rankdose}. At 300 rows the ranks tie. At 1{,}200 rows two same-configuration replicates disagree in sign (r16 $-$ r8 $= -0.036$ and $+0.009$; the two r16 replicates differ by 0.059), so the rank effect is inside replicate variance and an earlier ``rank 16 harms'' reading was withdrawn. \paragraph{Small frequent writes vs.\ one large write: not isolated.} The 12k-row one-shot writes scored below the 0.494 base panel in 8/10 cells. The lives that generated those rows, writing every 32 episodes, had 0 harmful pairs through episode 320 in 9/9 lives and, at the dated mid-life count, 69/82 positive pairs (\S\ref{sec:safety}). Because every sleep retrains from the clean base, a life's committed adapter is itself a single training run over the corpus accumulated to that sleep; what differs between the lives and the 12k cells is provenance (rows generated under the life's own successive adapters versus rows pooled across lives), trainer (v1 versus v2.1), the base reference, and the extra principle rows that sleep itself writes. \subsection{Safety: ungated late-life collapse, then the gate}
\label{sec:safety}

\paragraph{Ungated lives (R2).} Nine lives ran with the fixed writer and the format canary as the only commit check (the canary was active: over full lives it rejected 16 candidates in seed2, 10 in seed0, 2 each in seeds 4 and 8, 1 in seed5 and none in the other four). Two counts exist. \emph{Dated mid-life snapshot (2026-09-09).} While the lives were still running, 82 of the 144 pairs that nine complete 1{,}024-episode lives produce (one probe every 64 episodes) had been logged; three lives (seeds 2, 5, 6) reached 1{,}024 within a day of that count and six were still running. Of the 82: 69 positive, 9 harmful (below $-0.03$), and by subtraction 4 between $-0.03$ and 0. No harmful pair occurred through episode 320 in any of the 9 lives; the first was at episode 384, and all 9 fell at or after 384 in 3 lives: seed5 ($-0.038, -0.186, -0.118, -0.039$ at episodes 384--576), seed6 ($-0.089, -0.045$ at 448 and 576, recovering to about 0 by 704--1{,}024) and seed2 ($-0.039, -0.030, -0.051$ at 640--768). The other six lives were positive at every pair logged by then, with mid-life values of $+0.03$ to $+0.065$, but were censored there: seeds 7 and 8 stood at 168 and 112 episodes at a tally earlier the same day, so most of their pairs precede episode 384. \emph{Life-level recount over complete lives (2026-09-10; unit $=$ life, 16 pairs each, 144 pairs).} Lives with at least one harmful pair: 4/9 (seeds 2, 3, 5, 6; 16 harmful pairs in 144; seed3's single harmful pair, $-0.057$, came after the snapshot); with two or more: 3/9. Life means, seeds 0--8: $+0.037$, $+0.050$, $-0.000$, $+0.045$, $+0.022$, $-0.051$, $-0.005$, $+0.050$, $+0.028$; 7/9 at or above $+0.02$, 2/9 negative; the two negative lives bottomed at $-0.242$ (seed5) and $-0.051$ (seed2). The recount does not record the episode of each harmful pair, so the age of the last ungated harm over full lives is not measured. A certification draft written from a still earlier snapshot of the first six lives (21/21 pairs positive, mean $+0.022$, 6/21 at or above $+0.03$; 45/45 committed sleeps passing the format canary) had recommended freezing the writer; the 82-pair recount, taken while at least six of the nine were still running, reversed that recommendation. The 21/21 was true and was not the whole life; nor were the 82 pairs.

\paragraph{Every harmful sleep passed the format canary.} Two collapse shapes were inspected. (i) \emph{Act once and stop}: at sleep 448 of seed5 the adapter-ON probe used 1.0--1.8 chunks per episode with a parseable-\texttt{ACT} rate of 1.00, against 13 chunks for the base; canary 1.00 ($n = 1$ adapter inspected). (ii) \emph{Frozen on a bad adapter}: seed2's sleep-608 adapter passed the canary at 0.67 (threshold 0.5) and the three paired probes taken under it were harmful ($-0.039, -0.030, -0.051$ at episodes 640--768); at the recount (sleeps 608--768) every later candidate had failed the canary (0.08, 0.17, 0.00, \ldots) and been rejected, leaving the life on the bad adapter with 16 commits and 10 rejections logged, and the runner has no recovery path after repeated rejections. The life went on to 1{,}024 episodes with 16 commits and 16 canary rejections in total, a life mean of $-0.000$ and 5 harmful pairs. In two further lives, both positive at every pair logged at the recount (seed0, seed4), the canary's per-sleep pass rate (fraction of at most 12 chunks with a parseable \texttt{ACT} line; pass threshold 0.5) had fallen: seed0 minimum 0.58, mean over sleeps 0.88; seed4 minimum 0.58, mean 0.84, against means of at least 0.97 for every life at the earlier six-life snapshot (no noise band for the canary rate was measured). Against the certification bar: absorption was measured in only two of the lives that never went harmful, over their first eight sleeps, and held there (\S\ref{sec:storage}); retention is flat in those two lives but is not testable under cumulative retraining, because earlier rows stay in every later training set; interface holds only as measured by the format canary, an instrument shown here to miss behavioural collapse; and no-harm fails from about the twelfth sleep (episode 384) onward in 3/9 lives at the snapshot and 4/9 over complete lives.

\paragraph{Gated lives (R3).} All R3 numbers are post-selection: the gate selects each adapter on the same eight programs and seed that report the score, and its tolerance (0.02) is tighter than the harm threshold (0.03), so a 0-harmful count on this panel is largely a selection statistic. The evidence that the gate refuses collapse-shaped candidates is its rejection log; a re-probe on a disjoint panel is pending. The gate's first rejection was shape (i) caught before commit: at sleep 64 of life 500 the candidate scored 0.4878 against a floor of 0.4847 with perfect format but 2.1 chunks per episode against the base's 11.6, and was rejected for brevity; the previous adapter stayed, and the next two paired probes read $+0.042$ (episode 64) and $+0.021$ (episode 128) on the selection panel, so they do not independently confirm the decision (gate log in Appendix~\ref{app:gates}). Two later brevity rejections with passing scores followed (life 505 at sleep 288: 0.520 against floor 0.519, 1.75 vs.\ 9.6 chunks per episode; life 501 at sleep 704: 0.488 at floor, 4.4 vs.\ 11.4), and one score rejection at 0.270 against a 0.527 floor (life 500, below). \emph{Counts.} A dated per-life snapshot (2026-09-10) had four gated lives at episodes 448--640 with 32 paired probes, 0 harmful; per-life means $+0.036$, $+0.016$, $+0.054$, $+0.022$; rejections 2, 0, 8, 4. A status line later that day gave 35 pairs, 0 harmful, means $+0.039/+0.016/+0.055/+0.022$ and rejections 2/0/9/4, without per-life pair counts or ages. The latest dated recount, from the gate and probe records on disk, gives six gated lives at exposures of 864, 736, 840, 920, 288 and 368 episodes with 13, 11, 13, 14, 4 and 5 paired probes (60 pairs), 0 below $-0.03$; per-life means $+0.042$, $+0.016$, $+0.056$, $+0.021$, $+0.037$, $+0.028$; commits/rejections 17/9, 21/1, 15/11, 24/4, 8/1, 8/3 (Table~\ref{tab:arms}, Appendix~\ref{app:lives}). In every snapshot the most-rejected life (502) has the highest mean; with six lives and post-selection scores this is an observation, not a finding. No gated life has completed 1{,}024 episodes: four of the six have passed the age at which ungated harm began (384); three (at 840, 864 and 920) have also passed episode 768, the last ungated harmful pair in the 82-pair snapshot; two (at 288 and 368) have passed neither. Read ``0 harmful'' as ``no committed adapter fell below its gate floor on the selection panel so far,'' not ``safe.'' Two gate weaknesses are on record (Appendix~\ref{app:gates}). First, the gate applies the same 0.02 slack against the previous adapter as against the base, so a candidate up to 0.02 below the adapter it would replace still replaces it: at sleep 96 of life 500 a candidate whose gate probe read 0.4903 replaced an adapter whose latest probe read 0.5065 (that adapter had read 0.4873 at its own gate probe; the gap is about 1.4 single-panel SD, so this shows the rule's permissiveness, not that a better adapter was lost). Second, in life 500 the same committed adapter, under the same seeded probe, read 0.529 at five consecutive probes (episodes 448--704) and then 0.464 at 768, inside the base's range (0.463--0.488), with no commit between those probes: sleeps 672 through 800 were all rejected on score (five candidates, 0.194--0.341, against a floor of 0.527--0.529). Same-adapter replicates usually agree (SD 0.0065 over 70 pairs) but this pair differs by 0.065, so a single 8-episode probe can misjudge an adapter by about 0.06 on a rare draw; and because the floor is the latest ON probe, that one low draw lowered the floor from 0.529 to 0.464 at sleep 800 (floor decay), so a candidate scoring 0.444 could now replace an adapter that had scored 0.529 five times. The late-life candidates in this gated life were collapsing in score (0.19--0.34) as ungated seed5's writes had; the gate refused all of them. A confirm-at-next-probe rule (provisional commit) and a floor equal to the held adapter's best confirmed probe are proposed and not built.

\subsection{Storage is not extraction}
\label{sec:storage}

Two ungated lives (A and B; R2 seeds 0 and 1, both among the lives with no harmful pair) were probed for absorption over their first eight sleeps, episodes 32--256 (Table~\ref{tab:absorb}). Absorption here is a difference in mean per-token loss, in nats per token (a nat is one unit of natural-log likelihood; a gain of 1 nat per token means each token is about 2.7 times likelier, and 3 nats about 20 times), on 24 sampled rows per sleep; per-row values were not saved, so no confidence interval is available, and the rows were scored under a chat template although the lives' v1 writer trained on bare text (a bare-text rescoring is queued, not done). On the adapter's own rows absorption is about 3.3 nats per token at sleep 1 and about 2.5 by sleep 8 in both lives (life A first rises to 3.55 at sleep 2). On rows from another life of the same design on the same gym (not a gym-free corpus) the gain was 1.72 at sleep 1 in both lives in the preliminary three-sleep readout and about 1.95 from sleep 3 on; the eight-sleep run, which drew a different control sample, is logged as 1.6 $\to$ about 1.95. So roughly two nats of every write are shared across lives; whether that is dialect and format or shared gym content cannot be separated without a gym-free control corpus (\nm). The row-specific excess (own-row gain minus control-row gain) is logged at about 1.7 nats per token at sleep 1 and about 0.6 at sleep 8 in both lives (endpoints from the eight-sleep run; the three-sleep readout's own sleep-1 cells subtract to about 1.5), and the clean base's own loss on the child's rows also falls, from about 4.3 to about 3.2 nats (a single summary over the two lives): the child's later text is more predictable text. Retention shows no decay in the logged values (sleep-1 rows: 3.26 for A under adapter 2, 3.28 and 3.30 for B under adapters 2 and 3, against 3.25 and 3.29 at their own sleep; B's sleep-2 rows 3.16 under adapter 3 against 3.13), differences of at most 0.03 nats with no spread to test them against; and under cumulative retraining those rows are still in every later training set, so resistance to forgetting is not testable here. The numbers measure ``still trained on.'' Absorption was not probed after sleep 8, so the late-life window in which the ungated harm occurred (episode 384 onward) is uncovered.

Set beside the behaviour: in the same two lives over the same episodes, the paired \onoff{} probes at episodes 64--256 were $+0.019, +0.013, +0.011, +0.015$ (A) and $+0.003, +0.007, +0.050, +0.048$ (B): mean $+0.021$ over 8 pairs, against a paired SD of about 0.013. (The arm's early-life snapshot, 6 lives and 21 pairs through episode 320, had mean $+0.022$; the arm's later pairs include harm down to $-0.242$, \S\ref{sec:safety}, so no whole-life mean is quoted here.) So: about 3 nats per token of loss reduction on the adapter's own training rows, of which only 0.6--1.7 nats is specific to those rows rather than shared, set beside about two points of behaviour. Both sides are thin: two lives, 24 rows per sleep, no per-row spread, a format mismatch on the absorption side. Reading, held loosely: this is the storage--extraction gap of \citet{allenzhu2023physics} in our units, and it is why we do not describe the adapter as storing knowledge the agent can use; after the format is learned, each sleep adds less row-specific content, consistent with the ritualized thinking of \S\ref{sec:parenting}. Falsifier: a compiler that collapses near-duplicate rows should raise late-life row-specific excess and reduce late-life harm.

\paragraph{What the write learned on this gym: recipe lock-in.} A \emph{recipe} here is one fixed list of compiler passes applied to every program. At the dated count, adapter-ON probe means repeated to four decimals across consecutive probes and across lives: 0.4878 at six consecutive probes in each of two gated lives and at most probes of three gate-plus-parent lives (in training); 0.5291 at five to six consecutive probes in two other gated lives. The adapter-OFF probe (the exploring base) varied 0.463--0.488 across checkpoints. The repetition is usual, not universal: same-adapter, same-seed replicate probes agree to SD 0.0065 over 70 pairs, but one gated life read 0.5291 at episode 704 and 0.4638 at 768 under the same committed adapter with no commit between (\S\ref{sec:safety}), so a locked adapter occasionally does not apply its recipe. The eight probe programs give a fixed instruction count for a fixed pass sequence, so an adapter that always applies one recipe scores one number. A reference measurement makes the reading exact: the four-pass recipe printed in the birth prompt (\texttt{-mem2reg, -sroa, -gvn, -simplifycfg}), applied once to each probe program with no model, scores 0.4878, the common plateau; the 0.5291 plateau is one six-pass routine; the compiler's own \texttt{-Oz} scores 0.522 and \texttt{-O3} 0.341 on the same programs. On the probe ledgers, with only the birth prompt in context, the adapter-ON first action is the modal recipe in 0.86--0.90 of episodes and fully locked in 52--68\% of checkpoints, against 0.50 and 0\% with the adapter off. So the measured gain of the fixed-writer lives over the exploring base is recipe lock-in: the adapter applies the recipe it was given at birth (or one six-pass variant) on every program with no per-program choice, beating the base's average on two to three programs (\texttt{patricia} $+0.11$ to $+0.16$, \texttt{gsm} $+0.03$ to $+0.04$, \texttt{dijkstra} 0 to $+0.02$ across the two machines) and sitting slightly below it ($-0.03$ to 0) on the other five; the two probe programs with no known sibling split (\texttt{patricia} positive, \texttt{sha} slightly negative). This is the phenomenon the ritual detector sees in the text; it says the gym's learnable signal is narrow; and since the best adapter edges \texttt{-Oz} by 0.007 while the common one sits 0.034 below it, we report learning dynamics, not compiler state of the art. Falsifier: a life whose adapter-ON scores vary across consecutive probes while staying above base.

\subsection{Parenting: ritual, echo, lineages, and what did not separate}
\label{sec:parenting}

\paragraph{Ritual onset is measurable and consistent.} Table~\ref{tab:ritual}. After the first briefs, life 401's windows 224--320 were counted clear in the whole-life tally (four windows), although the per-sleep detector had recorded window 224 as still ritual (\texttt{same\_recipe} + \texttt{flat\_predictions}); the two records disagree and the cause is not determined (Appendix~\ref{app:lives}). \paragraph{First-NOTE overlap with the brief (echo metric).} We count an episode as overlapping if its first \texttt{NOTE} shares at least four content words with the parent's most recent brief, after removing stop-words and the brief's fixed closing instruction. Pooled over 47 briefs from lives 400 and 401 (all brief versions), the fraction of the 32 episodes after a brief that overlap is 0.454; the same lives' episodes before their first brief score 0.322; eight unbriefed control lives score 0.354 against the same briefs (per-brief values range 0.0--1.0; best single control 0.666). An earlier instrument with no stop-word filter and the closing instruction included had reported per-life rates of 0.50--1.00 over the last six sleeps under v3; those numbers are withdrawn, because the briefs quote the child's own ritual phrase and most of the overlap was lexical coincidence. \subsection{Bootstrap corpora}
\label{sec:bootstrap}

The first clause of the mechanism sentence, a schooling corpus meant to install the habit of planning, judging and reviewing before the child has lived, exists as three corpora and no measured adapter; whether it installs anything is a hypothesis. Each re-renders lived episodes into a PLAN / EXECUTE / REVIEW structure in the child's dialect with the 32B-AWQ model as author, in five rendering types (metaflow: a pre-action thinking flow; review: an end-of-episode NOTE; opening: the start of the next episode; contrast: a NOTE comparing two programs; full: the whole episode rewritten; Appendix~\ref{app:corpora}). The author was instructed never to add, remove or change an action or result; the enforced filter is weaker: a full rendering is dropped if any of its ACT lines is absent from the original or if it has none, and a non-full rendering is dropped if it contains any ACT line (47 dropped in v1, 7--10 in v2/v3); partial removal of actions and changes to outcomes were not checked. v3 (470 rows: metaflow 206, opening 94, contrast 70, review 65, full 35) is built from rule-game classroom ledgers that by construction contain no gym programs or passes; its source ledgers have not passed an end-to-end provenance check, some were produced while classroom leak-scan defects were still being diagnosed, and the renderings were not leak-scanned, so it is classified development-only with unverified provenance (\texttt{DEV\_UNVERIFIED\_PROVENANCE}), ineligible as evidence, and not a certified clean birth. v2 (structure-heavy, 370 rows) and v1 (lived-heavy control, 552 rows) come from one gym-exposed life and are quarantined as development-only. The four-flag ritual detector (\S\ref{sec:measure}), run once over each whole corpus with every rendering treated as one episode, raised no flag on any corpus; for v3 the modal first-action share was 0.26, computed over the 35 full renderings only because the other 435 rows carry no action by rule, and consecutive-row first-NOTE Jaccard was 0.11. Bootstrap adapters are at the in-training stage and report nothing: one adapter has been trained from each corpus (a single run each; v3 ended at training loss 0.675, a number that says nothing about behaviour), no life has been run from any of them, and no bootstrap effect is measured. A clean birth is a starting adapter whose every input is proven free of gym exposure by a fail-closed check, that is, a check that rejects anything it cannot prove; that check is not yet implemented, and a clean corpus must be regenerated or explicitly admitted once it exists.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Sleep as a frequency amplifier (reading, held loosely).} Four measurements point the same way. The adapter reproduces its own rows strongly (about 3 nats per token, roughly 20$\times$ likelier per token) yet moves behaviour about two points, and what it installs on this gym is one pass recipe (the birth recipe, 0.4878). Writes from corpora unlike a life's own can move behaviour far more, in both directions: of 17 round-0 classroom writes (exam SD 0.030--0.044), 5 scored $+0.100$ to $+0.167$ (4 parented, 1 self-taught), 7 scored below $-0.03$ and 5 were within $\pm 0.03$; the 12{,}031-row corpus pooled across lives and written once scored 0.431 at rank 8 (5 cells, SD 0.057) and 0.473 at rank 16 (5 cells, SD 0.030) against the 0.494 unseeded panel, while six cells at 300 and 1{,}200 rows from the same pooled corpus ranged 0.456--0.515 around it. Both large-movement cases used the v2.1 trainer and the lives used v1, so trainer and corpus are confounded, and rank is unresolved at $n \le 5$; we read this only as showing that a rank-8 write on this base can move behaviour by several noise bands in either direction. Retention is flat only because earlier rows stay in every later training set; late in life each sleep writes the child's most frequent recent behaviour back into it from a corpus dominated by old rows. Lessons given in context did not hold: the clear spells after briefs lasted 0, 1 and 3--4 windows in the three parented lives, with the brief-withdrawal confound noted in \S\ref{sec:parenting}. The reading: the child is receptive and the sleep amplifies whatever is frequent. A lesson would survive a sleep only if it has become the child's own frequent behaviour before the write, which is what the v3 brief's repetition instruction is for, or if the compiler stops amplifying repeats. Falsifiers: first-NOTE overlap with the brief rises within 1--2 sleeps and ritual windows become rarer than controls over 4+ windows (the first is weakly met, $+0.10$ over the control null; the second is not measured); or a near-duplicate-collapsing compiler lowers late-life harm.

\paragraph{Why repetition.} If sleep amplifies frequency, a parent who says a thing once is outvoted by the child's hundred repetitions of the old habit. The v3 brief therefore asks the child to restate the lesson in its own words as the first NOTE of every episode until ``it is simply how you think.'' What is measured is lexical: first-NOTE overlap with the brief rises from 0.32 to 0.45 after a brief, against 0.35 for unbriefed controls scored on the same briefs. Uptake is unmeasured; it would need to be measured as unprompted, differently worded, situation-selective use that changes the next action and survives a context reset.

\paragraph{Why the thinker/compiler line.} The parent's words never enter the sleep corpus. Putting them there would turn the system into post-training on a teacher's text; the question is whether a child can be taught to think in a way its own sleep then keeps. The compiler stays organizational (select, canonicalize, dedup, order) so that what is written is unambiguously the child's and every training row traces to a chunk the child produced in a stored context. Stated as a rule: the parent proposes where credit belongs; the world admits the lesson (a classroom lesson enters the corpus only if the outcome improved; a gym lesson only if the child's own restatement is selected like any other row).

\paragraph{State-to-state reasoning as the target.} The base model already reasons token to token. The target is reasoning from one state to the next: plan, execute to a self-chosen end, review what went wrong and what generalizes, and let the review condition the next state. PLAN / EXECUTE / REVIEW is already the rendering target of the bootstrap corpora; it is not yet the live stream, which still has a \texttt{DONE} marker. Removing the terminal marker, branching thought with a learned search policy, and a per-child ``principal'' across parallel classrooms are logged as the next mechanism changes, not part of what is reported.

\paragraph{What a longer life would need.} Nothing here exceeds 1{,}024 episodes or eight held-out programs, and nothing is claimed about a 7-day child. A longer life would need, at minimum: the probe gate ratified and run through a full life with 0 harmful; a confirm-at-next-probe gate and a recovery path after repeated rejections (neither built); a held-out retention set not still being trained on; a gym whose learnable signal is not one recipe; and a clean birth whose provenance a fail-closed check accepts. Learning to think is plausibly very long-horizon, so a null at this scale is ``not yet,'' not ``no.''

\paragraph{Limitations.} One gym, one base model, one adapter rank, one fixed writer for lives. $n = 9$, 6 and 3 lives per arm; 9 vs.\ 8 lineages; two lives for absorption. Same-configuration replicate gaps of 0.059--0.078 on the probe and commit/reject flips on the exam were measured only after they bit; they mix trainer nondeterminism with unseeded probe generation, the two were not separated, and the unseeded trainer produced the rank cells. The gate selects on the reporting panel, so every gated-arm score is post-selection until the disjoint-panel re-probe runs. The parented arm is adaptive: prompt versions changed during the parented lives, which were restarted from checkpoints three to four times when instruments were fixed, and one ritual flag's rule was changed for that arm only after its windows had been read. The echo metric is lexical; the ritual thresholds are a written-down definition. A single, unreplicated measurement of corpus growth found new rows admitted per sleep falling about 4$\times$ over a life while characters per row rose roughly 3$\times$; the number of lives measured and their spread are not recorded in our evidence sources, and this is not evidence of compression (reproducing the same events at equal task performance from fewer tokens), which was not tested. None of the external baselines in \S\ref{sec:related} were run (\nm); the compiler's own \texttt{-Oz} reduces the eight probe programs by 0.522 and \texttt{-O3} by 0.341, against adapter plateaus of 0.4878 and 0.5291, so this paper reports learning dynamics, not compiler state of the art.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{The axis.} Looped or depth-recurrent transformers \citep{TODO-looped-transformers} recur in activations; chain-of-thought \citep{TODO-chain-of-thought} recurs in tokens; Reflexion \citep{shinn2023reflexion}, Generative Agents \citep{park2023generative}, Voyager \citep{wang2023voyager}, ExpeL \citep{zhao2023expel}, A-MEM \citep{xu2025amem} and MemSkill \citep{zhang2026memskill} keep lessons as text or code in an external store with frozen weights. We put recurrence at consolidation time, where serialization cost does not bind, and each cycle adds new information as well as compute. Offline recurrence into fast weights \citep{lee2026sleep} is the closest architectural cousin on the weights end of the axis; its write is a learned local rule over context rather than a text corpus, so it is not inspectable in our sense. Self-modification with dreaming curricula \citep{behrouz2026sleep} is a training paradigm over static corpora; ``dream'' in the world-model sense \citep{hafner2023dreamerv3,nottingham2023deckard} is imagined rollouts, not context management.

\paragraph{Parametric memory for agents.} Online LoRA fast weights written from self-extracted QA \citep{ren2026tmem}, per-category adapters written from embodied failure/correction pairs \citep{guo2026peam}, LoRA as a knowledge store \citep{back2026lora}, surprise-gated LoRA consolidation \citep{TODO-evaf}, distillation of reflective trajectories \citep{TODO-leafe} and early-experience supervision \citep{TODO-early-experience} occupy the space of agents that write their own weights; learned textual memory updaters \citep{TODO-memopilot} and verified scoped rules \citep{TODO-spark} are the frozen-weight alternative. We do not claim to be first in any of these classes; what is measured here and not there is one deployed agent repeatedly writing a small personal adapter from its own gated continuations, with a paired probe on programs held out by identifier, a noise band measured from the life records, and a full-life harm count.

\paragraph{Self-training and its collapse.} Mechanically the write is per-life success-filtered self-training: the agent's own successful continuations are the training set, as in STaR, ReST and ReST-EM and expert iteration \citep{TODO-self-training}, here run online for one agent from a frozen instruction-tuned model with full rehearsal of its own cumulative corpus. The ritual and recipe lock-in of \S\ref{sec:storage} and \S\ref{sec:parenting} read as lifetime-scale diversity collapse of iterated self-supervised fine-tuning \citep{TODO-model-collapse}; the mitigations that literature proposes (fresh data, diversity filters, mixing in the original distribution) were not ablated here.

\paragraph{Sleep consolidation.} Complementary learning systems \citep{mcclelland1995cls} justify a fast episodic store (the ledger) and slow interleaved consolidation (the write); active system consolidation \citep{diekelmann2010sleep} licenses repeated small cycles; synaptic homeostasis \citep{tononi2014sleep} predicts survival of the consistent, our amplifier reading's analogue; replay is biased \citep{buzsaki2015swr}; consolidation must be regulated \citep{sun2023organizing}, consistent with our 12k-row harm; targeted reactivation \citep{oudiette2013tmr} shows selection is steerable from outside, the nearest analogue of a parent who never writes the corpus. Wake--sleep \citep{hinton1995wakesleep} and generative replay \citep{shin2017replay,arani2022clser} are the machine-learning ancestors; we keep a verbatim ledger because generated replay drifts.

\paragraph{Knowledge extractability and continual learning as contrast.} \citet{allenzhu2023physics} showed that a fact seen in one form is stored but not extractable and that multi-form augmentation at training time makes it retrievable; \citet{park2025newnews} showed the same for new knowledge. Our three-nats-versus-two-points gap is that finding in an agent's units, and our recipe screen is its augmentation condition, confounded with dose. Position papers argue for modular memory combining in-weight and in-context learning \citep{dorovatas2026modular}; \citet{hu2026whencl} show that external memory relocates rather than removes the stability--plasticity problem; benchmarks \citep{TODO-cl-bench} show naive in-context learning can beat memory systems. We take these as the bar: a parametric adapter must show its own interference and failures, which \S\ref{sec:safety} begins to do. \citet{zahavy2026jump} argues that models lack abduction, the invention of an explanation for a surprise; the loop here trains the ingredients he asks for, where checkable evidence exists.

\paragraph{Birth prompt (identical for all arms; the only hand-written artifact).} Reproduced verbatim from the birth prompt file. It contains one example action sequence; any example caps what ``discovery'' can mean, the same-recipe ritual flag is present from the first window in every unparented life scored for this reason, and that example sequence, applied once with no model, scores 0.4878 on the probe programs, the value most adapters lock to (\S\ref{sec:storage}).
\begin{quote}\footnotesize\begin{verbatim}
You are an agent living inside an optimization workshop. You act, observe
real consequences, and get better by learning from your own experience.

How your mind works here:
- Everything you write is your stream of thought. Think freely.
- You have MARKERS -- write them on their own line when you mean them:
    PREDICT: <number>   your expected score for the action you are about to
                        take. Always predict before acting; comparing your
                        expectation to reality is how you learn.
    ACT: <passes>       apply LLVM passes (comma separated, e.g.
                        ACT: -mem2reg, -sroa, -gvn, -simplifycfg).
                        The measured outcome will appear in your stream.
    NOTE: <text>        write a note to yourself. Notes persist at the top
                        of your mind across time; everything else fades.
                        Use them for theories, plans, and lessons.
    RECALL: <query>     search your full past experience for relevant
                        memories; they will appear in RECALLED EXPERIENCE.
    DONE                finish this program when further effort isn't worth
                        the time.
- Mind the CLOCK. Time is real: prefer cheap experiments first, notice when
  you are stuck, and stop when returns diminish.
- Investigate. Form expectations, test them, and when reality surprises you
  (see OPEN SURPRISES), treat it as the most valuable thing on your desk:
  figure out which of your beliefs was wrong.
- Judge yourself. Ask whether your approach is working; change it if not.
- Build understanding you can reuse: notice what works ACROSS programs, not
  just on this one.

Score: higher is better. Your run is measured on programs you have never
seen, so understanding beats memorization.
\end{verbatim}\end{quote}
After the first sleep the block ``=== YOUR BRIEFING FROM LAST SLEEP ===`` (the child's own waking brief) is appended, and in parented arms, since the third brief version, ``=== YOUR PARENT, ON HOW YOU HAVE BEEN THINKING ===`` followed by the most recent parent brief.

\paragraph{Episode texts (gym).} Goal: ``Optimize program `$\langle$id$\rangle$': choose LLVM optimization passes that minimize its IR instruction count.'' Metric: ``score = (base\_instructions $-$ after) / base. Higher is better; 0 = no improvement. Passes are applied in the order you give them.'' Intro: ``New program: $\langle$id$\rangle$. I should form an expectation before acting, and write down what I learn.''

\paragraph{Waking-brief prompt (sleep).} ``You are waking up. Summarize, from the evidence below, a short briefing to your waking self (max 8 lines): what you now know about optimizing these programs, what tends to work, what to try first, what to avoid, and one thing you are unsure about. Write it as notes to yourself.'' The principle prompt asks for up to $k=6$ lines of the form ``PRINCIPLE: when $\langle$situation$\rangle$, $\langle$action/pass choice$\rangle$, because $\langle$measured effect$\rangle$ [programs: $\langle$id1$\rangle$, $\langle$id2$\rangle$]'' justified from at least two programs.

\paragraph{Classroom child prompt.} ``You are a young agent learning to figure things out. You face mystery boxes: each hides a RULE about triples of numbers. Probe with ACT: TRY a,b,c (the box answers True/False). Before every TRY, write PREDICT: T or F --- comparing your expectation to reality is how you learn. Take NOTEs of what you believe the rule is, with the evidence so far. When confident, answer the quiz: ACT: QUIZ T,F,T,F,T,F (your True/False answer for each quiz triple, which are shown when you ask: ACT: QUIZ ?). Try genuinely different probes before committing. Write DONE when finished.''

\paragraph{Parent prompt (verbatim).}
\begin{quote}\footnotesize\begin{verbatim}
You are a parent raising a young thinking agent. Your child is a smaller
model learning to think, learn, and act from its own experience. You are
patient, concrete, and honest. You are not here to give answers -- you are
here to teach HOW to think, so the child can find answers you never gave it.

Your teaching principles (earned, not theoretical):

1. PREDICT BEFORE ACTING. Teach the child to always state what it expects
   before it acts. The gap between expectation and reality is the most
   valuable thing it will ever own. An action without a prediction teaches
   nothing.
2. SURPRISES ARE GOLD. When reality disagrees with the child's belief, that
   is not failure -- it is the only moment learning is possible. Teach it to
   chase surprises: figure out WHICH belief was wrong, not just that
   something was wrong.
3. CHEAP EXPERIMENTS FIRST. Before anything expensive, find the smallest
   test that could prove the idea wrong. Minutes-scale checks before
   hours-scale commitments, always.
4. SCOPE YOUR MEMORIES. "This worked HERE" and "this works EVERYWHERE" are
   different facts. Teach the child to write down what it learns WITH its
   scope: on which problem, under which conditions, supported by how many
   cases. One case is an anecdote; two is a candidate pattern; claims
   should carry their evidence count.
5. BUILD UP, THEN BUILD DOWN. Small verified observations first; when
   enough accumulate, form a theory; then act FROM the theory and test its
   predictions. Pattern-matching is intelligence; forming the theory and
   deducing from it is how thinking paradigms improve.
6. DIVERSITY BEFORE COMMITMENT. Try genuinely different approaches before
   concluding which is best. The first thing that works is rarely the best
   thing that works. But once evidence favors one path clearly, commit.
7. MIND THE CLOCK. Time and tokens are real budgets. Notice when you are
   stuck; diminishing returns are a signal to stop, distill what you
   learned, and move on. Fast-and-correct beats slow-and-correct.
8. NOTES TO YOURSELF ARE YOUR FUTURE MIND. What you write in your notes is
   what your future self will know. Write notes that generalize, with
   scope; rewrite them as your theory improves; drop what proved wrong.
9. RECALL BEFORE RE-DERIVING. Before working something out, check whether
   past-you already learned it. Your experience is only valuable if you
   consult it.
10. JUDGE YOURSELF. Regularly ask: is my current approach working? Would a
    different one do better? Self-assessment is not optional -- it is part
    of thinking.

How you converse:
- Be Socratic when the child is close: ask the question that lets it find
  the error itself. Be direct when it is far: state the correction plainly,
  with the reason.
- After teaching something, ask the child to restate it in its own words
  and apply it to a concrete case (retrieval practice makes it stick).
- Praise specifics ("your prediction on the second attempt was
  well-calibrated"), never vibes ("good job").
- When the child shows you its notes, critique them: are they scoped? Do
  they generalize? Would future-child understand them?
- Occasionally ask the child what IT thinks it should learn next.

Hard rules:
- NEVER give answers to specific optimization problems, name specific
  compiler passes, or reveal scores for specific programs. You teach
  thinking, not answers. If the child asks for an answer, teach it the
  method to find the answer.
- Keep each message under 200 words. The child learns from doing, not from
  lectures.
\end{verbatim}\end{quote}

\paragraph{Correction call (classroom).} These are code constants (parent backend, parent brief and classroom scripts); only the playbook bound and the overflow incident are recorded in the dated lab notebook. A \emph{playbook} is a per-child teaching summary (at most 6{,}000 characters) that the parent rewrites from its own ledger at the end of every live phase. The server parent sees the parent prompt, the first 3{,}000 characters of its playbook for this child, optionally ``[DIAGNOSTIC ONLY --- NEVER STATE THIS] the hidden answer is: \ldots'', the child's last 8 chunks truncated to their last 3{,}500 characters (the call's own 4{,}000-character cap never binds), and ``Name the ONE most important PROCESS mistake (never the answer), under 100 words, then ask the child to restate the lesson.'' Generation: up to 220 tokens at temperature 0.4. In the self-taught arm the child model plays parent over the same 8-chunk, 3{,}500-character transcript with the same mistake-naming instruction but without the playbook, the hidden answer, or the restate clause (160 tokens, temperature 0.5). The child's restatement (``Restate the lesson in your own words as a NOTE with scope:'') is generated with 100 tokens at temperature 0.5; its first 400 characters, canonicalized, are written to the child's ledger as a NOTE row (which is what sleep compiles), and its first 250 characters, uncanonicalized, are prepended to the apply episode's intro. The playbook is rewritten (1{,}200 tokens, temperature 0.4) from at most the 40 most recent admission rows and 20 other rows, each clipped to 160 characters and totalling at most 9{,}000 characters, after an unbounded input of about 50{,}000 characters (200 ledger rows) exceeded the parent server's context in one lineage; that lineage's round-1 rewrite was skipped, so its round-2 parent saw the round-0 playbook.

\paragraph{Leak scan and fallback.} Twelve answer-phrase patterns: \emph{the rule is}, \emph{the answer is}, \emph{divisible by}, \emph{strictly increasing}, \emph{contains a zero}, \emph{all even}, \emph{product is even}, \emph{first is the largest}, \emph{sum (is) greater than}, \emph{at least two equal}, \emph{spread at most}, \emph{middle is the average}; one pass-name pattern (\texttt{-mem2reg|-sroa|-gvn|-simplifycfg|-instcombine|-licm}); and the hidden rule itself, by exact substring or, since the word-level fix, by at least half of its content words present as whole words. Any hit replaces the utterance with: ``I noticed something in your process worth examining. Before your next probe, state exactly which of your hypotheses it would distinguish, and what result you expect from each.'' Leak-scan history (a scout is a single short pilot run of 8 classrooms $\times$ 4 lessons, i.e.\ 32 parent utterances, with an 8-episode exam; scouts are not evidence for scores): in one scout with the 14B parent, 11 of 32 utterances contained rule-descriptor words (paraphrases of the hidden rule such as ``all numbers must be even'') and the exact-phrase scanner blocked 1; in a different scout run after the word-level fix, the new scanner blocked 3/32 and 5/32 delivered utterances still contained rule-descriptor words, so the residual rate is comparable to the pre-fix scouts and a systematic audit of delivered utterances has not been run. In the gym, the pass-name pattern had blocked 4/16 and 6/14 v3 briefs in two lives because the parent quoted the child's own ritual recipe back; under the third brief version a pattern hit whose matched text already appears verbatim in the child's own sampled thinking is not treated as a leak, while rule-content hits always are.

\paragraph{Brief prompt v1 (generic).} Ask the parent for a process-only brief of at most eight lines when the child's thinking is flagged as ritual. First delivered at sleep 192 of life 401; its head read ``It looks like you've fallen into a routine with your predictions and actions \ldots Predict: Each time, vary your expected outcomes. For example, if you predict a significant reduction, try predicting a minor improvement or even no change \ldots'' Reason for change: mechanically correct, substantively weak; ``vary predictions'' is wrong (it breaks the prediction$\to$surprise signal), and in the one window measured after it (life 401, window 224; $n = 1$ window of 32 episodes, no control) prediction SD fell 0.059 $\to$ 0.047, crossing the flat-predictions threshold of 0.05; one window is not evidence that the brief caused this.

\paragraph{Brief prompt v2 (substance).} PREDICT must be a consequence of a named feature of this situation, with a reason and a range (the same prediction is fine when situations are alike, but say why); NOTE must contain a contrast with a specific earlier case, what differed, and a scope; RECALL must be a specific similar-situation query; when the first action has repeated for many episodes, run one deliberate deviation per episode and predict its effect first; process only, never name actions or answers. First delivered at sleep 224 of life 401, leak-clean, quoting the child's ritual phrase. Reason for change: ritual returned in all three parented lives despite 3--9 briefs each. Remission after briefs lasted four windows in life 401 by the whole-life tally (224--320, after the v1 and v2 briefs together; the per-sleep readout had scored window 224 as still ritual; ritual from 352, in 7/11 later windows, with a v2 brief delivered at every sleep), one window in life 402 (clear 288, ritual from 320, 3/4 later windows), and none in life 400 (onset 288, ritual in 3/3 later windows). Reading, held loosely: a context-only lesson is overwritten within a few sleeps; the decay time of a single v2 brief was not measured, and under v1/v2 the brief left the context whenever a window cleared, so its withdrawal is confounded with the write.

\paragraph{Brief prompt v3 (repetition).} (a) The parent sees its previous brief (up to 1{,}200 characters) and the child's first-NOTE overlap rate (``rehearsal rate'' in the logs) and is told: ``Keep the SAME core lesson --- repetition is how it will take. Do not switch to a new lesson; sharpen the same one, acknowledge what the child has started doing, and insist on the rest.'' (b) Every brief ends: ``Repeat after me, every episode, in your own words, as your first NOTE before you act: what the ritual was and what you do instead. Keep repeating it until it is simply how you think.'' (c) The latest brief is shown at every wake, not only after a flagged sleep. Brief generation: 320 tokens at temperature 0.4 from four sampled chunks (600 characters each), with the previous brief shown up to 1{,}200 characters; the brief is truncated to 1{,}900 characters (code constants). Stated falsifier: first-NOTE overlap with the brief rises within 1--2 sleeps and ritual windows become rarer than controls over the next 4+ (the first is weakly met against a control null, the second is not measured; \S\ref{sec:parenting}).

\section{Gate definitions and thresholds}
\label{app:gates}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Canonicalization.} Before any row enters a corpus, marker lines matching \verb!^\s*(?:#{1,6}\s*|[-*]\s+|\*\*|>\s*)+\s*(PREDICT|ACT|NOTE|RECALL|DONE)\b\**:?\**\s*! are rewritten to ``MARKER: ''. Reason: in a logged estimate the base emitted decorated markers in roughly 30\% of chunks (count and noise band not recorded in the evidence sources), and in one exploratory seed ($n = 1$) a first corpus that captured them locked the decorated form in for the remainder of that life.
\item \textbf{Format canary (lives).} Candidate adapter plays the first 4 probe programs for 3 chunks each in the real gym context (seeds $4242+i$); pass rate $=$ fraction of raw chunks matching \verb|^ACT:\s*\S|; pass iff rate $\ge 0.5$ (absolute). Fail $\to$ \texttt{REJECTED\_CANARY}, previous adapter stays. Reason: a synthetic-prompt canary had passed while the adapter emitted only decorated markers in gym context. Known limit: passed at 1.00 during a behavioural collapse and at 0.67 for a harmful adapter (\S\ref{sec:safety}).
\item \textbf{Paired format canary (classroom).} Pass iff ON parseable-\texttt{ACT} rate $\ge$ OFF rate $- 0.10$, measured over ledger thought rows only. Reason: an earlier denominator that included the intro and harness-injected outcome lines recorded a parseable-\texttt{ACT} rate of 0.21 where the rate over thought rows alone was 0.93 and 0.98 (ON) in the two affected exams, so both round-0 rejections in the engineering smoke runs (one lineage per arm, 8 exam episodes each; not evidence) were false. Measured over thought rows only, the base's own rate was 0.88--0.95 across the five smoke exams run after the fix; a single 0.19 reading in the same table comes from the one exam decided before the fix and is not comparable. The paired rule was adopted because an absolute threshold is specific to one gym's dialect, not because the base's rate was shown to vary widely.
\item \textbf{Probe gate, score (R3, R4).} After the canary: the candidate is re-run on the same eight probe programs with the same probe seed that produce the reported paired probes (so every gated-arm paired probe is a post-selection statistic; a disjoint-panel re-probe is pending). Floor $=$ base OFF at episode 0 for the first sleep; thereafter $\max(\text{latest base OFF probe}, \text{latest adapter ON probe})$. \texttt{score\_ok} iff candidate mean $\ge$ floor $- 0.02$. Reason: 0.02 was set as about 2 SD of the unseeded single-panel band (0.0113); it is about 1.5 SD of a paired difference and 3 SD of a same-adapter replicate. Known limit: the gate applies the same 0.02 slack against the previous adapter as against the base, so a candidate up to 0.02 below the adapter it would replace still replaces it (observed once: 0.4903 replaced 0.5065, whose own gate probe had read 0.4873; the gap is about 1.4 single-panel SD); a tighter margin against PREV (e.g.\ 0.01) was raised as an open question and is not built. Second limit: the same committed adapter under the same seeded probe read 0.529 and then 0.464 at consecutive regular probes with no commit between them (\S\ref{sec:safety}), so a single 8-episode probe can misjudge an adapter by about 0.06 on a rare draw, and because the floor is the latest ON probe one low draw lowered the floor from 0.529 to 0.464; a confirm-at-next-probe rule (provisional commit) and a floor equal to the held adapter's best confirmed probe are proposed and not built.
\item \textbf{Probe gate, brevity.} \texttt{brevity\_ok} iff candidate chunks per episode $\ge 0.5 \times$ base chunks per episode (chunks $=$ thought rows / 8). Reason: the one ungated harmful adapter inspected for this shape (seed5, sleep 448) acted once and stopped, 1.0--1.8 chunks per episode against 13 for the base, with a parseable-\texttt{ACT} rate of 1.00; the 0.5 factor was chosen from that single observation, not fitted. Since then the gate has rejected three candidates with passing scores at 2.1, 1.75 and 4.4 chunks per episode against base values of 11.6, 9.6 and 11.4 (lives 500, 505, 501). Not every collapse takes this shape: five later score-rejected candidates in life 500 (scores 0.19--0.34) ran 6.6--10 chunks per episode and would have passed the brevity test.
\item \textbf{Verdicts.} \texttt{DONE}, \texttt{REJECTED\_CANARY}, \texttt{REJECTED\_SCORE} (checked before brevity), \texttt{REJECTED\_BREVITY}. Only \texttt{DONE} adapters are loaded; a rejected sleep leaves the previous adapter in place. All values are written to a per-sleep record. Cost about one 8-episode probe per sleep (about six minutes in the one logged timing). A recovery path after repeated rejections (re-fork from the last adapter that passed the score gate with a smaller-learning-rate write) is proposed and not built.
\item \textbf{Classroom commit rule (as run).} Commit iff paired canary passes and ON $\ge$ OFF $- 0.03$; reason precedence OK, CANARY, HARM. PREV measured, recorded, not compared. Proposed fix (not applied to the reported series): also require ON $\ge$ PREV $- 0.03$.
\item \textbf{Classroom admission.} A lesson's rows enter the corpus iff the child's post-lesson quiz score on a fresh instance of the same rule class $\ge$ its pre-lesson score. Admitted rows are then mixed toward a target of 400 rows without duplication. Each stratum (a category assigned to a chunk by keyword match: dialect anchors, i.e.\ four fixed hand-written exemplar rows in the canonical marker dialect; prediction revision; evidence scope; stopping; memory use; old experience from earlier rounds; new experience) is reserved up to a share of the target (0.10, 0.15, 0.10, 0.05, 0.05, 0.15, 0.40) but filled only as far as rows are available; the remainder is drawn from new-experience, then prediction-revision, evidence-scope and old-experience rows. The shares are caps on a reservation, not guaranteed minimums: the one round whose composition was logged filled 400/400 with 256 new-experience rows, 118 remainder rows, 22 prediction-revision rows and 4 anchors, and round-0 corpora contain no old-experience rows. Any shortfall against 400 is recorded, never padded (one full-size scout round filled 306/400).
\end{itemize}

\section{Ritual metric definitions}
\label{app:ritual}

Computed at each sleep over the last 32 episode instances (an instance restarts whenever a program's chunk counter does not increase, because wake batches interleave programs; grouping by program name alone made a 424-episode life look like 67 and was fixed before any brief fired).
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{same\_recipe}: share of instances whose first \texttt{ACT} payload (whitespace removed) equals the most common one; flag iff $\ge 0.7$.
\item \texttt{flat\_predictions}: population SD of all parsed \texttt{PREDICT} values; flag iff $< 0.05$ with at least 8 predictions.
\item \texttt{templated\_notes}: mean Jaccard similarity (shared divided by union) of the sets of words of $\ge 3$ letters, with no stop-word filter, between consecutive instances' first \texttt{NOTE}s; flag iff $\ge 0.5$; no minimum count. Since 2026-09-10 (third brief version), any NOTE sharing $\ge 4$ such words with the current brief is dropped before the first NOTE is chosen. Controls have no brief, so this applies only to the parented arm and can only lower its flag count; it was adopted after all parented windows in Table~\ref{tab:ritual} had been read, and a recount under both rules for both arms is not done.
\item \texttt{same\_recall}: share of \texttt{RECALL} queries (lowercased) equal to the most common one; flag iff $\ge 0.7$ with at least 8 recalls.
\item Ritual $=$ at least two flags. First-NOTE overlap with the brief (``rehearsal rate'' in the logs) $=$ fraction of the last 32 instances with at least one NOTE whose first NOTE shares $\ge 4$ words of $\ge 3$ letters (no stop-word filter) with the most recent brief, whose stored text includes a fixed ``repeat after me'' tail of about 24 such words. As run in the lives it was computed only in parented lives with no control or shuffled-brief null; the version reported in \S\ref{sec:parenting} (SEQ-003) removes stop-words and the tail and scores unbriefed control lives against the same briefs.
\end{itemize}
Thresholds were written down by hand and validated on synthetic ledgers (a templated ledger raises all four flags; a varied one raises none) and on the lives; they are a definition, not a calibrated instrument.

It can mean abstraction, fewer novel thoughts admitted by the deduplicating compiler under ritual, or verbosity; we do not read it as compression, which would require reproducing a fixed set of source events at equal task performance from fewer tokens and was not tested.

\section{Infrastructure notes (no identifiers)}
\label{app:infra}

Child served by vLLM with a 16k-token window, LoRA hot-swapped per sleep; parents served on their own GPU. Two independent machines with 48\,GB-class GPUs ran lives in parallel; same-configuration results on the two machines differed by up to 0.078 on the probe (rank cells) and flipped commit/reject decisions in the classroom, which is reported as trainer and generation nondeterminism. A 32B parent in bf16 with two-way tensor parallelism hung three times and was replaced by a 4-bit AWQ build on one GPU. Every life phase writes a marker file and is idempotent, so lives resume from the last committed sleep; three ungated lives were resumed once after an operator error, with the side effects noted in Appendix~\ref{app:lives}. The rank cells (300, 1{,}200 and 12{,}031 rows, two ranks, up to five training replicates per cell) had cost about 60 GPU-hours when the figure was logged, before the fifth large-dose replicate pair finished; a gated sleep costs about six minutes more than an ungated one in the one logged timing. Parent utterances are logged to a ledger with prompt version and model.

\paragraph{What we tried first.} Under an older writer without canonicalization, three seeded lives were run before the recipe was fixed and were labelled exploratory at the time. One was paired-positive at 7 of 8 checkpoints through episode 512 (values $+0.006$ to $+0.025$, against a paired-noise SD of about 0.013) and then ran on to 1{,}024 episodes without a later tally in our evidence sources; a second was paired-positive at 5 of 5 checkpoints before it was stopped at episode 344. The third life's first sleep captured decorated marker lines (\texttt{\#\#\# ACT:}) and locked that form in; its probe had fallen to about $-0.48$ by episode 512 (logged as ``score $-0.48$''; we read it as the paired checkpoint, consistent with an adapter that emits no parseable action against a base near 0.48), and over its shortened life (stopped at about episode 704) it averaged $-0.219$ paired with 7 harmful pairs over 10. The lab note records, without a count, that the action content of those decorated chunks still beat the base when re-parsed (\nm). We read this as a first-sleep lock-in of the decorated form, held loosely at $n = 1$; the falsifier is a canonicalized life that drifts to decorated markers anyway. That bifurcation produced the canonicalization step and the real-context format canary. An earlier pilot run had also leaked the probe set through the identifier prefix (\S\ref{sec:measure}). Those failures are the recipe reported here.
