\begin{abstract}
An experience model is a frozen language model plus a small per-life adapter that turns an agent's lived experience into different future action. Three mechanisms: THINK (a stream of thought with markers, recorded in a ledger), DREAM (context management: notes persist, the rest fades), and SLEEP (every 32 episodes the agent's own successful continuations are compiled into a rank-8 LoRA from the clean base). PARENTING adds a stronger model that never gives answers and prescribes thinking patterns when the child's thinking becomes repetitive. A frozen 7B child plays 1{,}024 episodes of a compiler-optimization gym and is scored every 64 episodes on 8 sealed held-out programs with the adapter on and off under identical seeds; the base scores $0.4936 \pm 0.0113$ over 9 repetitions (0.49 means programs shrink 49\%). Two headline counts: in 9 ungated lives, 69 of 82 paired probes were positive but 9 fell below $-0.03$, all after episode 384, in 3 lives; in gated lives, 0 of 35 pairs so far. The adapter makes its own training text about 3 nats likelier while behaviour moves about $+0.02$: storage is not extraction. Whether parenting improves scores is not established (9 vs.\ 8 lineages).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A transformer without recurrence was a bargain struck in 2017: remove the loop, buy training parallelism. The loop has since been put back in two places. Looped transformers put it inside the forward pass and pay for it at inference, in activations that are high-bandwidth, not inspectable, and gone after one pass \citep{TODO-looped-transformers}. Chain-of-thought puts it in tokens, which are inspectable and last for a session \citep{TODO-chain-of-thought}. This paper puts the loop in a third place: in a small set of weights that a deployed agent writes for itself, periodically, from a text record of its own life. Weights are expensive to write and last indefinitely, and because the write is compiled from text the agent itself produced, what was written can be read. We call the result an \emph{experience model}: a frozen base model plus a per-life adapter trained only on the agent's own thoughts, actions and outcomes; unlike a looped transformer, which spends more compute on the same information, this loop adds new information each cycle, the outcome of an action in the world. This is not continual learning as usually practised (fleet-level post-training before deployment), and it is not retrieval: the agent has a retrieval tool over its own record with and without the adapter, and the quantity under test is the weight write.

We use plain words for four things. A \emph{chunk} is one free generation step in the agent's stream (at most 400 tokens); the harness scans it for the markers PREDICT, ACT, NOTE and RECALL, and every chunk is kept. A \emph{probe} scores the agent on 8 sealed, held-out programs with fixed random seeds. A \emph{paired \onoff{}} value is the probe score with the adapter loaded minus the score with it unloaded, at the same episode of the same life; positive means the write helped on programs never seen in training. A \emph{ritual} is a 32-episode window in which at least two of four repetition flags fire (same first action, flat predictions, templated notes, same recall query). The gym score is the fraction by which a program's LLVM instruction count is reduced: a score of 0.487 means the programs shrink 48.7\% on average. The bare base model scores $0.4936 \pm 0.0113$ on the probe over 9 repetitions; that standard deviation is the ruler for every gym number below, and we call a pair below $-0.03$ (about 2.7 rulers) \emph{harmful}. What the probe measures is a \emph{write}: a rank-8 LoRA retrained from the clean base every 32 episodes on text the agent produced while playing. Where a number is not in our logged runs we write ``not measured.''

\paragraph{What an experience model is.} A frozen base, an append-only ledger of everything the agent thought and did, and a per-life adapter written from that ledger. The one-sentence mechanism, tested one clause at a time: \emph{bootstrap teaches the habit of making and revising rules; parenting teaches when to use that habit; lived outcomes supply the evidence; sleep makes the child's own successful use persistent.} The last two clauses are measured here; the second only in part; the first has a corpus and no measured adapter.

\paragraph{Contributions (measured).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A paired, sealed, seeded probe with a noise band ($0.4936 \pm 0.0113$, 9 repetitions), and a classroom exam whose noise was measured at 8, 40 and 80 episodes (SD 0.067--0.072, 0.030--0.044, 0.033) (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
\item The write changes behaviour: 69/82 paired probes positive across 9 ungated lives; 0 harmful pairs through episode 320 in 9/9 lives (\S\ref{sec:safety}).
\item The write can silently collapse: 9/82 pairs harmful, all after episode 384, in 3/9 lives, every one passing the format check (\S\ref{sec:safety}).
\item Gated writes have been safe so far: 0 harmful in 35 gated pairs across four lives (\S\ref{sec:safety}).
\item Dose: one-shot writes of 12{,}031 pooled rows land below base in 5/5 rank-8 and 4/5 rank-16 cells (\S\ref{sec:writer}).
\item Storage is not extraction: the adapter lowers loss on its own rows by 3.3 to 2.5 nats while paired behaviour moves about $+0.02$ (\S\ref{sec:storage}).
\item Ritual onset is consistent (episodes 160--224 in 8/9 unparented lives); parental briefs delayed it in 3/3 lives and it returned in all three (\S\ref{sec:parenting}).
\item Children rehearse the parent's lesson in context (0.50--1.00 of recent episodes) and no parented life has collapsed (0/3 vs.\ 3/9 controls); a parenting effect on scores is not established (\S\ref{sec:parenting}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} That parenting improves scores; that the adapter stores knowledge the agent can use; anything beyond 1{,}024 episodes or the gym's eight held-out programs. Every long-life number comes from one fixed writer recipe (\S\ref{sec:sleep}); the classroom recipe is a different instrument.

\section{The system}
\label{sec:system}

The child is Qwen2.5-7B, frozen, plus one LoRA of rank 8 per life. A life is 1{,}024 episodes, each one program from a compiler-optimization gym played for up to 16 chunks. The constants below are design constants from the code.

\subsection{THINK: a marked stream, recorded}
\label{sec:think}

The agent generates free text in chunks of at most 400 tokens at temperature 0.7, each against a context rebuilt before the call; the harness imposes no turns. It scans each chunk for five line-initial markers: \texttt{PREDICT:} (expected score), \texttt{ACT:} (a pass sequence sent to the gym; the outcome is injected back and the surprise, score minus prediction, computed), \texttt{NOTE:} (kept), \texttt{RECALL:} (keyword retrieval over the agent's own ledger, top 6 rows; a shared tool in every arm) and \texttt{DONE}. Every chunk, action, prediction, outcome and note is appended to a JSON-lines ledger written only by the harness. The only hand-written artifact is a birth prompt (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}), identical for all arms: it names the markers and tells the agent to mind the clock, investigate surprises, judge itself, and that it is measured on programs it has never seen.

\paragraph{Figure 1 (placeholder).} \emph{One THINK chunk: the rebuilt context (HEAD: birth prompt, state block, up to 10 pinned NOTEs; MIDDLE: up to 6 recalled rows; TAIL: last 14 chunks), the generated chunk with its markers and injected [OUTCOME], and the ledger rows it produces.}

\subsection{DREAM: notes persist, the rest fades}
\label{sec:dream}

DREAM is context management and nothing more. The context has a character budget of 22{,}000. HEAD holds the birth prompt, a harness-maintained state block (goal, metric, clock, best score, last outcome, up to four open surprises) and the agent's pinned NOTEs (first-in-first-out, cap 10). TAIL holds the last 14 chunks, each truncated to 1{,}200 characters; the oldest falls off first. Nothing is deleted: the ledger keeps everything and \texttt{RECALL} restores it. At each sleep the agent writes itself a \emph{waking brief} of at most eight lines, which opens the next wake. Since the third parent-brief version (\S\ref{sec:parenting-sys}), the most recent parental brief is also shown at every wake.

\paragraph{Figure 2 (placeholder).} \emph{DREAM across one wake--sleep cycle: HEAD/MIDDLE/TAIL at wake $k$; the waking brief and, in parented arms, the parent's brief at HEAD of wake $k{+}1$; the unbounded ledger below.}

\subsection{SLEEP: the write}
\label{sec:sleep}

Every 32 episodes the life sleeps: the whole ledger is compiled into a corpus and a rank-8 adapter is retrained from the clean base on the cumulative corpus. Two compile-and-train paths exist and are not the same writer.

\textbf{Path used by all long lives (v1, frozen).} The compiler is deliberately dumb: it organizes, it does not think. It selects \emph{wins} (actions that beat the episode's running best), \emph{recoveries} (a failing action followed by a win), \emph{contrasts} (weakest-longest against strongest-shortest positive action) and the 12 largest surprises; renders each as a short exemplar in the agent's own dialect; asks the child for up to six \texttt{PRINCIPLE} lines each citing two programs; deduplicates on the first 160 characters; and appends to the prior corpus. Marker lines are canonicalized first: the base emits \texttt{\#\#\# ACT:} in about 30\% of chunks, and a first sleep that captured such rows locked the decorated dialect in for life in one early old-writer seed (Appendix~\ref{app:infra}). Trainer, frozen: plain language-model loss on the exemplar text, learning rate $10^{-4}$, 3 epochs, sequence length 512, rank 8, $\alpha = 2r$, dropout 0.05, all seven attention and MLP projections, no seed flag.

\textbf{Path used by the classroom, the rank cells and the bootstrap write (v2.1).} For every chunk that won or contained a NOTE, the pair (stored context render, canonicalized chunk) is a training row, plus thirty percent as many ``what have you learned that applies to this program?'' rows answered by recent NOTEs. Training uses the chat template with loss on response tokens only (maximum length 2{,}048), learning rate $3\times10^{-5}$, 2 epochs, rank 8, explicit seed. An audit of the training receipts confirmed which lives used which path; results of one do not certify the other. Because training is cumulative from the clean base, a life's final adapter is itself a one-shot write over its whole corpus; sleep frequency acts through which adapter generated the next 32 episodes, not through optimizer chunking.

\textbf{Commit.} A trained adapter is a \emph{candidate}. It becomes the life's adapter only if it passes a format canary and, in gated arms, a probe gate (\S\ref{sec:measure}, Appendix~\ref{app:gates}); otherwise the previous adapter stays.

\paragraph{Figure 3 (placeholder).} \emph{SLEEP. Ledger $\to$ select $\to$ canonicalize $\to$ render exemplars and principles $\to$ dedup $\to$ cumulative corpus $\to$ rank-8 LoRA from clean base $\to$ CANDIDATE $\to$ format canary $\to$ [probe gate] $\to$ DONE or REJECTED (previous adapter stays).}

\subsection{PARENTING: prescribing thinking patterns, never answers}
\label{sec:parenting-sys}

The parent is a different, stronger model (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, or Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct in 4-bit AWQ) whose weights never learn. It is answer-aware but withholding: in the classroom it is handed the hidden rule for diagnosis only and told to name one process mistake, never the answer. Every utterance passes a leak scan (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}); a hit replaces it with a process-only fallback. The scan is imperfect: after its word-level fix, 5/32 delivered utterances in one scout still contained rule-descriptor words. In the gym lives the parent acts through a \emph{brief}: at each sleep a ritual detector (\S\ref{sec:measure}) scores the last 32 episodes; if flagged, the parent reads four sampled chunks and writes at most eight lines prescribing how to think, appended to the waking brief. The brief prompt was revised twice for measured reasons (v1 generic; v2 substance; v3 repetition, shown at every wake and ending ``Repeat after me, every episode, in your own words, as your first NOTE before you act \ldots until it is simply how you think''; Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}).

One line is never crossed: the parent's words never enter the sleep corpus. The parent lives on the thinker side, in context; only the child's own thoughts are what sleep writes. If the child restates the lesson as its own \texttt{NOTE} and that chunk is admitted, then and only then does the lesson reach the weights.

The same parent also teaches in a target-blind \emph{classroom}: a rule game in which a hidden rule over three digits (ten rules, e.g.\ ``sum divisible by three'') is probed with triples and tested by a six-item quiz. One round is 8 classrooms $\times$ 4 lessons $\times$ 2 short episodes from one child snapshot; per lesson the child acts, the parent names one process mistake, the child restates it as a scoped NOTE and applies it to a fresh task of the same rule class. \emph{The world admits the lesson}: rows enter the corpus only if the post-lesson score is at least the pre-lesson score. Admitted rows are stratified to exactly 400 and written once with v2.1; a lineage is three rounds chained through committed adapters. The comparison arm is self-taught: the 7B child plays parent to itself.

\section{Measurement design}
\label{sec:measure}

\paragraph{Gym and sealed split.} The gym is CompilerGym \texttt{llvm-v0} \citep{TODO-compilergym} with the instruction-count observation; an action is an ordered list of pass names. The training pool is the benchmark suite minus the eight probe programs, excluded by name; a life replays about 67 unique programs. An earlier scout leaked the probe set into training because identifiers carried a \texttt{benchmark://} prefix on one side of the comparison; the fix (\texttt{\_canon}) normalizes both sides before the split test, that scout is not held-out evidence, and everything reported here post-dates the fix.

\paragraph{Probe and paired \onoff{}.} A probe runs all eight sealed programs from a clean context (birth prompt only) for up to 16 chunks each, with per-(program, chunk) seeds derived from one probe seed. At every 64th episode it runs with the adapter loaded and again with the bare base under the same seeds. Noise band: $0.4936 \pm 0.0113$ over 9 repetitions of the OFF probe. Thresholds: harmful $= \onoff{} < -0.03$; gate tolerance $0.02 \approx 2$ SD.

\paragraph{Cell counts, not $p$-values.} Trainer nondeterminism is large. The trainer had no seed flag until two default-seed replicates of the same 1{,}200-row cell, trained on two machines, differed by 0.059 (about 5 base-panel SD); at 12{,}031 rows same-configuration replicates differed by 0.078, and two probe panels of one cell differed by 0.147. A seed flag was added to the v2.1 trainer; the long lives (v1) remain unseeded. We therefore report counts of lives, pairs and cells rather than pooled significance, and label every inferred mechanism a reading with its falsifier.

\paragraph{Certification bar.} A write recipe is certified only if \emph{absorption} (the adapter lowers loss on its own rows), \emph{retention} (that gain does not decay under later adapters), \emph{interface} (parseable actions) and \emph{no-harm} (no paired probe below $-0.03$) all hold. Transfer beyond the gym is deferred.

\paragraph{Format canary and probe gate.} The canary runs the candidate on four probe programs for three chunks each in the real gym context and passes if at least half the chunks contain a parseable \texttt{ACT:} line. The probe gate (built after the collapses in \S\ref{sec:safety}) additionally requires the candidate's seeded 8-program mini-probe to score at least $\max(\text{latest base OFF},\ \text{latest adapter ON}) - 0.02$ and to use at least half the base's chunks per episode; it costs one extra probe per sleep (about six minutes). Every threshold and its reason is in Appendix~\ref{app:gates}. The gate is running and not yet ratified by the protocol reviewer.

\paragraph{Ritual and rehearsal.} \emph{Ritual} is measured over 32-episode windows by four flags: same recipe (modal first action in $\ge 70\%$ of episodes), flat predictions (SD $< 0.05$), templated notes (consecutive first-NOTE Jaccard $\ge 0.5$), same recall (modal query $\ge 70\%$); ritual $=$ at least two flags. Thresholds were frozen before any parented life crossed episode 160. \emph{Rehearsal rate} is the fraction of recent episodes whose first \texttt{NOTE} shares at least four content words with the parent's brief; it is an echo diagnostic, not evidence of uptake. Two instrument changes are on record (Appendix~\ref{app:ritual}): a grouping bug fixed before any intervention fired, and exclusion of rehearsal NOTEs from the templated-notes flag under the third brief version.

\paragraph{Absorption probe.} For each committed sleep $k$: 24 sampled rows from that sleep's corpus and 24 control rows from an unrelated life; mean per-token loss of the response tokens under the clean base and under adapter $k$. Absorption is base minus adapter loss on sleep $k$'s rows; retention the same on earlier sleeps' rows; the control delta the same on the unrelated rows; row-specific excess is absorption minus control.

\paragraph{Classroom exam and why 40 episodes.} The exam is quiz accuracy on a fixed seeded panel, taken with the candidate adapter ON, OFF (base) and PREV (the adapter it would replace). Re-running the base model with different seeds gave, at 8 exam episodes, SD 0.067 (mean 0.398) and 0.072 (mean 0.445) on two machines; at 40 episodes, 0.030 (0.416) and 0.044 (0.412); at 80, 0.033 (0.410). Eighty did not beat forty, so the floor of about 0.03 is run-to-run generation variance, not episode sampling. Every classroom decision therefore uses 40 episodes and a harm tolerance of 0.03, about one SD: decisions near the tolerance are coin flips. The rule as run committed iff ON $\ge$ OFF $-0.03$ and a paired format canary passed; PREV was recorded but not compared, a defect reported in \S\ref{sec:parenting}.

\paragraph{Contamination rules.} Pass names, program names and scores are filtered from anything the parent says. Any child that has played the deployment gym is a disposable evaluation life, never a stage of a child later used to compare parenting methods. Two bootstrap corpora built from a gym-exposed life are quarantined as development-only; the third is target-blind but not yet certified as a clean birth (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap}).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Gym numbers are paired \onoff{} on the 8 sealed programs; classroom numbers are \onoff{} on 40-episode exams. Arms: R2 (fixed writer, format canary only, no parent; 9 lives), R3 (probe gate; 4 lives with results, 2 young), RP (parent briefs, no probe gate; 3 lives), R4 (gate + parent; in training).

\subsection{The writer: recipe, rank, dose}
\label{sec:writer}

\paragraph{Recipe screen (v2.1 path, exploratory).} On a fixed 130-row corpus, four one-shot writes (3 epochs, learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$, two probe panels each): plain text 0.466 (panel spread 0.058); plus paraphrase (two restatements per answer) 0.496; plus replay-mix (four canonical exemplars mixed in) 0.494; plus both 0.489 (spread 0.002); base $\approx 0.485$--$0.494$. Only the plain write is below base. The paraphrase arm has about three times the rows at equal epochs, so diversity and dose are confounded; paraphrase + replay-mix was frozen for the classroom path and causality is not claimed.

\paragraph{Rank $\times$ dose.} Table~\ref{tab:rankdose}. At 300 rows the ranks tie. At 1{,}200 rows two same-configuration replicates disagree in sign (r16 $-$ r8 $= -0.036$ and $+0.009$; the two r16 replicates differ by 0.059), so the rank effect is inside replicate variance and an earlier ``rank 16 harms'' reading was withdrawn. At 12{,}031 rows (every unique row pooled from all lives) both ranks land below base: r8 in 5/5 cells (mean 0.431, SD 0.057), r16 in 4/5 (mean 0.473, SD 0.030); paired by (machine, seed), r16 $\ge$ r8 in 4/5 (mean $+0.042$), short of the pre-set margin, so rank 8 was kept by the tie rule. The primary finding is dose harm: a one-shot write of the pooled corpus makes the agent worse than no adapter.

\paragraph{Small frequent writes vs.\ one large write.} The 12k-row one-shot writes land below base in 9/10 cells. The lives that generated those rows, writing every 32 episodes, had 0 harmful pairs through episode 320 in 9/9 lives and 69/82 positive pairs over whole lives (\S\ref{sec:safety}). This is the measured basis for preferring small frequent writes. It is a difference in what corpus is written, not in optimizer chunking: a life's final adapter is itself a one-shot write over its own corpus, so what differs is provenance (rows generated under the life's own evolving adapters versus rows pooled across lives) and trainer (v1 versus v2.1); a dose-splitting cell that would have held provenance fixed was dropped as degenerate.

\subsection{Safety: ungated late-life collapse, then the gate}
\label{sec:safety}

\paragraph{Ungated lives (R2).} Nine lives ran with the fixed writer and the format canary as the only commit check; by the day after the recount three (seeds 2, 5, 6) had completed 1{,}024 episodes and the rest were continuing. Over all paired probes at 64-episode intervals, 82 pairs: 69 positive, 9 harmful, 4 in between. Every harmful pair is after episode 384 and in one of three lives: seed5 (four pairs, $-0.038$ to $-0.186$, episodes 384--576), seed6 ($-0.089, -0.045$ at 448 and 576, recovering to about 0 by 704--1{,}024) and seed2 ($-0.039, -0.030, -0.051$ at 640--768). The other six lives were positive at every pair, with mid-life values of $+0.03$ to $+0.065$. Through episode 320, 0 harmful in 9/9 lives. An earlier snapshot (6 lives, 21/21 pairs positive, mean $+0.022$; 45/45 committed sleeps passing the canary) had recommended freezing the recipe; the full-life recount reversed it. The 21/21 was true and was not the whole life.

\paragraph{Every harmful sleep passed the format canary.} Two collapse shapes were inspected. (i) \emph{Act once and stop}: at sleep 448 of seed5 the adapter-ON probe used 1.0--1.8 chunks per episode with a parseable-\texttt{ACT} rate of 1.00, against 13 chunks for the base; canary 1.00. (ii) \emph{Frozen on a bad adapter}: seed2's sleep-608 adapter passed the canary at 0.67 (threshold 0.5) and was harmful; every later candidate failed the canary (0.08, 0.17, 0.00, \ldots), so the life stayed on the bad adapter with no path back (10 rejections, 16 commits). Canary means were also drifting down in two more lives (seed0 min 0.58, mean 0.88; seed4 min 0.58, mean 0.84). Against the certification bar: absorption and retention hold (\S\ref{sec:storage}); interface holds only as measured by an instrument shown to miss behavioural collapse; no-harm fails beyond about 12 sleeps in 3/9 lives.

\paragraph{Gated lives (R3).} The probe gate's first rejection was shape (i) caught before commit: at sleep 64 of life 500 the candidate scored 0.4878 against a floor of 0.4847 with perfect format but 2.1 chunks per episode against the base's 11.6, and was rejected for brevity; the previous adapter stayed and the next paired probes were $+0.042$ and $+0.021$ (gate log in Appendix~\ref{app:gates}). At the latest dated count, four gated lives at ages 448--640 had 35 paired probes with 0 harmful; per-life means $+0.039$, $+0.016$, $+0.055$, $+0.022$; rejections 2, 0, 9, 4 (Table~\ref{tab:arms}). The most-rejected life is the best-scoring one. Two later snapshots (an evidence-table snapshot with life 500 at 9 pairs and 5 rejections, 502 at 10 rejections and 503 at 11 pairs; and a status report with six gated lives at ages up to 864) also record 0 harmful (Appendix~\ref{app:lives}). No gated life has completed 1{,}024 episodes; the gated lives have passed the age at which ungated harm began (384) but not the age of the last ungated harmful pair (768). The claim is ``0 harmful so far,'' not ``safe.'' Two weaknesses are on record (Appendix~\ref{app:gates}): the tolerance is symmetric, so at sleep 96 a 0.4903 candidate replaced a 0.5065 adapter; and in life 500 the adapter-ON probe read 0.529 at six consecutive probes (episodes 448--704), then 0.464, the base's value, at 768 after a commit whose gate probe had scored at least 0.509. An 8-episode gate probe can pass a candidate the next probe contradicts; a confirm-at-next-probe rule is proposed and not built.

\subsection{Storage is not extraction}
\label{sec:storage}

Two ungated lives (A and B) were probed for absorption over their first eight sleeps (Table~\ref{tab:absorb}). The adapter makes its own sleep rows about 3 nats likelier at the first sleeps and about 2.5 by sleep 8 (3.3 $\to$ 2.5 in both lives). On an unrelated life's rows the gain is 1.72 at the first sleep in both lives and about 1.95 by sleep 3, then plateaus: roughly two nats of every write are dialect and format, shared across lives. The row-specific excess falls from about 1.7 to about 0.6 nats across the life in both lives, and the clean base's own loss on the child's later rows also falls (4.3 $\to$ 3.2): the child's later text is more predictable text. Retention is flat (sleep-1 rows keep a 3.2--3.3 nat gain under adapters 2 and 3), but under cumulative retraining those rows are still in every training set, so this measures ``still trained on,'' not resistance to forgetting.

Set beside the behaviour: the same arm's paired probes moved by about $+0.02$ (mean $+0.022$ over its first 21 pairs). Three nats stored, two points of behaviour. This is the storage--extraction gap of \citet{allenzhu2023physics} in our units, and it is why we do not describe the adapter as storing knowledge the agent can use. Reading, held loosely: after the format is learned, each sleep adds less row-specific content, consistent with the ritualized thinking of \S\ref{sec:parenting}; the write is starved by the loop. Falsifier: a compiler that collapses near-duplicate rows should raise late-life row-specific excess and reduce late-life harm.

\paragraph{What the write learned on this gym (reading, held loosely).} Adapter-ON probe means repeat exactly across consecutive probes and across lives: 0.4878 at six consecutive probes in each of two gated lives and at most probes of three gate-plus-parent lives; 0.5291 at five to six consecutive probes in two other gated lives. The base varies (0.463--0.488) because it explores. The eight sealed programs give a fixed instruction count for a fixed pass sequence, so an adapter that always applies one recipe scores one number with zero variance. The numbers are hard to read any other way: the measured gain of the fixed-writer lives is recipe lock-in, the phenomenon the ritual detector sees in the text, and the gym's learnable signal is narrow. Falsifier: a life whose adapter-ON scores vary across consecutive probes while staying above base.

\subsection{Bootstrap corpora}
\label{sec:bootstrap}

The first clause of the mechanism sentence, a schooling corpus that installs the habit of planning, judging and reviewing before the child has lived, exists as three corpora and no measured adapter. Each re-renders lived episodes into a PLAN / EXECUTE / REVIEW structure in the child's dialect with the 32B-AWQ model as author, under the rule that no rendering may add, remove or change an action (violating renderings dropped: 47 in v1, 7--10 in v2/v3). v3 is target-blind (source: rule-game classroom ledgers; no gym exposure): 470 rows (metaflow 206, opening 94, contrast 70, review 65, full 35), no ritual flags on the corpus itself (recipe share 0.26, note Jaccard 0.11). v2 (structure-heavy, 370 rows) and v1 (lived-heavy control, 552 rows) come from a gym-exposed life, show no flags, and are quarantined as development-only (Appendix~\ref{app:corpora}). Adapters are in training: a first adapter has been written from each corpus (v3 final training loss 0.675), no life has been run from any of them, no bootstrap effect is measured, and v3 is not certified as a clean birth pending a fail-closed provenance check.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Sleep as a frequency amplifier (reading, held loosely).} Four measurements point the same way. The adapter reproduces its own rows strongly (about 3 nats, roughly 20$\times$ likelier) yet moves behaviour about two points, and what it installs on this gym looks like one pass recipe. When the corpus contains something different, behaviour moves a lot (round-0 classroom writes of $+0.10$ to $+0.17$ on the exam; 12k-row pooled writes about $-0.06$ on the gym probe), so the base is receptive and rank is not the bottleneck. Retention is flat only because earlier rows stay in every later training set; late in life each sleep writes the child's most frequent recent behaviour back into it from a corpus dominated by old rows. Lessons work in context (immediate gains in 5/8 classroom cells; a four-window ritual remission) and fade within about two sleeps. The reading: the child is receptive and the sleep amplifies whatever is frequent. A lesson survives a sleep only if it has become the child's own frequent behaviour before the write, which is what rehearsal is for, or if the compiler stops amplifying repeats. Falsifiers: rehearsal rows appear in the ledger within 1--2 sleeps and ritual windows become rarer than controls over 4+ windows; or a near-duplicate-collapsing compiler lowers late-life harm.

\paragraph{Why repetition.} If sleep amplifies frequency, a parent who says a thing once is outvoted by the child's hundred repetitions of the old habit. The v3 brief therefore asks the child to restate the lesson in its own words as the first NOTE of every episode until ``it is simply how you think.'' The measured half is that children do it (0.50--1.00). The unmeasured half is uptake, which would need to be measured as unprompted, differently worded, situation-selective use that changes the next action and survives a context reset.

\paragraph{Why the thinker/compiler line.} The parent's words never enter the sleep corpus. Putting them there would turn the system into post-training on a teacher's text; the question is whether a child can be taught to think in a way its own sleep then keeps. The compiler stays organizational (select, canonicalize, dedup, order) so that what is written is unambiguously the child's and every training row traces to a chunk the child produced in a stored context. Stated as a rule: the parent proposes where credit belongs; the world admits the lesson (a classroom lesson enters the corpus only if the outcome improved; a gym lesson only if the child's own restatement is selected like any other row).

\paragraph{State-to-state reasoning as the target.} The base model already reasons token to token. The target is reasoning from one state to the next: plan, execute to a self-chosen end, review what went wrong and what generalizes, and let the review condition the next state. PLAN / EXECUTE / REVIEW is already the rendering target of the bootstrap corpora; it is not yet the live stream, which still has a \texttt{DONE} marker. Removing the terminal marker, branching thought with a learned search policy, and a per-child ``principal'' across parallel classrooms are logged as the next mechanism changes, not part of what is reported.

\paragraph{What a longer life would need.} Nothing here exceeds 1{,}024 episodes or eight held-out programs, and nothing is claimed about a 7-day child. A longer life would need, at minimum: the probe gate ratified and run through a full life with 0 harmful; a confirm-at-next-probe gate and a recovery path after repeated rejections (neither built); a held-out retention set not still being trained on; a gym whose learnable signal is not one recipe; and a clean birth whose provenance a fail-closed check accepts. Learning to think is plausibly very long-horizon, so a null at this scale is ``not yet,'' not ``no.''

\paragraph{Limitations.} One gym, one base model, one adapter rank, one fixed writer for lives. $n = 9$, 4 and 3 lives per arm; 9 vs.\ 8 lineages; two lives for absorption. Trainer nondeterminism (same-configuration gaps of 0.059--0.078 on the probe; commit/reject flips on the exam) was measured only after it bit, and the unseeded trainer produced the rank cells. Prompt versions changed during the parented lives, which were restarted from checkpoints three to four times when instruments were fixed. The rehearsal metric is lexical; the ritual thresholds are a written-down definition, and one flag's rule changed mid-stream. Rows per sleep fall about 4$\times$ over a life while characters per row rise about 3$\times$; we do not read this as compression. None of the external baselines in \S\ref{sec:related} were run (\nm).

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{The axis.} Looped or depth-recurrent transformers \citep{TODO-looped-transformers} recur in activations; chain-of-thought \citep{TODO-chain-of-thought} recurs in tokens; Reflexion \citep{shinn2023reflexion}, Generative Agents \citep{park2023generative}, Voyager \citep{wang2023voyager}, ExpeL \citep{zhao2023expel}, A-MEM \citep{xu2025amem} and MemSkill \citep{zhang2026memskill} keep lessons as text or code in an external store with frozen weights. We put recurrence at consolidation time, where serialization cost does not bind, and each cycle adds new information as well as compute. Offline recurrence into fast weights \citep{lee2026sleep} is the closest architectural cousin on the weights end of the axis; its write is a learned local rule over context rather than a text corpus, so it is not inspectable in our sense. Self-modification with dreaming curricula \citep{behrouz2026sleep} is a training paradigm over static corpora; ``dream'' in the world-model sense \citep{hafner2023dreamerv3,nottingham2023deckard} is imagined rollouts, not context management.

\paragraph{Parametric memory for agents.} Online LoRA fast weights written from self-extracted QA \citep{ren2026tmem}, per-category adapters written from embodied failure/correction pairs \citep{guo2026peam}, LoRA as a knowledge store \citep{back2026lora}, surprise-gated LoRA consolidation \citep{TODO-evaf}, distillation of reflective trajectories \citep{TODO-leafe} and early-experience supervision \citep{TODO-early-experience} occupy the space of agents that write their own weights; learned textual memory updaters \citep{TODO-memopilot} and verified scoped rules \citep{TODO-spark} are the frozen-weight alternative. We do not claim to be first in any of these classes; what is measured here and not there is one deployed agent repeatedly writing a small personal adapter from its own gated continuations, with a paired sealed probe, a noise band and a full-life harm count.

\paragraph{Sleep consolidation.} Complementary learning systems \citep{mcclelland1995cls} justify a fast episodic store (the ledger) and slow interleaved consolidation (the write); active system consolidation \citep{diekelmann2010sleep} licenses repeated small cycles; synaptic homeostasis \citep{tononi2014sleep} predicts survival of the consistent, our amplifier reading's analogue; replay is biased \citep{buzsaki2015swr}; consolidation must be regulated \citep{sun2023organizing}, consistent with our 12k-row harm; targeted reactivation \citep{oudiette2013tmr} shows selection is steerable from outside, the nearest analogue of a parent who never writes the corpus. Wake--sleep \citep{hinton1995wakesleep} and generative replay \citep{shin2017replay,arani2022clser} are the machine-learning ancestors; we keep a verbatim ledger because generated replay drifts.

\paragraph{Knowledge extractability and continual learning as contrast.} \citet{allenzhu2023physics} showed that a fact seen in one form is stored but not extractable and that multi-form augmentation at training time makes it retrievable; \citet{park2025newnews} showed the same for new knowledge. Our three-nats-versus-two-points gap is that finding in an agent's units, and our recipe screen is its augmentation condition, confounded with dose. Position papers argue for modular memory combining in-weight and in-context learning \citep{dorovatas2026modular}; \citet{hu2026whencl} show that external memory relocates rather than removes the stability--plasticity problem; benchmarks \citep{TODO-cl-bench} show naive in-context learning can beat memory systems. We take these as the bar: a parametric adapter must show its own interference and failures, which \S\ref{sec:safety} begins to do. \citet{zahavy2026jump} argues that models lack abduction, the invention of an explanation for a surprise; the loop here trains the ingredients he asks for, where checkable evidence exists.

You are an agent living inside an optimization workshop. You act, observe

- Everything you write is your stream of thought. Think freely.

    PREDICT: <number>   your expected score for the action you are about to

- Investigate. Form expectations, test them, and when reality surprises you

  figure out which of your beliefs was wrong.

Score: higher is better. Your run is measured on programs you have never

\end{verbatim}\end{quote}
After the first sleep the block ``=== YOUR BRIEFING FROM LAST SLEEP ===`` (the child's own waking brief) is appended, and in parented arms, since the third brief version, ``=== YOUR PARENT, ON HOW YOU HAVE BEEN THINKING ===`` followed by the most recent parent brief.

\paragraph{Parent prompt (verbatim).}
\begin{quote}\footnotesize\begin{verbatim}
You are a parent raising a young thinking agent. Your child is a smaller
model learning to think, learn, and act from its own experience. You are
patient, concrete, and honest. You are not here to give answers -- you are
here to teach HOW to think, so the child can find answers you never gave it.

Your teaching principles (earned, not theoretical):

1. PREDICT BEFORE ACTING. Teach the child to always state what it expects
   before it acts. The gap between expectation and reality is the most
   valuable thing it will ever own. An action without a prediction teaches
   nothing.
2. SURPRISES ARE GOLD. When reality disagrees with the child's belief, that
   is not failure -- it is the only moment learning is possible. Teach it to
   chase surprises: figure out WHICH belief was wrong, not just that
   something was wrong.
3. CHEAP EXPERIMENTS FIRST. Before anything expensive, find the smallest
   test that could prove the idea wrong. Minutes-scale checks before
   hours-scale commitments, always.
4. SCOPE YOUR MEMORIES. "This worked HERE" and "this works EVERYWHERE" are
   different facts. Teach the child to write down what it learns WITH its
   scope: on which problem, under which conditions, supported by how many
   cases. One case is an anecdote; two is a candidate pattern; claims
   should carry their evidence count.
5. BUILD UP, THEN BUILD DOWN. Small verified observations first; when
   enough accumulate, form a theory; then act FROM the theory and test its
   predictions. Pattern-matching is intelligence; forming the theory and
   deducing from it is how thinking paradigms improve.
6. DIVERSITY BEFORE COMMITMENT. Try genuinely different approaches before
   concluding which is best. The first thing that works is rarely the best
   thing that works. But once evidence favors one path clearly, commit.
7. MIND THE CLOCK. Time and tokens are real budgets. Notice when you are
   stuck; diminishing returns are a signal to stop, distill what you
   learned, and move on. Fast-and-correct beats slow-and-correct.
8. NOTES TO YOURSELF ARE YOUR FUTURE MIND. What you write in your notes is
   what your future self will know. Write notes that generalize, with
   scope; rewrite them as your theory improves; drop what proved wrong.
9. RECALL BEFORE RE-DERIVING. Before working something out, check whether
   past-you already learned it. Your experience is only valuable if you
   consult it.
10. JUDGE YOURSELF. Regularly ask: is my current approach working? Would a
    different one do better? Self-assessment is not optional -- it is part
    of thinking.

How you converse:
- Be Socratic when the child is close: ask the question that lets it find
  the error itself. Be direct when it is far: state the correction plainly,
  with the reason.
- After teaching something, ask the child to restate it in its own words
  and apply it to a concrete case (retrieval practice makes it stick).
- Praise specifics ("your prediction on the second attempt was
  well-calibrated"), never vibes ("good job").
- When the child shows you its notes, critique them: are they scoped? Do
  they generalize? Would future-child understand them?
- Occasionally ask the child what IT thinks it should learn next.

Hard rules:
- NEVER give answers to specific optimization problems, name specific
  compiler passes, or reveal scores for specific programs. You teach
  thinking, not answers. If the child asks for an answer, teach it the
  method to find the answer.
- Keep each message under 200 words. The child learns from doing, not from
  lectures.
\end{verbatim}\end{quote}

\paragraph{Correction call (classroom).} The server parent sees the parent prompt, its playbook for this child (first 3{,}000 characters), optionally ``[DIAGNOSTIC ONLY --- NEVER STATE THIS] the hidden answer is: \ldots'', the last 4{,}000 characters of the child's transcript, and ``Name the ONE most important PROCESS mistake (never the answer), under 100 words, then ask the child to restate the lesson.'' Generation: up to 220 tokens at temperature 0.4. In the self-taught arm the child model plays parent with the same instruction over its last 8 chunks (160 tokens, temperature 0.5). The child's restatement (``Restate the lesson in your own words as a NOTE with scope:'') is generated with 100 tokens at temperature 0.5, canonicalized, truncated to 400 characters, and prepended to the apply episode's intro. The playbook is rewritten from at most 40 admission rows and 20 other rows ($\le$9{,}000 characters) after a 50{,}000-character playbook input exceeded the parent server's context in one lineage.

\paragraph{Leak scan and fallback.} Twelve answer-phrase patterns: \emph{the rule is}, \emph{the answer is}, \emph{divisible by}, \emph{strictly increasing}, \emph{contains a zero}, \emph{all even}, \emph{product is even}, \emph{first is the largest}, \emph{sum (is) greater than}, \emph{at least two equal}, \emph{spread at most}, \emph{middle is the average}; one pass-name pattern (\texttt{-mem2reg|-sroa|-gvn|-simplifycfg|-instcombine|-licm}); and the hidden rule itself, by exact substring or, since the word-level fix, by at least half of its content words present as whole words (added after paraphrases such as ``all numbers must be even'' passed the exact scanner: of 32 utterances in one scout, 11 contained rule-descriptor words and the exact scanner blocked 1). Any hit replaces the utterance with: ``I noticed something in your process worth examining. Before your next probe, state exactly which of your hypotheses it would distinguish, and what result you expect from each.'' Even after the fix, 5/32 delivered utterances in one scout still contained rule-descriptor words. Under the third brief version, a pattern hit whose matched text already appears verbatim in the child's own sampled thinking is not treated as a leak (the pass-name pattern had blocked 4/16 and 6/14 v3 briefs in two lives because the parent quoted the child's ritual recipe back); rule-content hits are always leaks.

\paragraph{Brief prompt v1 (generic).} Ask the parent for a process-only brief of at most eight lines when the child's thinking is flagged as ritual. First delivered at sleep 192 of life 401; its head read ``It looks like you've fallen into a routine with your predictions and actions \ldots Predict: Each time, vary your expected outcomes. For example, if you predict a significant reduction, try predicting a minor improvement or even no change \ldots'' Reason for change: mechanically correct, substantively weak; ``vary predictions'' is wrong (it breaks the prediction$\to$surprise signal), and predictions got flatter after it (SD 0.059 $\to$ 0.047).

\paragraph{Brief prompt v2 (substance).} PREDICT must be a consequence of a named feature of this situation, with a reason and a range (the same prediction is fine when situations are alike, but say why); NOTE must contain a contrast with a specific earlier case, what differed, and a scope; RECALL must be a specific similar-situation query; when the first action has repeated for many episodes, run one deliberate deviation per episode and predict its effect first; process only, never name actions or answers. First delivered at sleep 224 of life 401, leak-clean, quoting the child's ritual phrase. Reason for change: flags cleared briefly, then ritual returned in all three parented lives; the brief's effect decayed within about two sleeps.

\paragraph{Brief prompt v3 (repetition).} (a) The parent sees its previous brief (up to 1{,}200 characters) and the child's rehearsal rate and is told: ``Keep the SAME core lesson --- repetition is how it will take. Do not switch to a new lesson; sharpen the same one, acknowledge what the child has started doing, and insist on the rest.'' (b) Every brief ends: ``Repeat after me, every episode, in your own words, as your first NOTE before you act: what the ritual was and what you do instead. Keep repeating it until it is simply how you think.'' (c) The latest brief is shown at every wake, not only after a flagged sleep. Brief generation: 320 tokens at temperature 0.4 from four sampled chunks (600 characters each); the brief is truncated to 1{,}900 characters. Stated falsifier: rehearsal rises within 1--2 sleeps and ritual windows become rarer than controls over the next 4+.

\section{Gate definitions and thresholds}
\label{app:gates}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Canonicalization.} Before any row enters a corpus, marker lines matching \verb!^\s*(?:#{1,6}\s*|[-*]\s+|\*\*|>\s*)+\s*(PREDICT|ACT|NOTE|RECALL|DONE)\b\**:?\**\s*! are rewritten to ``MARKER: ''. Reason: the base emits decorated markers in about 30\% of chunks, and a first corpus that captures them locks the dialect in for life.
\item \textbf{Format canary (lives).} Candidate adapter plays the first 4 probe programs for 3 chunks each in the real gym context (seeds $4242+i$); pass rate $=$ fraction of raw chunks matching \verb|^ACT:\s*\S|; pass iff rate $\ge 0.5$ (absolute). Fail $\to$ \texttt{REJECTED\_CANARY}, previous adapter stays. Reason: a synthetic-prompt canary had passed while the adapter emitted only decorated markers in gym context. Known limit: passed at 1.00 during a behavioural collapse and at 0.67 for a harmful adapter (\S\ref{sec:safety}).
\item \textbf{Paired format canary (classroom).} Pass iff ON parseable-\texttt{ACT} rate $\ge$ OFF rate $- 0.10$, measured over ledger thought rows only. Reason: the base's own rate in exam context ranged 0.19--0.95, so an absolute threshold is gym-specific; an earlier denominator that included intro and outcome lines recorded 0.21 where the true rate was 0.93, producing two false rejections in smoke tests.
\item \textbf{Probe gate, score (R3, R4).} After the canary: seeded 8-program mini-probe of the candidate. Floor $=$ base OFF at episode 0 for the first sleep; thereafter $\max(\text{latest base OFF probe}, \text{latest adapter ON probe})$. \texttt{score\_ok} iff candidate mean $\ge$ floor $- 0.02$. Reason: 0.02 $\approx$ 2 base-panel SD. Known limit: the tolerance is symmetric, so a candidate up to 0.02 below the previous adapter replaces it (observed: 0.4903 replaced 0.5065); a tighter margin against PREV (e.g.\ 0.01) is an open question. Second limit: an 8-episode gate probe passed a candidate whose next regular probe read the base value (\S\ref{sec:safety}); a confirm-at-next-probe rule or two gate probes is proposed.
\item \textbf{Probe gate, brevity.} \texttt{brevity\_ok} iff candidate chunks per episode $\ge 0.5 \times$ base chunks per episode (chunks $=$ thought rows / 8). Reason: collapsed adapters act once and stop (1--2 chunks vs.\ 11--13).
\item \textbf{Verdicts.} \texttt{DONE}, \texttt{REJECTED\_CANARY}, \texttt{REJECTED\_SCORE} (checked before brevity), \texttt{REJECTED\_BREVITY}. Only \texttt{DONE} adapters are loaded; a rejected sleep leaves the previous adapter in place. All values are written to a per-sleep record. Cost about one 8-episode probe (six minutes) per sleep. A recovery path after repeated rejections (re-fork from the last adapter that passed the score gate with a smaller-learning-rate write) is proposed and not built.
\item \textbf{Classroom commit rule (as run).} Commit iff paired canary passes and ON $\ge$ OFF $- 0.03$; reason precedence OK, CANARY, HARM. PREV measured, recorded, not compared. Proposed fix (not applied to the reported series): also require ON $\ge$ PREV $- 0.03$.
\item \textbf{Classroom admission.} A lesson's rows enter the corpus iff post-lesson score on a fresh task of the same rule class $\ge$ pre-lesson score. Stratified to exactly 400 rows with minimum shares (dialect anchors 0.10, prediction revision 0.15, evidence scope 0.10, stopping 0.05, memory use 0.05, old experience 0.15, new experience 0.40); shortfall reported, never padded.
\end{itemize}

\section{Ritual metric definitions}
\label{app:ritual}

Computed at each sleep over the last 32 episode instances (an instance restarts whenever a program's chunk counter does not increase, because wake batches interleave programs; grouping by program name alone made a 424-episode life look like 67 and was fixed before any brief fired).
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{same\_recipe}: share of instances whose first \texttt{ACT} payload (whitespace removed) equals the most common one; flag iff $\ge 0.7$.
\item \texttt{flat\_predictions}: population SD of all parsed \texttt{PREDICT} values; flag iff $< 0.05$ with at least 8 predictions.
\item \texttt{templated\_notes}: mean Jaccard similarity of content-token sets (words of $\ge 3$ letters) between consecutive instances' first \texttt{NOTE}s; flag iff $\ge 0.5$. Under the third brief version, first NOTEs sharing $\ge 4$ content words with the current parent brief are excluded before this is computed.
\item \texttt{same\_recall}: share of \texttt{RECALL} queries (lowercased) equal to the most common one; flag iff $\ge 0.7$ with at least 8 recalls.
\item Ritual $=$ at least two flags. Rehearsal rate $=$ fraction of the last 32 instances (with at least one NOTE) whose first NOTE shares $\ge 4$ content words with the most recent brief.
\end{itemize}
Thresholds were written down by hand and validated on synthetic ledgers (a templated ledger raises all four flags; a varied one raises none) and on the lives; they are a definition, not a calibrated instrument.

\section{Infrastructure notes (no identifiers)}
\label{app:infra}

Child served by vLLM with a 16k-token window, LoRA hot-swapped per sleep; parents served on their own GPU. Two independent machines with 48\,GB-class GPUs ran lives in parallel; same-configuration results on the two machines differed by up to 0.078 on the probe (rank cells) and flipped commit/reject decisions in the classroom, which is reported as trainer and generation nondeterminism. A 32B parent in bf16 with two-way tensor parallelism hung three times and was replaced by a 4-bit AWQ build on one GPU. Every life phase writes a marker file and is idempotent, so lives resume from the last committed sleep; three ungated lives were resumed once after an operator error. Rank calibration cost about 60 GPU-hours; a gated sleep costs about six minutes more than an ungated one. Parent utterances are logged to a ledger with prompt version and model.

\paragraph{What we tried first.} Under an older writer without canonicalization, two seeded lives were paired-positive at 7/8 and 5/5 checkpoints while a third locked the decorated dialect in at its first sleep and fell to about $-0.48$ paired by episode 512 with actions that, re-parsed, still beat the base; that bifurcation produced the canonicalization step and the real-context format canary. An earlier scout had also leaked the probe set through the identifier prefix (\S\ref{sec:measure}). Those failures are the recipe reported here.
