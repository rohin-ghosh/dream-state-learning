Teaching excerpt: a close retelling of Chekhov's "Misery" supplied by Fable,
not an assertion about this learner or an original quotation from the story.

Iona drives a horse-cab through wet snow. His son died this week. He tries
to tell each passenger, but they dismiss him, demand speed, or fall asleep.
At night he goes to his little horse, eating hay in the yard, and tells her
how his son fell ill and what he said before he died. The horse chews,
breathes on his hands, and listens.

After delivering the passage, ask the child what response it has to it and
which detail prompted that response. Do not presume it feels lonely or shares
the character's human experience. Follow up on what it actually says.
