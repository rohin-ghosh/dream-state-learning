## 1. Area-chair verdict

**On v2 as received.** An honest, unusually well-provenanced characterization paper whose headline numbers are a null (the write installs the birth prompt's own recipe), a failure (late-life collapse in 4/9 ungated lives that a format canary cannot see) and a post-selection statistic (the gate). Every FATAL/MAJOR item of the harsh review was satisfied or disclosed in the text, the red-line phrases were gone, infrastructure identifiers were gone, and the STOP was respected. But it carried two fatal defects of its own making and about twenty majors, almost all text: the abstract's first number (7/9 life means at or above +0.02) was a miscount against the paper's own Table 2 (it is 6/9, with 3/9 below zero), inherited from a wrong sentence in the SEQ-001 notebook prose; and the PI's binding first-page positioning rule (TMEM as the shoulder we stand on) was unmet, with the intro's "usually done once, fleet-wide" clause reading as an implicit first-ness claim that the team's own notes forbid. Around those: a false "not measured" for per-pair harm episodes that are in the paper's own cited JSON; a recipe-lock-in mechanism sentence generalized from the gated arm to the ungated arm that carries the gain; per-program and ritual-in-weights numbers pooled over post-selection and old-writer lives while labelled "R2"; a mislabelled leak scanner; a firewall claim ("the parent's words never enter the corpus") the design does not make; fifteen `[?]` citations; a freeze footnote and an undefined "protocol reviewer" in the body; tables that do not fit the column; and no figure.

**On v3 after this pass.** Both fatal items are closed by text (the miscount corrected everywhere with a dated erratum in the notebook; TMEM named on page one with the positioning paragraph and the delta table in an appendix). Of the twenty-one distinct major items, nineteen are closed or disclosed by text; two are not fixable by text and remain: there is still no data figure, and the tables' fit and the 9-page count are still uncompiled estimates. Four other majors are closed as disclosures ("not measured") rather than measurements because the data live only on the GPU nodes: absolute adapter-ON values for the ungated lives, the held-adapter replicate tally, an R2-only per-program split, and the count of corpus rows that restate a parent brief. The bibliography is now real except for two keys, but sixteen canonical entries were added from the standard record and must be checked against arXiv before the 09-15 compile.

**Acceptance estimate for v3 as it stands.** Borderline reject, scores 3–5. The paper is now internally consistent and every claim traces; what it lacks is the one experiment its own finding makes decisive (a text-memory or recipe-in-context control) and any figure. Estimates from the five lenses on v2 were 0.15, 0.15, 0.15, 0.25 and 0.15 (mean 0.17). My estimate for v3: **0.18** with the bib verified and a Figure 1 placed; **0.30** if the text-memory/recipe-line control (about 5–10 GPU-hours on node 2, before 09-20) and the full disjoint-panel re-probe both land and are reported honestly, whichever way they go; **0.05** if the PDF ships with `[?]` citations or over nine pages.

**Strongest objection that survives.** "The title asks what a per-life adapter learns. The answer is the four-pass recipe printed in its own birth prompt. A single line of context would score the same, the agent already writes itself a brief carrying that recipe, and you stripped the brief from both probe arms and never ran a text arm." v3 now says this in the abstract's scope ("in gated lives … ungated adapter-on scores are untabulated"), in "What we do not claim" ("nothing here shows the weight write does what a line of text would not") and in Limitations; disclosure is the most a text pass can do. The control is the single most valuable experiment before 09-25 (see §6).
