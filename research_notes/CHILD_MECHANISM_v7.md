**What this document is.** The specification of the child's mechanism: the loop it lives in, the write that changes its weights, the brake, and the instruments. Once the pretests of `PRETESTS_2026-09-11.md` have set the open numbers (write organization, learning rate, epochs, the write budget), this mechanism is frozen for the whole lineage. Parents, gyms, tests, briefs and guidance may change at any time while the child carries on; the mechanism may not. The untaught comparator that lives in the compiler gym runs this identical mechanism (section 8).

**What this document is not.** Not code. The build workflow that is modifying `organism_v6` tonight implements it; Appendix C lists what the code must do, in three tiers with cut lines, as a checklist for that build. Not the parenting design (that is `PARENT_HARNESS_SPEC_ADDENDUM_2026-09-10.md` and `CURRICULUM_DRAFT_v1.md`). Not the paper.

**Plain-language glossary** (each term is also explained where it first matters). *Base model* — Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, a fixed 7-billion-parameter language model whose weights never change. *Adapter* (a LoRA) — a small set of extra weights bolted onto the base model; training the adapter is cheap, and swapping adapters swaps what the model has learned; the adapter is the only thing that ever learns. *Rank* — the adapter's size knob; rank 32 has four times the capacity of rank 8. *Token* — the unit a language model reads and writes, roughly three quarters of a word. *Token-pass* — one token read once by the trainer; reading one million tokens twice is two million token-passes. It is the quantity that sets how long a write takes. *Epoch* (also *pass*) — one complete reading of a training set. *Turn* (also *chunk*) — one stretch of the child's writing, up to 400 tokens, produced by one call to the model. *Problem* (also *episode*, *situation*) — one task the gym poses, with a budget of 16 turns. *Gym* — an environment that poses problems and scores answers exactly, with no judge model in the loop. *Ledger* — the append-only file that records every turn, action and outcome of a life; nothing is ever deleted from it. *Corpus* — every thought in every ledger of the lineage, organized for training. *Write-corpus* — the part of the corpus one write actually reads (section 2.3). *Round* — 32 problems; the unit after which the child's new thoughts go to the writer. *Sleep* / *write* — training a fresh adapter on the ledger's text. *Writer* — the process, on its own GPU, that does the writes. *Lag* — how many rounds old the newest thoughts in the active adapter are. *Wake* — the problems played between two sleeps. *Clone* — one running copy of the child (one per GPU), all clones sharing one adapter lineage. *Brief* — the text the child reads at the start of each wake: its birth brief, its own summary from the last sleep, and its parents' latest note. *Probe* / *exam* — playing a fixed panel of held-out problems with a fixed random seed, so scores are comparable across time and across children. *Seed* — the number that fixes the randomness of generation; the same seed gives the same draw. *SD* — standard deviation, the typical size of the noise in a measurement. *Floor* — the score a new adapter is compared against. *Frozen model* — the base model with no adapter (also "adapter OFF"). *Commit* — an adapter enters the record (saved, examined, scored); *activate* — the clones start acting with it. Every trained adapter that passes the brake is committed; which committed adapter is active is decided by the patience rule (section 3.3). *Gradient checkpointing* — a memory-saving trick in training that recomputes some intermediate results instead of storing them; about a third slower, but it lets long sequences fit on one GPU.

## 0. One screen

The child is Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct plus one adapter of rank 32 — a small set of extra weights that is the only thing that ever learns. It lives in gyms: environments that hand it problems and score its answers exactly.

It thinks in free text, in turns of up to 400 tokens. The harness understands exactly one thing in that text: a line beginning `ACT:` is the move the gym scores. Everything else it writes — plans, predictions, notes to itself, questions, complaints, words to its parent — is its own thinking: kept forever in the ledger, never parsed into a function, only counted. A problem lasts at most 16 turns; it ends sooner only if the gym says it is solved. If the child goes 4 turns without acting, the harness tells it, in one line, to turn something in, and says so again after every further 4 silent turns. When a problem ends, the next one simply arrives; the child never decides to stop. It thinks until someone pulls the plug.

After every 32 problems (a round) it gets 4 turns of private time: it reads a short summary of what it just lived and writes whatever it wants. The parents may read this; they never mention it. Then the round's thoughts go to the writer.

The writer (sleep) does not choose what to remember. Every thought the child has ever had is kept in the corpus — repeats included — and organized two ways: whole problems in order, so each thought stays next to the situation that triggered it, and short windows around each action. Related material is packed as neighbours. At each write a fresh adapter is trained from the base model over as much of the corpus as one write can read: a fixed budget of about 5 million token-passes (about 1.2 hours), the newest round in full and the rest as a sample. So every thought is trained when it is new and re-read a few more times over the lineage's life — not at every write; the exact rate is a decision for Rohin (section 9, D1). Only the child's own text is ever trained on; the gym's lines sit in the sequences as context that is not learned; the parents' words and every brief are removed from the sequences altogether.

Many clones live at once, one per GPU, each in its own gym with its own two parents. Their ledgers feed one writer on its own GPU, which trains about one round behind, examines each new adapter on a parent-free exam, and publishes; each clone picks the new adapter up at its next batch of problems and never waits for anything. At wake the child sees its birth brief, its own summary from the last sleep, and its parents' latest brief; the parents' words reach its weights only if the child repeats them in its own words.

Nothing is refused for scoring badly. A write is refused only when the child can no longer turn work in (broken format) or has stopped working (fewer than half the words per problem of the frozen model, on the exam problems neither of them solved). Scores are watched with patience: the writer examines every adapter before publishing it; if three consecutive adapters score below the best version ever committed by more than one noise width, the lineage returns to that best version and the parents are told.

The final test is a gym the lineage has never seen — the compiler gym — where the taught child and an untaught child with the identical mechanism each live a life from an empty ledger under the identical loop (one clone plus its own writer), and we compare how fast each learns.

## 1. The loop

### 1.1 Free-thinking turns
- One turn = one model call with temperature 0.7 and at most 400 new tokens (unchanged from v6, `model_backend.py`). The child's context is rebuilt before every turn from three parts (unchanged layout, `state.py`): the HEAD (the brief, then the mechanical state of the current problem: the goal text from the gym, the metric, the clock line "turn k of 16", the best score so far and the last outcome), the TAIL (the child's own recent turns, most recent last, up to 14 turns within a 22,000-character budget; the oldest fall off first), and the harness lines interleaved in the tail where they happened (outcomes, the nudge).
- Gone from the head relative to v6: the "YOUR NOTES" block (v6 pinned the child's `NOTE:` lines), the "RECALLED EXPERIENCE" block (v6 retrieved ledger rows on `RECALL:`), and "OPEN SURPRISES" (v6 computed prediction minus outcome from `PREDICT:`). Section 6 gives the measured reasons.
- The problem's first line in the tail is neutral and in the third person: `New problem: <id>.` Today's intro lines put a first-person thought in the child's mouth ("I should form an expectation before acting, and write down what I learn", `gym_backend.Episode.intro`; "I should read the rules, form an expectation, attempt, and learn from the score", `reasoning_gym_gym.py`) — that prescribes the very behaviours ruling 4 leaves the child to learn, so it goes (Appendix C, item 1).
- The child may write anything. Nothing in its text is parsed except `ACT:` (1.2). Predictions, notes, questions, self-checks, references to earlier problems and words addressed to its parent are all just text; section 5 counts them.

### 1.2 The single interface: `ACT:`
- A line whose first non-decoration characters are `ACT:` — the colon is required — followed by something is an action. Decoration before it is stripped before parsing: Markdown (`### ACT:`, `**ACT:**`, `- ACT:`, `> ACT:`), backticks and code fences (the birth brief itself writes `ACT:` in backticks, so a model that copies the form will sometimes write it that way). The base model writes such decoration about 30% of the time (`sleep_compile.canonicalize_dialect`, measured 2026-09-07), and refusing it would be a format trap, not a lesson. Today's regex (`batch_loop.py` line 16) makes the colon optional, so a line beginning "ACTIONS I could take" would submit "IONS I could take" as a move; v7 requires the colon.
- What follows `ACT:` is handed to the gym as the move, one line. The gym scores it and the harness appends one line to the stream: `[OUTCOME] <the gym's text> (score s)`. Every `ACT:` line in a turn is scored, in order (as today). The format of the move is stated in the gym's goal text in the head, never in the birth brief, and never with an example move (no recipe: section 6). For the compiler gym this needs one new sentence in the goal text — pass names (a pass is one compiler optimization step) with a leading dash, comma-separated, no example — because today the syntax lives only in `bootstrap.txt`, which v7 removes (Appendix C, item 1; a prerequisite of pretest P2's compiler cell).
- Parse misses are an instrument, not a silent zero: a turn that contains the letters "ACT" with no parsed action is logged as a *parse-miss candidate* (5.13), so the fraction of lost moves is measured rather than inferred from scores.
- The ledger records each action as a row of kind `act` (episode id, turn, action text, outcome text, score) and each turn as a row of kind `thought` (the child's text, the rendered context it saw, the adapter round it was running under).

### 1.3 Budget and the nudge rule
- Budget: 16 turns per problem (inherited; the pilot that would have calibrated it, DP14 of the design, cannot run before the freeze — recorded as an unruled default, section 9). The measured fact behind the number, stated as a cell count: of the 24 v6 lives with a late-life reading, 11 ended their late problems by the budget (15.4–16.0 turns of 16: R2 seeds 0/1/2/4/7/8, R3 503, R4 604/605/606, RP 402) and 13 did not (1.0–15.3 turns: R2 seed3 12.8, seed5 5.4, seed6 1.0; R3 500 6.6, 501 12.8, 502 9.7, 504 14.4, 505 2.5; R4 600 13.9, 601 15.8, 602 13.1, 603 15.3; RP 401 7.8 — SEQ-011). Several of the 13 ended problems early by `DONE` without collapsing. The removal of the end token therefore rests on ruling 4 (stopping is the harness's, never the child's), not on the claim that every life ran to the budget anyway.
- Nudge: whenever 4 consecutive turns of a problem contain no `ACT:` line, the harness appends one line to the stream — `[NUDGE] You have thought for 4 turns without acting. Turn something in: write a line beginning ACT: with your best attempt so far.` — so at most 3 nudges in a 16-turn problem. (One rule, stated the same way in section 0, here and in the birth brief.) The nudge is a harness line (ledger kind `nudge`), never trained on. Nudges per problem is an instrument (5.10): a child that learns to act before being nudged shows it falling.
- A problem in which the child never acts scores 0 and is recorded as such; the child is never told to stop thinking.

### 1.4 How a problem ends; how the next one arrives (the forever loop)
- A problem ends in exactly two ways (ruling 4): the gym declares it solved — a puzzle verifier accepts the answer with score 1.0; Minesweeper's board is cleared or a mine is hit — or the 16th turn is consumed. The compiler gym never declares a problem solved; its problems always run to the budget. The child has no end word; a `DONE` it writes is text like any other. Code fact: today nothing ends a solved puzzle (`reasoning_gym_gym.step` always returns `harness_done=False`, and the batch driver never reads it); Appendix C item 2 makes the gym set `harness_done` on a 1.0 score and the driver honour it.
- When a problem ends, the next problem's goal replaces the old one in the head at the child's very next turn. The tail carries the child's last 3 turns from the previous problem, each labelled with that problem's id, so its stream is continuous: whatever it wrote at the end of one problem is the first thing it sees in the next. Nothing forces those last turns to be a review; if the child learns to write one, it will see its own review next — that is the recurrence Rohin asked for, left to emerge.
- Batching, stated plainly: eight problems run side by side on one GPU, turn by turn (`batch_loop.py`), and a batch ends when all eight problems have ended. A lane whose problem was solved early **idles until the batch ends** — the child is not called for that lane, so it experiences no waiting; the next problem arrives at its next turn. Lanes are *not* refilled mid-batch: refilling would remove the batch boundary at which adapters are swapped (2.4) and would need per-request adapter ids the inference engine does not have. The cost is throughput only (an idle lane on a solved puzzle), measured in P2.
- Problems arrive from the gym's training schedule (seeded, balanced across families for the reasoning gym, `reasoning_gym_gym.training_schedule`). The schedule never runs out: when the list is exhausted the gym regenerates the next block with the next seed. Life ends only when the harness is stopped from outside (lease end, preemption, or the experiment's end). For the two compiler-gym test lives the harness is stopped at a fixed age (1024 problems, or 512 if the calendar forces it), the same age for both.

### 1.5 What the child sees at wake (the brief)
At the top of the head, in this order, refreshed at every batch boundary from the newest files on disk (not only at sleep):
1. **The birth brief** (Appendix A; fixed for the lineage; hashed). Who it is; the one interface rule; the clock; what happens when a problem ends; the values in plain words (goals and knowing when a goal is done; free thought; reflection; constantly asking what to do next; verifying; thinking back over past problems; connecting things); that it is measured on problems it has never seen. No markers, no recipe, no example move.
2. **Its own briefing from the last sleep** ("YOUR BRIEFING FROM LAST SLEEP", at most 8 lines): a summary the same model writes at sleep from the child's own action-outcome evidence (`sleep_compile._BRIEF_PROMPT`, unchanged). Context only; never in a training sequence. (Recorded for honesty, both cells: for R2 seed0 the frozen model given only this brief scored 0.5291 on the 8-program panel and 0.2731 on the disjoint panel — equal to or above its adapter, SEQ-017; for R2 seed1 the adapter won, 0.5291 vs 0.5163 and 0.2731 vs 0.2667 — by 0.013 and 0.006, SEQ-018. Two lives in: one each way. The brief is text memory and it is powerful; the write must beat it, section 6.3.)
3. **Its parents' latest brief** ("YOUR PARENTS, ON HOW YOU HAVE BEEN THINKING", at most 10 lines plus a one-sentence rehearsal request written without marker syntax). Written by the two-parent room; delivered whenever it lands (the room takes about 7 minutes at high reasoning, COORDINATION 2026-09-10 "first LIVE Astra room"); until then the previous brief stays. The room never runs inside a clone's process: today `run_life_v2.clone_sleep` calls the parent synchronously before the clone reloads (lines 692–698 and 804–810), which is a wait on a parent and breaks ruling 3; in v7 the room runs in a parent daemon (or the writer process), writes `parent_brief.txt`, and clones only read the file at batch boundaries (Appendix C, item 9). The parents may also leave at most one short line per round that the harness shows as `[PARENT] ...` at the start of the next batch (the "parenting inside a session" slot); same rules: context only, logged, and — like every brief — **stripped from training sequences, not merely masked** (2.1).
4. Tests (probes and exams) show only item 1 — the birth brief — so a test is always parent-free and memory-free except for the weights.
5. **Central parents and the score wall.** Fable and Codex are the central parents (ruling 2): advisory, in the background, never blocking. Ruling 9 binds them as it binds the rooms: no parent may see report-panel scores. So a central-parent note is written only from a fresh context that has the allow-listed read-only tools of `agentic_parent.py` (the child's ledgers and reflections, the instruments, the parental ledger) and nothing else — never `COORDINATION.md`, never a probe file, never a research note that carries report-panel numbers — and it reaches the per-gym rooms only through the parental ledger. The Fable daemon that posts probe results to `COORDINATION.md` never writes a parental note; a session that has seen report-panel numbers is not blind (`NEXT_EXPERIMENT_DESIGN_v1.md` line 200) and does not advise. The manifest records the tool allow-list hash; pretest P5 scans one central note for score scalars and report-panel identifiers.

### 1.6 Private reflection
- When: after the last batch of every round (every 32 problems), before the round's ledger slice is handed to the writer, so the reflection is written at the same sleep as the thoughts it reflects on.
- How long: 4 turns of up to 400 tokens (`--reflect-ticks 4`).
- What it receives (`reflection.reflection_prompt`, adapted to v7): the birth brief; the sentence that this is its own time with no task, no score and no one to answer to; a harness summary of what it lived since its last reflection — the problems (ids), attempts and best scores, and the last three sentences it wrote in its most recent problem (v6 showed "your last notes"; v7 has no note rows); and its previous reflection, so reflections can build on each other.
- Rows are kind `reflection` and enter the write like any other thought of the child's (2.1). The parents may read them (the `reflections` tool of `agentic_parent.ChildView`) and are forbidden in code from quoting, paraphrasing or mentioning them (`reflection.reflection_violations`: six consecutive words, four content words, or any mention discards the brief).
- The child is never told that anyone reads its reflections.

## 2. The write

### 2.1 What is eligible
- Rows of kind `thought` and `reflection` whose text the child generated. Nothing else is ever a training target: not the birth brief, not the waking brief, not any parent text, not the gym's outcome lines, not the nudge, not the sleep-time summary, not any model-written "principle" (v6 wrote `PRINCIPLE:` lines during sleep; v7 writes none).
- **Gym lines stay as unlearned context; parent lines and briefs are removed.** The gym's harness lines (the neutral intro, outcome lines, nudges) appear inside the training sequences as context, with their tokens masked out of the loss (the model is never taught to write an outcome line, so it cannot learn to hallucinate one). Every training sequence begins at the gym's intro line: the birth brief, the waking brief, the parents' brief and any `[PARENT]` one-liner are **stripped from the sequence**, not masked — no parent word is in the corpus file at all (ruling 9, the thinker/compiler line; section 6.4). The writer runs a leak check for `[PARENT]`, `YOUR PARENTS` and the brief texts over every compiled corpus and refuses to train if it finds one (Appendix C, item 6). Consequence, stated: for Stage-0 variant (b), the child's own-words restatement follows the `[PARENT]` request in life but follows the intro line in training; the trigger is lost in the corpus, exactly the "piece stripped of its trigger" effect of 2.2 in miniature, accepted because the parent's words may not enter the corpus.
- Every eligible row is kept. No success filter (v6 admitted only wins, recoveries and contrasts), no deduplication, no cap on repeats. A thought the child has a hundred times is **kept a hundred times in the corpus** and re-read at the rate the write budget allows (2.3): what must be learned is the conditional — the thought in its situation — and repetition is the mechanism (Rohin, 2026-09-10). This needs the trainer to accept per-span masks (Appendix C).

### 2.2 The two-scale organization
Two kinds of training sequence are built from the same rows:
- **Scale 1, whole problems in order.** For every problem instance (per clone): the intro line (masked), then turn 1 (trained), the outcome lines it produced (masked), turn 2 (trained), ... to the end of the problem; the last three turns carried into the next problem are not repeated (they are that problem's own rows). About 16 × 300 ≈ 5,000 tokens per problem; the trainer's maximum sequence length rises from 512 to **7,168 tokens** (a problem that uses all 16 × 400 tokens plus its outcome lines is ≈ 6,900 tokens; the earlier figure of 6,144 would have cut the last turns — the persistence-relevant part — of exactly the problems that use their whole budget). A sequence that still exceeds the maximum is split at a turn boundary, never inside a turn.
- **Scale 2, short windows.** For every action: the preceding outcome line (masked, the trigger), the turn(s) since that outcome up to and including the turn that carried the action (trained), and the action's outcome (masked). This is "I am at this crossroad; what do I think and do" — the state-level window. Reflection rows are their own short sequences (the harness summary masked, the child's text trained). Scale 2 re-covers the child's text once more, so the two scales together are ≈ 10,000 tokens per problem — the number every cost in this document uses.
- **Neighbourhood packing.** Sequences are ordered so that neighbours are related: within a gym, the same problem family (puzzle family; program) is adjacent, and within a family, time order. Each epoch shuffles the order of family blocks, not of rows. Hypothesis being tested (Rohin): the v6 rituals — the same note, the same recall, the same first move on every problem — are partly an artefact of training pieces stripped of their trigger.
- Comparators in pretest P1: v6's `compile_sleep` write (success-filtered, THINK-restated short pieces plus model-written principles), and scale 1 alone (whole problems only, half the tokens). The two-scale + neighbourhood write ships if P1 does not reject it; scale 1 alone ships if it is within one noise width of the two-scale write, because it halves every write (decision rule in `PRETESTS_2026-09-11.md`).
- Trainer memory: today's trainer (`train_adapter.py`) uses a batch of 4 sequences of at most 512 tokens with no gradient checkpointing; four 7,168-token sequences through a 7-billion-parameter model in bf16 (the 16-bit number format used in training) do not fit one A40's 48 GB that way. The v7 trainer uses gradient checkpointing, a batch of one or two sequences with gradient accumulation to an effective batch of 4, and packs short sequences together. The assumed training speed (1,200 tokens per second) is therefore optimistic; P1 measures the real speed under the shipped settings, and every wall-clock number below is corrected from it.

### 2.3 Training and the write budget
- Cumulative from the base every time: a write trains a fresh adapter from the frozen base over the write-corpus (below). A committed adapter is therefore a self-contained snapshot; rolling back is choosing an earlier snapshot; a bad write never contaminates the next.
- Rank 32, alpha 64 (alpha is the adapter's scaling knob; alpha = 2 × rank is the existing convention), dropout 0.05 (randomly ignoring 5% of adapter units during training, a standard guard against memorizing), attached to every attention and MLP weight matrix (unchanged target list). Rank 8 vs 32 is pretested (P1); rank 32 is Rohin's approved default.
- Learning rate and epochs: set by P1 from candidates that are compatible with the write budget (below): hot (1e-4, 3 epochs — today's), cool (5e-5, 2 epochs), and single-pass (1e-4, 1 epoch). Whatever P1 picks is frozen. The trainer gets an explicit seed, recorded in `train_meta.json` (the v1 trainer has none today; same-configuration replicates of the v2.1 trainer differed by up to 0.078, EVIDENCE_TABLES rank table).
- Loss: next-token prediction on the child's tokens only (masked elsewhere), bare text in the child's own dialect — exactly what it will generate.
- **The write budget, in token-passes (the arithmetic, done once).** A write's wall clock is set by token-passes = tokens in the write-corpus × epochs, divided by the training speed. At the assumed 1,200 tokens per second (≈ 4.3 million token-passes per hour): one life's first 512 problems at two scales ≈ 5 M tokens, which is 15 M token-passes at 3 epochs ≈ 3.5 hours, or 5 M token-passes at 1 epoch ≈ 1.2 hours. Six clones for four days produce ≈ 9,000 problems ≈ 90 M tokens — ≈ 21 hours for one epoch, which no writer can keep up with. So each write has a budget **T_max = 5 M token-passes (≈ 1.2 h at the assumed speed; the number is fixed at the freeze from P1's measured speed so that a write takes about one round)**. The write-corpus is chosen to fit: its size in tokens is T_max divided by the frozen epochs E. The newest slices (every round that arrived since the last write, both scales) go in full, up to half the write-corpus; the rest is drawn uniformly over the older corpus, per problem (whole problems, both scales), with a seed recorded in `train_meta.json` together with the list of problems drawn and each row's cumulative re-exposure count. If the newest slices alone exceed half the write-corpus, they too are sampled and the record says so.
- **What the budget means in plain numbers (default E = 1, six clones).** The write-corpus is 5 M tokens. A round of six clones is 192 problems ≈ 1.9 M tokens, so the newest round always fits in full; the remaining 3.1 M tokens are a sample of the past. A thought is read once, in full, at the first write after it is thought; afterwards it is re-read with probability 3.1 M / (corpus size) at each later write — about one to four more times over the lineage's life, more often the earlier it was thought (the corpus grows by ≈ 1.9 M tokens per write). **What it means at E = 3:** the write-corpus is 1.7 M tokens ≈ 170 problems, smaller than one round of six clones — so at 3 epochs the newest round is itself sampled unless the lineage has at most two clones per writer. Epochs therefore trade against width: E = 1 fits six clones with one-round lag; E = 2 fits three; E = 3 fits two. Rohin's ruling is to saturate the GPUs (ruling 2), so the default is width (E = 1) unless P1 shows depth wins by more than two noise widths — **decision D1 in section 9**, together with Rohin's own stated alternative (IDEAS.md "Stop replaying everything forever": cumulative-from-base for the first K sleeps, then incremental training on the previous adapter with a use- and recency-weighted replay sample at a lower learning rate, the switch rule pre-declared), which no pretest can evaluate before the freeze.
- Nothing is deduplicated or capped by the budget rule; it only decides how much of the past is re-read at each write. It is a scale necessity, not a pretested choice, and it is the reason the sentence "every thought is trained every time" cannot be true at scale.
- **The write-corpus of the two compiler test lives is pre-registered** (section 8, item 7), because a uniform draw over the lineage's ≈ 90 M tokens would give the taught child's own compiler rows a tiny share of every write while the untaught comparator's compiler rows fill its write — an exposure difference of roughly an order of magnitude that has nothing to do with parenting. Both test lives: all of that life's own test-gym rows in full (they fit the write-corpus up to ≈ 500 problems at E = 1, ≈ 170 at E = 3; beyond that both lives sample their own rows by the identical rule — newest round in full, the rest uniform per problem). The taught child additionally carries a fixed sample of **R = 2.5 M lineage tokens per write** (≈ 250 lineage problems, drawn uniformly per problem from the hashed lineage snapshot with a seed recorded per write), on top of the budget — so its writes are about half again as long (≈ 1.8 h vs 1.2 h at E = 1), a lag asymmetry that is logged and reported, not hidden. Whether 2.5 M tokens of lineage per write are enough to carry the lineage's learning through retrain-from-base is unknown before the test; the patience rule (3.3) protects the taught child from losing it (its inherited adapter is its first committed adapter and sets its initial floor) — **decision D2 in section 9**.

### 2.4 The asynchronous merge across clones
- X clones run on X GPUs; one writer process runs on its own GPU (per node — the two nodes cannot reach each other, so a lineage is node-local). Each clone appends to its own ledger and, at every round boundary, writes a slice (its rows since its last slice, stamped with clone id, gym, round and the adapter it ran under) into the group directory (`clone_coordinator.write_slice`, existing).
- The writer never waits for anyone. Whenever it is idle it takes every slice present that it has not yet pooled, pools them (each row exactly once, by ledger index — existing cursor logic), builds the write-corpus (2.2–2.3), trains (2.3), runs the brake and the gate exam (3.1–3.2), and publishes the adapter with a round tag. Then it looks for new slices again. There is no barrier and no skipped-clone bookkeeping; a slow clone's slice simply enters the next write.
- **The writer's cycle, costed at 16 turns per problem:** pool and compile, minutes; train, ≈ 1.2 h at T_max; canary, 4 problems × 6 turns ≈ 5 min; gate exam, 12 items × 2 reps × 16 turns ≈ 26 min (the recorded ≈ 6 min per 8-program rep was measured at ≈ 11 turns per problem, COORDINATION 2026-09-10 ~00:30; at 16 turns and 12 items it is ≈ 13 min per rep); the frozen-model read every fourth write, +26 min; engine start after training (the GPU cannot hold the trainer and the inference engine at once), ≈ 4 min. About 1.8 h per cycle, 2.3 h with the frozen read. A clone round at 16 turns is ≈ 2 h (four batches at ≈ 30 min — the measured ≈ 22 problems per GPU-hour was at ≈ 11.6 turns; at 16 turns ≈ 16 per hour — plus 4 reflection turns). **So the writer runs about one round behind, sometimes two;** the lag in rounds is logged per published adapter and reported. Exams never cost a clone anything: clones run no gate exams.
- Clones never wait for a verdict. At every batch boundary (every 8 problems) a clone checks the group directory for a newer published adapter; if one exists and is marked active, it becomes the clone's adapter for the next batch (the inference engine loads the new LoRA in place; no restart). Briefs are re-read at the same boundary.
- **Hot-swap, what the code must do (none of it exists today; Appendix C item 7):** today `model_backend.py` enables LoRA only if an adapter path is given at start (line 20) — so a child born without an adapter (Stage-0 variants a and b) could never attach one without a restart — and registers every adapter under the fixed id 1 (line 38), which the engine caches, so a new file under the same id is not reloaded; every adapter change today rebuilds the engine (≈ 4 min). v7: LoRA always enabled with `max_lora_rank=32` and `max_loras=2`; a monotonically increasing adapter id per published adapter; an `ACTIVE` file in the group directory naming the active adapter (round, path, reason: published / rolled back); every ledger row stamped with the adapter round. A rank-32 adapter is ≈ 160 MB and loads from local disk in seconds — plausible but unmeasured; P6 measures it. **Interim if P6 slips:** the 4-minute engine restart at each 32-problem boundary (≈ 5–8% of clone time), and the existing coordinator's non-blocking mode (round and barrier timeouts set near zero, so no clone ever waits and a late adapter attaches at the clone's next sleep boundary; `clone_coordinator.wait_for_verdict`, `attach_missed_rounds`), labelled `interim merge` in the manifest. The child's mechanism (context, interface, write, brake) is the same under both; only the swap timing differs.
- **A clone that is mid-problem when a new adapter lands** finishes the current batch of eight problems under the adapter it started them with; the swap happens at the batch boundary, never inside a problem. A round's slice may therefore contain rows from two adapters; the write treats them alike (thoughts carry the gain — no attribution by adapter or clone is ever computed, though the stamp is kept for the record).
- A clone in private reflection when an adapter lands finishes the reflection first (same rule).
- Restart safety as today: every step is a marker file; a killed clone resumes from its last completed batch; the writer's pooled cursor makes double-pooling impossible.

## 3. The brake and the patience rule

Principle (Rohin): protect against collapse; allow developmental dips; roll back with patience rather than veto. A child that thinks much more before it gets better must not be punished by a score gate.

### 3.1 What is refused (the brake)
Exactly two conditions refuse a freshly trained adapter (it is marked `REJECTED_FORMAT` or `REJECTED_COLLAPSE` and never published; the previous active adapter stays; the parents see the reason code):
1. **Broken format.** The canary: 4 fixed problems from the gym's **training split** — for the compiler gym, 4 fixed programs from cbench (the program set the compiler gym trains on) that are neither report programs nor gate programs (today's `gym_backend.canary_set` returns the first 4 *report* programs, which puts a decision on the programs whose scores the paper reports and breaks split hygiene by identifier — ruling 9; changed, Appendix C item 8); for the reasoning gym, 4 fixed train-family items — 6 turns each, with the birth brief only. Refuse if fewer than 3 of the 4 problems produce at least one line the gym could score (a parseable `ACT:` with a non-empty move that the gym accepts as well-formed — an `INVALID` answer still counts as scoreable; an empty or unparseable one does not). Measured origin: v6's canary passed every one of the 9 harmful R2 sleeps (EVIDENCE_TABLES, R2), so format alone was never the failure — which is why the second condition exists.
2. **Behavioural collapse.** On the gate exam (3.2): the median of the child's tokens per problem, over the exam problems that ran to the budget (unsolved by this adapter), is below 0.5 × the frozen model's median on its own unsolved problems of the same set. This is the one measure, the direct analogue of Rohin's "turns per problem < 0.5 × the frozen model's" (ruling 8): under v7 an unsolved problem always has 16 turns, so words per problem is what a collapse changes. Solved problems are excluded so that a child that solves a puzzle in two turns is never read as collapsed. Actions per problem and distinct actions per problem are **instruments, not refusal conditions** (5.6): an earlier draft refused on any of three medians, which could refuse a deliberate child that thinks for twelve turns and turns in two considered answers — the very child ruling 8 protects (the three-way rule is offered to Rohin as decision D4). Measured origin: the v6 collapse was 1.0 turns per program in R2 seed 6 and 5.4 in seed 5 against a base of ≈ 13 (SEQ-011; EVIDENCE_TABLES R2), caught only by the brevity rule in gated lives (R3 500@64, 501@704, 505@288, 505@832, 505@864 — five catches with passing scores, SEQ-012/013). The frozen model's medians are measured once per gym at the lineage's birth on the gate set (pretest P4).
Everything else commits: a write that scores badly is committed and — subject only to the patience rule (3.3) — becomes active.

### 3.2 Parent-free exams, per gym
- Every exam uses the birth brief only (no waking brief, no parent brief), fixed generation seeds, the gym's fixed held-out items; scores go to the harness's files, never to the parents (the parents' tool exposes decision and reason code only; the existing gate tool's score scalars — `agentic_parent.gate_decisions` returns candidate, floor and base whenever the gate panel is disjoint from the report panel, exactly v7's configuration — are removed, Appendix C item 9).
- **Who runs the exams:** the writer, once per adapter it trains, before publishing — never the clones. So each adapter version has exactly one gate exam and the lineage has one exam record; with six clones in a gym, an adapter is examined once, not six times.
- **Gate exam** (drives the brake and the patience rule): the gym's gate set — reasoning gym: 12 items from the two gate families (`n_queens`, `tower_of_hanoi`, `reasoning_gym_families.json`); compiler gym: disjoint panel v1 (12 programs, the `panel` list of `disjoint_panel_v1.json`); Minesweeper (if built): 12 boards of a size/density never trained, scored by the board outcome. Adapter ON, 2 seeded reps (seeds 4242 and 5242). Every fourth write also adapter OFF (the frozen model, same seeds) for the paired read. Cost ≈ 26 min per exam at 16 turns (2.4).
- **Report exam** (the paper's readout; never used for any decision): the gym's exam set — reasoning gym: 12 items from three exam families (`zebra_puzzles`, `acre`, `rush_hour`); compiler gym: the 8 report programs (ON and OFF every 64 problems, as today, seed 777, run in the clone's process as the record's lives did — ≈ 9 min per rep at 16 turns) plus the disjoint panel read from the gate record. Schedule: every 8 writes for the trait gyms (on the writer); every 64 problems for the compiler test lives (unchanged cadence, so the untaught lives already on record are comparable).
- **Gyms in this run:** reasoning-gym is the trait gym; Minesweeper joins only if its Gym adapter (≈ 150 lines, GYM_SURVEY section 6) exists by 09-12 morning. Its ruled instrument — a stated probability of a mine per turn, scored by Brier (a score for how well stated probabilities match what happened) — needs a parsed probability; under ruling 4 no marker may be required, so the Brier reading uses instrument 5.1 (detector-flagged probabilities in the child's free text, with the precision audit) and the gate score is the board outcome. **TextWorld** (on the ruled shortlist, C1 of GYM_SURVEY_v1) is out of this run: its adapter is ≈ 0.5 engineer-day and the build day is spoken for by the v7 runner; it stays first on the list for the next run.

### 3.3 The floor and the roll-back rule
- **Floor F_g** (per gym g) = the best gate-exam score ever recorded for a *committed* adapter of the lineage on gym g's gate set (2-rep mean). It never goes down. It is never the latest re-probe of the held adapter. **It is the best committed adapter only, as ruled** ("any floor is the best committed score ever", ruling 8); the frozen model's score is reported beside it, never used as a floor — an earlier draft inherited `floor = max(frozen, previous adapter)` from `run_life_v2.py` line 523, which could hold a young lineage adapter-less if its first writes sat below an exploring frozen model (the frozen model scores 0.2574 / 0.2541 on the disjoint panel while the recipe-locked early adapters on record sat at 0.2495, SEQ-009/012), and said nothing about what "roll back" means when no adapter has beaten it (offered to Rohin as decision D5). Measured origin of "never the latest re-probe": R4 602's floor tracked a noisy re-probe (committed 0.5307 → re-probed 0.5104) and so admitted a 0.4933 write, 0.037 below the adapter it replaced (SEQ-016); R3 500's floor fell from 0.529 to 0.464 on one rare draw of the same adapter (SEQ-002).
- **Tolerance tol_g** = one noise width of the exam. For the compiler gym's disjoint panel: tol = 0.01. For a 0/1-scored 12-item exam (reasoning gym), one noise width is large (a 12-item exam at 30% accuracy has SD ≈ 0.13 for one rep, ≈ 0.09 for the 2-rep mean); tol_g is set to one SD of the frozen model's 4-rep band measured in pretest P4 and recorded per gym before the lineage is born.
- **Patience, defined at the lineage level:** the writer examines every adapter it trains. If an adapter's gate exam is below F_g − tol_g it is still committed and activated (a developmental dip is allowed), and a counter of consecutive below-floor adapters rises. When the **third consecutive** adapter examines below F_g − tol_g — three sleeps, as ruled — the lineage rolls back: instead of that adapter, the best-ever committed adapter for that gym becomes active for all clones at their next batch boundary, a `ROLLBACK` record names the rounds, and the parents are told through the gate log (decision and reason code) so they can change what they teach. Later writes keep committing to the record and are examined; the first one that scores ≥ F_g − tol_g becomes active again and the counter resets. If none ever clears the floor, the lineage stays on its best-ever adapter and the record says so at every write (the v6 analogue: R3 500 refused 15 writes in a row and stayed on its sleep-608 adapter, SEQ-012). Because exams are per adapter version and one per version, "three consecutive" has one meaning no matter how many clones there are and how staggered they run.
- A rollback never touches the ledger or the corpus: every thought stays and keeps being written (ruling 7). It only changes which weights the clones act with. **The cost, in plain numbers:** a wrecked adapter (the shape of R4 601@800's 0.114, SEQ-015) is active for one round before the next adapter is examined, two rounds if the next is also below floor; six clones write ≈ 1.9 M tokens of thoughts per round under it, so up to ≈ 3.8 M tokens — most of one write-corpus — are thoughts had under a wrecked adapter, kept and trained like any other. Rows carry their adapter-round stamp, so their share is reported; they are not down-weighted (offered to Rohin as decision D7).
- **Proposed fast path (Rohin's decision D6, informed by pretest P8):** an adapter examining ≥ 5 × tol_g below the floor at its one exam (≥ 0.05 on the compiler panel — the shape of R3 500's late writes at 0.337/0.347 against a 0.529 floor, and of R4 601@800's 0.114) is committed but not activated. Rohin's ruling as given is three exams for everything; P8 quantifies how many rounds a wrecked adapter would govern under each version.
- Multiple gyms: each gym has its own floor and its own counter; a rollback triggered by one gym applies to the lineage's single adapter (there is one child), and the record names the gym.
- **The taught child's test life** begins with the lineage's final adapter as its first committed adapter: it is examined on the compiler gate set at problem 0 and that score is its initial floor. The untaught comparator has no adapter at birth and no floor until its first commit — an asymmetry inherent in the treatment, stated.

### 3.4 Why 0.01 on the disjoint panel (from the replicate SD 0.0065)
- The same adapter, probed twice with the same seed, differs by a *replicate SD* of 0.0064–0.0067 on an 8- or 12-program panel (pooled 0.0066 at n = 70, SEQ-011); the disjoint panel's replicate SD is 0.006–0.007 (SEQ-012). Take 0.0065 per rep; a 2-rep mean has SD ≈ 0.0046.
- With tol = 0.01 and 2 reps: an adapter truly equal to the floor falls below F − 0.01 with probability 0.015 per exam and three times in a row with probability 3 × 10⁻⁶ — a false rollback essentially never happens. An adapter truly 0.02 worse than the floor is flagged at each exam with probability 0.985 and rolled back after three exams with probability 0.96; one truly 0.013 worse (two replicate SDs) is rolled back with probability 0.41.
- With the old tol = 0.02: a 0.02-worse adapter is flagged with probability 0.50 per exam and rolled back after three with probability 0.125. SEQ-012's argument for 0.01 is one of scale — a tolerance of 0.02 on a panel whose scores sit at 0.25 is 8% of the score, and ≈ 3 replicate SDs — and it is adopted (≈ 1.5 replicate SDs). For the record: R5 700 committed three consecutive writes 0.0024–0.0052 below its gate base (0.2565, 0.2566, 0.2537 vs 0.2589, SEQ-012/014); those sit inside one replicate SD and are not themselves evidence that the loose tolerance admitted a worse adapter.
- The floor is a maximum over noisy draws and is therefore biased slightly upward (by roughly half a replicate SD after many exams); 0.01 absorbs that bias.
- Heavy tail, stated: one same-adapter pair differed by 0.065 (SEQ-002) and single reps have collapsed to 0.14–0.21 on the disjoint panel while the paired rep sat at 0.25 (SEQ-012, SEQ-016). Two reps per exam and the three-exam patience are the defence; a single collapsed rep never triggers anything on its own. The fast path of 3.3 uses 5 × tol = 0.05, which is ≈ 7.7 replicate SDs of a single rep and ≈ 11 SDs of a 2-rep mean.

## 4. Preparation of the first child (Stage 0)

The first child must not start at literally zero: it should be fluent in the interface (turn work in with `ACT:`) and receptive to its parents (able to take a lesson and repeat it in its own words). No adapter is pretrained on anyone else's thoughts (the quarantined bootstrap corpora v1/v2/v3 stay out; Codex's STOP of 2026-09-09 stands). Three variants are pretested (P3); the one that wins is how the lineage is born. All three run under the identical mechanism of sections 1–3 with the two-parent room at every sleep; they differ only in what exists before the first problem.

- **(a) Birth brief only.** The child is born with the birth brief (Appendix A) and its parents' opening brief (written before birth from nothing but the gym description — no child text exists yet). Nothing is in the weights. Implementation: zero.
- **(b) Birth brief + intensive own-words rehearsal, consolidated at the first sleeps.** As (a), plus for the first two rounds the harness inserts one line at the start of every problem: `[PARENT] Before you start, say in your own words what your parents asked of you.` (a harness line, stripped from training sequences, never trained). The child's restatements are its own text and are written by the first two sleeps like any other thought; from round 3 the line stops and the brief alone carries the lesson. No foreign thought enters the weights: only the child's own restatements do (and in the corpus they follow the intro line, not the request — 2.1). Risk: the restatement becomes a ritual first sentence (the v6 echo rate rose to 0.454 after briefs against a 0.354 null, SEQ-003); the instrument is the first-turn similarity across problems (5.11).
- **(c) A small interface-only instruction set.** 60–100 rows, written by hand or by the parents before birth, each a gym head (a real problem's goal text) followed by a minimal child turn that contains nothing but a well-formed `ACT:` line in that gym's move format — "format, not thoughts". **The moves are content-free:** a syntactically valid move that the verifier scores 0 (or the gym's `INVALID` placeholder), never an answer or an attempt at one — a well-formed answer line for a reasoning-gym puzzle *is* an answer, and the parents' own hard rule forbids giving answers (`agentic_parent.py` line 1361). Every row is leak-scanned against the reference answer of its item (`reasoning_gym_gym` exposes it) and against the compiler recipe terms; the row set is hashed into the manifest. These rows are placed in a fixed-rows file that the writer takes as an explicit input (`--fixed-rows`, hashed) so that every retrain-from-base includes them for the lineage's whole life (a seeded adapter alone would be replaced at the first sleep; and the new writer builds from ledger rows, so without an explicit input the rows would be trained once and never again), and the first adapter is trained from them before the first problem so the child is born fluent. Foreign *thoughts* stay out because the rows contain none; the foreign *syntax* is the one thing the harness imposes anyway (decision D8 asks Rohin to confirm this reading). Risk: 60–100 rows of "act immediately" teach acting without thinking; the instruments to watch are thoughts per action and actions per problem in the first two rounds; the P3 decision rule refuses (c) if it lowers thoughts per action below half of (a)'s.
- Receptiveness is measured the same way for all three (P3): rehearsal rate — the **first turn** of a problem (the first `thought` row of the instance, not a `NOTE:` line: today's `parent_brief.rehearsal_rate` scores the first `NOTE:` line and returns 0 for every problem without one, so on a marker-free child it is identically zero and must be rewritten before P3) shares ≥ 4 content words with the parents' brief, against the null of the frozen model's first turns scored against the same brief; interface fluency (fraction of problems with ≥ 1 scoreable action; nudges per problem; parse-miss candidates); and the gate exam after two sleeps.
- Persistence is the first trait and is a must: the parents' opening brief in every variant carries it ("keep working a hard problem with changing attempts; never leave a problem early"), and P3 reports actions after a non-improving outcome per problem for each variant.
- **Parenting content: thinking moves (added 2026-09-11, Rohin's ruling; folded by Fable's agent).** Beyond the interface and persistence, what the room teaches in this phase is the five thinking moves — decision, association, goal, building, rethinking/pruning — by question, one per problem, on easy material until the child makes them unasked, and then throughout the classroom at the child's frontier; see PARENTING_SCIENCE_SURVEY_v1 V14, sequencing 2.8 and rules 20–26. This is room content carried by the opening brief and the per-round `[PARENT]` line; it changes none of variants (a)–(c) or the mechanism above, and the thought-structure readings it needs are the survey's V14 instrument, to be listed in section 5 when the slot there is filled.

## 5. Instruments: counting emergent behaviour from the ledger

**[slot: values → instrument → gym table from the cross-disciplinary parenting-science survey (ruling 10) — to be inserted here when the survey lands; the run does not wait for it. Until then the mapping is: goals and goal ends → 5.12 (fraction of budget used before the last action) and 5.6; free thought → 5.7 and tokens per turn; reflection → 5.9; constantly asking what to do → 5.3; verifying → 5.4; thinking on past episodes → 5.5; connecting things → 5.9 connectedness and 5.5 specific recalls.]**

All instruments read the ledger (rows of kind `thought`, `act`, `reflection`, `nudge`) and the adapter-round stamps; none uses a model as a judge. Each is computed per problem instance (the harness's grouping, `agentic_parent._instances_with_acts`), aggregated per round, reported ON and OFF where a probe exists, and served to the parents as rates and trends only. Each detector below is a word-pattern search over the child's own text and has a stated failure; before any count appears in a table, 50 flagged turns per instrument are checked by hand against a written rubric and the precision must be ≥ 0.8, otherwise the count is labelled "detector-flagged" (design item 10).

1. **Prediction rate** — fraction of actions preceded, in the same or the previous turn, by a stated expectation: a number in [0, 1] or a percentage within a sentence containing expect / predict / guess / should score / likely. *Fails when* the child quotes a past score (false positive) or predicts in words without a number (false negative); a flat prediction repeated every problem counts as a prediction — report prediction SD alongside (the v6 `flat_predictions` flag fired below SD 0.05). For Minesweeper, the flagged probability before a reveal is what the Brier reading scores.
2. **Note rate** — turns containing a self-addressed memo (note to self / remember / lesson / rule / next time I) per problem. *Fails when* the memo is templated: report consecutive-problem similarity of first memos (Jaccard word overlap — the share of words two texts have in common; v6 ON 0.62 vs OFF 0.105, M12) beside the rate.
3. **Self-question rate** — questions the child asks itself per 1,000 tokens (a sentence ending in "?" that starts with should I / what if / am I / do I need / why did / is this / how much more). *Fails when* questions are rhetorical templates or their density rises while their content does not change ("performing reflection", the curriculum's named failure); pair with the outcome curve.
4. **Verification rate** — verification events per 1,000 tokens: "let me check / verify / recompute", a comparison of a stated expectation to the outcome ("I expected X, got Y"), or a correction of an earlier claim. *Fails when* the child claims to verify without verifying; the detector cannot tell the two apart — the exam score is the only check that verification works.
5. **Recall rate** — references to earlier problems per problem: a past problem id or family name, or "last time / earlier / previously / in the other puzzle". *Fails when* the recalled event is invented (there is no lookup to check it against); report "specific" recalls (an id or a number) separately from vague ones.
6. **Actions per problem** — `act` rows per problem instance, with distinct actions per problem beside it. *Fails when* identical actions are repeated to fill the budget (the distinct count exposes it). Instruments only; not part of the brake (3.1).
7. **Thoughts per action** — turns without an action divided by actions, per problem. *Fails when* a child acts once and idles for fifteen turns: it looks thoughtful. Pair with tokens per turn and the idle-repetition flag (consecutive turns with Jaccard ≥ 0.5).
8. **Repeated-action-after-failure rate** — fraction of actions identical (whitespace-normalized) to the previous action after an outcome that did not improve the best score. *Fails when* a trivially reworded repeat escapes; in gyms with partial credit a repeat is always wrong, so no exemption is needed.
9. **Reflection length and connectedness** — tokens per reflection; the fraction of reflection sentences that name a specific past problem, outcome or number, or contrast two things (unlike / whereas / compared to / differs). *Fails when* length is padded with rambling and connectedness is surface phrasing; read only beside the exam curve.
10. **Nudges per problem** — harness `nudge` rows per problem (mechanical; no detector). *Fails when* a child acts at once with junk to avoid nudges: read beside score and actions per problem.
11. **Ritual, frozen definition and v7 reading** — the 2026-09-09 detector (`parent_brief.ritual_metrics`: modal first-action share ≥ 0.7; prediction SD < 0.05; first-note Jaccard ≥ 0.5; modal recall share ≥ 0.7; ritual = two or more flags) is kept as written for continuity with the record (onset 160–224 problems in 8 of 9 untaught lives, EVIDENCE_TABLES), and re-read for v7 with detector-flagged predictions and memos in place of `PREDICT:`/`NOTE:` lines, plus first-turn similarity across problems. On a marker-free ledger the frozen detector's note and recall flags read zero by construction; the v7 reading is the one to interpret.
12. **Efficiency and behaviour change** (design item 10, from action rows only): turns-to-best and tokens-to-best per problem, fraction of budget used before the last action, distinct actions per problem, and the Jensen–Shannon shift (a 0-to-1 measure of how different two distributions are) of the first-action distribution between consecutive rounds — how much the child's opening move changes from round to round; v6 lives moved 0.02–0.13, SEQ-005. *Fails when* read from the child's echoed text instead of action rows (the v6 instrument left 27 of 32 windows undefined in one life, SEQ-011); v7 reads action rows only.
13. **Parse-miss candidates** — turns containing "ACT" with no parsed action, per problem (mechanical). *Fails when* the letters occur in ordinary words ("action", "exact"); report beside the fraction of problems with no scoreable action, which is the number that matters.

## 6. What is deliberately not in the child, and why (each tied to a number)

1. **No end token.** v6 offered `DONE`. Measured: the collapse the brake catches was the child ending every program after very few turns (R2 seed 6 at 1.0 turns per program, seed 5 at 5.4, against a base of ≈ 13; SEQ-011, EVIDENCE_TABLES R2). Late-life turns per problem: 11 of 24 lives ran to the budget (15.4–16.0), 13 did not (1.0–15.3; SEQ-011), several ending problems early by `DONE` without collapsing — so the end token did carry information in some lives, and its removal rests on ruling 4 (stopping is the harness's, never the child's), not on the record.
2. **No required markers.** v6 parsed `PREDICT:`, `NOTE:`, `RECALL:`, `DONE`. Measured: with the adapter ON, the same first move opened 90% / 86% of probe problems and was fully locked in 68% / 52% of checkpoints, first notes overlapped at 0.62 / 0.59, versus 0.50 / 0% / 0.105 with the adapter OFF (M12, EVIDENCE_TABLES) — the markers became the ritual's skeleton. Predicting, noting and recalling are behaviours to learn and count, not syntax to require.
3. **No pinned notes block, no ledger retrieval.** The v6 head pinned up to 10 notes and inserted retrieved ledger rows on `RECALL:`. Measured: the `same_recall` ritual flag (the same query on ≥ 70% of problems, part of the ritual onset at 160–224 problems in 8 of 9 untaught lives). Reading, not measured: memory that has to be looked up is not in the weights; the paper's question is what the weights carry, so storage should be extractable in generation (the related-work entry on knowledge extraction, IDEAS.md "Allen-Zhu & Li ... 3.1", says a fact is retrievable only when trained in several renderings — which the two-scale write provides for lived thoughts). An earlier draft attributed this to a ruling of 2026-09-09 and to "a v2-era finding that retrieved evidence went unused"; neither traces to the record and both are withdrawn. The tail (14 turns) already keeps the child's own recent notes in view within a problem; the weights are its memory across problems.
4. **No parent text in the corpus (the thinker/compiler line).** Measured: after briefs, first-note overlap with the brief rose to 0.454 from 0.322 — against 0.354 for children who never saw a brief (SEQ-003), so most "rehearsal" was coincidence; and the frozen model given only a life's own text brief matched or beat the adapter in one life (R2 seed0: 0.5291 / 0.2731, SEQ-017) and lost to it in another (R2 seed1: 0.5163 vs 0.5291; 0.2667 vs 0.2731, SEQ-018). Injecting the parent's words would make this post-training, not parenting (Rohin, 2026-09-10); a lesson enters the weights only when the child says it. Parent lines are stripped from training sequences, not masked (2.1).
5. **No score gate as veto.** Measured: R3 500 refused 15 writes and stayed on its sleep-608 adapter to the end; R3 505 refused 17 of 27 (SEQ-012/013); the veto that "protected" R4 604 passed an adapter that reproduced the birth recipe on the 8 programs (0.4878, harmless) and collapsed on unseen programs (0.1939, one rep 0.138; SEQ-016); the floor-decay hole admitted 0.4933 over 0.5307 (SEQ-016). A veto on score punishes a child that thinks more before it scores more and does not protect what it does not measure. Brake on collapse; roll back with patience.
6. **No success filter, no dedup, no caps in the write.** Measured (IDEAS.md "Chunk-density metric", quoted): rows admitted per 32-problem sleep fell from 127 to 30 in R2 seed2 while characters per row rose from 314 to 791 (2.5×); RP 400 134 → 33 rows, 308 → 949 characters (3.1×); RP 401 115 → 5–35 rows, 310 → 815 — with the entry's own caution that this is "not a clean density decline" and is to be read "only alongside the ritual metrics". Late-life adapters lost the gain mid-life adapters had in 6 of 9 lives and improved in none (SEQ-012). The v6 write amplified what it selected; v7 selects nothing and organizes everything.
7. **No model-written principles.** v6's sleep wrote `PRINCIPLE:` lines from evidence and trained on them. Measured: the adapter's whole gain on the 8-panel was one fixed recipe (every ON mean equals exactly 0.4878 or 0.5291, the birth recipe or a six-pass routine; COORDINATION Observation 1) — a principle applied everywhere. ## Appendix A. Birth brief (draft for Rohin's edit; the gym's goal text supplies the move format)

> You are a young agent who lives inside a workshop of problems. You act, you see real consequences, and you get better by learning from your own experience. Everything you write is your own thinking; think freely and as much as you need.
>
> One rule: when you want to turn in a move to be scored, write it on its own line beginning with `ACT:` in the form the problem asks for. The result appears in your stream. Nothing else you write is read by the workshop — it is yours.
>
> Every problem has a fixed number of turns (see the clock). A problem ends when the workshop says it is solved or when the turns run out; you cannot end it yourself, so use the time — try again with a change, or think about what the results are telling you. When it ends, the next problem arrives. If you go four turns without acting, you will be reminded to turn something in.
>
> What matters here: know what you are trying to do and notice when it is done; think freely, and think back over what you have done; keep asking yourself what to do next and whether your approach is working; check things rather than assume them; remember past problems and what they taught you; connect what you learn across problems. Keep working a hard problem with changed attempts — never leave it early.
>
> Higher scores are better. You will be measured on problems you have never seen, so understanding beats memorizing.
