# E0-r provenance adjudication: a fixed-render carrier ceiling, not self-learning

**Date:** 2026-09-13 UTC  
**Role:** fresh docs-only adjudication  
**Scope:** I compared the committed original `E0` and revised `E0-r`
specifications against Rohin's thinker/compiler boundary, the repository's
trained/learned vocabulary, and the full Dream--LoRA--Think paper objective. I
did not inspect any live perception or E0 output, edit builder/runtime code,
run a model or tokenizer, train an adapter, or touch a GPU.

## Verdict

`E0-r` is scientifically useful and should remain the next bounded writer
diagnostic, but its present claim label is too strong.

The fixed renderer preserves **event-source provenance**: the action bytes were
emitted by the child before the result, and the outcome came from the public
world. It does not preserve **memory-authorship provenance**: the researcher-
specified renderer, rather than the child, decides that those bytes become the
canonical semantic training target

```text
EVENT <handle> AT <scene> DID <action> GOT <outcome>
```

Under the repository's vocabulary, that is **training on researcher-prepared
material whose factual fields are endogenously sourced**. It is not `learned`
or `self-learning`, because Rohin defined learning as including the model
preparing its own training material. Under the current operational invariant,
it is also not an authentic SLEEP compiler: the compiler may select, order,
replay, and pack existing child-authored spans, but may not synthesize a
missing semantic row.

Therefore:

- keep `E0-r` as an excluded, fixed-render **carrier ceiling** that asks whether
  the chosen LoRA writer can store, extract, causally redirect, and expose a
  mechanically normalized record of a real child/world interaction;
- do not call an `E0-r` pass “own-experience learning,” “child-authored SLEEP,”
  “DREAM,” or evidence that the agent turned experience into knowledge;
- never merge its fitted adapter into a child lineage; and
- require the next claim-bearing connected experiment to restore child-
  authored semantic formation, not merely child-authored `LINK` syntax.

This is not a reason to return to the original `E0` as the immediate writer
gate. It is a reason to give the two experiments different jobs and names.

## The distinction that decides the question

Four provenance questions must not be collapsed:

| layer | original `E0` | revised `E0-r` |
|---|---|---|
| Who emitted the executed action bytes before the outcome? | child | child |
| Who supplied the outcome? | public environment | public environment |
| Who formed the semantic EVENT target? | child, under an exact schema prompt | fixed researcher renderer |
| Who chose the parameter-update rule? | researcher | researcher |

Both protocols use ordinary researcher-chosen post-training machinery; that
alone is not the problem. The novelty being tested is whether the agent
prepares useful material from its own life. Original `E0` contains that step,
although heavily scaffolded. `E0-r` removes it to isolate the downstream
writer. The revised protocol therefore cleanly tests layers 3--5 of a carrier
pipeline after supplying layer 2:

```text
endogenous event -> [researcher serialization] -> LoRA write
                 -> candidate-free read -> clean-actor use
```

It does not test the full proposed route:

```text
endogenous event -> child perception/articulation/organization -> SLEEP
                 -> read -> later thought and action
```

Calling the first route own-experience learning would make the renderer perform
the precise transformation that the paper says the agent must learn to do.
Calling it researcher-supplied *facts* would also be imprecise: the factual
values are not invented or chosen by the researcher. The correct description
is **endogenous payload, exogenous serialization**.

## Original E0 versus E0-r

### Original E0

Original `E0` requires the child, after seeing immutable receipts, to emit all
`16/16` supported EVENT rows exactly. The checker can parse, join, and copy but
cannot fill or repair a semantic field. This respects the thinker/compiler
line and, if followed by a successful write and sterile read, supports a
bounded child-authored event relay.

Its disadvantage is diagnostic entanglement. A punctuation, schema-following,
or articulation failure ends the experiment before the LoRA writer is tested.
That is especially costly after the repository has already measured weak
post-outcome articulation. It also does not establish spontaneous exploration:
the harness requires both legal actions and provides the schema.

### E0-r

`E0-r` correctly removes articulation as a prerequisite for answering the
immediate engineering question: can the fixed writer carry one complete local
event and expose it without candidates? Running AUTH and OUTCOME_SWAP to full
dose is more informative about that question than another update-one semantic
conjunction.

The cost of this isolation is a narrower interpretation. A child-authored row
logged only as a diagnostic cannot mediate the fitted effect. Even `16/16`
diagnostic rows plus a successful rendered fit would show two adjacent
capabilities, not that the child's rows caused the memory.

Accordingly, original `E0` is the better **full provenance path**; `E0-r` is
the better **writer calibration path**. Neither result may silently inherit the
other's interpretation.

## Exact operations allowed at the thinker/compiler line

For a claim-bearing child-authored SLEEP lane, the compiler may:

1. verify that a child span predates the scored future query and is joined to
   immutable public action/outcome receipts;
2. accept or reject the complete span under prospectively fixed, semantic-
   preserving rules;
3. select, order, group, shuffle, replay, and pack accepted spans;
4. add fixed input-side request wrappers, delimiters, and example boundaries;
5. mask loss so only eligible child-authored target bytes receive gradient;
6. schedule a fixed number of exact replays and mix exact older child spans
   under a predeclared policy; and
7. attach hashes, evidence identities, chronology, and checker decisions in an
   audit projection that is not a target.

It may not:

- invent, infer, repair, replace, canonicalize, summarize, paraphrase, contrast,
  connect, or complete a semantic target field;
- turn a raw environment receipt into first-person or EVENT prose when the
  child did not write that prose;
- use a later goal, hidden answer, admission result, score, or evaluator state
  to choose the semantic content of a target;
- translate parent text into child text, even faithfully;
- select only successful or convenient child interpretations after seeing the
  evaluation result unless that selection rule was prospectively part of the
  scientific estimand; or
- treat a mechanically deranged control row as a child's memory.

A fixed renderer may still do those first two forbidden target-shaping
operations inside a separately named **prepared-material ceiling** such as
`E0-r`. That exception changes the claim lane; it does not change the
thinker/compiler invariant.

### The `MISS` targets need explicit treatment

The 16 trained-negative handles in `E0-r` are researcher-supplied interface
anchors. They are half of the canonical training rows before replay. They are
legitimate for a fixed writer diagnostic, but the receipt must report this
mixture and a pass cannot be described as training only on the child's own
material.

For a later self-learning lineage, either:

- teach the `MISS` interface once in a separately labeled birth/bootstrap
  adapter shared identically by every arm, then measure life-specific writes
  separately; or
- omit trained `MISS` anchors and test false memory against genuinely unseen
  addresses.

Silently mixing supplied `MISS` targets into each life and calling the whole
adapter child-authored would violate the same provenance rule in a smaller
way.

## Required E0-r labels, controls, and receipts

Rename the pass label from
`DEV_E0R_OWN_EXPERIENCE_EVENT_CARRIER_PASS` to something no downstream table
can overread, such as:

```text
DEV_E0R_FIXED_RENDERED_EVENT_CARRIER_PASS
```

The strongest allowed sentence is:

> On one excluded opaque development root, a fixed renderer converted a
> child's precommitted action bytes and public world outcomes into canonical
> event targets. A fixed LoRA write later generated those records without
> candidates, and a separate clean actor used them; swapping only the rendered
> outcome bindings redirected both records and actions.

Retain these controls:

- `RENDERED_AUTH`: authentic receipt fields;
- `RENDERED_OUTCOME_SWAP`: identical work and marginals with only within-scene
  outcome bindings changed; always label it synthetic and exclude it from all
  lineages;
- `OFF`: no fit under the same requests and actor questions;
- `EXACT_TEXT`: lossless zero-fit assay ceiling, never a strong memory
  baseline;
- fresh-process unmounted `SHADOW`: sterility check;
- trained-negative and separately unseen-negative handles, reported
  separately; and
- the prospectively frozen shortcut panel for ID, order, length, renderer, and
  target marginals.

Add or make explicit these receipts:

1. an exact four-field renderer receipt proving every target value is a byte
   copy of the precommit/public receipt and that no hidden field or later goal
   was read;
2. per-target provenance classes:
   `child_action`, `environment_outcome`, `researcher_schema`, and
   `researcher_interface_anchor`;
3. exact counts and loss mass for authentic rendered EVENT targets versus
   supplied `MISS` targets;
4. a separately reduced `CHILD_EVENT_EXACT_DIAGNOSTIC` formation rate that is
   never used to label the fitted result; and
5. an assertion that neither fitted E0-r adapter nor its rows enter any
   authentic child lineage or paper confirmation root.

The held request renderer tests extraction across request wording, not a held
event or cross-root generalization. The paired handles are repeated measures,
and one excluded root remains an engineering release, not a paper replicate.

If a stronger sentence about the *child source* is desired, E0-r also needs a
prospectively matched source-author control (for example, harness-emitted or
wrong-child precommits with the same executed event tape). But this is low
value for the carrier decision: because the child is required to precommit
both legal actions, authorship does not currently change the information in
the fitted row. It is better to reserve wrong-child and actor-chosen
information-seeking controls for M, where source policy can matter.

## What M must restore

Yes: M must restore child-authored `LINK` **and** child-authored semantic event
formation if it is to support the full paper objective.

At minimum, before the later goal is visible, the child must produce:

1. supported event records that bind its executed actions to public outcomes;
   and
2. a connection/`LINK` whose endpoints and relation content were chosen by the
   child from those records.

The grammar may constrain syntax, but the compiler cannot choose the link
endpoints, fill its relation, or derive the later policy target. The exact
child bytes must be the fitted targets. The authentic link must be
behaviorally necessary against the same child event rows with an atoms-only
pad and against an evidence/shape-matched wrong link. Source binding must be
tested separately from link value. After the information-seeking action, the
new child-authored event row must be added in SLEEP 2; old-cut, new-cut,
no-write, wrong-life, and outcome-twin controls must show that the final action
uses both old organization and new experience.

There are two valid staging choices:

- **Cleanest full path:** after E0-r qualifies the writer, run a fresh
  child-authored EVENT bridge, then M from a fresh lineage. This restores the
  complete causal chain and keeps the E0-r adapter out of it.
- **Cheaper intermediate:** use fixed-rendered EVENT atoms but require a
  child-authored LINK in M. This can test the added value of child organization
  over normalized events, but the allowed claim is only “child-authored
  organization over researcher-rendered experience.” It does not close
  own-experience self-learning and cannot be the final headline path.

If M uses fixed-rendered atoms and a fixed renderer also creates the new
post-action row, it may still establish connected carrier mechanics and
prospective action utility. It cannot establish that the agent learned to turn
its experience into knowledge. The paper's full objective remains open until
the child's semantic records actually mediate the weight change.

## Recommendation

Proceed with E0-r if its implementation is already the shortest route to a
full-dose writer answer, but revise its nomenclature and keep it outside every
authentic lineage. Treat it as the fixed-render equivalent of a supplied-data
post-training ceiling with unusually strong action/world provenance.

If it passes, do not spend on E0-r replications. Freeze the carrier, require
child-authored formation in the next fresh M development path, and include the
fixed-render path only as an upper-bound/control arm. If it fails, stop the
current semantic LoRA writer route as already specified; the failure says the
writer could not carry even a mechanically prepared event target at the
registered dose.

This separation preserves both scientific efficiency and Rohin's core line:
the lab may build the learning machinery, but it cannot quietly do the child's
thinking and then call the resulting update self-learning.
