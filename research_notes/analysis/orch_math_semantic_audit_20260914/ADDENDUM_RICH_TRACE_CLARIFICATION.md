## Governing distinction and self-critique

An **explicit operation-result expectation** states what an operation on identified evidence establishes about the requested quantity or a problem-imposed relation. It can appear before, during, or after the arithmetic, in present or past tense. It need not say “I expect,” use future tense, propose a second method, or repeat the computation in a separate check section. Deriving a result does not disqualify a statement from also explaining its expected meaning.

By contrast, bare performed arithmetic without a stated problem-level interpretation, or an unsupported “I checked; it is correct,” does not by itself establish this feature. A named quantity, heading, or pronoun is neither sufficient nor automatically necessary evidence of substantive ownership/checkability; the surrounding reasoning must do the work. Singular and plural operational agency are equally admissible. An arithmetic self-check also remains different from an unseen external checker verdict.

**My strongest self-critique:** some frozen C/W reasons imposed an additional verification-distinct-from-derivation requirement even though the contract permits the expectation route.
