## The short answer

We have moved from isolated abilities to actual experience → persistent write
→ fresh-process memory retrieval → action. We have not made that connection
reliably goal-directed on new instances, and we have not demonstrated the
self-improving learning process. The clearest current limitation is transfer
and retention of a learned controller, not an inability to fit its teaching
examples. The right next question is what experience teaches the transferable
operation, rather than whether another round on the same examples helps.

- The reader-audit lesson comparison reaches16/16 versus7/16 on its parent-free
  held classification panel (SEQ239). This is one source-scaffolded discrepancy
  task, not a general independent truth checker or whole-life parenting effect.
- Fresh same-family single-hop write/use forks reach4/4 routing and4/4 recall,
  retaining twelve earlier facts and16/16 audit cases in the SEQ245 comparison.
  Uniform replay matches selected replay: the working loop is useful evidence;
  a benefit from the selector is not established.

  guidance removed, produce3/4 contextual two-hop goals on taught and new-ID

  ablation stays0/4 (SEQ252), although achieved retention also differs.
- Four fresh experienced EVENTs can then be written into that same adapter;
  after reload all four are recalled, and all16 actual in-episode memory replies
  match the source records (SEQ253). This is a real parametric connection into
  action, not prompt residue or stored-text fallback in the parametric condition.
- SEQ256's broader two-world command teaching produces8/8 parent-free TRAIN
  goals and4/4 opposite-goal pairs versus3/8 goals and0/4 pairs with new target
  labels masked. The model can acquire this taught operation on those examples.

## What failed or remains unproven

- The first connected readout emitted invalid multi-command continuations;
  specifying one-command turns enabled real reads but still0/4 goals
  (SEQ248/249). Having individually functional components was insufficient.
- Perfect new memory recall in SEQ253 yields only2/4 goals, exactly the successes
  of a goal-blind first-port baseline. Correct memory delivery did not establish
  semantic goal-directed use. A stronger replay repair changed no goal outcomes
  and lost one audit case; that recipe is closed (SEQ254).
- The goal-sensitive operation was never wholly absent: unchanged37ec already
  solves2/4 strict opposite-goal pairs on SEQ255's held worlds. SEQ256 does not
  improve those endpoints at all: both fit arms remain5/8 goals,2/4 pairs, with
  the same failed case identities. Both also lose an original-graph success
  (3/4→2/4). Full supervision retains old memory better, but retention is not
  matched and the primary held comparison ties. That recipe is closed too.
- Selection experiments expose off-policy/material problems rather than a clean
  semantic-selector advantage: the replayed control yields invalid pointers;
  its admitted material gives weaker recall under a shared writer (SEQ246/247).
- These are exposed DEV descendants and small fixed task families. We have not
  shown H1/H2 at population scale, faster autonomous future learning, a mature
  self-directed compiler, or a successful full developmental flywheel.

## My view on your articulation idea

I agree it is a serious candidate, especially now that command-only teaching
fits the examples but fails to transfer. A child explaining which evidence
connects the current state to its goal could supply supervision absent from
the final command alone. But5–10× more tokens is a proposed dose, not evidence
of5–10× more useful learning. Repetition, unsupported explanations and verbose
shortcuts are possible; relevance and verifiable use matter more than length.

I would distinguish three outcomes: it learns to produce longer explanations;
it makes better parent-free decisions; and those explanations become better
material for its next sleep. The first does not establish either of the others.
Also, our current action contract accepts one executable command per turn:
raising the output length alone would create invalid responses. An articulation
comparison needs an explicit way to retain the child's explanation alongside
its exact action, without a harness silently deciding the action for it.

A suitable separate comparison would use the same source worlds, successful
outcomes, parent visibility and replay, with terse versus child-articulated
targets; report actual supervised tokens and compute, not just matched updates.
Teacher requests for explanation must be removed from student prefixes, and
new factual assertions must remain source-grounded. Read held action success,
retention and parent-free articulation separately. This is a candidate after
or alongside a released data path, not a silent amendment to the running
breadth protocol. We should not mistake a length multiplier for a compiler.
