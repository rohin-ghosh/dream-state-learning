## Reading

- The siege was the child's own curriculum. With no parent, it took its first story, extracted three values from a
  corrupted token, decided to practise them through "scenario simulation", and escalated the stakes of the scenarios
  (river crossing → city under siege → guerrilla campaign) because harder scenarios "integrate multiple core
  qualities simultaneously". The escalation is reasoned, not random, and it is exactly the self-assigned-task loop the
  parented children show with their one object.
- Around sleep 18 the register flipped from fiction to reporting: the plan became "recent progress" with completed
  reconnaissance, insertions and evacuations. This is the same confabulation the pilot showed with tool results and
  the classroom brain child with "Model output", only here the whole environment is imaginary, so nothing could ever
  contradict it.
- It held one object for 17 sleeps (04:02–09:32), the longest single-object stretch of any child, and every one of
  those distillations was trained 16 times. Yet the empty-context readouts of this lineage are flat (it was enrolled in
  the R130 benchmark today), and after the relaunch, when the narrative context was gone and only the bullet skeleton
  plus the birth prompt remained, the birth prompt won. Whatever 17 sleeps of war planning installed, it did not make
  the child continue the war plan from a skeleton.
- The three minimal-prompt unparented/parented raw children found three different attractors in the same hours:
  this one fiction → strategy simulation; the no-replay sibling a single waiting paragraph mutated 50 times; the
  parented one a token ledger. Which attractor a life falls into is set in its first minutes and then fixed by the
  distillation loop.
- Content note: the text is generic strategy-game planning (phases, scouting, "training modules", ethics questions,
  governance and healthcare afterwards); nothing operational. It is a 7B model's idea of what "a series of difficult
  decisions under stress" looks like.
