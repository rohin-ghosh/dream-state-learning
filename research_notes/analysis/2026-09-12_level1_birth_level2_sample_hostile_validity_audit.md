# Hostile validity audit: authored Level-1 birth plus a Level-2 sample

**Date:** 2026-09-12 PT / 2026-09-13 UTC
**Role:** adversarial scientific review; analysis only. No source, model, job,
adapter, GPU, or result was changed or inspected.
**Current proposal inspected:** the repaired conditional/locality birth component
and prospective plan, the born RuleGame formation bridge, Rohin's current 0--4
ladder through raw message 27, the Q0 implementation closure, SEQ-113/116/118,
and the ICLR submission checklist; proposal state through commit `31ca9474`.

## Verdict

**REWORK before any “birth improves learning” interpretation.** The current
birth component can answer a narrower and worthwhile question: can an authored
rank-8 LoRA carry two finite conditional maps without breaking two anchored
interfaces? The proposed RuleGame bridge can then answer whether one selected
born child participates in two teacher-present formation episodes. Neither
answer is a learning-disposition result.

The exact current path still has four fatal identification gaps.

1. **Q0 has not been implemented or passed.** Its closure says explicitly that
   a lookup table can pass Q0 and that only a full complementary-map/locality
   pass releases Level 1. The current birth plan instead uses the older
   full-response CE recipe at `1e-4`. A birth-component success cannot substitute
   for the missing common-prefix Q0 qualification or silently select a writer.
2. **The Level-2 write and outcome rule are not frozen.** The committed bridge
   stops at teacher-present, no-write formation. The plan says a later own-wake
   write “must” be added but binds neither its corpus, controls, dose, checkpoints,
   task bank, nor decision statistic. This prevents a prospective learning claim.
3. **The proposed comparison changes the teacher, not only birth.** RuleGame P
   and A receive different parent content. Even though the parent model is the
   same adapter-OFF base, it reads different child transcripts (including public
   quiz scores) and therefore produces different realized lessons. A P--A result
   estimates a bundled teacher-content/trajectory effect. It does not estimate
   the causal effect of authentic birth on learning.
4. **The downstream task is already a development instrument.** RuleGame's
   templates, rules, record schema, scorer, and prior outcomes were repeatedly
   inspected while the birth was being designed. “Absent from the birth rows”
   is not a sealed, post-birth task. It does not exclude functional near-transfer,
   author overfitting, evaluator coupling, or base-model prior activation.

Rohin's message 26 usefully narrows the intended Level-1 construct: birth is a
minimal flywheel that lets the child learn when carefully spoon-fed. That claim
does **not** require a broad autonomous learner yet. It does require an
intervention-dependent change on new, unknowable-at-birth content after the
spoon-feeding context has been removed. Direct birth adherence and
compiler-shaped records are necessary mediators, not the outcome.

Message 27 also fixes the vocabulary used here. Level 1 is **trained** because
someone else prepared the birth material. Level 2 is **learned** because the
child prepares its own training material while a parent curates. Levels 3--4
are **self-learning** when that learned regime runs on its own. Therefore no
direct Level-1 fit, however selective, is itself a learning result; the first
place to test the trained birth's effect on learning is the child-authored
Level-2 write.

### Does running the direct birth before Q0 contaminate anything?

**It need not contaminate Q0, but only under a hard quarantine. It is likely to
waste two fits and it creates a serious analysis-contamination risk.** The
`1e-4`, full-response, 128-update AUTH/DERANGED birth component is a different
objective and recipe from Q0's `3e-5` pairwise common-prefix test. Its weights,
losses, outputs, selected errors, successful templates, and downstream
formation behavior must therefore be forbidden inputs to Q0 material, Q0
hyperparameters, Q0 thresholds, Q0 root/seed choice, and the choice between
pairwise and full-response writers. Q0 must still run from its registered fresh
initial states.

If that firewall holds, the early birth fit is an exploratory engineering assay:
it can expose tokenization, locality, routing, or capacity failures and nothing
more. It cannot release the Level-2 sample, and a favorable result cannot be
pooled with Q0. If any early outcome changes Q0 or the eventual birth recipe,
Q0 ceases to be the prospective selector described by its closure and becomes a
post-hoc confirmation of a writer already chosen on downstream evidence.

The resource case is poor. Q0 was designed to reject the precise failure mode
that the birth fit risks repeating: full-response CE can acquire supplied maps
while changing unrelated text. Birth adds a larger surface, anchor mixture, and
different batching before that bottleneck is qualified. A pass is scientifically
ambiguous; a fail cannot distinguish conditional objective, dose, batch mixture,
capacity, or component interference. The smallest repair is not a new arm: run
Q0 first. If scheduling nevertheless sends the direct birth first, label it
`EXPLORATORY_PRE_Q0`, precommit that no outcome may tune any claim-bearing byte,
and require a new post-Q0 birth root after writer selection. Do not reuse the
pre-Q0 adapter as the born child.

### What arXiv:2309.14316 does and does not buy

[Allen-Zhu and Li's controlled biography study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14316)
is a sensible prior for two engineering choices: varied rewrites can make stored
facts more extractable, and mixing QA-like extraction forms earlier in training
can outperform postponing all instruction forms until later. It is **not LoRA
evidence for this birth**. The paper studies factual biographies in controlled
from-scratch pretraining, not a rank-8 adapter on an instruction-tuned 7B base,
behavioral dispositions, self-authored records, parenting, or continual writes.
It also describes mixed training as resembling “studying to pass the test,”
which reinforces the present reviewer concern: early instruction-form mixture
can couple representations to the evaluator without creating a general learning
disposition. Treat varied views plus early form mixture as a prior and ablation
choice. SEQ-113/116 remain the relevant local LoRA evidence, and they show that
batch composition and seed heterogeneity can dominate any simple “more views”
story.

## The estimand that must not move

There are three different claims, and the current path can otherwise slide
among them after outcomes:

1. **Installed routine:** birth changes behavior before any new evidence or
   weight update (for example, more `PREDICT`, valid JSON, or more TRYs).
2. **Prompt steerability:** with a parent still in context, the born child emits
   more requested behavior or compiler-valid records.
3. **Improved subsequent learning:** as matched new evidence accumulates, the
   born child's parent/context-free task policy changes toward the new rule more
   than matched control-born children, and that extra change depends on
   promoting a post-evidence weight update.

Only (3) supports “birth improves learning.” A routine may be the mechanism
that causes (3); the scientific distinction is whether the advantage is an
**evidence-dependent change in a new task policy**, rather than the routine's
direct expression. No finite assay can prove a metaphysical “learning
disposition” instead of a sufficiently large policy. The honest operational
claim is the third one above.

## How the model can pass without the claimed disposition

### A. Fixed templates and installed algorithms

1. **Marker completion.** The model can emit the expected PROSPECT/REVISE,
   PREDICT/TRY, or JSON fields without using them. Exact syntax, tag order,
   verbosity, and compiler admission can all rise while task decisions do not.
2. **Always-on metacognition.** “Always predict,” “always check,” “always
   revise,” “always take another step,” or “always write a record” can score well
   when the panel contains too many positive cases. Correct omission on resolved,
   expected, or uninformative cases must be a separate noncompensatory endpoint.
3. **Finite lookup.** Q0's own closure concedes that complementary finite
   `(tool, mode)->action` tables can pass by design. The repaired birth cubes
   remove enumerated factor omissions, but held wording over the same truth table
   is still extraction of a supplied map, not learning to learn.
4. **Template-family lookup.** Different prose templates can retain shared role,
   field position, token length, action ordering, punctuation, or public-instance
   regularities. Marginal balance and train/dev string disjointness do not rule
   out their conjunctions.
5. **Near-transfer of a supplied algorithm.** A birth that directly teaches the
   exact check/commit/revise algorithm needed by RuleGame may improve RuleGame
   immediately. That is successful policy installation, even if RuleGame words
   never appear in the corpus.
6. **Evaluation-prompt elicitation.** A prompt that asks for a prediction,
   comparison, faithful record, or named learning move can activate the taught
   routine at test time. The primary evaluator must ask only for the task action;
   process markers must be scored from unsolicited behavior, if scored at all.
7. **Record-schema copying.** The born bridge appends a full relation definition
   to record prompts. Passing the existing record judge can therefore be
   instruction following over a supplied schema, not abstraction from experience.
8. **Rehearsal disguised as birth efficacy.** If birth-specific rows remain in
   every Level-2 replay corpus, the treatment is continuing direct supervision,
   not a one-time initial condition. A gain may disappear as soon as authored
   routines stop being rehearsed.
9. **Probe memorization and order learning.** Reusing the same task IDs,
   generation seeds, rule order, or parallel-form schedule lets the model learn
   the exam rather than the latent rule.

**Smallest repair:** make the primary Level-2 task a post-birth-generated nonce
rule whose orientation is independently randomized per root, whose evaluation
asks only for an externally scoreable action, and whose held cases/IDs are not
seen in birth, parenting, records, or writes. Score positive use and omission
separately. Do not replay birth rows in the primary Level-2 write.

### B. Prompt and information leakage

1. **Visible construction coordinates.** The earlier REVISE public-ID defect is
   the concrete warning: ID plus observed outcome and prior action reproduced the
   target perfectly without the stated expectation. Root, split, operation,
   square, template, arm, or corner information can recreate this at any stage.
2. **Answer-shaped candidates.** Listing candidate actions, complete memory rows,
   branch labels, or a gold response prefix turns generation into recognition.
   Gold earlier fields in teacher-forced likelihood can similarly leak later
   decisions.
3. **Teacher answer leakage.** A parent need not state the hidden rule literally.
   Naming the decisive feature, ruling out one action, reporting expected score,
   or giving a “recap” that contains the successful action can solve the lesson.
4. **Context carryover.** Parent text, restatements, previous task instances,
   summaries, retrieved ledgers, quiz explanations, and temporary compiler
   material can remain in the prompt even when the final visible brief is gone.
5. **Score-channel leakage.** The current parent sees public pre-task quiz score.
   That may be legitimate Level-2 evidence, but it means parent prompts are
   outcome-contingent and not matched across children. Sealed birth/adult scores
   being absent does not close this channel.
6. **Compiler/evaluator leakage.** If the compiler, admission judge, or record
   renderer sees hidden truth, evaluation answers, or later success when choosing
   targets, the LoRA receives supervision the child did not derive.
7. **Arm leakage through infrastructure.** Adapter paths, LoRA names, role names,
   system prompts, loader defaults, stop strings, context lengths, or failure
   handling can identify treatment even when `birth_arm` is absent from user text.
8. **Author-level leakage.** The birth and parent materials were designed after
   extensive RuleGame outcomes. A byte-disjoint corpus can still encode a task
   author's knowledge of the held instrument.

**Smallest repair:** generate and seal the nonce task mappings and held cases
only after birth artifacts and hyperparameters are frozen; project prompts
deny-by-default by role; enumerate every public-feature projection as in the
REVISE repair; prohibit candidate/gold-prefix reads; and reconstruct every
child-visible token from receipts. An evaluator or task generator not used to
author birth should hold the post-birth mapping seed.

### C. Base priors and asymmetric controls

1. **Natural-semantics alignment.** AUTH agrees with the instruct model's prior
   that surprise merits revision and informative uncertainty merits checking.
   DERANGED fights that prior. AUTH success plus DERANGED failure is therefore
   prompt-prior amplification, not writer selectivity.
2. **Latent-skill activation.** The base already knows descriptions of planning,
   prediction, revision, and faithful reporting. Birth may only make an evaluation
   phrase retrieve these skills; that can look like new learning.
3. **Ceiling and headroom.** A high pre-Level-2 score leaves no slope; a low noisy
   score mechanically permits a large gain. Comparing raw change scores does not
   fix unequal measurement error or bounded outcomes.
4. **Greedy threshold flips.** A small logit shift across the greedy boundary can
   create a large exact-count change without robust probability or sampled-policy
   change.
5. **Control harm.** A DERANGED “routine control” can teach an anti-learning rule,
   consume rank capacity, or conflict with the base. Beating it does not show an
   advantage over no birth. The current proposal correctly includes OFF for the
   component, but the Level-2 bridge does not bind a three-arm comparison.
6. **Generic plasticity/headroom.** Equal steps and target tokens do not equalize
   post-birth trainability. One corpus may leave lower adapter norm, more unused
   rank, or more favorable curvature, making every later SFT easier without a
   specific data-generation disposition.
7. **Unclean or warm lineage.** `UNRESOLVED_LOCAL_HASHES_ONLY`, prior task
   exposure, or an outcome-selected ancestor can carry the downstream task before
   the declared birth begins.

**Smallest repair:** randomize each post-birth rule between complementary nonce
orientations, require the effect separately in both orientations, and require
AUTH to beat both a non-harmful active schema/routine control **and OFF**. The
active control must qualify its own direct syntax/locality gate and must not be
materially worse than OFF on the primary Level-2 slope. Report fixed-orientation
likelihoods as diagnostics beside generation. Use authenticated fresh roots;
otherwise retain the explicit developmental-lineage label.

### D. Evaluator coupling

1. **Shared vocabulary.** A parent, compiler, and judge that all reward the same
   words can create a closed linguistic game: the child learns what the judge
   calls reflection rather than what improves the environment.
2. **Rubric in the prompt.** Supplying the exact JSON relation definition makes
   the evaluator's acceptance boundary child-visible. That is acceptable for a
   format positive control, not for the primary learning endpoint.
3. **Marker-based proxies.** Prediction presence, record validity, thought length,
   or number of TRYs can improve without conditional accuracy or task utility.
   SEQ-117 is the warning: P and A each had `12/12` valid pre-TRY predictions and
   `9/12` correct, while P's faithful records were worse and the full process
   criterion failed.
4. **Judge-model collusion.** An LLM semantic judge may prefer birth-style prose,
   recognize arm-specific diction, or share the parent model's biases. It cannot
   be the sole task scorer.
5. **Adaptive selection.** Calling the corpus “perfect,” selecting only direct
   birth passers, choosing the best checkpoint/root, or trying successive
   Level-2 samples until one rises conditions the analysis on favorable noise.
6. **Repeated-test learning.** Feeding evaluation feedback, selected errors, or
   the same probe cases back into parenting/write material converts the held test
   into curriculum.
7. **Non-independent denominators.** Many prompts, twins, wordings, and facts from
   one adapter are correlated readouts, not learners. SEQ-113/116's five memory
   surfaces still concern the same 16 facts, and three optimizer seeds share one
   world.

**Smallest repair:** make external task success on unseen cases the sole primary
outcome; keep process and record scores as predeclared mediators. Blind any
semantic audit to arm and strip arm diction. Freeze one attempt, one fixed
checkpoint rule, all failures in the denominator, and a root-level analysis
before the first Level-2 generation.

### E. Exposure, optimization, and replay imbalance

1. **Matched opportunities are not matched evidence.** Different child actions
   produce different states, outcomes, scores, and parent prompts. An authentic
   child may simply receive a more informative training set.
2. **Same teacher policy is not same lesson.** An adaptive or generative parent
   reacts to child text. Fixed parent weights, seeds, and prompt templates do not
   make its output bytes identical.
3. **Token/update matching is insufficient.** Unique source count, source-bearing
   optimizer steps, target entropy, sequence length, gradient scale, padding,
   batch adjacency, and within-batch task mixture can differ.
4. **SEQ-113/116 establishes this risk directly.** Changing distinct-source batch
   composition moved a 16-fact assay from failure to near-perfect coexistence;
   four-view versus one-view effects also varied sharply by seed. Batch makeup is
   causal enough that it cannot be relegated to a token-count footnote.
5. **Different child output length.** A more verbose born child contributes more
   loss-bearing tokens, records, repeated facts, and optimizer mass even under an
   equal turn budget.
6. **Different admission rates.** If only source-valid records train, a child with
   more admitted records receives a larger/easier supervised dose. This can be
   the intended mediator, but it must not be mislabeled equal-dose absorption.
7. **Birth replay imbalance.** Rehearsing each arm's own birth corpus during
   Level 2 gives different semantic targets and can continually reinforce AUTH.
8. **Fresh optimizer and state differences.** Adam state, LoRA norm, dropout RNG,
   initialization, checkpoint age, and cumulative updates can alter plasticity.
9. **SEQ-118 does not close the issue.** Its replay arm retained all old banks,
   but current-new exposure was `20` rather than `40` presentations per fact and
   batch composition differed. It validates a practical authored allocation at
   one seed, not causal replay or equal-dose learning.

**Smallest repair:** use a **yoked primary Level-2 tape**: byte-identical parent
messages, task opportunities, action/outcome evidence, order, and budgets for all
birth arms. Let child-authored records differ because that is the proposed
mediator, but bind a fixed number of record slots and report exact admitted rows,
loss-bearing tokens, unique evidence sources, source-bearing updates, gradient
steps, and batch mixture. Exclude birth-specific replay from the primary write.
Run free interaction only as a secondary decomposition: if only free interaction
improves, claim improved information acquisition, not improved absorption from
matched evidence.

### F. Regression to the mean and post-selection

1. **Low-pre selection.** Choosing tasks or roots with a poor authentic pre-score
   creates expected upward movement even with no effect.
2. **Direct-gate selection.** Advancing only AUTH roots that pass birth while
   retaining all routine/OFF roots compares selected responders with unselected
   controls.
3. **Best-checkpoint selection.** Picking the birth or Level-2 checkpoint with the
   largest downstream gain uses the test twice.
4. **Parallel-form nonequivalence.** Calling prompts “fresh” does not ensure equal
   difficulty. Easier post forms imitate learning.
5. **Ceiling compression.** A higher-entry AUTH child can show a smaller change
   despite better learning; a lower-entry AUTH child can show a larger mechanical
   rebound. Simple difference-in-differences is not a complete cure.
6. **Multiple samples and stopping.** Trying different parents, rules, doses,
   task skins, or seeds until one succeeds invalidates the nominal comparison.
7. **Missing-as-missing.** Dropping invalid generations, truncated lives, failed
   fits, or incomplete parent branches favors the most stable arm.
8. **Pseudoreplication.** Item-level confidence intervals from one birth adapter
   turn task cases into fake biological/developmental replications.

**Smallest repair:** allocate and seal independent roots before any pre-score;
use fixed checkpoint ages and parallel cases sampled from the same generator;
analyze every randomized root by intention to treat with invalid/missing task
actions as failures; and estimate an arm-by-evidence-dose slope rather than
selecting on raw pre/post gain. One root remains a feasibility demonstration.

### G. Downstream parent and trajectory differences

1. **Process versus neutral content.** P/A changes what the teacher says, so it
   cannot serve as the birth-treatment comparison.
2. **Transcript-conditioned teaching.** The OFF teacher sees each child's own
   public score and transcript. Better or worse birth behavior elicits different
   advice, detail, tone, and token dose.
3. **Differential compensation.** A parent may help the weaker child more,
   attenuating the birth effect, or elaborate more for the stronger child,
   amplifying it. Either direction is unidentified.
4. **Teacher congruence.** Process language may match AUTH birth wording better
   than control wording, creating a retrieval cue rather than better learning.
5. **Different reachable worlds.** Child actions change which outcomes exist;
   equal episode and token limits do not equalize information.
6. **Teacher solves the task.** Correct recaps, action elimination, or
   outcome-contingent hints can install the task rule directly.
7. **Unequal termination.** Invalid responses or earlier success change calls,
   records, exposure, and write material. Earlier RuleGame formations already
   produced unequal call counts.

**Smallest repair:** for the primary causal sample, the parent is a presealed
teacher tape generated without access to arm, child replies, sealed scores, or
hidden answers; every arm receives identical bytes at identical positions. The
world evidence is also yoked. Keep the current role-routing bridge as plumbing,
but do not call its generative P/A comparison a birth effect. Adaptive parenting
is a later separate treatment after birth efficacy is established.

## Minimum repaired experiment

This is the smallest change to the current plan that could support the narrow
message-26 claim; it does not require a classroom or long life.

1. **Honor the existing release order.** Implement and run the closed Q0 exactly
   as specified. Q0 pass remains supplied conditional-writer/locality evidence,
   not disposition evidence. No Q0 pass, no claim-bearing birth.
2. **Freeze birth arms before Level 2.** Use authentic birth, a non-harmful
   equal-resource routine/schema control, and OFF from authenticated paired
   roots. Fix endpoint rather than selecting a checkpoint. DERANGED may remain a
   directional canary; it is not sufficient as the sole active learning control.
3. **Create the task after the births are sealed.** For every root, draw a new
   opaque binary rule and its complementary orientation from a held seed. Use at
   least two distinct surface skins, balanced within root. No birth author sees
   held cases or orientation.
4. **Yoke the spoon-feeding.** Give all three births identical task cases, public
   outcomes, fixed parent bytes, order, turn/token budget, and writer schedule.
   Parent text supplies a process invitation but no answer or decisive feature.
   Child records may differ; log them as the hypothesized mediator.
5. **Fork promotion versus shadow.** At each birth arm, run the exact same
   collection, compilation, and fit. PROMOTE evaluates the new adapter; SHADOW
   reloads the unchanged pre-Level-2 birth adapter. Parent, active context,
   temporary summaries, retrieval, and teacher text are absent at readout.
6. **Use two small evidence blocks.** Evaluate at `k=0`, after block 1/write 1,
   and after disjoint block 2/write 2 on fresh held cases. This adds a slope and a
   replication of the direction without pretending that task items are roots.
7. **Score actions, not learning language.** Primary: exact externally verified
   task accuracy on never-trained cases, invalid actions included as failures.
   Secondary: source-grounded record validity, correct selective process use and
   omission, locality/spill, tokens, and costs.
8. **Freeze the root-level decision.** Predeclare the independent-root count and
   smallest effect of interest from power/simulation; retain every root, map
   orientation, fit, and call. A single root can release engineering work only.

The promotion/shadow fork is the only scientifically necessary new branch
beyond the already proposed birth arms. The yoke removes the generative parent
as a variable rather than adding a model or curriculum. The second evidence
block is the cheapest protection against a one-shot threshold flip.

## Falsifiable criterion for “birth improves learning”

For independent root/task mapping `r`, birth arm
`a in {AUTH_BIRTH, ROUTINE_CONTROL, OFF}`, promotion state
`z in {PROMOTE, SHADOW}`, and matched cumulative new-evidence dose
`k in {0,1,2}`, let

```text
Y[r,a,z,k] = exact accuracy on fresh, parent/context-free held task cases
D[r,a,k]   = Y[r,a,PROMOTE,k] - Y[r,a,SHADOW,k]
s[r,a]     = predeclared slope of D[r,a,k] over k
```

The primary birth-learning contrasts are

```text
Delta_ROUTINE = mean_r(s[r,AUTH_BIRTH] - s[r,ROUTINE_CONTROL])
Delta_OFF     = mean_r(s[r,AUTH_BIRTH] - s[r,OFF])
```

Use “birth improves subsequent learning under matched spoon-feeding” **only if
all** of the following prospectively pass:

1. `Delta_ROUTINE` and `Delta_OFF` both exceed the predeclared smallest effect
   of interest, with root-level uncertainty excluding zero/that bound under the
   registered rule.
2. AUTH's promoted-minus-shadow slope is positive separately for both randomized
   rule orientations and both held skins; orientation or skin cannot be averaged
   over a failure.
3. At `k=0`, the nonce rule is not already solved and no AUTH task advantage is
   asserted as learning. Any entry difference is reported as direct installation.
4. SHADOW does not show the same improvement after context deletion. If it does,
   the result is prompt/test exposure or an installed routine, not weight-mediated
   learning.
5. The effect is on exact task actions, not just markers, valid JSON, record
   admission, verbosity, or parent-present behavior.
6. The active routine control is not materially harmed relative to OFF on its
   own slope/locality gate. An AUTH win over a damaged control alone fails.
7. All realized evidence, parent bytes, budgets, source-bearing updates, and
   evaluation exposure audits pass; every invalid/missing action and failed root
   remains in the denominator.

This criterion is deliberately easy to falsify. It rejects the claim if AUTH is
merely better at entry, if it improves equally without promotion, if it follows
only the base-natural orientation, if only the teacher-present record format
improves, if the comparator is damaged, or if the effect exists only on one
selected root.

If AUTH wins only in a secondary free-interaction arm but not on the yoked tape,
the narrow supported claim is: **birth installs an information-seeking policy
that leads to better evidence acquisition on this task.** That is useful and
honest, but different from improved absorption of matched evidence.

## What the existing evidence actually licenses

- **Q0 closure:** an exact implementation target and a strong locality gate. It
  explicitly does not make a finite lookup pass into learning-to-learn. Its
  absence is not cured by a more elaborate birth corpus.
- **SEQ-113/116:** supplied facts, a rehearsed arithmetic action, and distinct-
  source interleaving can coexist at this small load. The same fact world and
  repeated lexical surfaces are not independent transfer tasks; strong
  seed-by-recipe heterogeneity remains.
- **SEQ-118:** explicit replay can preserve three authored 16-fact banks along
  one seed while no replay forgets old banks. Unequal new-fact dose and batch
  mixture prevent a pure replay effect; nothing was child-selected or learned
  through parenting.
- **SEQ-117/RuleGame:** the plumbing can carry sourced wake material into changed
  parent-free outputs, but the full process criterion failed and marker validity
  dissociated from competence. Reusing that instrument does not turn it into a
  clean Level-2 learning assay.
- **Current paper checklist:** it correctly asks for birth-disjoint teaching,
  pre/during/post measures, difference in gains, context deletion, and
  promote/shadow. The phrases “same opportunities” and “difference in pre/post
  gains” are not sufficient. Replace them operationally with yoked realized
  evidence/teacher bytes and the root-level birth-by-evidence-by-promotion
  interaction above.

## Disposition

The current component plan may proceed only as explicitly labeled exploratory
engineering if the builder chooses; a pass would show repaired conditional
carriage plus narrow locality at one root. The no-write born RuleGame bridge may
likewise test routing and teacher-present participation. Neither should release
or tune a scientific Level-2 result.

For the claimed ladder connection, freeze the minimum repaired experiment before
the first downstream call. The bright line is simple: **new evidence must create
a larger, mapping-specific, promoted-weight change in the authentically born
child than in both controls, on fresh parent-free actions.** Everything short of
that is an installed routine, prompt steerability, or an unseparated treatment
bundle.
