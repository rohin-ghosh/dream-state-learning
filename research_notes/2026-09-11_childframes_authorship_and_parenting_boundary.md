# Child-frame authorship and parenting boundary

Date: 2026-09-11

Status: interpretation of the already finished/running CF cells. This note
does not authorize a new cell, implementation, model/tokenizer call, training,
GPU use, parenting run, claim, or C11 work.

## Ruling

Finish the already-running CF replication cells and the queued taught
`t/u` cells unchanged. They are useful mechanism scouts. Do not describe them
as a durable parenting result or as evidence that the writer detects
self-authorship.

The exact supported chain is narrower:

1. An instruction changes what the frozen child writes while inspecting an
   event.
2. That changed prose becomes the supervised sleep corpus.
3. The resulting adapter can be compared after the instruction and childhood
   prompt are absent.

A positive `t-b` contrast would therefore show **instruction-conditioned
mechanism matching in this repaired assay**: immediate guidance caused the
child to produce a more write-compatible corpus. It would not show that the
child learned the guidance, transferred it to a new episode without the
guidance, or became easier to parent.

## Why self-authorship cannot explain `a` versus `b`

For both variants, the compiled row is bare text:

`child prose + exact canonical owner-colour sentence`

and loss is applied to every token. The generation prompt and the authorship
metadata are absent from training. Given identical prose, variants `a` and `b`
produce identical training bytes. In the roughly 14% of `b` lines that omit
the requested endpoint, the harness supplies it anyway.

The trainer therefore cannot know whether the endpoint was written by the
child or appended by the harness. The observed difference must be in the bytes
of the generated prose (or in training variance), not authorship itself.

SEQ-049 identifies one important textual difference. The planted colour occurs
in only 26--28% of `a` prose but 77--79% of `b` prose. The exact
owner--colour relation occurs in only 2--4% of `b` prose. This makes semantic
repetition a plausible mediator, not a proved one. It also corrects the strong
sentence that harness-appended endings fail to store: `a` completion varied
from 0.307 to 0.945 across the three banks.

## What the running cells can answer

- `b` versus the synthetic `F` cell: can frozen-child prose under an explicit
  endpoint instruction support the same bounded associative write?
- `t` versus `b`: does a fixed relation-restatement lesson alter the raw prose,
  and does that altered corpus change downstream completion/binding/spill?
- `u` versus `c`: exploratory only, because both the positive and negative
  instructions change.
- seed-1/2 and node replication: is the extreme bank variance stable material
  variation or writer/optimizer/node instability?

None of these cells includes an adaptive parent, sham feedback, a clean
child-checkpoint fork, lesson removal before a fresh authoring exam, or a
retention test. Those remain required for causal parenting.

## Next decision after the queued cells

Do not add another broad CF sweep before reading `t/u` and the fresh banks.
If the semantic-repetition hypothesis remains live, the smallest clean
follow-up is a 2x2 on the same child-generated bytes:

| THINK output | loss on full child row | loss on emitted endpoint only |
|---|---:|---:|
| matched sham/style instruction | S-FULL | S-END |
| grounded relation-restatement instruction | R-FULL | R-END |

The compiler may validate and mask bytes but must not append, repair,
paraphrase, pad, or invent an endpoint. A missing endpoint is a failed event,
not a repaired label. Hold event identities, endpoint touches, filler, row
order, optimizer seed and token budget fixed. This separates semantic content
from loss placement without crossing the THINK/compiler boundary.

Before that optional 2x2, prioritize the reviewed multi-key writer gateway.
It is the cheaper falsifier of the more fundamental question: whether the
current writer can carry several conditional native actions instead of one
broad habit. CF results cannot answer that question.

## Claim language

Allowed developmental wording:

> Immediate guidance changed the frozen child's authored training text; we
> tested whether the resulting corpus was more compatible with the same
> bounded write mechanism.

Forbidden from these cells alone:

- self-authorship makes the writer store a memory;
- the child learned how to remember;
- parenting survived removal;
- the adapter selectively remembers;
- the result establishes Dream--LoRA--Think continual learning.
