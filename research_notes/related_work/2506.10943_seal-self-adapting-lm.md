# Self-Adapting Language Models
arXiv: 2506.10943  
Authors: Adam Zweiger, Jyothish Pari, Han Guo, Ekin Akyürek, Yoon Kim, Pulkit Agrawal

## Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) are powerful but static; they lack mechanisms to adapt their weights in response to new tasks, knowledge, or examples. We introduce Self-Adapting LLMs (SEAL), a framework that enables LLMs to self-adapt by generating their own finetuning data and update directives. Given a new input, the model produces a self-edit-a generation that may restructure the information in different ways, specify optimization hyperparameters, or invoke tools for data augmentation and gradient-based updates. Through supervised finetuning (SFT), these self-edits result in persistent weight updates, enabling lasting adaptation. To train the model to produce effective self-edits, we use a reinforcement learning loop with the downstream performance of the updated model as the reward signal. Unlike prior approaches that rely on separate adaptation modules or auxiliary networks, SEAL directly uses the model's own generation to control its adaptation process. Experiments on knowledge incorporation and few-shot generalization show that SEAL is a promising step toward language models capable of self-directed adaptation. Our website and code is available at https://jyopari.github.io/posts/seal.

## Our differentiation
(Fable, 2026-09-13; from the abstract and the summary in WHAT_TO_PARENT_FROM_THE_LITERATURE_2026-09-12.md — verify against the paper before citing.)

SEAL is the closest precedent to our loop: an outer ReST-EM loop that keeps the self-edits which made held-out QA correct and SFTs the policy on them, with an inner LoRA (r=32–64) update on the self-edit, on Qwen2.5-7B — our base. Same as ours: self-generated fine-tuning data, filtered by a downstream check, written into a LoRA, restart-from-base outer loop (our sleep refits a fresh LoRA each cycle; organism_v6/train_adapter.py).

Where ours differs, and what has to be shown for the difference to count:
1. Source of the data. SEAL's self-edit restructures a passage it was given. Our records are first-person records of the agent's own acting — environment outcome, parent, person — admitted through a provenance gate (a record with no traceable source is treated as confabulation). Status 2026-09-13: own records are carried in weights (SEQ-149/153), the gate exists; no stored record has yet been shown to drive a later decision (SEQ-154).
2. Lifetime, not per-task. SEAL's inner update is per passage/task. Ours is one adapter rewritten over a lifetime at sleep, with in-batch rehearsal so earlier content survives (level 0: an unrehearsed habit is erased within 16 competing updates, SEQ-104; replay keeps 48/48 facts across 1,040 updates, SEQ-118; replay-in-write removes skill loss, SEQ-159).
3. The payload includes the call step. We aim to train the behaviour of cueing and using the memory inside reasoning (the junction skill: reasoning → emit the canonical recall frame in the trained direction → continue), not only the knowledge. Status: untested — composition from LoRA-stored facts has never been measured; the two-hop-from-weights cell is designed and unrun.
4. A parent shapes what is written (level 2), and the test is the 2×2 (parented × sleep running) on an unseen gym.

Claim discipline: until 3 and 4 are demonstrated, a reviewer can fairly collapse our loop to "SEAL in a gym". Do not present rejection-sampling self-SFT into a LoRA as new; cite SEAL for it, and claim only the self-targeted call step, the provenance-gated experiential records, and the lifetime refit under a parent — each with its own result.
