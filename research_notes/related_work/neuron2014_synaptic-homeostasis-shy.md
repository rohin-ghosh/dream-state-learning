# Sleep and the price of plasticity: from synaptic and cellular homeostasis to memory consolidation and integration
Source: Neuron 81(1):12-34, 2014. DOI: 10.1016/j.neuron.2013.12.025 (not on arXiv)  
Authors: Giulio Tononi, Chiara Cirelli

## Abstract
Sleep is universal, tightly regulated, and its loss impairs cognition. But why does the brain need to disconnect from the environment for hours every day? The synaptic homeostasis hypothesis (SHY) proposes that sleep is the price the brain pays for plasticity. During a waking episode, learning statistical regularities about the current environment requires strengthening connections throughout the brain. This increases cellular needs for energy and supplies, decreases signal-to-noise ratios, and saturates learning. During sleep, spontaneous activity renormalizes net synaptic strength and restores cellular homeostasis. Activity-dependent down-selection of synapses can also explain the benefits of sleep on memory acquisition, consolidation, and integration. This happens through the offline, comprehensive sampling of statistical regularities incorporated in neuronal circuits over a lifetime. This Perspective considers the rationale and evidence for SHY and points to open issues related to sleep and plasticity.

## Our differentiation
Claims: SHY — wake produces net synaptic potentiation that saturates capacity and degrades signal-to-noise; sleep's core function is global, proportional *downscaling* ("down-selection") of synapses. Strong/consistently-reused traces survive renormalization; weak, noisy, or redundant ones fall below threshold and are effectively forgotten. Forgetting is thus the default outcome of sleep, and it is what restores capacity and improves SNR ("smart forgetting" / "sleep is the price of plasticity").
Mechanism: activity-dependent down-selection driven by offline spontaneous activity that comprehensively samples lifetime regularities — competition, not per-item deletion decisions. Complementary (not strictly opposed) to active-consolidation replay accounts (Diekelmann & Born).
Sleeper relevance: this is the GC anchor — implement garbage collection as multiplicative score decay applied globally each sleep cycle, with reinforcement (retrieval hits, dedup merges, cue tags) as the only thing that keeps entries above threshold; survival-of-the-consistent beats explicit deletion lists, and downscaling doubles as the dedup tie-breaker.
