# Continual Learning with Deep Generative Replay
arXiv: 1705.08690  
Authors: Hanul Shin, Jung Kwon Lee, Jaehong Kim, Jiwon Kim

## Abstract
Attempts to train a comprehensive artificial intelligence capable of solving multiple tasks have been impeded by a chronic problem called catastrophic forgetting. Although simply replaying all previous data alleviates the problem, it requires large memory and even worse, often infeasible in real world applications where the access to past data is limited. Inspired by the generative nature of hippocampus as a short-term memory system in primate brain, we propose the Deep Generative Replay, a novel framework with a cooperative dual model architecture consisting of a deep generative model ("generator") and a task solving model ("solver"). With only these two models, training data for previous tasks can easily be sampled and interleaved with those for a new task. We test our methods in several sequential learning settings involving image classification tasks.

## Our differentiation
Claims (NeurIPS 2017): catastrophic forgetting can be avoided without storing past data — a generative model can stand in for the episodic buffer ("pseudo-rehearsal", after Robins 1995), explicitly framed on the generative role of the hippocampus.
Mechanism: a "scholar" = generator (GAN) + solver; when a new task arrives, the old generator samples fake past data, the old solver labels it, and the new scholar trains on real-new interleaved with generated-old. Consolidation is entirely into weights via interleaved SGD.
Limits: generator quality bounds retention; drift compounds across generations of scholars — an argument for keeping a verbatim episodic log as ground truth (which we do and DGR does not).
Sleeper relevance: DGR is "thinker generates / sleeper trains" where the sleeper's compile step is interleaved replay training — the ML instantiation of CLS interleaving; our sleeper differs in compiling into structured memory (dedup, abstraction, indexes) rather than only into solver weights, but any weight-consolidation stage we add should interleave generated/replayed old data exactly as here.
