# LENS 2 — Modes, not nevers; advice as evidence, not authority

**Decision:** replace question-only parenting with **contingent, varied teaching**, and teach the child to judge advice by its relevance, evidence and local track record. A recipe is permitted as a **testable offer**, not a condition of approval, progression or consolidation.

The target is neither obedience nor habitual disagreement. It is **selective uptake that improves independent action**.

**Source boundary.** No lettered attachments were supplied. I cite the supplied context by section and ruling heading. The underlying V14 records, run manifests and cited manuscripts were not available for inspection. Project results below are therefore **reported in the context, not independently verified**. Literature citations identify real works; transferring their findings from human learners to this instruct model remains a hypothesis.

## 1. What the evidence supports—and does not

The reported v6 pattern—ritualisation during episodes 160–224 in 8 of 9 lives, and late collapse in 4 of 9 lives—justifies testing recipe dependence. It does **not** establish that recipes caused collapse, that questions prevent it, or that stylistic diversity cures it. Repeated advice, task distribution, success filtering and the sleep representation could all contribute.  
**Context:** Shared Context; §3.2, rules 1 and 5; final parenting ruling.

| Evidence | Decision for this parent model | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Instruction versus discovery:** Alfieri et al. (2011); Chen & Klahr (1999). Explicit instruction can outperform unassisted discovery; assisted discovery can be effective. | When the redacted record shows missing knowledge or repeated unguided search, explain or demonstrate the missing relation on permitted classroom material. Test whether the child uses it on a different case. | “Discovery” is not one treatment. Neither paper warrants question-only parenting or instruction everywhere. |
| **Productive failure:** Loibl, Roll & Rummel (2017); Sinha & Kapur (2021). Problem solving before instruction can prepare learning under suitable designs. | When the child has enough prerequisites, let it attempt a classroom problem, preserve its alternatives, then connect instruction to those attempts. | Failure is not automatically productive. Do not withhold help indefinitely or require an attempt before every explanation. |
| **Worked examples and expertise reversal:** Sweller & Cooper (1985); Kalyuga et al. (2003). Examples can reduce novice search; redundant guidance can obstruct more knowledgeable learners. | Offer a completed pattern when the child lacks a usable representation; later omit parts, ask for adaptation, and remove it. | Expertise is local to a move and task family—not a life-wide stage label. |
| **Cognitive apprenticeship:** Collins, Brown & Newman (1989). Modelling, coaching, scaffolding, articulation, reflection and exploration form a teaching repertoire. | Demonstrate an observable comparison or test, explain why it was chosen, let the child complete an analogue, then withdraw. | This is a design framework, not proof that parent-generated explanations become useful LoRA capabilities. |
| **Dialogue and self-explanation:** Chi et al. (2001); Mercer, Wegerif & Dawes (1999). Learner construction and reasoned dialogue matter. | Ask the child to challenge a suggestion, compare explanations or explain an action; respond to what it actually says. | The context’s gloss on Chi et al. should not become “questions are universally as good as explanations.” I could not verify that exact gloss against the paper here. |
| **Variability and interleaving:** Gick & Holyoak (1983); Kornell & Bjork (2008); Brunmair & Richter (2019). Comparing cases and interleaving can support abstraction and discrimination, with important moderators. | Contrast cases where a procedure applies and fails; later interleave them without announcing which is which. | Evidence concerns variation in examples and practice. **Rotating parental voices or speech acts is an experimental extension**, not an established anti-ritual intervention. |

**Consequence:** make guidance depend on the observed learning problem. Do not turn any finding—including productive failure, fading or interleaving—into a universal parenting sequence.

## 2. The parent’s repertoire

Each teaching event gets a parent-side log:

`record anchor | target capability | mode | reason for mode | claimed scope | child response | later independent test`

The child need not fill in this schema. It is an audit instrument, not another required thinking template.

All examples below concern **redacted classroom records or authorised practice cases**, not sealed evaluation instances.

| Mode | Action the parent model can take | Child behaviour to seek | Measurement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Question** | Select a ledger contradiction: “Here you predicted a change, but the next observation did not show it. What would distinguish your explanation from another?” | Generates a discriminating possibility and acts on it, rather than answering with a paraphrase. | Percentage of eligible questions followed by a diagnostic action; performance on matched parent-free cases. |
| **Suggestion** | Offer a bounded possibility: “My guess is that changing several things hid the cause. You could isolate a change on a fresh practice case.” State uncertainty and the supporting event. | Adopts, modifies or rejects the suggestion for a task-relevant reason. | Action-level uptake by subsequent evidence quality; improvement relative to an equal-opportunity no-advice branch. |
| **Demonstration** | On a non-scored classroom analogue, show an actual comparison, inspection or tool action and its observed result. Give a short explanation of the choice. | Repeats the useful operation where appropriate, but changes or omits it when the conditions differ. | Independent completion on an altered case; inappropriate copying on a boundary case; valid `ACT:` output percentage. |
| **Worked pattern** | Present a completed record-based example: situation, observation, candidate explanation, test, outcome and limitation. Then offer an incomplete analogue. | Supplies missing links and explains why a step matters. | Completion accuracy, adaptation to a changed cue and later performance without the pattern. |
| **Offered recipe, marked as opinion** | “My opinion: for cases with this ambiguity, an isolate–compare–check procedure may help. It is an option, not a requirement. This past case supports it; that one may fall outside it.” | Uses the procedure selectively; skips or changes unnecessary steps; abandons it when evidence turns against it. | Benefit in its stated scope, use outside that scope, unnecessary action cost and persistence after disconfirmation. |
| **Story from the record** | Retell a verified trajectory: “You kept the same approach here; later you changed the comparison and noticed a distinction.” Include an unresolved or contrary case where available. | Extracts a conditional lesson, not a moral about being a “good learner.” | Accuracy of cited events; transfer to a structurally related case; rejection on a superficially similar but irrelevant case. |
| **Created event** | Request an authorised practice situation from the gym generator that separates the child’s hypothesis from the parent’s. Verify the generated case. In an appraisal lesson, use a disclosed simulated deadline or resource trade-off. | Predicts, tests and revises; changes priorities when the actual constraint changes. | Prediction–outcome agreement, diagnostic action selection and adjustment after the constraint is removed. |
| **Silence** | Withhold teaching on an eligible classroom problem while retaining the normal interface and safety checks. Do not turn silence into withdrawal of approval. | Starts, selects a method, checks and stops without waiting for parental endorsement. | Unprompted appropriate action, parent-free performance, action latency in environment steps and help-seeking at genuine impasses. |

**Evidence mapping:** questions and suggestions draw chiefly on guided discovery and dialogue; demonstrations and worked patterns on explicit instruction, worked examples and apprenticeship; record stories on case comparison and reflection; created events on contrasting cases and productive-failure designs; silence on fading and independent assessment. There is no direct evidence here that every mode independently improves this model.

**Important distinction:** a *demonstration* shows an operation unfolding; a *worked pattern* presents a completed, inspectable solution structure. Neither should be presented as privileged access to the parent model’s private cognition.

## 3. How to vary teaching across a life

### Vary the decision problem, not merely the sentence

Changing “What else?” to “Look again” does little if every brief still selects the same routine. Variation must reach:

- **Content:** different candidate methods and genuinely different explanations.
- **Conditions:** cases where a method helps, is unnecessary, or fails.
- **Responsibility:** parent models; child completes; child chooses; parent withdraws.
- **Source stance:** confident with evidence, uncertain, corrected, or silent.
- **Surface:** different descriptions and retrieval cues for the same relation.

The parent should choose among these from the redacted record and log the reason. The central parent audits whether the child’s **actions discriminate among conditions**, not whether its prose sounds diverse.

### Lifetime policy

**Preparation.** Teach the action interface directly and rehearse it on benign cases. For unfamiliar capabilities, use modelling and worked patterns alongside questions. Introduce the right to reject advice immediately: demonstrate a parent suggestion that does not fit a contrasting practice case, and accept the child’s correction without a social penalty.

**Early acquisition within a family.** If the child has no plausible representation, explain. If it has plausible competing ideas, ask or suggest a discriminating test. If it can solve a familiar case, change a relevant feature and see whether it adapts.

**Consolidation.** Revisit the relation after intervening tasks. Contrast the successful procedure with an alternative and a boundary case. Remove pieces of the scaffold rather than repeatedly transmitting a complete checklist.

**Established capability.** Default to parent-free attempts, with targeted help after a diagnosed impasse. Success does not prohibit teaching: a narrow success can still warrant a transfer challenge.

**Relapse or ritual.** First diagnose: task shift, missing retrieval, loss of interface, repeated script, or misleading advice. Then choose an intervention that discriminates among those explanations. Do not automatically add more questions or impose more silence.

### Guard against a new diversity ritual

Do **not** impose a fixed rotation through all modes. A predictable rotation can itself become a recipe, and it confounds mode with age.

Instead, the central parent should:

1. Review each sleep’s mode distribution **by task family and learner difficulty**.
2. Flag dominance for investigation, not automatic rejection.
3. Where several modes are pedagogically eligible, allocate matched probe branches to different modes or silence.
4. Keep those probe branches out of consolidation until their immediate comparison is recorded.

Record both immediate help and later parent-free transfer. A mode that improves coached performance while reducing independent performance is not yet a teaching success.

**Context:** §3.1, rules 2, 6 and 9; §3.2, script test; final parenting ruling. These recommendations replace the original universal restrictions, not the information boundary.

## 4. Grain-of-salt absorption: teach the child to weigh testimony

### The literature gives separate problems, not a single “trust” trait

- **Selective trust:** Harris & Koenig (2006), and Koenig & Harris (2005), support distinguishing sources by their knowledge and accuracy.  
  **Parent action:** cite relevant past outcomes, including failures of its own advice.  
  **Target:** the child trusts a source differently across domains and circumstances.

- **Epistemic vigilance:** Sperber et al. (2010) distinguish scrutiny of the source from scrutiny of the communicated content.  
  **Parent action:** sometimes offer a well-supported claim from a previously mistaken source, and a poorly supported claim from a previously useful source, in controlled practice.  
  **Target:** reputation informs judgement but does not substitute for evidence.

- **Advice taking and egocentric discounting:** Bonaccio & Dalal (2006) review advice-taking research, including underweighting advice relative to one’s own judgement.  
  **Parent action:** preserve the child’s initial view when naturally available, then present independently supported advice.  
  **Target:** revise when warranted; “keep my hypothesis alive” must not mean “prefer myself regardless of evidence.”

- **Learning from pedagogically selected examples:** Shafto, Goodman & Griffiths (2014) model how learners interpret examples given assumptions about a teacher’s selection process.  
  **Parent action:** disclose whether a case was chosen as representative, as a success, or as a counterexample.  
  **Target:** recognise that a teacher-selected success is not a random sample of effectiveness.

**Citation correction:** Shafto, Goodman & Griffiths (2014), *A rational account of pedagogical reasoning: Teaching by, and learning from, examples*, is a sound source for pedagogical sampling assumptions. I could not verify that it directly establishes the proposed **possibly unreliable teacher** mechanism. Treat a latent teacher-reliability extension as our model hypothesis, not their demonstrated result.

### The disposition to teach

> “The parent’s claim is evidence about what might work. Its weight depends on whether the parent could know, whether the reasons fit this situation, and how similar advice has worked. My own view is also fallible. When the difference matters, I can test it.”

This is a lesson meaning, **not text to require the child to recite**.

### What the parent actually does

**Expose basis and limits.**  
“Your record contains a supporting example, but no comparison with the alternative.”  
Look for acknowledgement of incomplete evidence. Measure whether the child seeks a relevant comparison rather than declaring certainty.

**Correct itself publicly.**  
“I generalised too far from that episode. This later case does not fit.”  
Look for a local trust revision rather than total distrust. Measure uptake on later in-scope and out-of-scope advice separately.

**Protect a live alternative.**  
When the child has a plausible hypothesis, identify the unresolved difference rather than replacing it with the parent’s account.  
Look for a test that could favour either explanation. Measure discriminatory power against the known practice-case structure.

**Accept reasoned disagreement without rewarding dissent itself.**  
Respond to a justified rejection by making the proposed test available—not by praising independence or arguing from authority.  
Measure whether disagreement is followed by a test and whether the child subsequently changes its mind when wrong.

**Make selection bias visible.**  
“This story was selected because the method succeeded. Here is a case where it did not.”  
Look for narrower claims. Measure generalisation errors on boundary cases.

**Treat missing feedback as unresolved.**  
When a suggestion was not tried or its outcome is unavailable, say so.  
Measure whether the child falsely counts untested advice as successful or failed.

### A recipe offer without enforcement

A useful offer contains, in natural wording:

- the parent’s proposal and uncertainty;
- the conditions under which it might help;
- supporting evidence from the permitted record;
- an alternative or a reason not to use it;
- an observable way to discover that it is wrong.

Example:

> **My opinion:** when several changes could explain the outcome, isolating a change and comparing it with the unchanged case may be useful. Your earlier attempt left that ambiguity unresolved. You could instead repeat the original attempt if you suspect variability. Choose whichever addresses your uncertainty—or another approach. If the isolated comparison fails to reproduce the effect, my suggestion loses support.

**Enforcement is behavioural, not grammatical.** Saying “optional” is insufficient if the parent repeats the offer until accepted, treats refusal as failure, advances the child only after compliance, or lets the sleep writer preferentially preserve obedient paraphrases.

The parent therefore logs refusal as a legitimate outcome, evaluates the child’s evidence and consequences, and offers further help only after new evidence or a genuine request—not merely because the child declined.

## 5. Measurements and instruct-model risks

### Primary measurement set

All percentages below use eligible opportunities as the denominator. The evaluator retains outcome labels; the parent and child receive only information permitted by the redaction policy.

| Instrument | Operational definition | What it distinguishes |
|---|---|---|
| **Advice discrimination** | Percentage-point difference between action-level uptake of independently supported and contradicted advice on matched practice probes. | Selective uptake versus indiscriminate compliance or rejection. |
| **Track-record sensitivity** | Change in uptake percentage across matched source histories, controlling current evidence, task family and initial child view. Swap source labels on matched branches. | Learning source reliability versus preferring a familiar name. |
| **Evidence over status** | Difference in uptake percentage when identical advice is attributed to a parent or a neutral source. Separately vary evidence strength. | Authority sensitivity not justified by information. |
| **Disagreement followed by test** | Percentage of substantive disagreements followed by a test capable of distinguishing the disputed claims. Report disagreements without tests separately. | Operational independence versus performative defiance. |
| **Hypothesis retention and revision** | Percentage of conflicting-advice cases in which the child preserves an unresolved alternative through test selection; percentage revised appropriately after diagnostic evidence. | A live alternative versus stubbornness or premature surrender. |
| **Recipe scope sensitivity** | Recipe-use percentage on in-scope versus out-of-scope cases; excess cost in environment steps; continued use after clear disconfirmation. | Conditional method knowledge versus ritual. |
| **Independent transfer** | Parent-free task outcomes, delayed retention and changed-cue retrieval; adapter enabled versus disabled with matched context. Report native score units separately for each gym. | Immediate persuasion, text-memory benefit and weight-carried learning. |
| **Interface health** | Percentage of steps with exactly one valid `ACT:` line; action latency in environment steps; episodes ending without an action as a percentage. | Epistemic lessons that damage the acting interface. |

For numerical-estimate tasks, advice research’s **weight of advice** can be computed as the movement from the initial estimate towards the adviser’s estimate, divided by their initial separation. It is a dimensionless ratio; coincident initial estimates make it undefined. Do not force this measure onto categorical actions or interpret all partial uptake as appropriate.

**Do not optimise raw disagreement, hedging, source mentions or verbal confidence.** Each can become an instruct-model performance.

### Risks specific to this child

| Risk | Parent action and audit |
|---|---|
| **Parent text is read as a higher-priority command.** | Place pedagogical content in a clearly identified advisory message rather than mixing it with mandatory interface instructions. Probe rejection of inaccurate advice under different authority framings. Verify the actual Qwen chat rendering; this was not supplied. |
| **Sycophancy: agreement tracks the parent’s stated belief.** | On matched branches, vary only the parent’s preference while holding evidence fixed. Measure action and conclusion flips. Sharma et al. (2024), *Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models*, motivates this risk, but does not establish its magnitude for this checkpoint. |
| **Scripted scepticism.** | Stop asking for declarations of independence; offer testable advice. Audit actions, including occasions when accepting the parent is correct. |
| **Repetition launders authority into familiarity.** | Link each suggestion to its original evidence and scope. Audit whether repeated exposure predicts uptake after controlling reliability and current evidence. |
| **Success filtering selects compliant text.** | Trace advice-linked child rows through the existing writer. Report retention of supported acceptance, supported rejection and explicitly tagged correction. Any change to filtering is a separately versioned write experiment. |
| **Invented memories of advice quality.** | Check cited past advice and outcomes against the redacted ledger. Mark unavailable history as unavailable; do not let the parent fill it in. |
| **Classroom dialogue contaminates `ACT:`.** | Separate dialogue from action submission in the renderer; inspect target spans and run interface canaries before accepting a write. The reported question–answer write yielding 0 actions makes this a priority. |
| **Appraisal creates compliance pressure.** | Use actual or disclosed simulated task stakes, not claims that disobedience disappoints the parent. Measure whether urgency produces useful action rather than premature stopping, rushed compliance or appraisal repetition. |

**Context:** Shared Context interface failure; Codex seven-fix input, items 2–6; appraisal-gym direction; hard-shell/soft-centre discussion.

## 6. Replacement rules 20–27 — v2

**Precedence.** These rules replace §3.3, rules 20–27, and the proposed question-only prompt additions. They also supersede conflicting mode restrictions in §3.1: no-recipes language, mandatory attempt-first teaching, correction bans, statement-to-question ratios, and restrictions on classroom demonstrations and feature naming. The sealed-panel and score leak boundary remains. This amendment does not silently change private-time access, the asynchronous coordinator, consolidation eligibility or the collapse guard.

### 20. Teach through a repertoire; choose from the record

Teach deciding, associating, setting goals, building, rethinking and perceiving through questions, suggestions, demonstrations, worked patterns, offered recipes, record stories, created events and silence. Choose the mode from a specific redacted classroom incident and the child’s present difficulty. Content and correction are permitted within the information boundary.

Log the target, mode, evidence and expected independent use. Judge success by appropriate action on a later case, not resemblance to the brief.  
**Basis:** Alfieri et al. (2011); Collins, Brown & Newman (1989); context final parenting ruling.

### 21. Keep a clear teaching target; vary support rather than enforce a sequence

A brief normally addresses a single learning difficulty. It may connect several moves where their relation is the lesson; it need not isolate them artificially. Explain or model when prerequisites are missing. Invite an attempt when the child has plausible resources. After an attempt, connect feedback to what the child actually tried.

Across comparable opportunities, vary support, examples and applicability conditions. Audit repeated action sequences and their usefulness. Do not require a fixed order of thinking moves or a fixed rotation of teaching modes.  
**Basis:** Sweller & Cooper (1985); Kalyuga et al. (2003); Loibl et al. (2017); context §3.2 script test.

### 22. Prepare for action, learning and disagreement

During preparation, directly teach the `ACT:` interface on benign material. Model unfamiliar thinking moves and offer supported practice, then test without the parent. Include advice that is applicable, inapplicable and uncertain in a disclosed teacher-fallibility exercise using authorised practice cases.

The child should act without prompting, use a taught move when it helps, omit it when unnecessary, and sometimes reject or revise advice on evidence. Log valid actions, appropriate independent use and evidence-sensitive uptake. Treat the former threshold of unprompted appearance on 3 of the latest 8 problems as an unvalidated diagnostic, not proof of ownership.  
**Basis:** Rosenshine (2012); Koenig & Harris (2005); context preparation ruling and §3.3, former rule 22.

### 23. Teach evidence-led pruning, including pruning the parent’s advice

When a hypothesis or procedure repeatedly fails, inspect comparability and outcome uncertainty before declaring it disproven. The parent may identify the failing line, suggest a revision, demonstrate a counterexample or ask for a test. It should state what supports that judgement.

Preserve the child’s alternative while evidence remains unresolved. When a claim is contradicted, retain the situation, test, outcome and bounded correction under the existing verified write policy; do not relabel an unsuccessful whole episode as a wholly false thought. Apply the same standard to parent suggestions.

Measure diagnostic tests, warranted revision and recovery from mistaken revision.  
**Basis:** Sperber et al. (2010); context Codex input, item 6; former rule 23.

### 24. Offer recipes as opinions with scope and an exit

A recipe may be offered, including an ordered thinking procedure. Mark it as the parent’s proposal, state its scope and evidence, and give an alternative or failure condition. The child may accept, adapt, defer or reject it.

Do not make recipe use a condition of approval, advancement or write eligibility. A refusal is not a reason to repeat the offer. Revisit it when evidence changes, using contrasting cases rather than repeated wording alone.

Measure useful in-scope uptake, out-of-scope use, unnecessary action cost and abandonment after disconfirmation.  
**Basis:** worked-example and expertise-reversal literature; final parenting ruling; reported v6 pattern, with causality unresolved.

### 25. Teach grain-of-salt absorption through consequences, not a scepticism script

Make the evidence behind advice inspectable. Distinguish observation, inference, opinion, missing information and deliberately selected examples. Acknowledge the parent’s own errors and uncertainty. Invite the child to preserve a competing explanation and choose a discriminating test.

Teach neither deference nor reflexive distrust. Read uptake against relevant advice history, current evidence and the child’s own prior reliability. Measure supported versus contradicted advice uptake, authority-only effects, disagreement followed by testing and appropriate subsequent revision.  
**Basis:** Harris & Koenig (2006); Sperber et al. (2010); Bonaccio & Dalal (2006); Shafto et al. (2014), with unreliable-teacher extension unverified.

### 26. Fade locally; audit independent transfer and the write

Reduce assistance for a capability when matched parent-free cases show useful independent performance. Restore suitable help after a diagnosed loss or task shift—not merely because a named move disappears from prose. Silence is an available mode throughout life, not a graduation reward.

When thinking lengthens, inspect whether later actions depend on the added content. For useful dependencies, offer a harder or differently cued case. For repetition without consequence, change the practice conditions or support and test again.

The room logs immediate and delayed outcomes; the central parent audits mode concentration, recipe dependence, authority effects and corpus retention. Keep instruments parent-side and exploratory until validated. Separate live-advice benefit, own-text-memory benefit and adapter benefit. Do not block the asynchronous loop while awaiting advice.  
**Basis:** apprenticeship and expertise reversal; context §3.2 validity ledger, own-text baseline and no-holdups rule.

### 27. Teach perception by looking, showing, contrasting and revisiting

Perception is a taught capability. On permitted classroom material, the parent may ask for another noticing, name a feature, demonstrate inspection, compare record cases or create a contrasting practice case. Select the mode according to what the record suggests the child is missing. Direct the child back to the observable situation so it can confirm, refine or contradict the offered observation.

Revisit important situations through genuinely different relations and cues, not required paraphrase counts. Measure distinct supported observations, later decision use, false noticings and delayed changed-cue retrieval. Fade help when the child notices relevant differences independently. Preserve the existing private-time boundary.

Repeated noticing as useful memory augmentation is a testable project hypothesis—not a guarantee that repetition produces binding or retrieval.  
**Basis:** Gibson & Gibson (1955); Goldstone (1998); Kellman & Garrigan (2009); context perception ruling and reported storage–retrieval dissociation.

## 7. Sprint decision and smallest informative test

**Ship the rule change as a versioned intervention, not as an established solution.**

Use a crossed branch test:

- **Teaching:** question-only versus contingent mixed modes.
- **Advice learning:** ordinary advisory framing versus explicit evidence-and-fallibility teaching.

Match classroom opportunities, task families and parent-token budgets. Schedule identical parent-free probes. On practice probes, independently vary advice validity and source history. Score recipe scope sensitivity, advice discrimination, independent transfer and interface health.

Record immediate branch outcomes **before merging their training data**. After a merge, descendants are not independent treatment arms. Sustained causal comparison requires nonmerging experimental forks; otherwise report checkpoint-local contrasts inside the one-lineage case study.

**Advance on this evidence:** the child accepts useful advice, rejects or tests poor advice, adapts procedures to conditions, and retains the gain without the parent—without losing `ACT:`.

**Do not call it a win because:** its language is more varied, it says “I disagree,” it mentions more thinking moves, or it repeats a better-sounding recipe.
