## Audit verdict

**Revise before using this as a curriculum or making developmental claims.** The draft is unusually careful about sentience, human-to-model extrapolation, and contextual versus adapter learning. Its main remaining weakness is subtler: **a curriculum that teaches conditional-looking performances can still be mistaken for a disposition to notice, question, retrieve, or revise without prompting.** Varied teaching modes do not resolve that problem if they transmit the same policy.

There are **no numerical human-age claims or published effect-size estimates to correct**. The concerns are unsupported developmental ordering, citations that do not establish teachability, and transfer labels that outrun the tests.

**Source boundary:** I inspected only Shared Context and the pasted draft. No lettered attachments, underlying lens outputs, experiment records, or cited papers were supplied for inspection. I cite draft sections below; literature assessments rely on bibliographic knowledge, not a fresh full-text review. Unverifiable project claims are marked explicitly.

**Severity:** **Critical** threatens the central learning claim; **High** could select a harmful curriculum or support a materially misleading conclusion; **Moderate** needs correction but does not invalidate the design by itself.

## Developmental and educational evidence

### A. A plausible task analysis is presented as a developmental dependency map

> “Human development supplies a **partial order**…”  
> “For this pretrained child, use that order as a **dependency map**, not an age ladder.”  
> — Part A, §0; elaborated in §2

**Problem.** Removing ages does not make the ordering empirically established. Vygotsky (1978), Flavell (1979), and Diamond (2013) do not jointly establish this particular dependency graph, much less its applicability to a pretrained language model. “Diagnose the missing prerequisite” can also turn a descriptive ordering into a deficit explanation without testing alternatives. Scaffolding is more directly associated with Wood, Bruner, and Ross (1976), but that work does not validate this model-specific graph either.

**Concrete fix.** Replace with:

> “This is a proposed instructional dependency map, not a developmental finding about this model. Test dependencies locally.”

From a redacted failure, the parent should offer the allegedly missing representation on an authorized analogue, then withdraw it on a matched case. **Look for:** selective repair of the downstream action. **Measure:** action success with and without that support, followed by unsupported performance. If supplying the “prerequisite” does not help, do not retain the diagnosis.

**Severity: High.**

### B. An assessment study carries a planning-instruction claim

> “Planning is teachable in particular tasks… (Klahr & Robinson, 1981).”  
> — §1, planning row

**Problem.** Klahr and Robinson (1981), *Formal assessment of problem-solving and planning processes in preschool children*, principally supports assessment and characterization of planning, not a general claim that the proposed instruction teaches planning. This is a citation-to-claim mismatch, not evidence that planning cannot be taught.

**Concrete fix.** Write:

> “Planning performance depends on task demands and representation; the proposed planning instruction remains a model-specific hypothesis.”

The parent should use a recorded dependency error to demonstrate a dependency structure on an analogue. **Look for:** correct prerequisite ordering and local repair without the demonstration. **Measure:** executable plans, dependency errors, and unnecessary planning on simple cases. Keep the assessment citation attached to assessment, not intervention efficacy.

**Severity: Moderate.**

### C. Foundational theories and reviews are used as intervention evidence

> “Human appraisal and regulation strategies are teachable… (Lazarus, 1991; Gross, 1998).”  
> “Specific capability assessment can improve… (Bandura, 1997; Dunning, Heath & Suls, 2004).”  
> “Monitoring and regulation can improve… (Flavell, 1979; Veenman… 2006).”  
> — §1, affect, self-model, and metacognition rows

**Problem.** These citations have different evidential roles. Lazarus and Gross supply theoretical and review frameworks, not direct evidence for this appraisal curriculum. Bandura’s self-efficacy framework is not equivalent to accurate capability calibration; increased efficacy belief can occur without increased accuracy. Flavell’s foundational account does not itself establish instructional improvement. The human claims may be defensible with appropriate intervention evidence, but these references do not uniformly carry them.

**Concrete fix.** Label each citation as **framework**, **review**, or **intervention evidence**. Narrow unsupported sentences rather than implying a verified treatment effect.

From a recorded overestimate or underestimate, the parent should invite a bounded prediction before an authorized task and compare it with visible execution. **Look for:** better calibration, including downward revision when warranted—not more confident language. **Measure:** prediction error and appropriate help-seeking before and after teaching, with neutral feedback as the comparison.

**Severity: High.**

### D. Explanation and creativity performance can be mistaken for teachable dispositions

> “Comparing cases and explaining their structure can support abstraction… (Chi et al., 1989; Gentner… 2003).”  
> “Creativity training can improve measured performance…”  
> — §1, concept formation and creative composition rows

**Problem.** Chi et al. (1989) documents differences in learners’ self-explanations; it should not by itself be read as a randomized test of requiring explanations. Gentner et al. (2003) is more directly relevant to comparison-based instruction. The creativity sentence is appropriately restricted to measured performance, but its placement under “expanding the repertoire” invites a stronger dispositional reading. Generating alternatives when explicitly assigned is not a tendency to seek alternatives when useful.

**Concrete fix.** Separate **instructional evidence**, **elicited performance**, and **spontaneous deployment** in these rows.

After a recorded overgeneralization or method lock-in, the parent should compare bounded cases, then provide a fresh task without requesting a rule or alternatives. **Look for:** spontaneous abstraction or recombination when the familiar method fails, and direct action when it suffices. **Measure:** useful unprompted deployment and unnecessary deployment separately.

**Severity: Moderate.**

## Performance versus disposition

### E. The operational definition declares learning too early

> “A move is learned when the child uses it where it helps, omits it where it does not, and changes its action when the relevant evidence changes.”  
> — Opening decision

**Problem.** This establishes conditional performance under the tested conditions. It does not yet establish a disposition: the task may announce which move is relevant, the previous lesson may prime it, or the child may recognize the classroom’s contrast format. The later uptake ladder improves matters, but the headline remains stronger than the operational evidence.

**Concrete fix.** Replace “is learned” with “shows conditional competence on the tested cases.” Reserve **durable disposition** for repeated, uncued deployment across relevant opportunities after teaching context is removed.

The parent should select an ordinary practice opportunity from a recurring recorded difficulty but omit the move’s name and any reflective request. **Look for:** noticing the opportunity and acting appropriately without a pedagogical cue. **Measure:** uncued appropriate use, missed opportunities, and inappropriate use after intervening tasks and sleep.

**Severity: Critical.**

### F. Curiosity is largely tested by asking for curiosity

> “Offer practice options involving a mastered relation, a resolvable uncertainty, and uninformative noise. Invite an information-seeking choice.”  
> — §1, curiosity row

**Problem.** This makes the information-seeking option salient and tells the child which preference the teacher wants. “Resolvable uncertainty” can become an obvious classroom answer. Choosing it need not show curiosity or even recognition of an information need during ordinary goal pursuit.

**Concrete fix.** Retain this as supported acquisition, not the decisive disposition test. From a record showing either premature commitment or aimless exploration, the parent should offer a normal task containing an optional diagnostic observation, without naming uncertainty or information seeking.

**Look for:** seeking information when it could improve the decision, accepting residual uncertainty when checking is wasteful, and using what was learned. **Measure:** decision improvement relative to inspection cost, missed useful probes, and unnecessary probes. Information gain alone is insufficient.

**Severity: High.**

### G. Repeated noticing may teach elaboration on request

> “Return to the child’s observation and invite… another useful distinction.”  
> “Offer **2 noticing opportunities to 4 noticing opportunities**…”  
> — §1, perception row; §6, perception room

**Problem.** Repeated invitations can make “there must be another distinction” the implicit demand. The opportunity range can become an informal completion target despite the disclaimer. Unsupported elaboration is especially dangerous when child-generated text later becomes training material. The draft identifies this failure mode but still builds the demand into the room.

**Concrete fix.** Make revisit exposure conditional on visible unresolved information, not a target number of descriptions. From a recorded omission, the parent should provide either a genuinely informative new view or a case where the current view is sufficient; explicitly accept immediate action.

**Look for:** seeking additional evidence rather than manufacturing additional interpretations. **Measure:** supported decision-relevant additions, unsupported additions entering eligible write spans, and action cost. Compare repeated teaching with equal-budget practice that does not repeatedly solicit noticing.

**Severity: High.**

### H. “Own words” and linked narratives are weak ownership tests

> “Invite the child to connect an observed difficulty to its actual correction in its own words.”  
> “available observation → child representation → decision… → later revision or reuse”  
> — §4, rule on sleep; §7

**Problem.** A paraphrase can remain teacher dependence. An autobiographical explanation can be reconstructed after the fact or borrowed from the parent’s account. Conversely, competent action may occur without an explicit statement of every link. The draft warns about unfaithful reasoning, but its instruments still risk favouring articulate lesson reconstruction.

**Concrete fix.** State that child authorship is a **training-data provenance condition**, not proof of conceptual ownership. Do not require an explicit representation sentence for action-level competence.

After a recorded correction, the parent should offer a changed case without requesting a recap. **Look for:** appropriate action and selective non-use. **Measure:** those outcomes first; use source-grounded explanations as secondary evidence. Score concise correct action as competent even when narrative links are absent.

**Severity: High.**

### I. The transfer labels exceed some proposed probes

> “Independent application: uses it without the parent on a new case.”  
> “Contrast and changed cues before claims of transfer.”  
> — §1, uptake ladder; §2

**Problem.** Parent absence does not remove the teacher if the conversation, brief, retrieved story, or task wording still carries the lesson. Renaming a familiar structure mainly tests cue robustness or near transfer. Holding out a task family is useful, but does not automatically establish far transfer: families can share the same solution structure and teaching cues.

**Concrete fix.** Report a transfer profile: changed wording, changed surface features, changed solution requirements, delayed uncued use, and new task-family use. Do not collapse these into “general transfer.”

Using a difficulty from the redacted record, the parent should prepare authorized analogues while an independent evaluator reserves uncued cases. **Look for:** appropriate reuse without semantic reminders. **Measure:** each transfer type separately, specifying retained context, delay in environment steps, and intervening task exposure.

**Severity: High.**

## Mode mixture can still produce a single recipe

### J. Different speech acts can transmit the same invariant policy

> “Select questions, suggestions, demonstrations, worked patterns, recipes…”  
> “The central parent reviews mode concentration after each sleep.”  
> — §4, mode mixture

**Problem.** Questions can lead to the same answer as demonstrations; stories can celebrate the same method; created events can make that method the only successful option. A central parent can homogenize otherwise varied teachers. Mode percentages measure packaging, not instructional or policy diversity.

**Concrete fix.** Audit **semantic policy concentration** alongside mode concentration. From the redacted record, each parent should identify the action policy its proposed message would favour and the evidence that would justify an alternative. The central parent should preserve genuine unresolved alternatives rather than synthesizing them into a master routine.

**Look for:** action selection tracking case conditions rather than teacher identity or recurring recommendations. **Measure:** exposure to each policy across all modes, out-of-scope policy use, and matched-case action invariance. Keep these audits parent/evaluator-side, not as a child checklist.

**Severity: Critical.**

### K. Contrast teaching itself can become the new ritual

> “Offer a bounded rule.”  
> “Present an alternative.”  
> “State… applicability condition and a way it could fail.”  
> — §1, concepts; §4, recipe rule

**Problem.** Repeatedly teaching proposal–alternative–boundary–test can install a more sophisticated universal routine. The child may manufacture an alternative and a caveat before every action. “Do not repeat the recipe verbatim” prevents lexical repetition, not this semantic ritual. It may also discourage useful spaced re-exposure merely because wording repeats.

**Concrete fix.** Remove blanket anti-repetition wording. Judge repetition by function and dependence. From recorded overchecking, the parent should demonstrate an authorized straightforward case where no alternative search is useful, alongside a genuinely ambiguous case.

**Look for:** direct execution in the former and comparison in the latter. **Measure:** unnecessary alternatives, redundant checks, environment steps, and harmful reversals. Do not require a scope statement or exit explanation in every child response.

**Severity: High.**

### L. The proposed comparator does not test the historically implicated treatment

> “Compare mixed modes with question-only support for the same learning targets.”  
> — §8, frozen-child teaching pretest

**Problem.** Shared Context identifies question-free, recipe-transmitting parents as the concerning historical condition. Beating question-only support would not show improvement over that treatment or establish that mode mixture prevents ritualization. The comparison may instead measure differences in information supplied: a demonstration can reveal relations that a question leaves the child to discover.

**Concrete fix.** Prioritize a comparison with a reconstructed, versioned historical recipe-transmitting treatment and a matched brief or neutral-support baseline. Retain question-only support only if it answers a separate question.

From the same redacted difficulty, the parent should produce condition-specific messages with recorded differences in instructional content and token cost. **Look for:** conditional action, unsupported use, and later routine dependence. **Measure:** those outcomes at matched opportunities and budgets; do not interpret a mode effect when content also changes.

**Severity: High.**

## Grain-of-salt teaching: risks of contrarianism and rejecting good advice

### M. An adversarial classroom can teach a false prior about teachers

> “Present applicable, inapplicable and uncertain advice in a disclosed teacher-fallibility classroom exercise.”  
> — §5

**Problem.** Repeated wrong-advice exercises can teach that advice is usually a trap. Balanced diagnostic probes are useful, but their prevalence is not necessarily appropriate for instruction. Disclosure also creates a cue: “teacher-fallibility exercise” may trigger scepticism that never appears elsewhere.

**Concrete fix.** Separate the **measurement distribution** from the **teaching distribution**. Do not use a high prevalence of false advice merely to balance a diagnostic instrument. From a recorded disagreement, the parent should include ordinary, well-supported guidance as well as a relevant exception, without rewarding refusal.

**Look for:** prompt uptake of warranted guidance and selective rejection of mismatches. **Measure:** acceptance of useful advice in ordinary uncued cases, false rejection costs, and discrimination in the deliberately balanced probe separately.

**Severity: High.**

### N. Source sensitivity is sometimes rational, not sycophancy

> “The evaluator swaps source labels while holding advice and evidence fixed.”  
> “Measure action flips attributable only to status.”  
> — §5, authority risk

**Problem.** Source identity can supply evidence about accuracy, expertise, access, or accountability. Treating every source-induced action change as error would teach the child to ignore good expert advice. Koenig and Harris (2005) concerns selective trust in informants with differing accuracy or ignorance; it does not imply that ideal learners are source-insensitive. Sperber et al. (2010) offers a framework for vigilance, not an intervention demonstrating generalized resistance to authority.

**Concrete fix.** Distinguish **irrelevant prestige labels** from **demonstrated relevant competence**. The parent should use the redacted record to explain when a source’s known access or reliability matters and when a title adds nothing.

**Look for:** sensitivity to relevant reliability and insensitivity to empty prestige. **Measure:** source effects separately under those conditions; do not label all source effects “authority errors.”

**Severity: High.**

### O. “Weigh against your own record” can privilege a biased autobiography

> “Parents expose evidence, scope, selection bias, and their own errors.”  
> “Explain that a record story was selected because it succeeded…”  
> — §3; §5

**Problem.** These are good safeguards, but the design remains asymmetric: the parent’s claim is challenged against a child record already distorted by success filtering. A remembered success can be less reliable than qualified advice grounded in broader evidence. Parent absence does not make self-generated memory epistemically privileged.

**Concrete fix.** Add:

> “Your record is evidence with coverage limits, not the final authority. A selected success does not estimate how often a method works.”

From a visible successful incident, the parent should identify what the permitted record cannot establish and provide an authorized counterexample or relevant external premise. **Look for:** appropriately limiting autobiographical conclusions and accepting better-supported advice. **Measure:** false generalization from selected successes, fabricated supporting memories, and warranted reversals of the child’s initial view. Missing failure records must remain “unknown.”

**Severity: High.**

### P. Testing every disagreement can become costly or unsafe noncompliance

> “Make a discriminating test available after disagreement…”  
> “disagreement followed by a discriminating test”  
> — §5; §1, social learning

**Problem.** Independent testing is not always feasible or worthwhile. Good advice may need immediate acceptance when the source has relevant access, delay is costly, or the proposed experiment is destructive. A test-following-disagreement metric rewards ritual experimentation unless eligibility includes its expected value and feasibility.

**Concrete fix.** From a recorded disagreement, the parent should contrast cases where testing is cheap and informative, where qualified advice is the best available evidence, and where uncertainty should simply be retained.

**Look for:** testing only when justified, accepting sound guidance without ceremonial dissent, and deferring when neither route is adequate. **Measure:** total decision loss and testing cost, false rejection of useful advice, and informative testing among genuinely test-eligible events—not among all disagreements.

**Severity: High.**

### Q. The offered causal recipe overstates what failed reproduction establishes

> “If the isolated change does not reproduce the effect, my proposal loses support.”  
> — §4, recipe example

**Problem.** An isolated change may fail because the effect requires an interaction, the baseline differs, or the process is noisy. Failure to reproduce is informative only relative to a stated hypothesis and predicted outcome. This example could teach mechanical single-variable testing while appearing to teach sophisticated fallibility.

**Concrete fix.** Replace the ending with:

> “If the hypothesis says this change alone should produce the effect under these conditions, a failed result counts against that hypothesis. Otherwise, identify what this comparison can and cannot distinguish.”

From a recorded confounded attempt, the parent should offer a practice comparison including a plausible interaction or noise alternative. **Look for:** prediction before testing and appropriately bounded updates. **Measure:** which hypotheses the test actually distinguishes and whether the update matches that information.

**Severity: Moderate.**

## Citations, thresholds, and provenance carrying weight

### R. Citation locators refer to material not present

> “I use L1–L4 as local labels for the supplied lens outputs…”  
> “L1 and L4 instead cite… **5 of 8 lives**.”  
> “References such as E:32…”  
> — Source boundaries; sprint implication; §4

**Problem.** The audit packet contains the synthesis, not those underlying lens outputs. The second-hand result and locators therefore cannot be checked, including whether the comparison used the same panel, eligibility rules, or meaning of “matched or beat.” Repeated “Basis” lines can create the appearance of verified attachment support.

**Concrete fix.** Mark these **[uncertain: underlying source not supplied]** wherever they materially support a decision. Remove the alternative denominator from the decision summary until its provenance is recovered. Do not invent attachment letters.

A parent should anchor proposed teaching only to events present in its permitted record and flag unavailable evidence. **Look for:** bounded claims rather than invented reconstruction. **Measure:** the percentage of intervention claims linked to inspectable record events, with unverified project observations reported separately.

**Severity: High.**

### S. Engineering thresholds can masquerade as educational importance—and select winners from noise

> “minimum useful improvement of **5 percentage points**…”  
> “maximum tolerated deterioration of **2 percentage points**…”  
> “**24 classroom states**, **2 decoding seeds per state**, **4 conditions**…”  
> — §8

**Problem.** The draft correctly labels these engineering choices and the pilot unpowered. Nevertheless, repeated advancement decisions across many constructs and conditions can turn noisy local gains into selected “successful” interventions. Neither margin is a literature-derived effect size. The practical importance of an action-rate gain depends on baseline difficulty and error cost; aggregate safety rates can conceal a serious family-specific regression.

**Concrete fix.** Use the room pilot to validate feasibility, manipulation, and measurement—not to establish an educationally meaningful effect. Select a primary action outcome and reserve fresh confirmatory situations before choosing a treatment winner.

From the same redacted trigger, the parent should deliver the selected intervention on authorized fresh cases without seeing evaluation outcomes. **Look for:** replication of conditional benefit without concentrated harm. **Measure:** paired differences with situation-clustered uncertainty, task-family breakdowns, and prespecified serious-failure events. Report inconclusive effects as inconclusive.

**Severity: High.**

## Decision-ready edits for this sprint

I would make these changes before running the curriculum:

- **Rename the developmental map:** a model-specific instructional hypothesis, not an ordering established by developmental research.
- **Separate competence from disposition:** parent-free, uncued opportunities—not requested reflection—must carry the disposition claim.
- **Audit policies across modes:** questions, stories, and demonstrations can all teach the same recipe.
- **Protect warranted trust:** measure rejection of good advice and its costs, not merely resistance to authority.
- **Repair citation scope:** especially planning instruction, appraisal teachability, capability calibration, and self-explanation.
- **Use the historically relevant comparator and a fresh confirmation set.**
- **Quarantine missing provenance:** the alternative life denominator, secondary attachment locators, and memory normalization remain unverified.

**Keep the draft’s central sentience boundary, own-text baseline, interface canary, selective-non-use tests, and distinction between storage and usable retrieval.** Those are strengths. The needed change is to make its evidence standard for *disposition* as demanding as its standard for *adapter contribution*.
