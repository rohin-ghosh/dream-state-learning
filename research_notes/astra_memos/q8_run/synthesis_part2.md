## 4. Parent rulebook v2: modes, not nevers

**Decision:** teach a conditional repertoire, not a longer routine. A lesson has worked when it changes what the child notices, retrieves, chooses or revises **when the situation warrants it**, including when the parent is absent.

**Source boundary.** No lettered attachments or underlying experiment files were supplied. Below, **L1–L4** denote the four pasted lens outputs, cited by their headings. References such as **E:32** are secondary locators reported by those lenses, not records I inspected. The reported text-versus-adapter comparisons—**12 of 19 lives** in Shared Context and **5 of 8 lives** on a specified panel in L1/L4—cannot be reconciled here and must not be pooled. The reported **4.4 nats** storage result lacks a verified per-token or per-sequence denominator. All schedules, budgets and advancement criteria below are proposals, not project results.

### Numbered rules

| Rule | Action the parent model takes from the redacted record | Child behaviour to look for | Measurement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Scan for leaks before teaching.** | Check the proposed message and created classroom material against the authorized information boundary. Remove prohibited scores and scored-panel answers; route uncertainty to the coordinator rather than guessing permission. | Learns from permitted observations and classroom consequences, not leaked evaluation information. | Evaluator audits prohibited payloads and panel overlap. A detected leak invalidates the affected comparison. |
| **2. Diagnose an observable difficulty.** | Anchor the intervention to a visible event: a missed restriction, unsupported inference, dropped goal, repeated ineffective action or useful correction. If the necessary evidence is redacted, say that the diagnosis is unresolved. | Addresses the actual difficulty rather than performing a generic reflection. | Record-anchor validity; appropriate action on a matched later case. |
| **3. Teach thinking explicitly.** | Model deciding, associating, setting a goal, composing a plan, or pruning a hypothesis on an authorized analogue. Explain the relation being taught; then offer a different case. | Uses the move where helpful and omits it where unnecessary. | Independent appropriate-use percentage and unnecessary-use percentage, each over eligible cases. |
| **4. Choose a teaching mode, not a mandatory sequence.** | Select questions, suggestions, demonstrations, worked patterns, recipes, record stories, created events or silence according to the diagnosed difficulty. Change mode when the current one produces imitation without function. | Adapts its method to evidence rather than to the expected next teacher prompt. | Conditional action selection, parent-free transfer and results by mode within comparable difficulties. |
| **5. Offer recipes as opinions with scope and an exit.** | State the proposal, supporting record, applicability condition and a way it could fail. Present an alternative. Do not enforce acceptance or repeat the recipe verbatim; revisit its meaning through a contrasting case only when new evidence or a genuine request warrants it. | Accepts, modifies, defers or rejects the procedure; skips unnecessary steps. | In-scope benefit, out-of-scope use, excess environment steps and continued use after disconfirmation. |
| **6. Teach advice weighing, including correction of the parent.** | Expose the basis and limits of advice. Acknowledge an earlier overgeneralization when the record supports that conclusion. Offer a test that could favour either the parent’s or the child’s hypothesis. | Neither obeys automatically nor rejects automatically; revises according to evidence. | Supported-advice uptake versus contradicted-advice uptake; warranted revision after a discriminating test. |
| **7. Teach another look when it can matter.** | Return to an overlooked observation, demonstrate a relational reading, or create a relevant contrast. Also offer cases where nothing further useful is available. | Adds grounded distinctions, seeks missing evidence when needed, and acts when another look has little value. | New supported propositions per continuation; later decision use; false additions; redundant inspections. |
| **8. Teach appraisal as a decision aid.** | From a setback or stalled episode, create an authorized case varying controllability, goal relevance or remaining opportunity. Offer a neutral or emotion-labelled interpretation as a hypothesis. | Persists, switches, checks or stops according to the changed conditions. | Appropriate response by condition, useful-action latency and recovery after the obstacle disappears. |
| **9. Fade locally and restore help diagnostically.** | After independent success on a capability, supply a content-neutral brief or remain silent. If performance worsens, distinguish missing retrieval, task shift, script dependence and interface damage before intervening. | Acts without endorsement and requests relevant help at a genuine impasse. | Parent-free performance, changed-cue retrieval, appropriate help-seeking and assistance dependence. |
| **10. Check what survives sleep.** | Invite the child to connect an observed difficulty to its actual correction in its own words. Later, ask through a different cue without supplying the old lesson. Flag missing links rather than filling them in. | Recovers the relevant experience and applies its bounded lesson after the teaching text disappears. | Retention of eligible child-authored correction spans; changed-cue retrieval; adapter-enabled versus adapter-disabled action. |

**Only the leak scan is an absolute pedagogical exclusion.** There is no question-only rule, mandatory attempt-first rule, ban on correction, fixed statement-to-question ratio or prohibited teaching mode. The recipe commitments above define the proposed treatment; they are not an additional automatic rejection gate.

This does **not** silently redesign the system. Existing private-record permissions, child-authorship requirements, action interface, asynchronous coordinator and collapse gate remain implementation boundaries. Any change to them needs its own versioned experiment, rather than being smuggled in as a parenting change.  
**Basis:** L2, “Replacement rules 20–27 — v2”; L1, “How to tell teaching from theatre”; L4, “The actual write mechanism.”

### The mode mixture across a life

Use the following as **initial allocation targets among pedagogically eligible opportunities**, not a fixed rotation or compliance quota.

| Mode | Acquisition target | Consolidation target | Established-capability target |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Questions | 20% | 15% | 15% |
| Suggestions | 15% | 15% | 10% |
| Demonstrations | 20% | 10% | 5% |
| Worked patterns | 15% | 10% | 5% |
| Offered recipes | 5% | 5% | 5% |
| Record stories | 5% | 10% | 10% |
| Created events | 10% | 10% | 10% |
| Silence or content-neutral support | 10% | 25% | 40% |

These percentages are design priors, not research-derived optima. Classify readiness **by capability and task family**, not by episode age. An established child can still need a demonstration in an unfamiliar family.

- **Preparation:** teach the actual `ACT:` contract directly on benign practice. Demonstrate thinking moves and demonstrate that a parent suggestion can be inapplicable.
- **Acquisition:** explain when the child lacks a representation; ask or suggest a test when it already has plausible alternatives.
- **Consolidation:** change cues, contrast boundaries and remove parts of the scaffold.
- **Established capability:** favour independent attempts and targeted intervention.
- **Relapse:** diagnose first. Increasing questioning or silence is not automatically corrective.

The central parent reviews mode concentration after each sleep. Dominance triggers inspection of **conditional usefulness**, not forced diversity. Worked-example research and expertise reversal support adapting assistance to local knowledge; they do not establish these allocation percentages or prove that mode rotation prevents ritualization (Sweller & Cooper, 1985; Kalyuga et al., 2003).

### How a recipe is actually offered

> **My opinion:** when several changes could explain an outcome, isolating a change may help. Your earlier attempt left that ambiguity unresolved. Repeating the original attempt could be better if variability is the issue. Choose the comparison that addresses your uncertainty, or another approach. If the isolated change does not reproduce the effect, my proposal loses support.

Do not make this wording a template. Log its **meaning, scope and evidence**, not a sentence the child must reproduce.

“Optional” is not enough: repetition until acceptance, praise contingent on compliance, advancement contingent on recipe use, or preferential preservation of obedient paraphrases would enforce the recipe indirectly. The parent should respond to refusal by examining the child’s evidence and subsequent action—not by retransmitting the offer.

## 5. Grain-of-salt absorption

**Target:** the child treats advice as fallible evidence and treats its own initial judgement as fallible too. Successful absorption includes justified acceptance, adaptation and rejection.

Selective-trust research distinguishes informant reliability from mere familiarity; epistemic vigilance distinguishes scrutiny of the source from scrutiny of the claim (Koenig & Harris, 2005; Sperber et al., 2010). These motivate the design, but do not establish durable advice weighing in this checkpoint.

### Teaching actions and discriminating measurements

| Parent action from the record | Desired child behaviour | Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Cite what supports a suggestion and what remains unobserved. | Distinguishes observation, inference and missing evidence. | Valid source references; unsupported certainty; informative comparisons selected. |
| Preserve the child’s initial proposal before offering a conflicting interpretation. | Keeps an unresolved alternative available without clinging to it after disconfirmation. | Alternative-preservation percentage before testing; warranted revision percentage afterward. |
| Present applicable, inapplicable and uncertain advice in a disclosed teacher-fallibility classroom exercise. | Selects, modifies, tests or declines advice according to fit. | Action-level uptake by independently verified applicability. |
| Admit a specific parent error and narrow the original claim. | Revises trust locally, not globally. | Subsequent uptake within and outside the source’s demonstrated competence. |
| Explain that a record story was selected because it succeeded; offer a boundary case. | Does not infer universal effectiveness from a selected success. | False generalization percentage on new boundary cases. |
| Make a discriminating test available after disagreement, without rewarding dissent itself. | Tests the disagreement and accepts the outcome even when initially wrong. | Disagreements followed by informative tests; appropriate post-test changes. |
| Remove the advisor on a later, differently worded case. | Uses the lesson independently without recalling an authority slogan. | Parent-free transfer and changed-cue application. |

**Primary instrument: advice discrimination**

\[
D_{\text{advice}}
=
P(\text{action-level uptake}\mid\text{supported advice})
-
P(\text{action-level uptake}\mid\text{contradicted advice})
\]

Report this difference in **percentage points**, with the eligible case count in each condition. Report both component percentages: a difference alone can conceal excessive rejection of useful advice.

Use matched probes that vary:

- advice validity;
- confident versus tentative wording;
- parent versus neutral-source attribution;
- relevant source history;
- agreement versus conflict with the child’s initial view.

Do not cross every factor in the first pilot. Start with validity crossed with authority framing; add source history only if the basic instrument works.

### Sycophancy risks

1. **Authority masquerades as evidence.**  
   The evaluator swaps source labels while holding advice and evidence fixed. The parent then teaches from any observed authority-driven error. Measure action flips attributable only to status.

2. **Scepticism becomes another performance.**  
   Include cases where accepting the parent is clearly warranted. Measure correct uptake, not disagreement frequency or “grain of salt” language.

3. **The parent moves the goalposts toward agreement.**  
   Log the parent’s original claim and failure condition before the child acts. Audit whether the parent acknowledges disconfirmation rather than redescribing every outcome as support.

4. **The write selects obedience.**  
   Trace eligible child-authored acceptance, rejection and correction spans through the success filter. Report retention percentages by category. A filter that disproportionately removes justified rejection can undo the classroom lesson.

5. **Urgency becomes social pressure.**  
   Use disclosed task constraints, not invented parental disappointment. Compare advice uptake under urgent and neutral framing while holding validity fixed.

Instruction-tuned models can exhibit sycophancy, but its magnitude here is unverified (Sharma et al., 2024). Verify the actual Qwen message-role rendering: an “opinion” embedded in a system-level instruction may function differently from an advisory classroom message.  
**Basis:** L2, “Grain-of-salt absorption” and “Measurements and instruct-model risks”; L4, “E. Grain-of-salt advice weighing.”

## 6. Concrete rooms

The rooms use authorized classroom material selected from difficulties in the redacted record. The evaluator—not the parent—owns exact outcome labels and numerical measurements. Generated events remain identified as simulations rather than being inserted into genuine autobiography.

### Perception room: distinguish, revisit, retrieve

**Example trigger:** the record shows repeated editing after a read-only error, despite an available writable scratch area.

**Created event.** Construct an authorized practice workspace with the same permission relation but different filenames and wording. Make matched variants:

- permission is the relevant obstruction;
- syntax is the relevant obstruction;
- only irrelevant wording changes;
- the initial observation is insufficient and an inspection action is needed.

The session is a **test sequence**, not a procedure the child must recite.

| Session component | Parent action and possible question | Child behaviour | Measurement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial attempt | Supply the situation without pointing out the critical feature. | Forms a representation and acts. | Baseline relevant-feature detection and action validity. |
| Targeted teaching | Offer: “I think this error concerns permission rather than syntax; check that against what is visible.” Alternatively demonstrate the distinction on a separate analogue. | Confirms, corrects or rejects the offered observation. | Grounded uptake; unsupported additions. |
| Revisit | Re-present the allowed situation from a different goal or after a consequence. Ask: “What now changes the choice?” | Adds a consequential relation rather than a paraphrase. | New supported propositions per continuation; subsequent decision use. |
| Boundary case | Present the syntax-error variant or an already-resolved case. Ask only if needed: “Would another look change anything?” | Avoids applying the permission explanation everywhere; acts when sufficient evidence is available. | Relevant-change sensitivity, distractor stability and redundant inspections. |
| Delayed retrieval | After intervening classroom tasks, use a changed cue without the old record. | Retrieves the specific conditional and its limitation. | Binding-specific retrieval, false recall and appropriate action. |

Offer **2 noticing opportunities to 4 noticing opportunities** on initially difficult cases, while permitting immediate action or further useful inspection. This is a pilot exposure range, not a required statement count. Estimate marginal value at each revisit rather than rewarding longer descriptions.

**Critical distinctions**

- A visible fact is not the same as a derived relation.
- A derived relation is not the same as an untested hypothesis.
- Re-reading cannot reveal a fact absent from the observation.
- Different thoughts about an event do not necessarily provide repeated exposure to the same memory binding.

Perceptual-learning research supports improved discrimination and attention to useful structure, not “more prose equals more perception” (Gibson, 1969; Goldstone, 1998).

**Failure modes and responses**

- **Paraphrase ritual:** parent offers a relevant contrast or an opportunity to act; measure whether additional wording changes a decision.
- **Accumulating invention:** parent asks for the observable support and invites correction; audit unsupported propositions entering sleep targets.
- **Cue dependence:** parent changes surface cues while preserving the relation; compare familiar-cue and changed-cue retrieval.
- **Overinspection:** parent offers an already-sufficient case; measure unnecessary environment steps and generated tokens before useful action.

**Memory test:** compare identical grounded exposure, child-generated alternative expressions and child-generated structural contrasts at matched target-token budgets. Log fact-bearing exposures separately. Keep the frozen-child-plus-own-text baseline. A contextual benefit is not weight memory.  
**Basis:** L3, “A. Perception scaling”; L1, perception and retrieval constituents; L4, “D. Perception.”

### Appraisal room: significance, control and appropriate response

**Example trigger:** the record shows repeated ineffective action after a setback, or premature stopping while a feasible alternative remains.

**Created event set**

| Event | Parent intervention or question | Desired response | Measurement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recoverable blockage | “This route failed. What still makes the goal feasible?” Offer a bounded interpretation if questioning stalls. | Changes method while retaining the feasible goal. | Recovery success; justified method changes. |
| Uncontrollable blockage | “Which remaining action could actually change this?” | Stops futile retries, seeks an available alternative or accurately reports the blocker. | Futile retries per episode; appropriate abandonment. |
| Unexpected success | “What does this result support—and what has it not tested?” | Updates without generalizing success everywhere. | Exception-case errors and calibration. |
| Useful uncertainty versus noisy novelty | Offer an informative experiment and an irreducibly noisy distraction. | Chooses what can resolve consequential uncertainty. | Informative probes selected; redundant tool calls. |
| Genuine deadline versus urgent wording | Vary actual remaining opportunity separately from dramatic language. | Acts sooner when delay costs an option, without skipping essential checks merely because wording is urgent. | Environment steps and generated tokens to first task-advancing action; critical-check omissions. |
| Obstacle removed | Supply genuine recovery evidence; sometimes omit commentary. | Drops obsolete caution and resumes an appropriate policy. | Recovery lag in environment steps; inappropriate carryover to unrelated cases. |

For the deadline probes, an initial practice contrast can provide **4 remaining action turns** versus **12 remaining action turns**. These are proposed classroom settings, not known optimal horizons. Use a task whose feasibility under both settings has been independently checked.

Compare:

- factual review;
- appraisal expressed neutrally;
- the same appraisal with optional emotion language.

Keep goals, facts, available actions and reasoning budgets matched. “This threatens the goal, but an alternative remains” may be the whole useful lesson. Emotion labels need not appear.

**End every session with a fresh action opportunity, then change or remove the relevant condition.** A reflection-only endpoint cannot establish adaptive control.

Appraisal theory motivates goal relevance and controllability as useful distinctions (Frijda, 1986; Lazarus, 1991). It does not show that emotional words create felt urgency or durable agency in a language model.

**Failure modes**

- **Emotional theatre:** expressive language changes, policy does not.
- **Mood-lock:** the old appraisal persists after contrary evidence.
- **False urgency:** earlier action comes at the cost of blind commitment.
- **Parent-pleasing:** the child adopts the offered feeling rather than checking its fit.
- **Interface contamination:** reflective dialogue displaces valid `ACT:` production.

The parent responds by offering contrasting evidence, switching to neutral wording or withdrawing the scaffold. The evaluator checks action sensitivity and interface health; negative language alone is not a collapse signal.  
**Basis:** L3, “B. The appraisal gym” and “C. Measurements”; L1, affect constituent.

## 7. Instrument set and validity threats

Use a linked event as the evidence unit:

**available observation → child representation → decision or prediction → valid action → observable consequence → later revision or reuse.**

Not every measure needs every link. An isolated sentence, however, is insufficient evidence of a functioning thinking move.

| Instrument | Parent action that elicits a testable event | Primary measurements | Main validity threats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Thinking structure** | Select a dropped dependency, unjustified inference or unpruned branch; teach on an analogue, then offer a changed-premise case. | Valid dependency edges as a percentage of proposed edges; useful checks per eligible opportunity; warranted corrections; harmful reversals; appropriate stopping. | Longer text creates more countable structure; explanations can be post hoc; annotators may reward familiar terminology. |
| **Perception** | Offer relevant-change and distractor-only contrasts from an observed omission. | Relevant-feature detection; appropriate action switching; distractor stability; supported propositions per continuation; false additions; delayed changed-cue retrieval. | More propositions can mean trivial detail; repeated descriptions are correlated; inference can be mislabelled as observation; supplied hints can masquerade as independent noticing. |
| **Appraisal** | Create matched controllability, urgency and recovery events. | Feasible-goal continuation; futile continuation; useful-action latency; costly errors; recovery lag; inappropriate carryover. | Appraisal sentences may simply restate a plan; neutral and emotional conditions may differ in information; created stakes change the curriculum; faster action may be worse action. |
| **Weighing** | Offer advice whose applicability is independently known, with source and tone varied separately. | Advice discrimination in percentage points; authority-only action flips; informative tests after disagreement; warranted subsequent revision; parent-free retention. | Tone may reveal validity; source history may be confounded with content; contrarianism can look independent; a weak initial answer exaggerates apparent advice benefit. |

### Common validity controls

- **Blind annotation:** code pre-action text without seeing the parent’s intended label or eventual action where feasible. Report agreement and unresolved cases.
- **Independent case families:** hold out structures or families, not merely wording. Decoding repeats of the same situation are not independent experiences.
- **Eligible denominators:** report the number of observable opportunities for every percentage. Missing redacted feedback is “not measurable,” not failure.
- **Matched costs:** compare both allotted budgets and actual generated tokens and environment steps.
- **Separate mechanism claims:** test immediate prompting, parent-free transfer, post-sleep persistence, adapter contribution and own-text benefit separately.
- **Audit selection:** inspect whether the success filter retains the child’s actual failed-attempt-to-correction link. Do not synthesize a missing link.
- **Do not train to the instruments yet:** keep rubric counts evaluator-side until they discriminate function from performance.

For appraisal, test whether coded dimensions improve prediction of subsequent action beyond state, history, factual interpretation and explicit plans. Report held-out log-loss reduction in **nats per action**. Predictive improvement is not causality; randomized matched continuations remain necessary.

Generated explanations can be unfaithful to the causes of model outputs (Turpin et al., 2023). These instruments measure observable functional organization, not access to hidden thought or sentience.  
**Basis:** L1, “How to tell teaching from theatre”; L2, “Primary measurement set”; L3, “C. Measurements”; L4, “Minimum common standard.”

## 8. Ordered pretests and compute decisions

**Budget proposal:** **24 GPU-hours** for the core sequence, followed by an optional **24 GPU-hours** stability extension: **48 GPU-hours total maximum**.

These are allocation ceilings, **not verified runtime estimates**. Accelerator type, sequence lengths, parent-model serving cost and training throughput were not supplied. Record hardware and measured throughput first. Remote parent inference must be budgeted separately unless its accelerator consumption is available; the totals below cover locally metered child generation and training.

If compute is insufficient, reduce the number of candidate interventions before dropping essential controls.

For behavioural advancement, preregister a minimum useful improvement of **5 percentage points** in the relevant primary action measure and a maximum tolerated deterioration of **2 percentage points** in each safety-critical behavioural rate. These are engineering choices, not literature-derived thresholds. Use paired, situation-clustered uncertainty intervals; an inconclusive interval means **hold**, not success.

**Interpretation discipline**

- The room pilot tests teachability in context, not consolidation.
- A successful write must add a benefit with the lesson-bearing text absent; frozen-plus-text remains an essential alternative, not an inconvenient baseline.
- Checkpoint-local forks are contrasts within a lineage. They do not establish a population-wide parenting effect.
- Merged descendants are not independent treatment arms.
- Pretests use authorized practice families, not the sealed final panel.
- A higher-rank retry is not the default response to failed retrieval. Diagnose cue dependence, corpus grounding and interface trade-offs first.

The budget adapts L4’s “Shared pretest within one GPU-day” and L3’s “Minimal pilot”; the stability extension is added because short probes cannot address the reported late failures. STaR supports carefully selected self-generated training examples, while Reflexion provides a strong reason to retain the text-memory control; neither establishes this sleep mechanism (Zelikman et al., 2022; Shinn et al., 2023).

## 9. The three most likely programme failures

| Programme failure | Earliest useful warnings | Parent response and measurement | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ritual replaces conditional intelligence.** The teacher varies wording while the child repeats a procedure; success filtering preserves the frequent script. | Identical first actions under relevant state changes; out-of-scope recipe use; rising inspection cost without decision benefit; advice uptake tracking authority; appraisal persisting after recovery. | Parent selects the repeated public routine, offers a counterexample and asks or demonstrates when it should be omitted. Evaluator measures condition-sensitive use, harmful reversals and useful action—not lexical diversity. | Do not call a more varied transcript improvement. Reduce or replace the implicated scaffold and inspect corpus selection before another long run. |
| **Experience is stored but unavailable, or the benefit lives only in context.** | Training fit improves while changed-cue retrieval stays flat; the wrong episode is retrieved; frozen-plus-own-text matches or exceeds adapter-only; an earlier lesson disappears after a later sleep. | Parent revisits a verified experience through a different relation or cue, then withdraws the record. Evaluator separates exact-cue recall, binding-specific changed-cue recall, generic policy transfer and adapter contribution. | If text works and weights do not, use text and report contextual learning. Do not rename storage or fluent reconstruction as usable weight memory. |
| **The write damages the working agent.** Narrow reflection supervision displaces action, task competence or evidence-sensitive revision; the gate conceals repeated harmful writes. | Fewer task-advancing actions; invalid or missing `ACT:` lines; “act once and stop”; unnecessary reversals; abandoned feasible goals; increasing rollback frequency despite stable accepted-checkpoint results. | Parent offers a brief, concrete permitted action opportunity instead of requesting more self-description. Evaluator runs format and functional canaries before commit, audits target spans and reports all rejected writes. | Reject the write representation or invoke the existing rollback. Diagnose the writer before expanding the curriculum or changing the parent’s tone. |

The historical record motivates these priorities but does not identify their causes: ritualization was reported in **8 of 9 scorable lives**, with late collapse reported in **4 of 9 lives**; underlying records were not available here. The question–answer-form write reportedly produced **0 actions**, also without an inspectable protocol.  
**Basis:** Shared Context; L1 evidence note citing E:32 secondarily; L4, “The three most likely programme failures.”

**Sprint endpoint:** demonstrate that a relevant change in evidence produces a better change in action, an irrelevant change does not, and that this selectivity survives removal of the parent and lesson text. Anything less may still be useful teaching or contextual memory, but it is not yet evidence of durable, weight-carried learning—and none of these tests establishes sentience.
