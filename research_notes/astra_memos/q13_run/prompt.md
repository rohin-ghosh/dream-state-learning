You are asked to specify one small piece of a learning system before it is built. Ideas first; concrete; cite the evidence lines you rely on; mark what you cannot verify; no fetching. Up to ~1,800 words.

# Setting
A frozen 7B agent (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) lives in a compiler-optimisation gym: 1024 episodes, each 16 ticks of PREDICT / ACT / NOTE / RECALL; every 32 episodes it 'sleeps': its own notes and thoughts from the successful episodes are compiled into a corpus and a rank-8 LoRA is retrained on it (whole-text loss, 3 epochs), then the adapter is kept only if a fixed panel score does not fall. The project's thesis (formalised today): the thing to teach the child is the behaviour of producing good post-training data about its own experience — first-person procedural records with outcomes — so that every input turns into weight change at sleep. The parents (a 14B model in a classroom, never seeing scores) teach thinking moves; the current bootstrap text tells the child to 'form expectations before acting' and 'write down what you learn'.

# Evidence (today's audit of 25 finished lives; every sleep corpus is cumulative, so rates are on the notes NEW at each sleep)
- In 17 lives the first person disappears from the notes after the first sleep and the notes lock into a RECIPE ritual: 'Initial passes: -mem2reg, -sroa, -gvn, -simplifycfg. Expect 55% reduction based on past experience. Will observe outcome and adjust strategy accordingly.' (the same line 33 times, 13 distinct, in one parented life at sleep 768).
- In 8 lives the notes flip mid-life into a first-person regime (0.7-1.0 of new notes) that is a SLOGAN ritual — the bootstrap's own instructions repeated: 'Form expectations before acting. Write down what I learn to understand the outcome.' (9 new notes at sleep 896, 4 distinct, zero episode-specific content); 'Reality will surprise me, and I will learn from it. Form a plan, stick to it.'; 'Formed expectation before acting. Will write down what I learn regardless of the outcome. Initial passes (...) expected to reduce instructions by 55-70%.'; a parented life echoes the parent's phrasing: 'Avoid generic "this worked before" - be concrete.'
- In neither ritual does a note say what was done to THIS program and what happened. Outcome words in late notes: 0.00 (gate arm), 0.01 (plain), 0.11 (gate+panel), 0.16 (parented) — and where they occur they are past-tense slogans or the prediction ('Initial expectation was 0.65'), not the measured result.
- Provenance: factual tokens in notes (pass names, numbers, percentages) are sourced somewhere in the life in >= 98.5% of cases in 24 of 25 lives; the residual is truncated tokens and passes NAMED but not RUN in that episode (two 'catalogue writers' name unrun passes in 47% and 68% of their notes). The child repeats and catalogues; it does not invent.
- The harness already appends to every corpus item the line 'So I did: <passes> -> instructions 857 -> 470 (45.2% reduction)', so the outcome IS in the training text — but as harness text, not as the child's own sentence.
- Separately (the 'bridge' experiment on planted facts): when the child must END each of its perceptions with a canonical sentence 'Owner X's car is <colour>', its prose restates the fact in 78% of lines vs 26% when the harness appends the sentence, and the fact then stores in the LoRA (0.82 completion vs 0.91 for hand-written templates). Requiring the artifact form changed what the child wrote.
