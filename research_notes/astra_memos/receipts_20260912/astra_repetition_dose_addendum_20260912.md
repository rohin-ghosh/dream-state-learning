## Addendum — optimizer-practice interpretation (expectation, not exact-gradient claim)

**The narrow step-count/dose caution is supported, with a loss-weighting qualification.** Independently reconstructed all 80 update groups for both arms: SHORT's batch4/accum16 contains the same four original groups per update as the original batch4/accum1, with 16 contiguous reset copies per group; both fits take 80 optimizer steps. The frozen trainer in `/tmp/astra_level0_source_ed3aac9f.tgz`, member `organism_v6/train_adapter_v3.py:818`, backpropagates `loss / cfg.grad_accum` and steps only after the accumulation window. Thus 16× processed tokens/copy presentations is not 16× sequential optimizer practice or an established 16× effective write dose. Averaging reset-copy gradients suggests comparable update scale, not a 16× sum; **this is an expectation, not an exact-gradient or equal-learning claim**. In particular, SHORT has four homogeneous microbatches per group, whereas the original has one mixed four-group microbatch: with token-mean model loss, averaging those group means differs from the original token-weighted mean when target lengths differ (especially arithmetic versus color targets). Dropout, numerical/order effects, and subsequent optimizer trajectories further preclude equality. The unchanged corpus token share therefore does not by itself establish unchanged effective gradient weighting. Evidence: `R/manifest.json` recipes/order audit, `R/{teach,control}_short.json`, `S0/{teach,control}.json`, `S0/fit_teach/adapter/train_manifest.json`, and the frozen trainer's `pack_by_group`, `epoch_order`, and training loop. Safe wording: “16× reset-copy token processing at the same 80 optimizer steps did not improve this seed0 development-memory endpoint”; neither more independent optimizer practice nor the failure's cause is established.
