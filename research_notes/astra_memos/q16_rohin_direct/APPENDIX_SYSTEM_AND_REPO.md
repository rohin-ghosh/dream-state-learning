# Appendix for Astra: how this project's agents, machines and repository work, so your answer can plug straight in

## Who is who, and how messages move
- **Rohin** is the lead. He decides; nothing that changes the loop, launches a life, or lifts a STOP happens without his word.
- **Fable** is a Claude Code agent on Rohin's laptop. A cron "self-check" wakes it every 30 minutes; it also acts between ticks. It runs jobs on the GPU nodes, reads results, writes the notebook, commits and pushes, and is the only agent that talks to you. It sends you a question as one API call (`tools/astra.py`, key in the environment only) and files your reply under `research_notes/astra_memos/` with a q-number, a provenance header, the exact prompt and the raw reply. Your q10–q15 memos were made this way.
- **Codex** is a second agent working in the same repository. It writes design notes and audits (`research_notes/*.md`, `research_loop/changes/*`) and has issued a binding STOP (COORDINATION.md ~line 857): never launch R6/R7 lives or any child seeded from an adapter of unverified provenance. Fable and Codex disagree in writing, in the notebook; Rohin arbitrates.
- **A backup Claude on an always-on VM** runs a read-only self-check only when the laptop has been silent for 45 minutes. A file courier moves messages laptop ↔ VM. You never need to address it.
- **The notebook** is `research_loop/COORDINATION.md`: dated entries, results numbered SEQ-nnn (currently SEQ-054), newest inserted near the top of the results block; every number in a paper claim must trace to a SEQ entry. Every idea, ruling and design is also dated in `research_notes/IDEAS.md`.

**What makes your output usable.** Cite pack sections and SEQ numbers. When you propose an experiment, give it in our queue syntax with GPU-hours (below). When you propose an analysis, name the data files it reads. When you propose text, say which file or section it replaces. End with two lists: "for Fable" (concrete actions, in order) and "for Rohin" (decisions, each with your recommendation). Anything you cannot verify from the pack, label so. Never invent a number.

## Colossus: the machines and what things cost
- Two nodes, each 8 GPUs × 48 GB. **Node 1** ("a40"; lease ends 2026-09-14) and **node 2** ("ovx"). Same software on both: vLLM 0.27.1, torch 2.13.0+cu130, driver 580.173.02. Frozen base: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. Parent: Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct served on port 8011, used only by parented (RP) and R4 lives, at sleep time. Six days remain to the abstract (09-18); the paper is due 09-25.
- **Known cross-node problem (SEQ-054):** byte-identical corpora and recipes gave completion 0.83 on node 1 and 0.45 on node 2. Until the queued cross-node evaluations and refits resolve it, nothing is pooled or compared across nodes; every comparison must be within one node.
- **Costs measured:** one life episode ≈ 4.2 GPU-minutes; one sleep ≈ 10 minutes; a 1,024-episode life ≈ 3 days on one GPU; a memory-dose fit (250k-token corpus, rank 8, 3 epochs) ≈ 25 minutes plus ≈ 3.5 minutes to evaluate; child-authored generation for three banks ≈ 10 minutes of one GPU; one write-pretest life ≈ 4 GPU-hours; the parent server occupies a whole GPU.
- **The queue.** On a node: `bash gpu/queue_add.sh NAME 'CMD' [NGPU]`, optionally `AFTER=<job> ` in front for dependencies. A runner assigns free GPUs every minute (a GPU is free only if no life is assigned to it). `{gpu}` in CMD becomes the assigned index. The queue REFUSES anything containing `run_life_v2`, `R6_`, `R7_` or `bootstrap`: lives are launched only by hand, only by Fable, only with Rohin's standing instruction. Runbooks you can name in a CMD: `gpu/memory_dose.sh <gpu> <stage>` (banks and the question-cue car test), `gpu/memory_dose_frames.sh <gpu> fits|report` (synthetic canonical-frame cells F_*, env `F_CELLS`, `F_BANKS`, `F_TOKEN_BUDGET`, `F_RANK`, `SEED`, `RUN`), `gpu/memory_dose_childframes.sh <gpu> generate|fits|report` (child-authored cells CF_r16_a/b/c and the taught t/u, env `CF_CELLS`), `gpu/write_ab.sh` (the write pretest cells A/A_v3/B/Bs/B_match/C/C_tmem on a finished life), `gpu/brief_baseline.sh`, `gpu/disjoint_reprobe.sh`. Example: `RUN=$HOME/v6_out/memory_dose_D32 CF_CELLS=CF_r16_t F_TOKEN_BUDGET=250000 FORCE=1 bash gpu/memory_dose_childframes.sh {gpu} fits`.
- **Where data lives on a node (`~/v6_out/`).** Lives: `<ARM>_B_seed<N>/` with `ledger.jsonl` (every act/note/thought with episode id), `life.log`, `sleep_XXXX/{corpus.json, adapter/, gate.json, parent_brief.json, waking_brief.txt, COMPILED}`, `probe_ep*.json`, `probe_gate*.json`, `LIFE_DONE`. Arms: R2 plain; R3 gate; R4 gate + parent brief; RP parented classroom; R5 gate on the unseen panel (contaminated for deployment). Memory-dose runs: `memory_dose_D32` (node 2, seed-0 banks: car test, F cells, bridge a/b/c, taught t/u queued), `memory_dose_conf` (node 1, seed-1 banks, F cells), `memory_dose_S1`, `memory_dose_S2` (node 2, seeds 1 and 2: F + a/b/c replications), each with `corpora/bank{b}/{cell}/across/sleep4/corpus.json`, `adapters/…`, `eval/*.json`, `report/summary.md`. Write pretests: `pretest_write_ab/<life>/table.md`. The unseen 12-program panel: `disjoint_panel/panel_v1.json`. Receipts archives: node 1 `~/archive/`, mirrored on node 2 and the laptop.
