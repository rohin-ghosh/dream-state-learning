## 1 — The final comparison does not yet identify the claim

**Where:** Spec §§0, 2.3, 3.3; pretest one-screen; ruling “Two final-test conditions.”

**Problem.**

Three distinct questions are being conflated:

1. Does the taught checkpoint behave better with its weights frozen?
2. Does the complete taught system—including lineage replay and its different writer lag—learn better during deployment?
3. Did *parenting*, rather than extra experience or training, cause the difference?

The proposed experiment can address the first two. Without an exposure-matched unparented prehistory, it cannot isolate the third.

Further:

- The ruled **frozen-weight condition is relegated to an optional +18 GPU-hour add-on**.
- Historical v6 untaught lives are not controls for v7: stopping, interface, context, writer, and gate all change.
- The taught child receives extra lineage training and later updates. Logging that asymmetry does not remove it.
- “Empty ledger” must mean a new **deployment ledger**, with lineage replay loaded separately from a frozen, read-only snapshot.
- The control’s Stage 0, birth brief, self-summary, parent availability, training seeds, and initial floor must be explicit.

**Fix.**

Freeze two estimands:

- **Primary:** frozen-checkpoint transfer, taught versus frozen base, identical compiler harness and problem schedule, parent-free and without waking text memory.
- **Secondary:** 512-problem continual-learning curves of the two specified systems. Call this an **end-to-end deployment comparison**, not isolated evidence of improved weight-based learning efficiency.

Both deployment arms get the same birth/interface scaffold and self-summary mechanism; neither gets adaptive parents or inherited textual briefs. Match compiler schedules and generation seeds where possible. Report score against both **problem age and elapsed time**, with actual adapter exposure histories.

The new untaught v7 life is mandatory. Historical controls remain context only.

**Cost:** 2–3 h protocol/manifest work; roughly the budgeted **18 GPU-h** for the frozen condition, subject to its actual horizon. This displaces optional breadth, not the control.

## 2 — The training sequences may still remove the very condition the hypothesis needs

**Where:** Spec §§1.1, 2.1–2.2; survey §4, “Consolidate improvement sequences.”

**Problem.**

The gym goal, metric, and mechanical state live in the **HEAD**. Scale 1 is described as beginning with the neutral tail line `New problem: <id>.` That line is not the problem statement. Unless the compiler explicitly restores the head’s gym state, the writer is still training thoughts without their triggering situation.

Additional unresolved points:

- The three carried turns condition generation in the next problem but are omitted from that problem’s training sequence. They can appear as **masked context** without becoming duplicate targets.
- Multiple `ACT:` lines in one turn can cause Scale 2 to duplicate the same thought several times.
- Silent text after the last action is not necessarily re-covered by action windows.
- Reflection context includes previous reflection and a summary; its training reconstruction needs specification.
- Neighbourhood ordering only supplies contextual neighbours if attention can cross sequence boundaries. If it cannot, it merely changes minibatch order. If it can, cross-episode context and loss boundaries must be deliberate.
- A textual leak scan is not a substitute for source-based exclusion. A child legitimately repeating parent words must remain eligible under the ruling.

**Fix.**

Make a rendered-sequence audit a release blocker:

1. Masked goal and necessary gym state precede child targets.
2. Every target span has ledger provenance.
3. Parent/brief-origin spans are structurally excluded.
4. Child-origin restatements are retained.
5. Carry-over and reflection context policies are explicit.
6. Multi-action turns have defined multiplicity.
7. Packing has explicit attention boundaries.
8. Print and inspect ten real compiled examples, including failures and long episodes.

Also remove the guarantee that masking outcomes means the model “cannot learn to hallucinate” them. Masking avoids directly supervising their emission; it does not guarantee non-emission.

**Cost:** 4–6 h plus a 0.5–1 h GPU training smoke. **Build this before adding more P1 cells.**

## 3 — The write-budget arithmetic and exposure promises disagree

**Where:** Spec §§0, 2.2–2.4, 9/D1–D2; pretest yardsticks.

**Checks.**

- \(5\,\mathrm{M}/1{,}200 = 4{,}167\) seconds = **1.16 h**, as stated approximately.
- Six clones × 32 problems × 10,000 tokens = **1.92 M tokens per round**.
- At three epochs, the corpus budget is **1.67 M**, but the newest-data allocation is only half: **0.83 M**. The stated width trade-off is broadly sensible, but depends on **one round arriving per writer cycle**, not merely clone count.
- The explicit overflow sampling rule contradicts “every thought is trained when it is new” and “the newest round in full.”
- **Scale 1 does not halve write duration under a fixed, fully consumed token-pass budget.** It either doubles the number of problems sampled or leaves part of the budget unused. P1’s simplicity rule must specify which.
- The stated 10,000 tokens/problem is an estimate, not an identity: action multiplicity, silent tails, outcomes, and packing alter it.
- Replay probability should use the eligible **older** corpus denominator, and cumulative re-exposures should be computed from the sampler. “One to four more times” is not a reliable general guarantee.
- The extra 2.5 M lineage tokens cost **0.58 h per epoch**. At three epochs they add **1.74 h**, not 0.58 h.
- At E=1, a full-budget taught test cycle is approximately **2.4 h**, including exams and amortized frozen reads, versus a roughly **2 h clone round**. The taught writer is not stably “one round behind” at these assumptions.
- In the synchronous fallback, cumulative training through 512 problems costs about **10.1 h** for the untaught child alone. Adding R on each of 16 writes adds another **9.3 h** for the taught child, before exam/restart overhead. “Both wait ≈10 h” is wrong.

**Fix.**

Replace prose arithmetic with a small executable budget sheet driven by:

- actual context tokens processed and target tokens;
- sequence lengths/padding;
- epochs and measured throughput;
- clone slice arrival rate;
- exam and restart time;
- lineage replay tokens;
- own-row omissions and exposure counts.

State plainly: **all rows are retained; not all are necessarily trained at their first eligible write.**

Freeze a token-pass number, not “about one round” as a moving budget.

**Cost:** 2–3 h; benchmark piggybacks on P1. Do not launch six clones per writer merely because six fit the instantaneous corpus calculation.

## 4 — Gate reliability is substantially overstated; P8 cannot validate it counterfactually

**Where:** Spec §§3.3–3.4; P8; new R4 604 and floor-decay facts.

**Problem.**

The normal-tail calculations in §3.4 are approximately correct **for a known, fixed, noiseless floor and independent Gaussian exams**. That is not this gate.

- The floor is a maximum of noisy committed scores.
- Candidate and floor both have measurement error.
- Repeated failures share the same noisy floor, creating dependence.
- The observed failures are heavy-tailed: the new **0.138 rep** is directly relevant.
- The claimed maximum-selection bias of “roughly half a replicate SD after many exams” is unsupported. Under a simple independent Gaussian model, maxima over dozens of two-rep means can be elevated by roughly **0.009 or more**, already comparable with tol=0.01.
- A single collapsed rep **can** affect a two-rep mean enough to trigger the fast path. For example, 0.138 and 0.250 average to 0.194, more than 0.05 below a 0.250 floor.

P8 replays scores from trajectories generated under the old policy. After a hypothetical rollback, subsequent thoughts, corpora, and adapters would have differed. It therefore measures **policy exposure on a fixed recorded sequence**, not the resulting life. Its “false rollback” label is not ground truth.

**Fix.**

- Keep the immutable best-committed floor required by the ruling.
- Delete the “false rollback essentially never happens” claim.
- Re-estimate tail frequencies and floor-selection effects from available replicate records; show individual reps.
- P8 remains a descriptive policy replay. Do not tune tol from report-panel outcomes in it.
- For catastrophic score drops, **hold the candidate inactive and run a fresh-seed confirmation**. Keep ordinary three-sleep patience.
- Define the state machine: committed, eligible as floor, active, held, rejected, rollback recovery, and counter handling after format rejection.

**Cost:** 3–4 h CPU analysis/state-machine tests; approximately **0.43 GPU-h per triggered confirmation**. This is cheaper than contaminating several rounds with an unconfirmed wreck.

## 7 — Several behavioral measures are either incomparable or structurally unmeasurable

**Where:** Spec §§1.3–1.4, 3.1, 4; survey values 1–5 and 12.

**Problem.**

- “Words” and “tokens” are used interchangeably for the collapse brake.
- §0 compares problems **neither model solved**; §3.1 compares each model’s **own unsolved subset**. Those subsets can have different difficulty.
- There is no defined behavior when the common unsolved set is empty or tiny.
- Fable’s “valid-action rate <0.5” has no denominator and may conflate wrong answers with interface failure.
- Under the forever loop, **not stopping early is enforced**, not learned persistence.
- Knowing when to stop cannot be measured by episode termination: the gym or harness stops it.
- The survey tells the child “stop when trying has stopped moving anything”; the spec forbids child termination.
- Goal selection is listed as a value, but the child cannot select its next task.
- The nudge rule yields a nudge at turns 4, 8, 12, **and 16** unless terminal nudges are explicitly suppressed.
- The late-life list places **R4 601 = 15.8** among the “1.0–15.3” non-budget cases. The 11/13 categorization needs correction.

**Fix.**

Use **generated tokens on the common-unsolved items**, with a minimum support requirement and a logged “not estimable” outcome. Keep a separate interface acceptance measure with a fixed denominator.

Persistence = changed, valid attempts after non-improvement, with success and wasted-action costs reported. Knowing-done = recognition in free text on suitable tasks, not harness termination. Mark goal-selection as untested this run.

Accept 16×400, four silent turns, and three-turn carry-over; suppress terminal nudges and stop scoring further actions after a terminal gym outcome.

**Cost:** 3–4 h implementation/audit, using P2/P4 data.

# 3. The survey’s two tensions: recommendation

## A. No cap on repeats versus decay of identical lesson text

**Keep every generated occurrence and do not introduce text-based decay this run.**

The survey’s proposed “different context keeps full weight, identical lesson text decays” distinction is not yet operational. The same words can be a useful conditional response in one situation and a ritual in another. Text similarity alone cannot decide which.

Separate:

- **storage:** every occurrence retained;
- **sampling:** explicit finite-budget exposure;
- **diagnosis:** repeated text, context diversity, action diversity, and subsequent utility measured.

Test the trigger-preserving writer first. If it still produces lock-in, duplicate-weight decay becomes a named ablation. Do not combine both changes now and then attribute any improvement to context restoration.

## B. Fade-the-unused versus protect rare exceptions

**Reject pure least-frequently-used replay as the phase-B default.** Rare but important exceptions are precisely what it would erase.

If phase B qualifies, use an explicit protected earlier-family/exception replay allocation, with the rest recency-weighted. Define exception strata from legitimate training metadata and outcomes—not an unvalidated free-text “surprise >0.05” detector. Retention failure blocks publication under the predeclared rule.

Neither neuroscience nor Rohin’s cache analogy settles the weighting. **Measured retained conditional behavior does.** No ledger deletion, and no automatic “contradicted thought” exclusion: exploratory mistakes are part of the learning history.

# 4. What the current evidence honestly supports

## One-paragraph statement

The current v6 evidence supports a narrow and largely negative conclusion: the learned adapters sometimes preserve a useful fixed compiler routine, but do not yet demonstrate robust transferable agency or a reliable advantage over textual memory. In the completed 21-cell disjoint re-probe, mean final changes are −0.025 for unguarded lives, +0.004 for gated R3, and −0.024 for R4; every above-base cell is the 0.2731 routine, and gate approval did not prevent a 0.138 replicate. Across four text-memory lives, brief-only matches or exceeds adapter-only in three—including the two collapsed lives—and loses in one routine life by about 0.013 on the eight-program panel. In the one crossed cell, adapter plus brief is worse than either alone on the eight-panel and scores 0.1619 on the disjoint panel. These observations undermine claims that the current write reliably adds useful memory or that adapter and text memory combine monotonically; they do not establish that weight memory cannot work, that the proposed v7 writer fixes it, or that parenting causes improved general learning. Those remain prospective tests.

## Minimum abstract numbers

The abstract should carry these **numerical bundles**, not just the best checkpoint:

1. **Evidence size:** 21 disjoint re-probe cells, with independent-life counts and reps distinguished; four text-memory lives.
2. **Final disjoint changes:** unguarded **−0.025**, R3 **+0.004**, R4 **−0.024**, each labelled with its group denominator.
3. **Text-memory comparison:** brief ≥ adapter in **3/4** lives; sole adapter win approximately **0.013** on the eight-panel.
4. **Crossed failure:** **0.4987 combined versus 0.5291 brief-only and 0.5262 adapter-only**; combined disjoint **0.1619**.
5. **Routine explanation:** all above-base disjoint cells coincide with the **0.2731** routine; give the numerator/denominator once exported.
6. **New experiment, if completed:** number of independent taught lineages; frozen-transfer effect; prespecified 512-problem learning-curve statistic and endpoint versus fresh v7 controls; uncertainty and compute/update exposure.

The **0.138** tail rep and **0.5307→0.4933** floor failure belong at least in the results table; include the tail in the abstract if claiming stability.

Do not call the three collapsed unguarded **reps** three collapsed independent lives. Do not manufacture confidence intervals from the aggregate means supplied here. Export the per-life data first.
