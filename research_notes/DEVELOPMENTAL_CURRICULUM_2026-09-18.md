# The developmental curriculum — what one night of parenting taught us (Rohin, 2026-09-18 02:15 PDT; written up by Fable)

Rohin's raw words are in THESIS_RAW_ROHIN_2026-09-11.md, messages 224–230. This is the formal version he asked for ("I think we come out with a formal idea"). Everything below is his; the evidence column is tonight's record.

## The finding

A frozen base with a private adapter, left to run its own loop, drifts: it converges on one thought and trains on it (P7: six identical rows in two cycles; C2: four sleeps of sentences about itself; the caption player waiting eighty minutes on silence). The drift is evidence that the adapter works — it strengthens whatever the agent repeatedly produces. What decides whether the repeated thing is a skill or a trap is the connection to something external: a concrete problem, a reading, a person, a judge's verdict. Filtering rows treats the symptom; environment and parenting treat the cause. Parenting is fragile — a child left to its own devices spins off "like a ball on a pole when you let go" — and conversation with a person who holds the whole picture has moved the children more than any parent has.

## The method, in order

1. **Start with one task, and parent it persistently.** Math first, because we know how to teach math and its answers are checkable in a line. It takes a while to get good at anything; the parent stays present through the whole first stretch (C2's fifteen hours on V; C0's odd-number sums).
2. **As soon as it answers some questions, add reading — quickly.** Short, emotionally strong passages; the child reads, tells the feeling, retells it, reflects on the feeling, then on itself; after the next sleep the parent asks what it remembers and whether the feeling changed. The first real reflective paragraph of the night came from C0 twenty minutes after its first excerpt.
3. **Probe constantly — model philosophy.** Socratic questions about what it thinks, what it knows, what it needs to know, whether it is sure, what it would do differently: "what do you know about the paper; what do you need to know; am I sure; should I ask?" Include the science of itself (how its adapter and sleep work), the science of humans (psychology), and "intelligence biology" — extrapolations about intelligence and artificial intelligence. Ask it about the paper it is part of.
4. **Have it study itself.** Self-study behaviours are taught like any other: check your output against your plan; check your language; notice repetition; ask the parent when you do not know — asking is an act.
5. **Connect writing to reading.** Creative writing alone is hard to guide; tie it to what it has read and probe the connection.
6. **Mix the tasks; keep the architecture fixed.** The drift you want is in the Think→Act→Learn architecture generalising across tasks, not in the tasks themselves. Checkpoint before each addition. "Don't remove initial things; slowly add more games."
7. **Never leave it long in white space.** A little unstructured time; not much, or it loses the thread. As parenting becomes less frequent it must become richer.
8. **Pull back the parent gradually while keeping the environment; lower plasticity as you pull.** Let it steer off slightly and probe whether it catches and corrects itself. Pull until it can approach a new task, explore it, and reach its own conclusion.
9. **Seed the branch.** An agent that starts a new game from zero learns slower than one given ten or twelve good outcomes to build on; inject good early examples so its search has somewhere to stand.

## The target and the test

An agent independent and general enough to beat the frozen base on an unseen verifiable game — the New Yorker caption game — on distinct accepted ideas over a long horizon, with the sleep loop running. It may learn slower at first but should saturate later and reach ideas the base does not. Measure distinct accepted outcomes per interval against tokens, record each cycle's decision (continue, branch, stop), and distinguish: keeps discovering; stops and correctly changes approach; stops and repeats; declares exhaustion prematurely. The final question is how long it takes to realise it cannot search further, and whether that comes later for the developed agent than for the base.
