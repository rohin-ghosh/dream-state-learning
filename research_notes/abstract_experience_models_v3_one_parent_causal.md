## Abstract

Language agents can improve from deployment feedback through evolving textual
memory, batch experience distillation, and online low-rank parameter updates.
What remains unclear is whether a prior developmental intervention can improve
an agent's *later ability to learn* from its own experience, rather than only
its initial competence. We study one fixed-weight parent policy that adapts
its teaching through a bounded, evidence-backed notebook, and one child.
On generated non-compiler tasks, the answer-aware parent withholds direct
answers while selecting a process lesson
about prediction and revision, discriminating hypotheses, or scoping beliefs;
the child must apply the lesson through action, and public task outcomes alone
determine whether the resulting native continuation is eligible for a
low-rank write. The parent's prose and private childhood record are then
deleted; the mature reusable pedagogy core remains. In a fresh
compiler-optimization deployment, we cross adaptively taught versus matched
non-parented adults with the mature parent absent versus present. Both
parent-present cells receive the same pedagogy core and a fresh empty
root-local child record, while every cell retains the same evolving active-text
mechanism, tools, context, generated-token budget, task opportunities, and
SLEEP writer. We report retained solo entry performance, entry-adjusted
fresh-task acquisition, autonomous lifetime improvement, and the interaction
between adult type and audited tutoring. A separate matched commit-on/commit-off
fork with identical experience tests whether SLEEP caused persistent change.
