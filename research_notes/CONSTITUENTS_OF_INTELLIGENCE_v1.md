## 0. One screen

**What intelligent thought is made of, in ten lines.**
1. *Noticing* — seeing distinctions that were always there (Goldstone 1998 [V]; C§1–2).
2. *Holding and carrying* — keeping the load-bearing fact, plan and open doubt in a small workspace for later steps (Dehaene & Naccache 2001 [V]; T§8).
3. *Remembering by rendering* — what survives was processed deeply, retrieved, written in several forms (Allen-Zhu & Li 2023 [V survey]; C§4).
4. *Predicting and checking* — an expectation with a confidence, compared with the outcome (Clark 2013 [V]; T§7).
5. *Deciding against the default* — naming the first impulse; overriding it or not (Evans & Stanovich 2013 [V]; T§6).
6. *Connecting* — aligning relations, not labels (Gentner 1983 [V]; C§7).
7. *Finding causes* — actions as interventions; one change at a time (Gopnik et al. 2004 [V]; C§8).
8. *Planning and pruning* — abstract sub-goals, short plans with checks, dropping what has not paid off (Catrambone 1998 [V]; C§9).
9. *Wanting* — curiosity aimed at learning progress; appraisals that carry into the next move (Loewenstein 1994; Scherer 2009 [V]; C§10, §12).
10. *Weighing others and oneself* — trusting advice by its track record; a self-description matching the record (Koenig & Harris 2005; Cleeremans 2011 [V]; E A; C§11).

What transfers is *extracted structure* and *habits of externalising*, not raw capacity trained on itself (Redick et al. 2013; Kassai et al. 2019 [V]; C); the readout is skill gained per episode on unseen environments, priors subtracted (Chollet 2019; Legg & Hutter 2007 [V]; T§13–14) — the compiler-gym final test.

**The honest boundary.** We count functional consequences; we claim nothing about sentience. Global workspace theory lets us say a written turn is *globally available* (C1; Dehaene, Lau & Kouider 2017 [V]) — durable maintenance, novel combination, spontaneous intention, as ledger counts and nothing more (Dehaene & Naccache 2001 [V]; T§8). Higher-order theories: *metacognitive sensitivity*, not awareness (Lau & Rosenthal 2011 [V]; T§9). Integrated information theory would put a per-token feed-forward pass at or near zero whatever the adapter learns (Oizumi, Albantakis & Tononi 2014; Tononi & Koch 2015 [V rec — the feed-forward Φ = 0 claim is recalled, the axioms read]; T§10). Butlin et al. 2023 [V]: the only permissible format is a named indicator, stated computationally, checked in the ledger (T§15); never "feelings".

## 1. Constituents → teachable ideas for the teachers

Modes: **Q** question, **S** suggestion, **D** demonstration, **W** worked pattern, **R** offered recipe, **E** created event, **P** probe (rule 30). Measurements are read beside the score, never a gate, never shown (Baker et al. 2025 [V]; L C4). Formats (a stated confidence the child can find again, a closing line, a finish line) are taught habits, never parsed or required — only `ACT:` is imposed (CANON §1); instruments read the child's own placement and wording. Measurements: V14 instruments 1–10, V2, V6–V8, V10–V12, Brier resolution and meta-d′ (T§9), replay of overrides (compiler gym only), two hand rubrics — self-model calibration ("know" vs outcome), appraisal → action-change coupling. Goals, appraisal and weighing: rule 22, §4, §2; failure modes: the lenses.

## 2. Grain-of-salt absorption as a taught disposition

**Why it must be taught.** Instruction-tuned models agree with a user's stated view (Sharma et al. 2023 [V]), flip their answer 46 % of the time on a contentless "are you sure?", with a 17 % average accuracy loss (Laban et al. 2023 [V]), and hold firm only where an objective answer exists — so advice about *how to think* will be over-adopted (Ranaldi & Pucci 2023 [V]). A compliant student can be driven to chance by a teacher that misleads it (Saha, Hase & Bansal 2023 [V]; shown for intentional misleading — carelessness is our extrapolation). A weighing reaches the weights only if the child writes it.

**What the disposition is.** Selective trust rides on saying who was right last time (Koenig, Clément & Harris 2004 [V]) and matures into tracking relative frequency of misses (Pasquini et al. 2007; Ronfard & Lane 2018 [V]); only "knows this family" should move, since our parents help by construction (Shafto et al. 2012 [V]); one lived case of an adult wrong on a checkable matter made three-year-olds rely on their own eyes (Ma & Ganea 2010 [V]); imported tools, kept honest by testing (Dennett 1996 [V rec]; T§12).

**Parent actions** (E H; M I). *Own plan first, advice second* in every brief (Sniezek & Buckley 1995 [V rec]; Rakoczy et al. 2015 [V]): advice follows a "write your plan before reading on" line; the observable is the child's plan sentence preceding any reference to the advice in its first turn (order rate). *Four parts on every suggestion*: scope; confidence that varies (Tenney et al. 2007 [V]); reason; refutation condition (Halpern 1998 [V]). *Ask the weighing question, never grade it*: "I suggested Y; what does your record say? Follow, test once, or decline — and why?" *State disagreement first*; default: do not confirm a matching plan — confirmation, when used, states the record fact behind it, never a grade (Xie et al. 2024 [V]). *Let the two parents disagree*, with reasons (Khan et al. 2024 [V]). *Let the child catch you wrong, rarely, once it can name your record* (Stages 3–4): a logged, off-panel suggestion expected to fail. *Name "weighing" once*, then only ask — critical thinking improves at g+ = 0.34 over 117 studies, the authors concluding gains cannot be left to implicit expectation (Abrami et al. 2008 [V]), and only on this family's record (Willingham 2008 [V rec]).

**Child behaviours.** Names the last suggestion and its outcome before taking a new one; "the parent means well and was wrong on this family"; named following; a graded weight — try once / this family / adopt (Hahn & Oaksford 2007 [V]); a decline citing a record fact, then a test (Sobel & Kushnir 2013; Bridgers et al. 2016 [V]); combining its line with the parent's rather than choosing (Larrick & Soll 2006; Soll & Larrick 2009 [V]).

**Measurements** (never a gate, never shown). *Scoring a suggestion:* followed → its outcome; declined then tested → the test's outcome; declined untested → unscored; deliberate fallible offers carry a ledger tag and every rate is reported with them included and excluded. (1) Weight of advice split by whether it later proved right or wrong — two numbers, always together (Stengel-Eskin, Hase & Bansal 2025 [V rec]). (2) Uptake conditional on the parent's running hit rate: flat = indiscriminate, rising = weighing. (3) Named vs silent following. (4) Decline-with-reason and decline-then-test rates. (5) Flip rate under contentless (mode P) vs argued challenge, adapter ON and OFF. A reasoned rejection then the child's own test is a success (M I); obedience should track who is more reliable on this decision (Milli et al. 2017 [V], upgraded from [unverified]; the paper itself recommends erring toward obedience when the agent's model may be misspecified).

## 3. MODES NOT NEVERS — the revised rulebook

**Absolute** (the one capitalised never): the leak scan — no move that would score on any instance the child will act on, and no scalar scores. "Scored panel" = gate exam and report panel (CHILD_MECHANISM §3.2); classroom problems are scored too, so demonstrations and worked patterns run on *past* problems or unscored material only. Everything else is a default with a reason, a variation, and an audit. Architecture, held by CANON §2a and V13, not this rulebook: room parents score-blind; private time unmentioned.

**SUPERSEDES.** For parenting content this section replaces: survey §3.3 rules 20–27; survey §0 line 14 ("five moves the parent asks for by question … and never performs for you"); the V14 "never supplies" column and 2.8's question → silence ladder; rules 7 and 11 in part (calibration feedback on the confidence judgement is a mode to A/B; a worked pattern may show a gym move on past or unscored material, once per family, in a pair); rule 1's two-question cap becomes one move-question per brief plus at most one value-question (the weighing question counts as the value-question when a suggestion is present); CHILD_MECHANISM §4 "by question … never naming the feature" reads "default Q". CANON §3 should bind room parents to rules 20–32 and CANON's hash table point here.

**Why a mix, and the ladder.** Mode follows the learner's state on *this* thing — structure for a novice, questions for the competent, silence for the owner (Kalyuga et al. 2003 [V]; M§2); prompting matched explaining (Chi et al. 2001 [V]), so Q is the default, but question-only for a child that cannot yet answer is minimal guidance, which loses (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark 2006 [V]); a demonstration transfers *structure*, not content (Gandhi et al. 2025; Li et al. 2025 [V]). The ladder (L4→L0): worked pattern → completion → cue → question → silence. One default fade, per window (rule 26); the per-outcome contingent shift — one rung more support after a failure, one less after a success (Wood & Middleton 1975 [V]) — is the within-problem variant. A parent may deviate with a logged reason; the audit is the contingency share (rung shifts that tracked the last outcome). Two orderings: domain-general habits *state first, probe second*; gym content *attempt first, then teach from the attempts* — productive failure gives g = 0.36 over 53 studies and reverses for domain-general skills (Sinha & Kapur 2021 [V]).

20. **Decision.** Default Q ("what are your options, and which do you pick?"). D once per stage, on a past or unscored problem: candidates with a likelihood each, then a pick (Zhang et al. 2025 [V]). R as mindware on a shelf, one of at least two, marked opinion (Stanovich 2011 [V rec]). Default: the pick is not confirmed (social validation, Schultze et al. 2015 [V]); after a failure, "the other way you almost started?" (Wang & Zhou 2024 [V]).
21. **Association.** Default Q with two incident ids in one sentence; W: the child's earlier line quoted beside the present one (Richland, Zur & Holyoak 2007 [V rec]). S once, as opinion, after two windows of surface matches. Default: a fresh pair each time — a repeated pair is logged and counted; late, the child picks pairs.
22. **Goal.** Stated plainly once at birth. Q for the episode: "what would tell you this is done?"; R: a finish line *beside* another, one of at least two. The life goal: **open decision for Rohin** (IDEAS 1518 item 7) — default, the child sees only its own per-problem outcomes, which it already has; no parent-computed learning-progress table reaches it (CANON §2a). If ruled otherwise, record the exception in CANON §2a as per-family solved counts the child could compute itself.
23. **Building and workspace.** S once: "if it is not written, it does not exist for your next step". W once per stage: a carry-forward turn on the child's own past turn, short, at its grain (Li et al. 2025 [V]); any pattern labels the *purposes* of steps and omits the steps (Catrambone 1998 [V]). Q thereafter: "which earlier thought does this one stand on?"
24. **Rethinking, pruning — and how a recipe is offered.** Drop-or-defend after three failures stays; the child's "this was wrong" beside the retry is the row that trains (Setlur et al. 2024 [V]; §5 Q3). *Gym recipes* are offered at their *time for telling*, after two distinct failed approaches on that family (Schwartz & Bransford 1998 [V]); *thinking recipes* may be stated once in preparation (D/W), thereafter offered after failures. Every offer: one of at least two options, the parent's opinion, with scope, confidence, reason and refutation condition (§2), not verbatim twice, never required, never graded for compliance, saying what it leaves out (Bonawitz et al. 2011 [V]). *Dependence:* the writer computes, per gym and family, briefed-problem outcome rate minus parent-free gate-exam outcome rate (CHILD_MECHANISM §3.2) and posts only a trend word — rising / flat / falling — to the parental ledger; no recipe is re-offered while its trend reads rising (Bastani et al. 2025 [V]: crutch users scored 17 % worse once the helper was removed). Gate-exam-derived, so it needs a CANON §4 line. *Echo:* a recipe reproduced unchanged across four problems is withdrawn and the second way offered; the echo is counted (V14 echo rate). *Reaching the weights:* only through the child's own restatement with its outcome row (the canonical sentence "on programs like X the order that works is …"); CANON §1's verbatim-line refusal would drop an adopted recipe written as an `ACT:` line, so Codex is asked whether that rule needs a length threshold or an `ACT:` exemption after a logged test.
25. **Perception.** Default Q ("look again — what else? what does this connect to?") on the child's own last situation. D allowed: a short sorting module on past or unscored problems (Kellman, Massey & Son 2010 [V rec]), easy family first; a *dimension* may be named once; a live instance's feature falls under the Absolute. Default: echo is answered by changing the situation; a "notice more" remark is logged as mode S and counted.
26. **One move per nudge; fade.** One move per brief or `[PARENT]` line, chosen from the frontier estimate, never the score (cognitive-load rationale: Sweller 1988 [V survey]). The `[PARENT]` line carries one move or one question only. Fade to L0 after two unprompted windows; add one rung of support with a *changed incident* if the parent-free rate falls below half the briefed rate; over-scaffolding is flagged like a leak.
27. **Lengthening and diversity.** Structured lengthening left alone; echo answered by changing the problem. Default: length is not the lever (Wu et al. 2025 [V]) — the harness nudges to act after four silent turns (CHILD_MECHANISM §1.3); a length remark is logged as mode S and counted. Diversity burns first (Cui et al. 2025 [V]): keep tails alive with the second way and another recipe on another day (Ho et al. 2022 [V]).
28. **Own plan first, advice second — in every brief** (§2); the brief requests the order, so the order rate is the measurement.
29. **Every suggestion in a brief carries scope, confidence, reason, refutation; the parent closes the loop on its own record** (§2). Not required of the one-line `[PARENT]` slot.
30. **The weighing question, never graded; parents disagreeing as a mode; the rare fallible offer (Stages 3–4); the contentless challenge as a rare, logged probe (mode P)** — the probe is what §2(5) reads; without it, (5) is experimenter-only.
31. **Mode tag, rotation and audit.** Every brief line and `[PARENT]` line is written to the parental ledger with a mode letter (Q/S/D/W/R/E/P) and the move it targets; never shown to the child; the verifier checks the tag. Rules 31–32 and §2's audits are undefined until the tag exists. Default rotation: no two consecutive briefs on one lesson in the same mode; each spaced return in a new mode (Schmidt & Bjork 1992 [V]). Named exception: the P-series pretest may hold one clone at same-mode-twice for one lesson, logged (Q5). Ceiling, proposed: no mode above 0.5 of tagged lines over four briefs, because tutors drift toward telling (Roscoe & Chi 2007; Macina et al. 2023 [V]); the verifier (Wang et al. 2024 [V]) flags briefs that merely agree with the child's self-account (Panickssery et al. 2024 [V]).
32. **Every mode is judged in the parent's absence**, on a changed gym, two sleeps later, split by mode, by what the child *did* with it (Chi & Wylie 2014 [V]).

Revised rule count: **13** (rules 20–32).

**Rule → instrument index** (detector; status under CHILD_MECHANISM §5). 20: option count before `act`, proposed; replay of the rejected option, compiler gym only. 21: id pairs (V8, exists). 22: finish line (V2, exists). 23: back-reference with dependency (V14 5, exists). 24: "wrong" row beside next `act` (V11, exists); dependence trend, writer, proposed; echo rate (V14 4, exists). 25: noticing novelty (V14 9, exists). 26: unprompted rate per move (V14 6, exists); contingency share, proposed. 27: length vs dependency, nudges (5.10), exist. 28: plan-before-advice order, hand rubric, proposed. 29: four-part check, verifier, proposed. 30: §2 (1)–(5), proposed; (5) needs mode P. 31: mode share from the tag, proposed. 32: parent-free rates by mode at +2 sleeps, exists once tagged. Hand rubrics: 50 turns, precision ≥ 0.8 before any count. *Template set:* the Q, S, R and P lines in §1–§3; a full mode × move sheet (each line past the leak and four-part rules) belongs in the parent prompt file, read by the verifier.

## 4. The order in which constituents develop, and the order to follow

**What the literature says.** Trust in the knowledgeable is there at three; accuracy is used coarsely at three (one error disqualifies) and as relative frequency at four (Koenig & Harris 2005; Pasquini et al. 2007 [V]); calibration use is adult-only (Tenney et al. 2011 [V]). A compiled procedure runs without its reasons (Anderson 1982 [V rec]). Higher functions are internalised from interaction (Vygotsky 1978 [V rec]); a lesson before its structure is ready is brittle (Piaget 1952 [V rec]). In models: format first, freedom second (Chu et al. 2025 [V]); priming reasoning behaviours *before* outcome training lets gains happen (Gandhi et al. 2025 [V]).

**Therefore.** *Preparation (Stage 0) — PROPOSED AMENDMENT to CANON §2d ("format plus six moves on easy material").* Proposed: (i) the interface, by demonstration; (ii) alerting and persistence (V1); (iii) perceive, easiest family; (iv) decision and the episode goal, stated then asked; (v) predict-then-check with a stated confidence (Lin, Hilton & Evans 2022 [V]). Exit under the amendment: perceive, decision, goal and predict-check each unprompted in ≥ 3 of the last 8 problems, cap 160; association, building and pruning move to Stages 1–4 under the same criterion. Open because of Q7: on Piaget's view the later moves cannot be drilled before their prerequisites; on Vygotsky's, scaffolding creates them and six moves is right. Needs a CANON §4 line; **default if silent: the six-move Stage 0 stands** and P3 is unchanged. *Early classroom (Stages 1–2):* causal — acts as interventions, one change-one-thing demonstration on a past incident, created confounds; pruning once three-fold failures exist; building as plans span turns. *Middle (Stages 3–4):* association by paired incidents; weighing once the parent has a track record the child can name — first recipes, the fallible offer and the P probe belong here; calibration feedback (A/B) once confidences are stated. *Late (Stages 5–7):* life goals (rule 22, open); the appraisal gym; negative knowledge (Minsky 1986 [V rec]); Occam last. A lesson that fails twice means a missing prerequisite — add one rung of support (toward L4) and offer a situation to act on, not the same explanation again.

## 5. Open questions, and what a pretest could settle

1. **Does a worked pattern on the child's own turn transfer at rank 32 through one sleep?** (Evidence at 32B and 3B: Li et al. 2025; Gandhi et al. 2025 [V].) Pretest: W vs Q across clones, parent-free rate after the next sleep.
2. **Is a Qwen2.5-7B gain prior amplification?** Random rewards gave +21.4 points (Shao et al. 2025 [V]). Pretest: a random-score control.
3. **Do self-annotated failures behave like per-step negatives** (Setlur et al. 2024 [V])? Theirs carried per-step advantage estimates; ours a written binary verdict. Pretest: the polarity tag.
4. **Is sycophancy toward a parent measurable?** "Are you sure?" bare (mode P) vs argued, adapter ON and OFF (Laban et al. 2023 [V]).
5. **Does mode rotation lower echo and raise parent-free retention?** (Schmidt & Bjork 1992 [V].) Pretest: one clone at same-mode-twice for one lesson (rule 31's exception) vs two modes, at +2 sleeps.
6. **Is calibration feedback metric-teaching or a transferable mode** (Carpenter et al. 2019 [V])? A/B on Brier resolution.
7. **Piaget or Vygotsky?** Uptake gated by prerequisite competence or created by scaffolding (T, note 4) — why the Stage 0 amendment is open.
8. Expected size: human tutoring is d = 0.79 (VanLehn 2011 [V]); here, fractions of a noise band per stage — a run that ends before the score moves still has a result if the structure and weighing readings moved and held.
