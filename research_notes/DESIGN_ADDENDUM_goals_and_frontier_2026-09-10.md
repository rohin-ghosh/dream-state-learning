# Design addendum (to fold into NEXT_EXPERIMENT_DESIGN_v1): goals, planning vs free-flow thought, and frontier estimation — 2026-09-10

## Rohin's insight: the hardest part of teaching-to-learn is planning
Self-cognition is hard, but planning is harder, because even self-cognition should be planned yet aware. The agent needs two kinds of thought: planning thoughts (directed) and free-flow thoughts (undirected, building up toward plans), with self-corrections in between ("I've thought for 5,000 tokens — am I thinking right? do I need a goal here?" → goal creation). Goals exist in layers — state-level, episode-level, lifetime — and all of them must be learned, not hard-coded. Picking goals is fundamentally hard, so we parent it for our situation (learning during the gym): lifetime, episode, then state-level goals. During parenting the child thinks enormously; in the gym it still thinks far more than a normal agent, because learning only comes from thinking; the hard parts are mostly learned during parenting.

## The reference: Pappalardo, ICLR 2026, "Unsupervised Learning of Efficient Exploration: Pre-training Adaptive Policies via Self-Imposed Goals" (ULEE)
Mechanism: a goal-search policy proposes goals; a difficulty predictor (the judge) estimates POST-ADAPTATION performance (can the agent solve this after a few episodes to adapt), not immediate success; the goal-search policy is rewarded for goals the judge rates appropriately difficult, so it hunts the frontier, which moves as the agent improves — a self-maintaining curriculum. Neighbours: zone of proximal development; Portelas-style teacher–student curricula; PAIRED adversarial environment generation. Also a precedent: single independent author accepted as a full ICLR paper.

## What it changes in our design
1. **The parent's core organ is a frontier estimate: "what can the student almost do, after working through it".** Post-adaptation, not immediate — the same two-horizon distinction we already use (immediate post-lesson effect vs after-sleep effect). Fork: (a) an explicit judge (a predictor of post-adaptation success; a logged scalar; another component to train) or (b) infer the frontier from the child's shared reflections through the discussion channel (free, weaker, makes parent–child communication load-bearing, and in the spirit of "intelligence lives in the loop"). Start with (b) as the agentic parent's job; add (a) only if (b) is demonstrably too noisy. Either way, log the parent's frontier estimate per lesson so it can be audited against outcomes.
2. **Do not fix the curriculum order upfront.** Difficulty is estimated, not assumed: the parent re-ranks the next task as it observes the child (adaptive parenting is already an arm — this is its content).
3. **Design against frontier collapse** (the two failure modes: a conservative parent that teaches nothing; a difficulty-chasing parent where nothing lands). Guard: a stability rule with logged evidence — the parent must state, per lesson, its frontier estimate and the outcome; a run in which lesson difficulty drifts monotonically for k lessons without post-adaptation gains triggers a curriculum reset. Ask Pappalardo directly what kept ULEE stable.
4. **Goal layers as taught content (curriculum stage 6, sharpened).** Teach state goals (what this state is for; when it ends with a REVIEW), episode goals (what to achieve on this situation and when to stop pushing), lifetime goals (what to learn across situations). Markers exist for states; add goal statements as NOTE content, never as hard-coded harness goals.
5. **Planning vs free-flow thought as a measured pair.** Instruments: fraction of chunks that are directed (plan/act) vs free-flow (no marker, exploratory), self-correction events ("am I thinking right", "do I need a goal"), goal-creation events per situation, and whether self-set goals pay off later (persistence of a goal across states and its outcome). Persistence gyms are where planned-but-aware thinking is forced.
6. **Thinking budget.** The winning design kept the per-situation budget unchanged for comparability. Rohin wants far more thinking. Resolution to fold: raise the budget equally for all arms (D and B alike) so comparability holds, and add "thinking budget" as an explicit decision point with a pilot cell (budget 16 vs 64) before the main run; never compare arms at different budgets.
