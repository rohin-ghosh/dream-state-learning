Your split is right, and H2 is the actual paper. Two things to tighten before you write it.

H1 is less answered than you're giving it credit for. TMEM shows fast LoRA weights help within a single episode on retrieval-flavored benchmarks. Physics of LMs shows facts get stored and become extractable when paraphrases are diverse. Neither shows that a procedural skill, like self-evaluation or metacognitive pacing, survives a sleep consolidation and still gets used once it's out of context. That's a smaller claim than H2, but prior work doesn't cover it. H2 also can't stand without it: if the skill isn't retained, there's nothing to kickstart. It's the same logic as your finding that parenting is a precondition. So I'd frame H1 as a necessary condition you verify yourself, with TMEM and Allen-Zhu as the reason to expect it holds, not as the evidence.

"Kickstart self-learning" needs one operational meaning. The confound is that a parented agent might just start higher and then learn at the same rate. That would be an H1 result dressed up as H2. H2 has to be about slope: with the teacher gone, on a gym the teaching never touched, the parented agent climbs faster from its own experience. The clean test is a 2×2 of parented vs. not and sleep loop running vs. frozen at deployment. H1 is the parenting effect with sleep frozen. H2 is the interaction: parenting pays off more when the agent keeps consolidating. If parented+frozen matches parented+sleep, the skills are good but they aren't driving learning.

A rough skeleton for you to put in your own words: H1 says skills taught through think-then-sleep are retained in the LoRA and expressed outside the context where they were taught. H2 says an agent carrying those skills improves faster from self-generated experience on an unseen verifiable task than the same model without them, and the gap depends on continued consolidation.
