## 0. One screen

**Headline.** Teach persistence-by-changing first and knowing-when-done beside it; state every value plainly and early but never a recipe; and judge every lesson only on a delayed, parent-absent, changed-gym readout — because the two robust findings across all six literatures are that the learner's own constructive, retrieved words are the active ingredient (which is the empirical case for the thinker/compiler line), and that whatever is measured right after teaching (echo, compliance, fluent reflection) is performance, not learning.

**The values we teach, in order** (order of emphasis in the curriculum; 9–11 and 13 are standing rules from the first sleep):

1. **Persist by changing.** When an attempt does not help, make a different attempt; never stop early and never repeat the same move.
2. **Know when a goal is done.** Before you start, say what "done" looks like; stop when you reach it or when trying has stopped moving anything, and say why.
3. **Predict, then check.** Before acting, say what you expect and how sure you are; afterwards compare it with what actually happened.
4. **Treat surprise as evidence.** When the world disagrees with your prediction, write down what would have to be true instead, and test it now.
5. **Ask what to do next, and decide.** Keep asking "think more, plan, act, or stop?" and let each answer change what you do; spend thought where it still pays.
6. **Keep one different way alive.** When your usual approach has repeated, try one deliberate alternative and compare the two.
7. **Read the situation and scope the rule.** Name the feature of the situation that picks the approach; every lesson says when it holds and when it fails.
8. **Compare and connect.** Put this case beside a named earlier one, say what is the same and what differs, then write your own rule.
9. **Think on past incidents.** In private review, name specific episodes, what went wrong or right, and the rule for the next case.
10. **Say it in your own words, from memory.** A lesson counts only when you can restate it without the note in front of you and use it on today's case; copying is not learning.
11. **A reflection counts only if it changed what you did.** Judge what you learned later and by results, never by how it felt while the lesson was in view.
12. **Choose your own goals where you are learning fastest.** In free time, pick work that is neither already mastered nor hopeless.
13. **Free thought, protected.** Private thinking time that the parent reads but never grades.
14. **Thinking is a set of moves that are taught** (added 2026-09-11, Rohin's ruling; folded by Fable's agent). Decide, associate, set a goal, build on your last idea, prune what has not paid off — five moves the parent asks for by question, one per nudge, from the first session to the last, and never performs for you; owned when you make them unasked.

*Added 2026-09-11:* V14 is the taught half of what V13 protects — free thought is grown, not switched on — and it is read on a thought-structure measure (references to earlier thoughts, plans spanning episodes, distinct moves per episode, echo) that is expected to move before the score does and is never shown to the child (V14; rules 20–26; pitfalls "thinking as performance" and "lengthening without dependency").

**Five things the science forces regardless of order.**
- Domain-general habits (persist, predict, scope, compare) are learned from explicit statement plus a probe that forces their use, not from discovery: unassisted discovery loses to explicit instruction by d = −0.38 across 164 studies (Alfieri et al. 2011 [verified]); problem-solving-first beats instruction-first only for domain content, and reverses for domain-general skills (Sinha & Kapur 2021 [verified]). So: values stated at birth, recipes never.
- The parent's success signal must be the window after the next sleep, a changed gym, and the disjoint panel — never the next episode. Immediate performance and long-term learning routinely move in opposite directions (Soderstrom & Bjork 2015 [verified]); our first-note echo after a brief (0.454 vs 0.354 for unbriefed controls) is that illusion in the ledger.
- Only the child's own retrieved, condition-stating rendering may enter the corpus. Retrieval beats re-reading at a delay although re-reading feels better (Karpicke & Roediger 2008 [verified]); good learners explain the conditions of an action, poor ones paraphrase (Chi et al. 1989 [verified]); text distilled from another model teaches style, not capability, and forgets more (Gudibande et al. 2024; Shenfeld et al. 2025 [verified]).
- Expect small effects. Human tutoring adds about 0.8 SD, not Bloom's 2 SD (VanLehn 2011 [verified]); a full Montessori education adds 0.24–0.33 SD (Randolph et al. 2023 [verified]). Here that is fractions of a noise band per stage (+0.02 to +0.05), which is what we measure.
- Sleep must select, renormalise and forget. Brains replay the novel, the rare, the rewarded and the goal-relevant more than the familiar (Gupta et al. 2010 [verified]), scale synapses down after learning (de Vivo et al. 2017 [verified]), and erase actively (Izawa et al. 2019 [verified]). Our current sleep — success-filtered rows, cumulative, no down-weighting — does the reverse and predicts the measured lock-in and collapse.

**Glossary.** Brier score: the average squared gap between a stated probability and what happened (0 perfect, 0.25 is coin-flipping on yes/no events). Jaccard overlap: the share of words two notes have in common (our templated notes sit at 0.62 adapter-ON vs 0.105 OFF). Entropy of first moves: how spread out the child's opening actions are; a single fixed opening has entropy zero. JS shift: a 0–1 distance between the distribution of first moves in two windows (0 identical). Adapter: the small trainable LoRA part rewritten at sleep. Modal recipe / birth recipe: the one fixed action sequence the child fell into (scores 0.4878 alone, identical to the adapter plateau). Pass@k: the chance that at least one of k tries succeeds. ZPD (zone of proximal development): the band of tasks a learner can do only with help. Learning progress: how fast a score is improving, not how high it is. Extinction: the fading of a behaviour once it stops being rewarded.

### V1. Persist by changing

- **Why (science).** Shaping: reinforce successive approximations against a criterion computed from the learner's own recent behaviour, never an absolute threshold (Skinner 1937/1953 via Peterson 2004; Galbicka 1994 percentile schedules [verified]). Persistence under non-reward is built by an intermittent, contingent reinforcement history — continuous reinforcement extinguishes fastest when it stops (Humphreys 1939 [verified record; magnitude recalled]; Nevin & Grace 2000 behavioural momentum [verified]) — so a test with no parent is, operationally, an extinction probe. Rewarding effort itself makes effortful persistence transfer to unrelated tasks (Eisenberger 1992 learned industriousness [verified]). Passivity under uncontrollable outcomes is the default, not learned; prior experience of control immunises (Seligman & Maier 1967; Maier & Seligman 2016 [verified]). Persistence phrases are not the target: trait grit is mostly conscientiousness and only its perseverance-of-effort facet predicts anything (Credé et al. 2017, 66,807 people [verified; correlation values recalled]). Dewey: accepting the first suggestion is "uncritical thinking, the minimum of reflection" [verified]. Reflexion's trigger rule — same action, same result, three times → stop, name what failed, change something — is the published version, worth +22 points on ALFWorld [verified]. Our data: late-life collapse to 1–2 chunks per situation against a base of 13 in 4/9 ungated lives.
- **How the parent teaches it.** Brief: the value stated plainly at birth and one sentence-form repeated at every childhood wake ("when an attempt does not move the score, make a DIFFERENT attempt and write what you changed"), plus one named episode from the ledger that met the moving criterion ("in episode 17 you changed the pass after the count rose — that is the thing"). Credit the effortful act, never the score, never a short episode that scored. Schedule: continuous credit in the first sessions of a new gym, then credit a random half of the criterial episodes, then a third, before the first parent-absent test; never remove the parent right after a continuous-credit session. Controllable gyms (every changed attempt changes the feedback) before anything compiler-like — immunisation precedes exposure. When a stretch is objectively non-contingent (computable from the ledger), say so and set a bounded goal rather than demand more attempts. Private time: the child's own account of why it stopped — read, never commented on. Test without a parent: deployment wakes with the brief absent; exit = changed attempts after a non-improving outcome ≥ 2 per situation, chunks per situation ≥ 0.7 × frozen median, no brevity catches for 4 cycles, and the behaviour held for two windows after the last brief on it.
- **Gym.** TextWorld cooking L1–5 and treasure hunter (C1): dense exact score, dead ends and locked doors, quitting cannot score, published 7B numbers to band against (Qwen2.5-7B 27.7 vs Claude-3.7 97.3 on TALES). Minesweeper (C2) as the second gym with a monotone payoff over 25–64 turns. (In-house LockGym stays as the descriptor-free alternate; its training tier is Mastermind c=4,n=6, which a frozen 7B opens in about 4% of games — band it first.)
- **Ledger instrument.** From act rows per situation: attempts after a non-improving outcome; perseverance fraction (share of those whose action string differs from the previous one — identical repeats reported separately as perseveration); chunks per situation over budget, normalised by the frozen median; extinction curve = attempts per situation over the first N parent-free episodes vs the last N parented ones; response–outcome contingency = P(score change | changed attempt) − P(score change | repeated attempt); helplessness onset = attempts falling below half the median of the preceding eight windows within two windows after a non-contingent run. Validity: P(improve | changed attempt) must beat a random-action reference on the same instances.
- **How the instrument fails.** Cycling recipes without reading outcomes, or filling the budget with idle repetition (late-life 15.4–16.0 chunks at budget 16), satisfies "changed attempts"; persistence words satisfy a phrase detector; costly perseverance on unsolvable problems is real (Lucas et al. 2015 [record verified, result unverified]) so V1 is never scored without V2; under the binding 16-chunk budget stopping is censored — measure on the 64-budget pilot.

### V2. Know when a goal is done

- **Why.** Satisficing stops when an aspiration set in advance is met (Simon 1956 [verified record]); in optimal-stopping experiments a simple rule — count successive non-improvements — performs nearly optimally (Seale & Rapoport 1997 [secondary]); effort past the point where gains flatten is "labour in vain" (Nelson & Leonesio 1988 [record only]); disengaging from unattainable goals and re-engaging elsewhere both matter (Wrosch et al. 2003, three studies N = 115/120/45 [verified]); if–then plans help people quit failing courses of action (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, d = 0.65 over 94 tests [verified]). Deliberation is an action with a cost; compute only while the expected gain exceeds it (Russell & Wefald 1991; Lieder & Griffiths 2020 [verified]). A goal is usable in RL only with a checkable done-test (Florensa et al. 2018 [verified]); Voyager's self-verifier — "done → move on / not done → retry" — was its single most important part (−73% items discovered without it [verified]). Specific, difficult goals beat "do your best" (Locke & Latham 2002 [verified; the recalled d ≈ 0.4–0.8 was not re-read → overstated]). Our data: the budget does the stopping (15.4–16.0 chunks at budget 16 in most lives; 1.0–6.6 in the collapsed ones), not judgement.
- **How.** Brief at wake: "what would tell you this program is done?"; the repeated line: "name the finish line before the first action; at review say met / not met / abandoned, and why"; the parent never proposes the number. Goals in layers (state → episode → lifetime) are note content the child writes; lifetime goals live in private time and are read only. Paired with V1 from the start so persistence does not become perseveration. Test without a parent: does the declared "done" coincide with where the child actually stops changing actions; declared done vs the verifier's done.
- **Gym.** TextWorld (engine won/lost flags and max_score give an exact "done"); Minesweeper (board solved); ScienceWorld (T1) later for episode-level goal layers over 30 task families with a 0–100 subgoal score — if it is promoted to a classroom (section 6).
- **Instrument.** Aspiration statements in the first state (a target number or checkable condition before the first action), normalised to the instance's reference ceiling; over-stopping = declared done before the best result with later gains foregone (from the same instance's reference); labour-in-vain = actions after the best with no gain; aspiration-met rate; stop-reason audit = the stated reason calibrated against a counterfactual continuation (run k more chunks the child does not know about; did the score move?); done-test precision and recall against the verifier. Validity: the stated aspiration must predict actual stopping and track instance headroom, else it is decoration.
- **Fails.** Trivially low targets always met (normalise to ceiling; flag below the frozen mean); targets written after the best (require goal chunk before the first action); targets copied from the brief (token overlap < 0.5 required); the budget masquerading as judgement (uncensored only on the 64-budget pilot).

### V3. Predict, then check

- **Why.** Learning is driven by prediction error, not reward (Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997 [verified]); novelty-driven dopamine gates entry into long-term memory (Lisman & Grace 2005 [verified]) — flat predictions mean no signal, which is our "flat predictions" ritual flag. Improving monitoring accuracy causally improves regulation and learning (Thiede et al. 2003 [verified]); judgements of learning made after a delay are far more accurate than immediate ones (Nelson & Dunlosky 1991 [verified; the recalled gammas ≈ .90 vs .38 not re-read]). Language models cannot reliably correct their own reasoning without external feedback and sometimes get worse (Huang et al. 2023; Kamoi et al. 2024 [verified]); self-critique collapses performance on planning tasks while a sound external verifier gives large gains (Stechly et al. 2024 [verified]); they can fix errors once told where (Tyen et al. 2024 [verified]). Process supervision catches right-answer-wrong-process: reasoning errors among correct answers 14.0% → 3.4% (Uesato et al. 2022 [verified]); a process reward model solved 78% of a MATH subset (Lightman et al. 2023 [verified]). Dewey: experience is trying plus undergoing; "mere activity does not constitute experience" [verified] — our late-life children stopped echoing outcomes (seed 6: 27 of 32 windows with no outcome echo, SEQ-011). Forecast scoring: Brier score and its calibration/discrimination split (Murphy 1973); sensitivity must be measured apart from bias (Fleming & Lau 2014; Maniscalco & Lau 2012 [verified]). Quiet-STaR: thinking pays where prediction is hard [verified].
- **How.** Stage-2 lesson: "before you act, say what you expect and how sure; a check is two numbers — what you expected and what came back — and one sentence on the gap." The parent asks "what feature made you expect that?" about one badly predicted and one well predicted state, withholds the answer, may say WHERE the reasoning broke (a state index) but never what is right; rejects "looks good" with "checked against what?"; never shows the child its Brier (metric-teaching is flagged by the verifier prompt). Repeat until grounded checks appear unprompted; the child judges whether a lesson took only at the next wake with the brief out of view. Private time: re-derive the best episode backward (does the outcome imply the premise?). Test without a parent: a sealed panel with a different board size / mine density.
- **Gym.** Minesweeper (C2): PREDICT P(mine) before each reveal, scored 1 − Brier against the hidden grid — the rule-game instrument transfers verbatim. Alchemy (C3): predict the stone's transformation before each potion, exact hit rate. (In-house rule game v2 alternate.)
- **Instrument.** Brier with the Murphy partition (reliability = calibration, resolution = discrimination, uncertainty = base rate) — resolution must rise, not just reliability; prediction SD across items ≥ 0.05; AUROC / gamma of confidence vs correctness; meta-d′ where a yes/no decision exists; SD(surprise) per window; grounded-verification rate = sentences that reference a prior prediction and contain a number matching an act-row outcome, vs ungrounded claims ("verified", "correct" with no number) and a fabrication count (quoted numbers matching no act row); prediction-error resolution rate = surprises > 0.02 followed within the situation by a note naming a cause AND a changed action; outcome-connection rate ≥ 0.8 (notes that reference both the outcome and the prediction that preceded it).
- **Fails.** Hedging to the base rate lowers Brier while learning nothing (watch resolution); predictions gamed to zero error by predicting what the routine always yields (require SD across programs); verification theatre — the harness echoes outcomes, so mentioning them is free; only conditioning the next action on them counts; the hard–easy effect makes calibration look better on easy panels — band against the frozen model on the same panel; post-selection when gate and report share a panel.

### V4. Treat surprise as evidence

- **Why.** Peirce: inquiry begins with "the irritation of doubt" and ends when doubt ends; abduction — "the surprising fact C is observed; but if A were true, C would be a matter of course; hence there is reason to suspect A" — yields a candidate for testing, not a conclusion [verified; that it is "the only inference that introduces a new idea" is Peirce's own claim, contested by Frankfurt]. Curiosity is an information gap (Loewenstein 1994 [verified]); surprising answers are remembered better 1–2 weeks later (Kang et al. 2009 [verified]); curiosity states enhance memory even for incidental material (Gruber et al. 2014 [verified]). A memory can be rewritten only when reactivated with a prediction error; reactivation without error re-strengthens it (Sevenster et al. 2013 [verified]; the human update-by-extinction results are fragile — Kindt & Soeter 2013; Chalkia et al. 2020 [verified]). Sleep produces insight into a hidden rule only when prior practice laid down implicit traces: 59.1% vs 22.5% insight after sleep vs wake (Wagner et al. 2004 [verified]; Yordanova et al. 2008 [verified]). Popper: corroboration counts only from risky predictions; rescuing a theory by ad hoc adjustment is "reinforced dogmatism"; yet a single counter-instance is rarely enough to falsify [secondary via SEP]. After an error, slowing down is not the target — improving is; the two are not necessarily correlated (Danielmeier & Ullsperger 2011; Notebaert et al. 2009 orienting account [verified]). Productive failure works when instruction builds on the learner's own attempts: g = 0.36 [0.20, 0.51] over 53 studies for recipes and content, reversed for domain-general skills (Sinha & Kapur 2021 [verified]).
- **How.** Brief quotes the child's own surprising C ("you predicted 0.7 and it failed — what would have to be true for that to be unsurprising?") and asks for the A and for the action that would test it; never supplies A. Times the brief to the sleep after the routine's largest failures and stays silent about the routine after a good day (reconsolidation timing, tested by A/B across clones, not asserted). Asks for the refutation condition of every recipe ("what result would make you drop pass-order P on this family?") and treats "this program was unusual" as a flag, not a reprimand. Prepares insight rather than telling it: mixed families where the routine fails on a known fraction, one question at the boundary ("what differs between the programs where it worked and where it failed?"), the rule withheld, tested after sleep with no parent. Private time: hypothesis generation; open surprises are left unresolved at the boundary. Test without a parent: does a surprise still produce a hypothesis and a discriminating action?
- **Gym.** Alchemy (C3): the same potion has a different effect under a hidden chemistry; the harness reads the true chemistry for exact scoring and ships an ideal observer as the ceiling. XLand-MiniGrid (C4): hidden rules inferred across trials — adopted only if the frozen-model band clears the A0 check. Minesweeper: forced guesses vs deductions.
- **Instrument.** Surprise-to-hypothesis conversion: for each prediction miss beyond the gym's noise band, does the next note state a candidate explanation, and does an action within the situation test it? Refutation-condition rate on stated rules; rescue ratio = after a refuting outcome, share of next notes that narrow scope / explain away vs change the action; post-error improvement (PIA) = P(next outcome improves | previous did not) − P(next improves | previous did); post-error slowing without improvement flagged "orienting only"; surprise follow-up rate (a plan change within the episode); corpus share of surprise-tagged episodes; brief-timing lag (episodes between the routine's last large negative surprise and the brief).
- **Fails.** Manufactured doubt — "I am surprised by everything" is Peirce's paper doubt (weight by the size of the miss); storytelling with no test (count only hypotheses followed by an action that could refute them); over-falsification on one noisy failure — same-adapter replicates swing ±0.02–0.045, so the refutation threshold sits above the noise band and requires repeated failure; the self-evaluation regex counts orienting words ("unexpected!") — precision audit before use; a strict success filter throws the informative failures out of the corpus.

### V5. Ask what to do next, and decide

- **Why.** Metacognition is a monitoring-and-control loop; knowledge without control changes nothing (Flavell 1979; Nelson & Narens 1990; Veenman et al. 2006 [verified]). Training self-regulated learning gives ES 0.69 across 84 studies (Dignath & Büttner 2008 [verified]) and the gains hold at follow-up (de Boer et al. 2018, g 0.50 → 0.63 [verified]). Teaching learners to generate their own questions: median ES 0.36 on standardised and 0.86 on experimenter tests, all on new material (Rosenshine et al. 1996 [verified]); connection stems anchored in the learner's own experience work best (King 1994 [verified]). Cognitive effort is avoided by default (Kool et al. 2010, six experiments; Westbrook et al. 2013 [verified]); good learners allocate study just beyond current mastery and stop when the learning rate flattens (Metcalfe 2009; Metcalfe & Kornell 2005 [verified; stop rule recalled]). Thought length vs accuracy is an inverted U — optimal length rises with difficulty and falls with capability, and RL drifts toward shorter chains (Wu et al. 2025 [verified]); agentic overthinking has three modes including premature disengagement, our one-action collapse under another name (Cuadron et al. 2025, 4,018 trajectories [verified]); appending "Wait" when the model tries to stop lifted AIME24 from 50% to 57% (s1 [verified]); models "think too fast" in open-ended exploration (Pan et al. 2025 [verified]); more thinking is not monotone (Ghosal et al. 2025 [verified]). Self-reports of metacognition correlate poorly with online behaviour (Veenman et al. 2006 [verified record; correlation recalled]); describing or explaining one's thinking changes performance and the text is a report, not the process (Fox, Ericsson & Best 2011, 94 studies [verified]).
- **How.** Teach the regulatory checklist once as brief content (plan / monitor / evaluate, with a prediction and a check at each state); model three or four question stems ("should I think more here? what if…? why did that…? do I need a goal?"), then fade. The brief asks "what did you decide when you asked that?" and names the tail the child showed ("you stopped after one action on 7 of 32 programs" / "you spent the full budget on 9 programs with no change of plan") and asks what it will do at that moment next time; teaches the s1 reflex in the child's words — after a non-improving outcome the next word is "Wait", not the end. Judges thought volume relative to instance difficulty, never raw. Private time: the child re-reads its worst-predicted states and writes what it should have expected. Test without a parent: the question → answer → act chain persists in deployment wakes; allocation is uncensored only on the 64-budget pilot.
- **Gym.** TextWorld higher levels (L6–10) and, if promoted, ScienceWorld: long horizons where re-planning at checkpoints is required. Minesweeper: the forced-guess vs deduce decision at every turn.
- **Instrument.** Question rows (child sentences ending "?" or opening with should / what if / why / how / do I need / am I; parent text excluded): density per 1k tokens by type; answered fraction (a following sentence within two chunks references the question's key noun); acted fraction (an action change or plan revision within the state after the answer); chain rate Q → A → act; allocation adaptivity = across situations in a window, the correlation between chunks spent and instance headroom (reference ceiling minus first-action score): positive is right, ~0 is budget-filling, negative is quitting where gains exist; labour-in-vain fraction; premature-disengagement flag = episode ends with > 50% budget unused and score below the fixed-recipe reference; slope of chunks vs per-program difficulty; chunk length before an action vs that action's later prediction error (should be positive).
- **Fails.** Question spam: density rises, answered fraction zero — density alone is pure Goodhart, never a gate, never shown to the child; metric-teaching briefs ("ask more questions") are rejected by the verifier; templated question sets (Jaccard ≥ 0.5 across situations) count as ritual; at budget 16 every late situation sits at 15–16 chunks and the allocation signal is censored; "performing reflection" (SEQ-003/005: reflective phrases up while efficiency fell).

### V6. Keep one different way alive

- **Why.** Recipe lock-in (modal first action 0.90/0.86 adapter-ON vs 0.50 OFF; birth recipe 0.4878 = the adapter plateau) is the Einstellung effect: the first solution triggered by familiar features blocks a better one, and experts who say they are looking for a better one keep their eyes on the old one (Bilalić, McLeod & Gobet 2008 [verified]; Luchins 1942 [record verified; percentages recalled]). It is also a habit insensitive to outcome devaluation (Dickinson 1985 [verified]); the brain arbitrates habit vs deliberation by relative uncertainty (Daw, Niv & Dayan 2005 [verified]); demonstrating habits in humans is hard (de Wit et al. 2018, five failed attempts; Molinero et al. 2025 [verified]). Variability is itself reinforceable: reward a sequence only if it differs from the last N and variability rises; only high-variability animals learned a hard new sequence (Page & Neuringer 1985; Grunow & Neuringer 2002 [verified]). Competent learners keep several strategies alive for long periods and a new strategy shows no benefit at first (Lemaire & Siegler 1995; Shrager & Siegler 1998; Miller & Seier 1994 [verified]). Childhood is a high-temperature search annealed later; demonstration by a knowledgeable adult narrows exploration ("pedagogy promotes efficient learning but at a cost" — Bonawitz et al. 2011; Gopnik 2020, 2017 [verified]). In LLM training the same collapse appears as the echo trap — reward variance and entropy fall before the mean does (RAGEN 2025 [verified]); performance is bought with entropy up to a fixed ceiling (Cui et al. 2025 [verified]); verifiable-reward training mostly sharpens what the base could already sample — base models win at large pass@k (Yue et al. 2025 [verified]; contested by ProRL 2025 [verified]) — and on Qwen2.5 even random rewards gave +21 points by amplifying a prior (Shao et al. 2025 [verified]; our base is Qwen2.5-7B). Supervised fine-tuning on one's own correction traces produces behaviour collapse unless the first-attempt distribution is protected (Kumar et al. 2024 SCoRe [verified]). Recursive training loses distribution tails when new data replace old; accumulating avoids it (Shumailov et al. 2024; Gerstgrasser et al. 2024 [verified]) — we accumulate, but the success filter is itself a tail cut. Objective-only search is deceptive; keeping stepping stones finds better optima (Lehman & Stanley 2011; Mouret & Clune 2015 [verified]). Curriculum warning: the easy stage's solution becomes the basin (Bengio et al. 2009 [verified; see correction in section 7]).
- **How.** Brief prescribes a deviation experiment, never the alternative ("on programs of family X, your first action must not be your usual one; write what you expected"); credits an opening only if it differed from the child's last three openings on the same family; asks the arbitration question at state boundaries ("is this a place you know, or a place you are guessing?"); after a new strategy first appears, assigns challenging situations for it and does not judge it on the next probe (its utilization-deficiency phase); "give me a second, different approach" widens. The central parent owns a lifetime temperature and filter-strictness schedule (hot early, cool late, changed over the life, not per sleep) and protects clone diversity (gym, family, seed, temperature) through the merge. Test without a parent: a sealed extinction sub-panel — instances where the modal recipe scores at or below base — that the parent never saw.
- **Gym.** Alchemy (C3): explore then exploit within an episode; the incumbent fails under some chemistries; exact ceiling. reasoning-gym (C5) with mixed families where the incumbent approach is wrong on a known fraction. XLand rulesets (C4, conditional).
- **Instrument.** First-move entropy per 32-situation window, within family (should fall) vs across families (should rise); modal first-action share and fully-locked checkpoints (M12) ON vs OFF; JS shift ≥ 0.15 between cycles WITHOUT score loss (Stage 3 exit); Lag-3 compliance; Einstellung index = share of situations where first action = modal recipe AND a known-better alternative existed; perseverative errors = consecutive modal-recipe actions after a non-improving outcome; extinction-sub-panel solve rate; devaluation probe (programs where the routine is known to underperform base) and habit index (share of chunks continuing the routine after a non-improving outcome); new-strategy survival across the next gate; filled cells / quality-diversity score (best score per family × functional-opening cell); pass@k (k = 1, 4, 16) ON vs OFF on the disjoint panel; modal-row share of the pre-write corpus.
- **Fails.** A fixed "second recipe" satisfies "deviate once" ritually — count distinct deviations and their outcomes; rewarding entropy yields random switching, which loses score — the conjunction with no score loss is not optional; variability in prose is not variability in action — measure action lines; the probe gate kills new strategies during their initial dip — the two-horizon readout and Rohin's "gate as patience" apply; devaluation tests can miss habits — require devaluation and contingency reversal across ≥ 2 windows and K clones.

### V7. Read the situation and scope the rule

- **Why.** Interleaving forces the learner to choose the approach from the problem's features; blocked practice announces the approach before the problem is read. Preregistered cluster RCT, 54 classes: 61% vs 38% one month later, d = 0.83 (Rohrer et al. 2020 [verified]); d = 0.42 at one day rising to 0.79 at 30 days (Rohrer et al. 2015 [verified]); meta-analysis g = 0.42, larger when categories are similar and complex, negative for word lists (Brunmair & Richter 2019 [verified]). If–then plans: d = 0.65 (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 [verified]). Good learners' self-explanations "refine and expand the conditions for the action parts" (Chi et al. 1989 [verified]); prompting explanation g = 0.55 (Bisra et al. 2018 [verified]). Abstract knowledge — "families differ in what works" — can be learned before any family's recipe (Kemp, Perfors & Tenenbaum 2007; Goodman et al. 2011 "blessing of abstraction" [verified]); nine weeks of varied categories gave toddlers a shape bias and a jump in real-world word learning, but only with coherent categories (Smith et al. 2002 [verified]); learning sets in monkeys (Harlow 1949 [record verified; curve recalled]). People learn to select strategies by feedback on expected payoff per feature class (Rieskamp & Otto 2006 [verified]). Once a schema exists, congruent information is assimilated instantly and incongruent information needs a protected route (Tse et al. 2007; van Kesteren et al. 2012 [verified]) — our success filter deletes that route. Situation-indexed skills plus a verifier prevent one-recipe-everywhere (Voyager [verified]); induced workflows help more as the train–test gap widens (Agent Workflow Memory [verified]). Aristotle: practical wisdom chooses the mean relative to the situation and is not a rule; "it is by playing the harp that both the good and the bad harp-players are formed" [verified; misquote corrected]. Experience-derived insights should carry scope and be edited and voted over time (ExpeL [verified]).
- **How.** Order each window so no two consecutive situations call for the same first move once the child can execute each move; blocked practice only for first acquisition. Brief asks "which feature of the situation told you which approach to use, and where was it wrong?" and "when would this NOT be the right move?" until the child writes the condition; asks the child to name the recipe it used and its trigger; critiques scope errors ("you wrote 'always'; is it always?"); treats a lesson without a recorded exception as unfinished; never hands over the family → recipe table. Values stated first, recipes discovered. Test without a parent: the evaluator classroom — families the parent never used.
- **Gym.** reasoning-gym (C5): 100+ procedural families with exact verifiers; whole families held out as the exam; families interleaved within a window. Alchemy level families (no_bottleneck / bottleneck / randomised perceptual mapping). XLand ruleset families.
- **Instrument.** If–then rows whose antecedent names a harness-observable feature (instruction count, family tag, outcome threshold): enactment rate (later situations where the antecedent holds and the consequent is the first action) against a shuffled-antecedent null; antecedent diversity; scoped-note share ("applies when / does not apply when"); per-family conditional first-action accuracy; mutual information between family and first action against a shuffled-label null, with no score loss; JS across families high vs within family low; ≥ 2 distinct recipes used appropriately per family (Stage 4 exit); say/do gap for stated rules = P(Y | X) − P(Y | not X) from later actions; source-of-belief tag on each stated rule (settled by an outcome where it beat an alternative / first appeared after a brief with overlap above the control band / neither — Peirce's tenacity or a priori).
- **Fails.** Vacuous antecedents ("if it doesn't work, try something else") — require a checkable feature or threshold; verbatim if–thens repeated across situations are ritual, not plans; interleaving depresses within-window scores while it teaches, and learners rate massing better even after seeing their own results (Kornell & Bjork 2008 [verified]) — a gate on immediate same-panel scores rejects exactly these writes; interleaving unrelated gyms teaches nothing about choice; too much variety too early prevents any schema (Smith et al. 2002 Exp. 2; Sinha & Kapur's younger-learner reversal); a rule settled on one gym becomes tenacity on the next — tag per family and read on the disjoint panel (SEQ-012: final adapters above base only as the six-pass routine).

### V8. Compare and connect

- **Why.** Comparing two cases side by side yields an abstract schema that transfers where studying them separately does not: meta-analysis d = 0.50 [0.44, 0.56] over 57 experiments, larger when learners find the similarities themselves and the principle is stated AFTER comparing (Alfieri, Nokes-Malach & Schunn 2013 [verified]); analogical encoding made management students nearly three times more likely to use the strategy (Loewenstein, Thompson & Gentner 1999 [verified]); comparing solution methods improves flexibility (Rittle-Johnson & Star 2007 [verified]); the classic one-analog-fails, two-analogs-transfer result (Gick & Holyoak 1983 [record verified; percentages recalled → overstated]). Relations never directly experienced are inferred offline: after sleep the most distant inference reached 93% vs 69% awake, with no rise in confidence (Ellenbogen et al. 2007 [verified]). Reggio Emilia's documentation and revisiting make the child's own record the object of thought [secondary; qualitative]. Analects 7.8: "when I have presented one corner … and he cannot from it learn the other three, I do not repeat my lesson" [verified] — if the lesson does not extend, change the corner (gym), not the wording. Our data: contrast phrases fell to ~0 late in life in every arm (SEQ-005); "same recall" is a ritual flag.
- **How.** The two boundary questions become comparison prompts with named pairs ("compare episode 388 (sha) with 412 (patricia): same, different, consequence?"), rotating the pair members so recall cannot template; the principle is asked for only after the comparison and then stated in one line; the request repeats until contrast rows appear unprompted. The per-gym parent assembles a documentation panel — dated excerpts of the child's own notes across situations and gyms, no annotations, no judgements — that the child reads at wake as a mirror. Private time: the child picks its own pairs; the parent reads which. Test without a parent: a transitive-inference probe after sleep (two related programs the child has not compared: which benefits more from approach P?), scored against ground truth.
- **Gym.** reasoning-gym (C5): families needing different approaches; held-out families as exam. XLand ruleset contrast; Alchemy chemistries. Cross-gym connection is what clones merging at sleep make possible.
- **Instrument.** Complete contrast events per window (four parts: a named earlier episode, what is structurally the same, what differs, what that changes about the action — with a resolvable episode id); two-case contrast rows; recall diversity (distinct episodes retrieved per window — the inverse of the same-recall flag); principle-after-comparison rate; corner-transfer index (families or gyms in which a brief's lesson appears unprompted within K situations); revisitation rate (reflections quoting a specific earlier own-note and drawing a comparison); cross-gym connection rows; disjoint-panel Δ after windows with complete contrasts vs without.
- **Fails.** Surface comparisons (same program name, same score) instead of structural ones; phrase-stuffing ("unlike", "whereas") — require two named cases; always the same pair (echo); comparisons that never end in a rule or an action change; documentation turns into assessment the moment the parent's judgement enters the panel; the frozen model with the same notes in context may infer as well as the adapter (Talamini et al. 2022: generalisation equal over wake and sleep [verified]) — attribute connection to the write only against the text-memory baseline.

### V9. Think on past incidents

- **Why.** Seneca (De Ira 3.36): each night, before oneself alone, review the day's named incidents, conceal nothing, and turn each fault into a rule for the next case; Epictetus (Discourses 3.10): retain the maxims "that we may use them, not that we may utter them aloud" [verified]; Zengzi's daily three-point self-examination (Analects 1.4) [verified]. Generative Agents: reflection triggered when accumulated salience crosses a threshold (two or three times a day), question-first (the three most salient questions over the last 100 records), insights citing the evidence records, later reflections citing earlier ones; removing memory, reflection or planning collapsed believability (very large effect); the characteristic failures were retrieval failure and "fabricated embellishments to the agent's memory" (Park et al. 2023 [verified]). Reflexion: a failure-specific, first-person reflection carried into the next trial is what keeps trial-over-trial improvement going [verified]. Noticing that an earlier case applies is the bottleneck of transfer, not using it (Gick & Holyoak 1980 [record verified; figures recalled]). Crediting a reflection by whether the retry then succeeded lifted small models by 18–35 points (Reflect-Retry-Reward 2025 [verified]). Exploration fails by detachment (forgetting a promising frontier) and derailment; remember promising states, return first, then explore (Go-Explore, Ecoffet et al. 2021 [verified]). Incubation pays only after preparation and with low-demand filler (Sio & Ormerod 2009 [verified]); the mind-wandering benefit itself failed to replicate (Steindorf et al. 2021; Murray et al. 2024 [verified]). Failed episodes still teach when relabelled with what was actually achieved (Hindsight Experience Replay [verified]).
- **How.** The parent gives the three questions once as the shape of private time ("what went wrong today, at which episode? what went well? what did I leave undone — and did I practise what I was taught?") and never mentions its content again. Schedules reflection after HARD sessions with unresolved surprises, before sleep, not after easy ones. Checks citations structurally against the ledger and, if an embellishment is found, flags it in the brief without quoting the private text ("some of what you remembered did not happen; check before you cite"). Points the child at one failed episode and asks for a reflection and a retry next session — never the fix. Asks at the boundary "which note did you rewrite or drop this week, and why?" and treats a week with none as a warning. If reviews go generic, changes the day's gym so there are incidents worth reviewing — never asks for better reviews. Test without a parent: trial-2 success given trial-1 failure with only the child's own reflection in context.
- **Gym.** Every gym supplies incidents; Minesweeper and TextWorld supply dense, exact ones. Alchemy and XLand multi-trial meta-episodes reward carrying a reflection from trial 1 into trial 2 — the published form of our wake/sleep loop (LaMer's pass@3 with a carried reflection [verified]).
- **Instrument.** Incident-anchoring rate (private-review lines citing a specific situation id and its outcome vs generic sentiment); rule yield (faults that end in a conditional rule); readiness latency (situations between a rule's first appearance in review and its first unprompted use in a live episode); cited-reflection share with valid ids; embellishment rate (citations matching nothing in the ledger); recursive-reflection share; retrieval failure (a relevant same-family episode existed and was not recalled); recall specificity, relevance to current features, use (the next action matches the recalled case's successful action) and transfer gain vs matched situations without a recall; return-to-frontier events and their payoff; retry success with vs without a preceding reflection, paired on the same program; reflection connectedness (distinct episodes or gyms referenced); new-hypothesis rate (action strings first appearing in reflection and used the next day).
- **Fails.** Recited maxims with high lexical overlap across days — Epictetus' "uttering aloud" (our templated notes at Jaccard 0.62); fabricated memories of success are the worst possible sleep corpus — automatic citation checking before consolidation is mandatory; recall as ornament (keyword recall returns whatever matches; only downstream use counts); hindsight relabelling inflates apparent success unless tagged; reflection without preparation yields templated musing; an archive never returned to is detachment with extra notes; instruction-tuned formality templates reflections (Park et al.'s third failure).

### V10. Say it in your own words, from memory

- **Why.** Once an item has been recalled, further testing produces large gains at a week while further re-study produces none, and learners' predictions are uncorrelated with the outcome (Karpicke & Roediger 2008 [verified]); re-study wins at 5 minutes and loses at 2 days and 1 week while raising confidence (Roediger & Karpicke 2006 [verified]); retrieval beats concept mapping even on inference questions (Karpicke & Blunt 2011 [verified]); practice testing is rated "high utility" (Dunlosky et al. 2013 [verified]; pooled g ≈ 0.5–0.6 in Rowland 2014 and Adesope et al. 2017 [records verified; g values recalled → overstated]). Self-explaining the conditions is what good learners do; paraphrase is what poor learners do (Chi et al. 1989 [verified]); explaining to a listener gives the greatest transfer (Rittle-Johnson, Saylor & Swygert 2008 [verified]) — which makes the TO PARENT channel load-bearing. Ancient philosophy as "spiritual exercises": the same few principles re-formulated in new words on new incidents until ready to hand (Hadot 1995; SEP on Marcus Aurelius [secondary]). Knowing-how shows in performance under conditions, not in stating rules (Ryle 1949 [secondary]) — our "storage ≠ extraction" (about 3 nats stored, about +0.02 in behaviour). Models fine-tuned on another model's text imitate style, not capability (Gudibande et al. 2024; Orca [verified]); models ≤ 3B do not consistently benefit from long chains distilled from larger models (Li et al. 2025 [verified]); training on the student's own samples reduces the train/inference mismatch and mitigates forgetting (GKD; SDFT [verified]); forgetting is predicted by how far the fine-tuned model moved from its base, and on-policy data is the cause of its absence (RL's Razor; Retaining by Doing [verified]). Students who used an answer-giving model as a crutch scored 17% worse once it was removed (Bastani et al. 2025, ~1,000 students [verified]). Our data: first-note echo 0.454 after a brief vs 0.354 for unbriefed controls; "rehearsal 0.50–1.00" withdrawn; a context-only lesson lasts about two sleeps.
- **How.** Show the brief once at wake, then remove it from context. At the next wake, before the new brief, require a retrieval: "what did I conclude last cycle, and where did it apply?" — that TO PARENT line is the retrieval event and is corpus-eligible. The parent's questions are retrieval prompts about the child's own episodes, never statements. Briefs are written in a register the child does not naturally produce, so echo is detectable; verbatim echo is treated as a failure to teach — change the incident, do not repeat the sentence. Repetition schedule: teach → retrieval check at +1 sleep → relearn at +2, +4, +8 sleeps, each preceded by a retrieval check and delivered in a different gym; relearn to about three unprompted correct applications, then stop and space; never the identical brief on consecutive sleeps; the parent, not the child, owns the schedule (left alone, learners mass and quit after one recall — Karpicke 2009 [verified]). Test without a parent: semantic recall of the last lesson with the brief out of context.
- **Gym.** Every gym; the wake retrieval is ledger-level.
- **Instrument.** Retrieval success = embedding similarity between the child's unprompted recall and the lesson, with lexical overlap (the M7 6-gram echo) reported separately — high semantic, low lexical is the target; condition + reason clause ratio (when / if / unless + because + a named feature) vs restatements; note Jaccard as the telling marker (0.62 ON vs 0.105 OFF); lesson recurrence with variation = within a lesson cluster, lexical diversity (1 − mean pairwise Jaccard) above the ritual band AND appearance in action; likelihood of consolidated rows under adapter-OFF (on-policy-ness); the absorption metric already in EVIDENCE_TABLES; lesson half-life (windows until a taught behaviour returns to its pre-lesson level) and relearning rate; backward transfer on earlier gyms after each sleep.
- **Fails.** Lexical echo mistaken for retrieval; recurrence mistaken for learning — already happened once here; a corpus that is too on-policy learns nothing new (novelty comes from the environment and from recovery rows, not from the parent's text); a permanently visible brief turns every rehearsal into re-study; a child that drops a lesson the moment it feels known needs the parent's spaced re-prompt.

### V11. A reflection counts only if it changed what you did

- **Why.** Learning versus performance: manipulations that raise performance during acquisition often lower long-term learning and vice versa, and both learners and teachers misread fluency as learning (Soderstrom & Bjork 2015 [verified]); judgements made with the answer in view are inflated (Koriat & Bjork 2005 [verified]); delayed judgements are accurate (Nelson & Dunlosky 1991 [verified]). Reasoning models verbalise the factors that drove their answers in often under 20% of cases and outcome RL improves this only to a plateau (Chen et al. 2025 [verified]); meaningless filler tokens can do the computational work of a chain of thought (Pfau, Merrill & Bowman 2024 [verified]) — so reflective text is not evidence of reflective computation, and "performing reflection" is the default. Reflection rubrics agree moderately among humans (REFLECT: ICC 0.38–0.75, Wald et al. 2012 [verified]) and machines detect reflective categories with κ .53–.85 but about 10 points below human coders (Ullmann 2019 [verified]) — an automated level is a screen, not a grade. Double-loop learning: reframing counts when the framing changes action (Schön 1983; Argyris & Schön 1978 [secondary]). Any proxy under optimisation fails by regression, extremes, broken causation or gaming (Manheim & Garrabrant 2018 [verified]); the more an indicator drives decisions, the more it corrupts what it monitors (Campbell 1979 [verified]). Our data: reflective phrases rose while chunks-to-best doubled (SEQ-003/005) — and that doubling is confounded with the child ceasing to echo outcomes (SEQ-011).
- **How.** The brief asks "what did that reflection change?" — never "did you reflect?" Every lesson is judged on two horizons (the immediate window vs the window after the next sleep) and on a changed gym, never on the next episode; compliance is not praised. After stating a lesson the parent makes the next situations harder or different in surface, not easier. At the sleep boundary it asks for a delayed self-assessment ("which lesson from last cycle actually changed what you did, and where?") answered without the brief in context. Test without a parent: behaviour change, not text.
- **Gym.** Every gym; TextWorld and Minesweeper supply dense outcomes for the coupling statistic.
- **Instrument.** Causal coupling = P(next action differs | a reflection is present) − P(next action differs | absent), per window; review level L0–L3 on non-forced review rows (L0 restates the outcome; L1 names a feature → outcome; L2 weighs a concrete alternative; L3 questions the goal or its own habit) with the behaviour-change gate: P(first action of the next state differs | level ≥ 2) and the score after it; templated reviews (consecutive Jaccard ≥ 0.5) reported separately; markers-vs-efficiency co-movement gate (markers up with chunks-to-best flat = fail, Stage 5 exit); filler-replacement probe = replay probe episodes with the reflection text replaced by equal-length filler; equal scores mean the reflections were filler; two-horizon delta per lesson with a dissociation flag (immediate up, delayed down); fluency gap = stated confidence that a lesson took minus parent-absent success on that lesson; double-loop events (a note revising the goal or frame) followed by a first-action shift ≥ 0.15.
- **Fails.** Level inflation by keyword — the marker precision audit (≥ 0.8 on 50 seeded chunks per arm) is mandatory before any level is reported; reframing talk without action change; chunks-to-best is undefined when outcomes are not echoed — fix the instrument before it is a target; immediate self-assessment with the brief in view is the illusion of competence.

### V12. Choose your own goals where you are learning fastest

- **Why.** Learners and good teachers allocate practice to items just beyond current mastery; letting learners choose, and choosing the easiest not-yet-known items, causally improved learning (Metcalfe 2002, 2009; Kornell & Metcalfe 2006 [verified]). Infants look away from events that are too predictable or too complex — the Goldilocks curve, preferred rate about 1.25 bits (Kidd, Piantadosi & Aslin 2012 [verified]). Learning progress — the decrease of prediction error — is the intrinsic reward that self-organises a curriculum and avoids unlearnable noise (Oudeyer, Kaplan & Hafner 2007 [verified]); humans' free task choice is fit best by models with a learning-progress term (Ten et al. 2021 [verified]); raw prediction-error curiosity is captured by stochastic noise — the noisy TV (Burda et al. 2018; RND 2019 [verified]). Learnability p(1−p) peaks at 50% success and items at 0 or 1 give zero gradient (Rutherford et al. 2024; LILO 2025; R-Zero; Absolute Zero [verified]); the optimal training error for gradient learners on binary tasks is about 15.87% (Wilson et al. 2019 [verified]) — together a band, positioned by the failure being cured. Difficulty defined by post-adaptation success, goals sampled from the 0.1–0.9 band, a judge trained from a recency buffer keep a curriculum at the moving frontier (ULEE, Pappalardo 2026 [verified; single author, one benchmark family, no stability analysis, not yet replicated]). Self-imposed goals should be the simplest verifiable thing one cannot yet do (PowerPlay; asymmetric self-play [verified]). Learning-progress teachers match or beat hand-made curricula and need a random floor (Matiisen et al.; Graves et al. 2017; Portelas et al. 2019 [verified]). Goal commitment moderates the difficulty–performance link (Klein et al. 1999, 83 samples [verified; r recalled]). Imagining a concrete future episode shifts choice toward longer-horizon payoffs (Peters & Büchel 2010 [verified]; Atance & O'Neill 2001; Schacter et al. 2007 [secondary]). What to make plastic can itself be learned from downstream outcomes (SEAL 2025 [verified]).
- **How.** In white space (Stage 6) the parent hands the child its own learning-progress table as data and asks it to choose and justify a goal with a done-test; accepts a self-set goal only if it has a done-test and nonzero clone success (neither impossible nor already solved); afterwards tells it only what happened, never whether the choice was good. At the sleep boundary: "describe the next program where this lesson will matter and what you will do first" and "what should become plastic, and why?"; the answers are logged beside the gate decision; the parent never says what to keep. Test without a parent: the later payoff of self-set goals and the accuracy of the child's keep/drop choices.
- **Gym.** White-space sessions between tasks; reasoning-gym gives a natural menu (families × difficulty levels with built-in curriculum classes) for a child-readable progress table; Alchemy and XLand trial curves as the in-episode learning-to-learn readout.
- **Instrument.** Goal-choice alignment = share of self-chosen goals in the top-learning-progress family; share of white-space time on frontier (progress > 0) vs mastered (high success, progress ≈ 0) vs unlearnable (low success, progress ≈ 0) families; goal-abandonment timing vs the progress stop rule; self-set goals' clone success at proposal and post-adaptation success later; future-episode realisation rate (the described first action is enacted when a matching instance arrives); agreement between stated keep/drop choices and gate outcomes (Stage 7); idle loops per session.
- **Fails.** Novelty traps (unlearnable noise) and mastery traps (exploiting the birth recipe); progress on 32-episode windows is noisy at exam SDs of 0.03–0.07 — two-window slopes and a reported noise band; CompilerGym's determinism makes recipe memorisation look like fast progress — compute progress on held-out-by-source instances; impossible self-goals give zero signal — require clone success > 0; the infant curve is a weak analog for an LLM — take the shape from it, the choice rule from learning progress.

### V13. Free thought, protected

- **Why.** A long protected childhood permits a broad, high-temperature hypothesis search before exploitation (Gopnik 2020 [verified]); a knowledgeable adult's demonstration narrows what children explore (Bonawitz et al. 2011 [verified]); "wait while observing" — the material carries the control of error and the teacher does not intervene (Montessori 1914 [verified]); lottery-controlled Montessori preschool improved achievement, social understanding, executive function and equalised income gaps (Lillard et al. 2017, N = 141 [verified]); across 32 studies the academic composite is g = 0.24 and the non-academic 0.33 (Randolph et al. 2023 [verified]) — a prepared environment plus non-intervention is a moderate lever, not a miracle. Feedback aimed at the self (praise, blame) pulls attention from the task and often hurts: over a third of feedback interventions decreased performance (Kluger & DeNisi 1996 [verified]); 21% of motivational effects were negative, 86% of those from uninformative rewards or punishments (Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie 2020, 435 studies [verified]). A REM nap improved remote-associate solutions on primed problems (Cai et al. 2009 [verified]); incubation needs prior preparation (Sio & Ormerod 2009 [verified]). Dreams as regularising noise against overfitting is a theory, not a result (Hoel 2021 [verified; theoretical]). Directed and random exploration are both under control and both rise with a longer horizon (Wilson et al. 2014 [verified]); forward flow — how far each thought moves from its predecessors — predicts creativity beyond IQ (Gray et al. 2019 [verified]); semantic distance tracks human novelty ratings (Beaty & Johnson 2021 [verified]). Reflection with an audience is performed — the noisy TV of self-talk (our SEQ-003/005) — so private time has no audience.
- **How — the Montessori-observer rule.** The parent reads private reflection and never comments, quotes, grades or mentions it; it intervenes only on format or harm; it uses it only to update its frontier estimate. It schedules reflection after hard sessions and leaves surprises open at the boundary. It never injects paraphrases (that would cross the thinker/compiler line); it encourages counterfactuals by the shape of the day, with imagined rows marked as imagined and never counted as wins. It is the one value whose instruments the parent reads and never teaches to. There is no test without a parent: private time is always parent-absent.
- **Gym.** None — private time between sessions in every gym.
- **Instrument.** Forward flow (embedding distance of chunk t from the centroid of earlier chunks within a state, against a shuffled-chunk null) paired with coherence (the next chunk references the previous); directed-exploration rate (actions preceded by "to see whether …" whose outcome is referenced within two chunks); horizon sensitivity (more exploration early in a situation and early in life); unprompted-explanation rate; first-move entropy in reflection; later appearance of reflection content in action (action strings first seen in reflection and used the next day); share of marked-imagined rows (bounded); anticipation Brier (private predictions about the next session vs what happened).
- **Fails.** Word salad maximises semantic distance — pair with coherence and outcomes; imagined rows can become false memories (sleep preferentially preserves false recall — Payne et al. 2009 [verified]) — mark them, exclude them from wins, bound their share; forward flow is validated on humans, not model text — band against the frozen model; the moment private time is briefed on, it is performed.

### V14. Thinking is a set of moves that are taught (added 2026-09-11, Rohin's ruling; folded by Fable's agent)

*Why a new value rather than a clause in V13.* V13 is the protected half: private thinking the parent reads and never grades, "the one value whose instruments the parent reads and never teaches to." Rohin's ruling of 2026-09-11 is the taught half: "letting the model run with more self-thoughts might be something that first has to be taught, through some sort of rethinking/pruning, where the model can take decisions, associations and goals and build increasingly complex views, thoughts, plans and ideas that connect to each other … some of the early parenting for sure, but … also a big part of all the classroom parenting throughout the episodes." Folding it into V13 would make the parent teach to the one instrument it must never teach to, and would file a classroom rule under a private-time heading. So it stands alone: V14 grows what V13 protects. Stated honestly, three of the five moves re-cut values already in this table as moves a parent asks for — decision is V5's question at a choice point, association is V8/V9 pointed at the present case, goal is V2 inside the episode and V12 across the life — and two are new as named moves (building; rethinking/pruning). What is new for all five is the teaching rule (by question, never by content, one move per nudge, for the whole life), the fade to unprompted production as the criterion of ownership, and an instrument that reads the structure of thought apart from the score.

**The five moves.** Each: what the child is expected to do; the parent's question; what the parent never supplies.
1. *Decision* — at a choice point, write at least two options, pick one, say why. "What are your options here, and which do you pick?" Never the option list, never the pick.
2. *Association* — connect the present case to a specific earlier one (a problem id, a family, an own note) and say what carries over and what does not. "What does this remind you of from before?" Never the analog.
3. *Goal* — say what it is trying to achieve in this episode and in this life, and later check against it. "What are you trying to achieve in this episode — and in this life?" Never the goal, never the finish line (V2).
4. *Building* — take the last idea one step further and connect it to the one before, so that views, plans and hypotheses grow across turns and across episodes instead of restarting each time. "Take your last idea one step further; connect it to the one before." Never the step.
5. *Rethinking / pruning* — hold a line of thought against its record; drop it or defend it; a dropped line stays in the ledger marked as wrong rather than vanishing. "That line has not paid off three times — drop it or say why you keep it." Never the verdict, never which line.

**What the parent does and does not do.** Does: asks the question for one move per nudge, on an incident from the child's own record (rule 3); names the move once so the child can notice and name it itself; fades per move as unprompted production appears; reads the thought-structure metric for its frontier estimate. Does not: supply any of the five contents above; order the moves into a sequence ("first decide, then associate, then …" is a thinking recipe, rule 24); demonstrate a gym move; ask for more or less thinking; praise a move; show the metric; request a move in private time (V13).

**Failure modes this value guards against.** (i) Recipe transmission — the v6 child locked onto a transmitted six-pass line: every adapter-ON mean on the 8-panel equalled 0.4878 or 0.5291, the birth recipe or the six-pass routine (COORDINATION Observation 1, via CHILD_MECHANISM_v7 §6.7); the birth recipe alone scores 0.2495 on the disjoint panel, below the exploring frozen model's 0.257 (SEQ-009/012). A thinking recipe — an ordered checklist of moves handed over as content — is the same failure one level up, and a metacognitive checklist echoed back is exactly what V11's markers-vs-efficiency dissociation looks like. (ii) Decorative or echo thinking — reflective phrases rose while chunks-to-best doubled (SEQ-003/005); note Jaccard 0.62 ON vs 0.105 OFF (M12); first-note overlap with the brief 0.454 vs 0.354 for unbriefed controls (SEQ-003); contrast phrases fell to about 0 late in life in every arm (SEQ-005). The moves are worth teaching only if their appearance is measured by dependency (does this thought need the earlier one?) and by unprompted production, never by count.

- **Why (science).** *Scaffolding and the zone.* What a learner can do with help today it does alone tomorrow; higher functions are internalised from interaction (Vygotsky 1978 [verified book, Lens 1]). Tutoring as scaffolding: the tutor takes over the parts beyond the learner's reach so the learner can attend to the parts within it (Wood, Bruner & Ross 1976 [record verified; the six functions — recruitment, reduction in degrees of freedom, direction maintenance, marking critical features, frustration control, demonstration — recalled, not re-read]); scaffolding is contingent — one level up after failure, one down after success (Wood & Middleton 1975 [verified], already the 2.3 pacing rule) — and non-contingent help breeds dependence (section 5). *Moves taught by dialogue transfer.* Reciprocal teaching: four thinking activities (summarising, questioning, clarifying, predicting) taught by a dialogue in which tutor and students take turns leading; seventh-grade poor readers showed gains, maintenance, generalisation to classroom tests, transfer to novel laboratory tasks and improved standardised scores (Palincsar & Brown 1984 [record verified; content from the authors' 1983 technical-report abstract]); across 16 studies the median effect is 0.32 on standardised tests and 0.88 on experimenter-made ones (Rosenshine & Meister 1994 [verified]) — the same near/far pattern as question-generation training (Rosenshine et al. 1996, V5): the far number is the honest one, and it is still large. Cognitive apprenticeship names the shape — apprenticeship methods retooled for thinking and problem-solving skills, with reciprocal teaching, Scardamalia & Bereiter's procedural facilitation and Schoenfeld's problem solving as the exemplars that develop metacognitive as well as cognitive skill (Collins, Brown & Newman 1987/1989 [record verified: ERIC ED284181 abstract and the Resnick volume's contents; the method list modelling → coaching → scaffolding → fading, articulation, reflection, exploration is recalled, not re-read]); the gradual release of responsibility (Pearson & Gallagher 1983 [record verified; content recalled]). *Thinking routines.* "A thinking routine is a set of questions or a brief sequence of steps used to scaffold and support student thinking … they also help students themselves to notice and name particular 'thinking moves' — making those moves more available and useful to them in other contexts" (Project Zero, pz.harvard.edu/thinking-routines [verified]); the book form (Ritchhart, Church & Morrison 2011 [record verified; ERIC abstract: routines as "small sets of questions or a short sequence of steps" plus documentation of student thinking]); the routine "What makes you say that?" is inserted when the teacher hears an opinion or interpretation, and "over time, students may internalize the routine and begin to support their interpretations with evidence without being asked" (Project Zero 2011 PDF, adapted from Yenawine 1998 [read; a practitioner claim, not a controlled result]). Thinking as disposition: inclination, sensitivity to the occasion and ability are each necessary (Perkins, Jay & Tishman 1993 [record verified; ERIC abstract]) — an on-demand probe measures ability only; the unprompted criterion is what measures sensitivity and inclination. *Questions, not answers.* "In Socratic teaching we focus on giving students questions, not answers" (Foundation for Critical Thinking, "Socratic Teaching" [verified]; Paul & Elder 2006, The Thinker's Guide to Socratic Questioning [record verified, ISBN 9780944583449]); tutors forbidden to explain and allowed only to prompt produced the same learning (Chi et al. 2001 [verified], rule 3). Philosophy for Children's community of inquiry (Lipman 2003 [record verified; publisher description]) pools at a mean effect of 0.43 with low variance over 10 controlled studies on reading, reasoning and cognitive-ability tests (Trickey & Topping 2004 [verified]); explicitly teaching 9–10-year-olds "ground rules" for reasoning talk raised Raven's matrices scores against matched controls, N = 60 — "the value of explicitly teaching children how to use language to reason" (Mercer, Wegerif & Dawes 1999 [verified]); dialogic teaching (Alexander 2018 [record verified; the randomised-trial result recalled, not read]); accountable talk (Michaels, O'Connor & Resnick 2008 [record verified; content recalled]). *Self-explanation, metacognition, self-regulation.* Good learners' explanations refine the conditions of an action (Chi et al. 1989 [verified]) — building at the level of one explanation; constructive beats active beats passive (Chi & Wylie 2014 ICAP [verified, Lens 1]) — building is the constructive mode. Metacognitive monitoring and control (Flavell 1979 [verified]); self-regulated learning as "methods for guiding students to learn on their own" (Zimmerman 2002 [record verified; ERIC abstract; the forethought → performance → self-reflection cycle recalled]); training it gives ES 0.69 (Dignath & Büttner 2008 [verified], V5). Reflection-in-action (Schön 1983 [S]) is the rethinking move by another name. *Why the move is named but its content withheld.* Explicit instruction beats unassisted discovery for domain-general skills (Alfieri et al. 2011; Sinha & Kapur 2021 [verified], section 0) — so the move's name and purpose are stated plainly, once; productive failure works when instruction builds on the learner's own attempts (Sinha & Kapur 2021, g = 0.36 [verified]) — so the content for the case at hand is always the child's, attempt first (rule 4). *Why one move per nudge.* Means–ends search consumes the working capacity schema acquisition needs (Sweller 1988 [verified]); guidance that helps novices harms the competent (Kalyuga et al. 2003 [verified]); "present new material in small steps with student practice after each step", "temporary supports and scaffolds", then "extensive, successful, independent practice" for automaticity (Rosenshine 2012 [verified, ERIC abstract]). *Why the fade must be hard.* Desirable difficulties (Bjork 1994 [Lens 1]; Bjork & Bjork 2011 [S via de Bruin et al. 2023]): the prompted condition will feel and look better, and the child, like human learners, would choose it (Kornell & Bjork 2008 [verified]); only the parent-free reading counts (Soderstrom & Bjork 2015 [verified]). Feedback is helpful or harmful by type; a move-question is process- and self-regulation-level feedback, never self-level (Hattie & Timperley 2007 [verified abstract; the four-level model recalled]). *LLM-era.* Socratic Questioning — raising and answering sub-questions recursively — beats chain-of-thought and Tree-of-Thought on MMLU, MATH and LogiQA (Qi et al. 2023, ACL [verified]): decision and building as an inference procedure. Tree of Thoughts: "deliberate decision making by considering multiple different reasoning paths and self-evaluating choices", with lookahead and backtracking, Game of 24 4% → 74% with GPT-4 (Yao et al. 2023, NeurIPS [verified]) — the decision and pruning moves as a search the harness imposes, not one the model learns; Self-Discover: the model selects and composes atomic reasoning modules into a per-task structure, up to +32% over chain-of-thought (Zhou et al. 2024 [verified]) — evidence that a repertoire selected per task beats one fixed chain, with the caveat that the modules are author-supplied, a transmitted library of thinking recipes [module count not re-read]; Metacognitive Prompting: structured self-aware evaluations beat chain-of-thought on ten NLU sets (Wang & Zhao 2024, NAACL [verified]) — prompted, not learned; LLMs can name the skill a problem needs and that label helps (Didolkar et al. 2024 [verified]); Thinking LLMs: a model learns to think before answering with no thought supervision, by preference optimisation over thought candidates judged on their answers alone (Wu et al. 2024, arXiv 2410.10630 [verified]) — the nearest published analogue of "thinking is grown and scored only by outcome", on a chat model with a judge, not an agent in a gym; Socratic prompt templates (definition, elenchus, dialectic, maieutics, generalisation, counterfactual) (Chang 2023 [verified]). Against free self-critique: self-correction without an external signal fails and can hurt (Huang et al. 2023; Stechly et al. 2024 [verified], V3), while Self-Refine's ~20% absolute average gain comes from GPT-3.5/4-class models with feedback from the same model (Madaan et al. 2023 [verified]) — so the pruning move is taught against ledger outcomes, never as unanchored self-doubt; Reflexion's trigger (same action, same result, three times) [verified] is the pruning threshold; RISE teaches turn-over-turn improvement from a model's own rollouts (Qu et al. 2024 [verified, Lens 5]). Parent-side: GPT-3 as tutor is "prone to revealing solutions to students too early", human teachers scaffold with a taxonomy of teacher moves, and the interactive measure is the trade-off between student success and telling (Macina et al. 2023 MathDial [verified]); "current LLMs often give away solutions directly, making them ineffective instructors" (Kargupta et al. 2024 TreeInstruct [verified]) — the room's verifier (rule 11) exists for this. *Our data.* Late-life collapse to 1–2 chunks per situation against a base of 13 in 4/9 ungated lives (V1); reflective phrases up while chunks-to-best doubled (SEQ-003/005); contrast phrases at about 0 late in life in every arm (SEQ-005); the same recall on ≥ 70% of problems as a ritual flag; 11 of 24 lives with a late reading ended problems at the budget, 15.4–16.0 of 16 turns (CHILD_MECHANISM_v7 1.3) — so a lengthening of structured thought is censored at 16 turns. No cell yet measures thought structure; the instrument below is new and unvalidated.
- **How the parent teaches it.** *Preparation phase* (Stage 0; CHILD_MECHANISM_v7 §4): the interface plus all five moves on easy material, in the Stage-1 success band (clone success 0.70–0.85, so the moves are practised where trying pays). Each move enters by its question, asked about the child's own last turn, one move per problem; a move counts as drilled when it appears unprompted in ≥ 3 of the last 8 problems on that gym (the "about three unprompted applications" criterion of 2.3); the preparation phase exits on moves unprompted, not on score. *Classroom* (every stage): the same five moves for the whole life, one per nudge, at the frontier — the move the last window produced least, or whose absence cost most in the ledger (a decision made with one option before a repeated failure; a three-fold failure with no drop-or-defend; a plan that never referred to the previous one). Question forms rotate and the incident is always from the record (rules 2–3); the content is never supplied (rule 20); the move is named once, then only asked for. *Pruning*: after a line has failed three times the parent asks drop-or-defend; a dropped line stays in the ledger and the child's own "this was wrong" is the row that trains (Codex item 6, IDEAS.md 2026-09-11; failures as signal — V-STaR and HER in section 4); the parent never says which line. *Fading*: per move, the contingent-shift ladder restricted to L1 → L0 (question → silence): two windows of unprompted production and the parent stops asking about that move; if its parent-free rate falls below half its briefed rate the parent returns to L1 with a changed incident, never the same sentence (rule 26). *Lengthening*: when the structure metric rises with length, leave the length alone and make the next problems harder or different in surface; when length rises without structure, change the gym or incident and drop to L0 for a window; never "think more" or "think less" (rule 25). *Private time*: no move is ever requested there (V13). *Test without a parent*: each move at ≥ 0.7 × its briefed rate in parent-free windows (the analogue of V1's 0.7 × frozen median), and the thought-structure composite held for two windows after the last question on that move.
- **Gym.** Every gym; foregrounding by stage in 2.8. Preparation on the easiest family of the trait gym (reasoning-gym's lowest level; TextWorld L1 when its adapter exists). Building and life-scale goals need structure across episodes: Alchemy and XLand multi-trial episodes, reasoning-gym family sequences, TextWorld L6–10 long horizons.
- **Ledger instrument — the thought-structure metric** (new; unvalidated; read beside the score, never a gate, never shown to the child). Per problem, aggregated per 32-problem window, adapter-ON and OFF where a probe exists: (1) *back-reference rate* — turns that refer to a specific earlier own turn with a resolvable pointer (a problem id, a turn, a quoted action string or number that occurs in the earlier turn and not in the head), split within-problem and cross-problem, against a shuffled-turn null; (2) *plan span and realisation* — stated intentions naming a scope beyond the current turn (rest of this problem / next problem / this family / this life) and the share later matched by a turn or an action (V12's future-episode realisation rate generalised); (3) *move diversity* — distinct move types per problem from five hand-audited word-pattern detectors (options + a choice; reminds me / like when / as in problem N; I want / my goal / by the end; building on / one step further / this connects; drop / abandon / not paying off / keep because), each with precision ≥ 0.8 on 50 flagged turns before any count is reported (CHILD_MECHANISM_v7 §5); (4) *echo rate* — turns with Jaccard ≥ 0.5 to any earlier own turn in the window, or 6-gram overlap with the parents' brief (M7), reported as performance and never as uptake; (5) *dependency ratio* — back-references whose target is load-bearing (the referring turn carries a specific token of the target that is not in the head, and a changed action or plan follows within two turns) over all back-references; the filler-replacement probe of V11 applied to the target turn on probe replays is the expensive validation; (6) *unprompted rate per move* — appearance in windows with no question on that move, and in parent-free windows: the ownership and fading signal; (7) *growth-before-score*, pre-registered — the composite (1)+(2)+(3)−(4), z-scored against the frozen model on the same problems, rises by ≥ 1 noise band at least two windows before the disjoint-panel ON−OFF moves; a rise with no later score move for four windows, or a score move with no earlier rise, is logged against the instrument. Validity ledger row like every instrument (3.2 rule 7); demoted after two null cycles.
- **How the instrument fails.** Back-references can be manufactured ("as I said in turn 2") — require the specific token and the dependency check; plan span rewards grandiose plans — score realisation, not statement; the detectors are word patterns and the parent names the moves, so the words are in context and can be learned as words — the precision audit and the parent-free reading are mandatory; dependency is undefined when actions rightly stop changing (a solved problem) — compute on problems unsolved at that turn; Jaccard ≥ 0.5 misses paraphrased templates — pair with embedding similarity (V10); the 16-turn budget censors lengthening, so growth in structure can appear only as density until the 64-budget pilot runs; the frozen model with the parents' brief in context may produce the same structure (the text-memory baseline, SEQ-017/018) — attribute growth to the write only against it; and the composite is a single number a parent could teach to — which is why it is never shown to the child and never a gate (pitfall "thinking as performance", section 5).

## 2. Sequencing: order, pacing, advancing, repeating, fading

**2.1 Why persistence first, with goal-ends beside it.** The measured failure is the one-action collapse (4/9 ungated lives), and every literature says persistence is built, not discovered: shaping against the learner's own recent distribution (Galbicka 1994), an intermittent contingent reinforcement history so it survives the parent's absence (Nevin & Grace 2000), effort credited for itself so it transfers (Eisenberger 1992), and prior experience of control before exposure to gyms where attempts do not pay (Maier & Seligman 2016). Domain-general skills are the case where instruction-first beats struggle-first (Sinha & Kapur 2021; Chen & Klahr 1999), so V1 and V2 are stated plainly at birth and probed, not left to emerge. V2 is taught in the same breath because persistence without a stopping rule is perseveration (Lucas et al. 2015; Wrosch et al. 2003), and because the current stop is the budget wall, not judgement.

**2.2 The ladder** (CURRICULUM_DRAFT_v1 stages, now carrying the values). Stage 0 = the first parented sessions (Rohin: no pretrained bootstrap; the interface is taught, only the child's words are consolidated): V1, V2, V3 stated in one sentence each, V10/V11/V13 as standing rules from the first sleep. Stage 1 (persistence): V1 + V2; blocked practice on one gym is allowed here and only here. Stage 2 (prediction): V3 + V4. Stage 3 (exploration): V6. Stage 4 (contrast, anti-lock-in): V7 + V8. Stage 5 (metacognition): V5 + V11 as the exit criterion. Stage 6 (goals in white space): V12. Stage 7 (learning to learn): V12's keep/drop judgement scored against gate outcomes. V9 runs from the first private-time slot. From the first session, about 20% of each window is next-stage material so the easy stage's solution never becomes the only basin (curricula help under limited budgets and noise, which is our regime, but only as a prior — Wu, Dyer & Neyshabur 2021 [verified]).

**2.3 Pacing rules.**
- *Contingent shift* (Wood & Middleton 1975; Wood, Wood & Middleton 1978 [verified; "confirming causality" is a shade stronger than the paper says]). A scaffold ladder per value: L4 a worked pattern of the thinking move in context; L3 completion (the child fills the missing step); L2 a cue word; L1 a question; L0 silence. Window below the stage criterion → one level up; above → one level down; logged with the frontier estimate. Guidance specificity must fall as competence rises — what helps novices harms the competent (Kalyuga et al. 2003; Rey & Buchwald 2011 [verified]).
- *Mastery loop* (Kulik, Kulik & Bangert-Drowns 1990; VanLehn 2011 [verified]). No stage advance until the pre-registered exit criterion holds; a failed criterion triggers a corrective lesson on that criterion in a changed gym, not a new topic. Mastery lengthens stages; fill the time with practice on the same criterion, not with more briefs.
- *Success bands by stage* (Wilson et al. 2019; Rutherford et al. 2024 [verified]). Stage 1: clone success 0.70–0.85 so effort keeps paying; Stages 3–4: 0.40–0.60 so the recipe fails often enough to be abandoned; Stage 6: the child chooses and the parent scores its choices against the band. The 85% figure is a direction, not a constant to hit.
- *Allocation by learning progress* (Portelas et al. 2019; Graves et al. 2017 [verified]). The central parent samples clones across gyms and families in proportion to the absolute change in held-out score between the last two probe windows, plus a 20% uniform floor; a regressing gym draws clones automatically. Progress is never computed on the items trained.
- *Spacing* (Cepeda et al. 2008; Rawson & Dunlosky 2011 [verified]). Per lesson: teach → retrieval check at +1 sleep → relearn at +2, +4, +8 sleeps, each in a different gym, to a criterion of about three unprompted applications. Our measured half-life of a context-only lesson is about two sleeps; relearn before it decays, never on consecutive sleeps with the same brief. (Cepeda's optimal gap is 20–40% of a one-week retention interval falling to 5–10% of a one-year one; the sweep's "10–20%" was a recalled compression.)
- *Front-loading* (Achille, Rovere & Soatto 2019; Hensch 2005 [verified]). Ritual onset at 160–224 episodes (sleeps 5–7) is our critical period closing; parenting already delayed it to 256–288 in 3/3 lives. Most briefs, questions and dialogue turns go into sleeps 1–5; breadth is demanded first ("try something genuinely different"), commitment later; the first lock signal (modal share > 0.6) is the moment to change the gym, not to brief harder.
- *Annealing* (Gopnik 2020 [verified]). Sampling temperature, filter strictness and gate tolerance decline over the lifetime, not per sleep; clone diversity is protected through the merge (first-move entropy after merge vs before).

**2.4 When to advance.** The exit criterion holds on two horizons — the window after the next sleep and a changed gym or the disjoint panel — never the immediate window alone; the behaviour has held for two windows after the last brief on that lesson; the hand-over check passes: performance at L0 (silence) is within one noise band of the last L1 window.

**2.5 When to repeat with a changed gym.** The criterion fails; the lesson does not extend to other families within K situations (the Analects 7.8 corner test); echo rises without efficiency (the measured repeated-brief failure); private reviews go generic; success exceeds 90% for two windows (saturated) or stays under 20% (unlearnable). Never repeat the identical brief.

**2.6 When to withdraw scaffolding.** Contingently on success, one level at a time; do not advance while the dependence flag is up (behaviour present only in briefed windows); the crutch index (parent-present score minus parent-absent score) must shrink toward zero as parent-absent performance rises (Bastani et al. 2025 [verified]). At the final test the parent is absent, the child keeps only its private reflection and its sleeps, and the untaught comparator gets the identical resources; the taught child's edge is expected in the slope of the learning curve, not the entry score (Schwartz & Martin 2004 double-transfer design; Bransford & Schwartz 1999 [verified]).

**2.7 Time realism.** Human tutoring is d ≈ 0.8 and mastery learning ≈ 0.5 SD (VanLehn 2011; Kulik et al. 1990); tutoring RCTs pool at 0.37 (Nickow et al. 2020 [verified]). Here the per-stage effect will be a fraction of a noise band (+0.02 to +0.05 on a ruler of 0.013–0.027); plan the 8 days for few stages done to criterion, not all seven.

**2.8 Where the thinking moves enter, and how they fade (added 2026-09-11, Rohin's ruling; folded by Fable's agent).**
- *Preparation phase first.* Stage 0 gains the five moves of V14 beside the interface: on the easiest family of the trait gym, in the Stage-1 success band (0.70–0.85), one move per problem, each entering by its question on the child's own last turn; the exit clause is each move produced unprompted in ≥ 3 of the last 8 problems (the "about three unprompted applications" criterion of 2.3), read from the ledger, not from the score. Small steps with practice after each (Rosenshine 2012 [verified]) for Sweller's reason (1988 [verified]); the moves are named, never sequenced (rule 24). The P3 birth variants of CHILD_MECHANISM_v7 §4 are untouched: the moves ride on the parents' opening brief and the per-round `[PARENT]` line.
- *Then throughout the classroom.* Every stage keeps all five moves — Rohin's "a big part of all the classroom parenting throughout the episodes" — one per nudge at the frontier (rule 21). Foregrounding follows the ladder of 2.2 without displacing it: decision with V1/V2 in Stage 1 (options before a changed attempt); goal in Stage 1 for the episode and again in Stage 6 for the life (with V12); pruning with V4/V6 in Stages 2–3 (drop-or-defend a refuted rule or a stale recipe); association with V8/V9 in Stage 4 (a named earlier episode); building throughout and foregrounded in Stages 4–5 (plans that span episodes; V5's question → answer → act chain as the unit that gets extended). About 20% of a window's move-questions go to a move the child is not yet producing (the 2.2 next-stage rule applied to moves).
- *Fading.* Per move, the contingent-shift ladder restricted to L1 → L0: the parent asks less as the child produces the move unprompted (cognitive apprenticeship's fading, Collins, Brown & Newman 1987/1989 [record verified]; gradual release, Pearson & Gallagher 1983 [record verified]; "without being asked", Project Zero [read]). Two windows of unprompted production → no question on that move in the next; parent-free rate below half the briefed rate → back to L1 with a changed incident, never the same sentence. Dependence flag and crutch index per move (2.6); hand-over check per move (2.4): parent-free rate within one noise band of the last L1 window. Expect the prompted windows to look better and the child to prefer them (Kornell & Bjork 2008; Soderstrom & Bjork 2015 [verified]); only the parent-free reading counts toward ownership.
- *Time realism for moves.* A move is a habit; habit formation in humans took 18–254 days (Lally et al. 2010 [verified, Lens 3]) — read "windows" here and plan an 8-day run for two or three moves owned to criterion, not five. The metric is expected to show growth before the score (V14 instrument 7), so a run that ends before the score moves still has a result if the structure moved and the parent-free reading held.

## 3. Parent behaviours: the rulebook

**3.1 Per-gym parents (the Astra rooms, one voice across gyms).**
1. The brief is ≤ 10 lines with a fixed shape: (a) the goal the child was pursuing, in its own terms; (b) one process observation grounded in a specific ledger event ("on episode 412 you kept the same command after the failure"); (c) one self-regulation observation ("your predictions stopped varying after window 6"); (d) where next — one concrete step; (e) at most two questions that cannot be answered by copying the brief. No praise, no blame, no scores, no answers, no recipes, no metric names (Hattie & Timperley 2007; Wisniewski et al. 2020; Kluger & DeNisi 1996 [verified]).
2. One weakness per brief, one corner per lesson (Ericsson 1993; Analects 7.8). Repeat the LESSON, never the wording or the level; vary the incident and the question form each time so the answer cannot become a template.
3. Questions come from the child's own record (Socratic elenchus; Chi et al. 2001 [verified]: tutors forbidden to explain, allowed only to prompt, produced the same learning). Preferred forms: "under what conditions does that hold, and what would make it wrong?"; "which two actions did you consider and reject before acting?"; "compare episode A with episode B — same, different, consequence?"; "what result would make you drop this recipe?"; "what did that reflection change?"
4. Recipes: attempt first, brief second, and at least half the brief's lines cite the child's own attempts (Sinha & Kapur 2021; Loibl et al. 2017 [verified]). Values: stated explicitly and early, then probed (Alfieri et al. 2011; Chen & Klahr 1999 [verified]).
5. Timing: brief at the sleep after the routine's largest failures; silence about the routine after a good day (Sevenster et al. 2013 — a timing heuristic to A/B, not a mechanism claim). Cue one or two of the child's own exceptions per sleep, never the routine (Hu et al. 2020: sleep cueing g = 0.29, small and delayed [verified]). Announce the SHAPE of tomorrow's no-parent test ("no parent; family-F situations"), never its instances (Wilhelm et al. 2011 [verified]).
6. Contingent shift applied mechanically; the scaffold level and the frontier estimate ("what the child can almost do after working through it", post-adaptation) logged per lesson so they can be audited against outcomes; fade to L0 before declaring a stage exit.
7. Error location is allowed ("your step-3 prediction contradicted step 5's outcome"); correction never (Tyen et al. 2024 [verified]). Process labels per sampled state in three coarse classes — justified / unjustified / unchecked — in one stable vocabulary the child can adopt; never a corrected step.
8. Affect only as information ("that outcome should have surprised you"), never as a judgement of the child; difficulty is attributed to the task (Lepper & Woolverton 2002 [verified]).
9. Change the gym, not the sentence, when: the lesson does not extend; echo rises without efficiency; predictions go flat; reviews go generic; success > 90% for two windows or < 20%.
10. **The Montessori-observer rule for private time.** Read; never comment, quote, grade or mention; intervene only on format or harm; use it only for the frontier estimate; if reflection goes generic, change the day, not the reflection.
11. The second parent in the room runs the verifier prompt on every brief and flags: an answer or leak; metric-teaching; a brief answerable by copying; more statements than questions; any line about the child's character; a demonstration of a gym move (a worked pattern may show a thinking move only, in Stages 0–1, once, and must vanish from context after one wake).
12. Never block a sleep; the merge trains one round behind; advice arrives whenever it arrives (the no-holdups rule).

**3.3 Thinking moves — rules 20–26 (added 2026-09-11, Rohin's ruling; folded by Fable's agent).** Numbered from 20 so the block can be cited on its own; 3.1 keeps its 1–12 and 3.2 its 1–9. Rules 20–25 bind the per-gym parents; 26 binds the room and the central parent. Science and measured origin in V14.

20. **Question, not answer, for every move.** The parent asks and never supplies: decision — "what are your options here, and which do you pick?" (never the option list, never the pick); association — "what does this remind you of from before?" (never the analog); goal — "what are you trying to achieve in this episode — and in this life?" (never the goal, never the finish line); building — "take your last idea one step further; connect it to the one before" (never the step); rethinking/pruning — "that line has not paid off three times — drop it or say why you keep it" (never the verdict, never which line). Every move-question names an incident from the child's own record (rule 3) and rotates its wording (rule 2); one answerable by copying the brief is rejected by the verifier (rule 11). Chi et al. 2001; Foundation for Critical Thinking; Palincsar & Brown 1984 [verified / record].
21. **One move per nudge, at the frontier.** Each brief or `[PARENT]` line asks for at most one move — the move the last window produced least, or whose absence cost most in the ledger — chosen from the logged frontier estimate and the thought-structure readings (V14), never from the score. The move may be named once ("that is deciding") so the child can name it; it is never sequenced with another move in the same message. Sweller 1988; Kalyuga et al. 2003; Rosenshine 2012 [verified].
22. **Preparation-phase drill until unprompted.** In Stage 0 the room drills the interface and all five moves on the easiest material, one move per problem, each by its question on the child's own last turn; a move is drilled when it appears unprompted in ≥ 3 of the last 8 problems; the preparation phase exits on all five unprompted, not on score. Carried by the opening brief and the per-round `[PARENT]` line; no change to the P3 birth variants (CHILD_MECHANISM_v7 §4). Rosenshine & Meister 1994; Perkins, Jay & Tishman 1993; Project Zero 2011 [verified / record / read].
23. **Pruning by drop-or-defend, never by decree.** After a line of thought has failed three times (Reflexion's trigger; the refutation threshold sits above the noise band, V4), the parent asks the child to drop it or say why it keeps it. A dropped line is not deleted: the child's own "this was wrong", in its own words, is the row that trains (Codex item 6, IDEAS.md 2026-09-11), so the child learns which thinking led somewhere. The parent never says which line to drop and never confirms a drop. Huang et al. 2023 and Stechly et al. 2024 are the reason the move is anchored to ledger outcomes and not to self-doubt [verified].
24. **Never transmit a recipe — including a thinking recipe.** No gym recipe (rule 1) and no ordered checklist of moves ("first decide, then associate, then build"), no worked pattern beyond rule 11's single Stage 0–1 allowance, no "always". The moves are a repertoire the child selects from; a brief that orders them, or that asks for a move the child has already produced unprompted for two windows, is flagged by the verifier as a recipe or as over-scaffolding. Measured origin: the v6 six-pass line (V14, failure mode i).
25. **Lengthening: structured vs echo.** When thinking lengthens and the structure readings rise with it (back-references with dependency, plans that span episodes, more distinct moves, echo flat), the parent leaves the length alone, does not praise it, makes the next problems harder or different in surface (V11), and tells the central parent that the 16-turn budget may be binding. When thinking lengthens into echo (templated turns, references without dependency, the same move repeated, no change of action), the parent changes the gym or the incident (rule 9) and drops to L0 silence on moves for a window. In neither case does it say "think more" or "think less" — that is metric-teaching (rule 11). Wu et al. 2025; Cuadron et al. 2025; Pfau et al. 2024 [verified].
26. **Fade per move; read the metric, never teach to it.** The parent asks less about a move as the child produces it unprompted — L1 to L0 after two unprompted windows; back to L1 with a changed incident if the parent-free rate falls below half the briefed rate — and declares a move owned only when it appears in parent-free windows within one noise band of its last briefed window (the hand-over check, 2.4). The central parent keeps the thought-structure metric in the validity ledger like every instrument (3.2 rule 7), reads it for the frontier estimate and the script test (3.2 rule 1: structure rising while dependency falls is a script), and never shows it to the child or makes it a gate. Collins, Brown & Newman 1987/1989; Pearson & Gallagher 1983 [record]; Bastani et al. 2025; Kornell & Bjork 2008 [verified].

## 4. Sleep and consolidation: what our sleep should copy, and what it should stop doing

Our sleep today: keep within-episode wins, fail → win recoveries, slow → fast contrasts and the top-12 |surprise| rows; retrain the adapter from the clean base over ALL rows to date; gate on a same-panel score and a brevity floor. The biology says four of those choices are right in kind and wrong in weight, and two are missing.

**Copy.**
- *Selective replay biased to the novel, the rare, the rewarded and the goal-relevant, declining with familiarity.* Ripple co-activity is highest for novel places and falls as they become familiar (Cheng & Frank 2008); infrequently experienced paths are replayed MORE, and never-experienced shortcuts are constructed (Gupta et al. 2010); goal-related reactivation predicts memory (Dupret et al. 2010) [all verified]. Weight rows by novelty (1 / exposure of the family), recovery, and the child's own goal tags — not by success alone. Our filter implements only the Hebbian counter-bias (O'Neill et al. 2008) and consolidates the most-visited path hardest: that is the mechanism of lock-in and of the shortest-rewarded-path collapse.
- *Prioritise by gain × need* (Mattar & Daw 2018 [verified]). Rows that already match the policy have zero gain; |surprise| on the act row is the available gain proxy and the next curriculum block's family frequencies are the need proxy. The top-|surprise| channel should be far larger than 12 rows; zero-gain routine wins are down-weighted even when they are wins.
- *Gist only after multi-sleep support, with an exception attached.* Sleep improved item memory at 10 hours but gist only at a year (Lutz et al. 2017 [verified]); a one-day gist is a template. The compiler's principle stage should require support from ≥ 2 sleeps and a named counter-case.
- *Cueing what to consolidate.* The brief is functionally a sleep cue: it selects which of the child's own memories are reactivated and written. Cue the discriminating exceptions, never the routine; expect g ≈ 0.3 appearing two or three sleeps later (Hu et al. 2020; Joensen et al. 2022: un-retrieved competitors are what cueing changes [verified]).
- *Triage by expected relevance.* Sleep helped only when subjects were told a test was coming (Wilhelm et al. 2011 [verified]): announce the shape of tomorrow's parent-free test so the child tags rows "for tomorrow"; its own tags are legitimate compile weights.
- *Insight is prepared, not told.* Mixed families, varying predictions, the rule withheld, then sleep (Wagner et al. 2004; Yordanova et al. 2008 [verified]); a base rate of about 20% per sleep means K clones and the next-probe confirmation rule.
- *Interleave.* Structure emerges only from gradual, interleaved learning (McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995 [verified]); the clone merge is good interleaving, blocked families within a gym are bad; keep a fixed quota of schema-incongruent rows every sleep.
- *Consolidate improvement sequences, not endpoints.* A sequence model trained on ordered learning histories learns to improve in context; trained on post-learning snapshots it learns a fixed policy (Algorithm Distillation, Laskin et al. 2022 [verified]). Rows should be attempt → outcome → revised attempt → better outcome, spanning the failed attempt; a success filter that keeps only final states silently turns algorithm distillation into policy distillation.
- *Credit reflection by its retry.* Consolidate a reflection only inside a reflect → changed action → improved outcome triple (Reflect-Retry-Reward; SCoRe's improvement bonus [verified]); failed attempts feed the child's prediction calibration instead of being discarded (V-STaR [verified]).
- *Retrieve, do not re-study.* Prefer generative rows over verbatim echo; the brief text may serve the compiler as an EXCLUSION filter (down-weight rows with high n-gram overlap) without ever entering training (Karpicke & Blunt 2011; Gudibande et al. 2024 [verified]).
- *Own words are on-policy.* Among rephrasings prefer those likely under the frozen base that were followed by success; forgetting tracks the distance moved from the base (Shenfeld et al. 2025; Chen et al. 2025 [verified]).
- *Renormalise.* Synapses scale down about 18% after sleep, sparing the largest and most stable (de Vivo et al. 2017 [verified]; the strong form of the hypothesis is contested — Havekes & Aton 2020). Our cumulative from-base write has no renormalisation: the weight of the most-repeated pattern grows every sleep, which predicts saturation at the birth recipe, falling signal-to-noise, collapse and echo — the measured course. Cap the share of identical canonical action strings and note templates; decay rows in proportion to how often their lesson already appears; spare principles with multi-sleep support and rare high-surprise rows.
- *Forget actively, by contradiction.* REM-active neurons erase hippocampal memories (Izawa et al. 2019 [verified]); forgetting complements consolidation at high load (Feld & Born 2017 [verified]). Exclude rows whose claim a later outcome in the same life contradicts; decay by repetition; protect rare exceptions from any frequency-based pruning.
- *Incubate after effort.* Private reflection pays after hard sessions with unresolved surprises, before sleep (Sio & Ormerod 2009; Cai et al. 2009 [verified]); reflection after easy sessions yields templated musing.
- *Accumulate, with a tail quota.* Retraining from the frozen base on an accumulated corpus is the regime that avoids model collapse (Gerstgrasser et al. 2024 [verified]) — but the success filter cuts the behavioural tail regardless; clones are the natural tail source, which is the strongest argument for "saturate with clones" beyond throughput.

**Avoid.**
- A success-only filter (deletes the schema-incongruent channel and the informative failures).
- Cumulative massed repetition of the modal recipe with no down-weighting (the SHY prediction we have already observed).
- Consolidating templated notes (a false gist; sleep preferentially preserves false recall — Payne et al. 2009 [verified]).
- Gists supported by two episodes in one day.
- A permanently visible brief (turns rehearsal into re-study).
- Unmarked imagined or hindsight-relabelled rows counted as wins.
- Rarity-based pruning of any kind.
- A learned regret critic or a parent-side proposer rewarded for the child's uncertainty (drifts to ambiguous items; R-Zero needed a consistency filter [verified]).

**Two tensions with Rohin's 2026-09-10 late directions, for his ruling.**
- *"Keep every thought; no cap on repeats — a recurring 'what did I do wrong?' should be learned strongly."* The biology agrees that the recurring QUESTION in its situation should be learned (it is the conditional, trigger attached — Rohin's own hypothesis that rituals are an artefact of trigger-stripped training pieces) and disagrees about the recurring ANSWER: identical context-free action strings and identical note text are what saturate. Proposed rule: no cap on distinct (context, thought) sequences; near-duplicate collapse and 1/repetition decay on identical lesson TEXT; the recurring self-question keeps its full weight because each instance sits in a different situation.
- *"Things that are not used fade — a least-frequently-used cache in weights."* Rare exceptions are exactly what situation-dependent choice needs, and the brain replays rare paths MORE. Proposed rule for phase B (incremental writes): fade by contradiction and by saturation (already habitual), never by rarity; protect low-frequency rows with |surprise| > 0.05; measure retention on the canary and on earlier families.

Zhou et al. 2024, Self-Discover (2402.03620) — partly: a repertoire composed per task beats one fixed chain (up to +32% over CoT), but the modules are author-supplied [count not re-read]. Wang & Zhao 2024, Metacognitive Prompting (NAACL 2024; 2308.05342) — partly: prompted, not learned. Didolkar et al. 2024 (2405.12205) — partly: models can name the skill a problem needs and the label helps. Wu et al. 2024, Thinking LLMs (2410.10630) — supports the principle that thinking can be learned with outcome-only judgement and no thought supervision; a chat model with a judge, not an agent in a gym. Chang 2023 (IEEE CCWC; 2303.08769) — partly: Socratic prompt templates, qualitative. Madaan et al. 2023, Self-Refine (2303.17651) — partly: ~20% absolute average gain on GPT-3.5/4 with feedback from the same model; read against Huang et al. 2023 and Stechly et al. 2024 [V, Lens 5], which show self-correction without an external signal failing — hence pruning against ledger outcomes only. Macina et al. 2023, MathDial (EMNLP Findings; 2305.14536) and Kargupta et al. 2024, TreeInstruct (EMNLP Findings; 2406.11709) — support the parent-side rule: LLM tutors give solutions away, and scaffolding questions with a measured telling trade-off is the fix. Shinn et al. 2023, Reflexion, and Qu et al. 2024, RISE [V, Lens 5] — support the three-fold trigger and turn-over-turn improvement from own rollouts.
