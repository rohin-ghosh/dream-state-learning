## The hypothesis we are testing

Persistent parenting repeatedly elicits useful thinking and action from the
child. Training on the child's own experience may consolidate those tendencies
in its LoRA adapter: the **slow loop**. The **fast context loop** then uses those
tendencies to interpret feedback, choose checks, and act in the current situation.

The proposed mechanism is a change in the reliable initiation and use of
capabilities, not proof that the agent has acquired a general new intelligence.
Repeating useful, grounded checks is different from repeating intentions or
the parent's vocabulary. Reflection counts as useful only when its relationship
to an actual action and outcome is supported by a trace.

## Experimental priorities and boundaries

1. Verify the chain from actual task and feedback through THINK, ACT, sleep,
   and parent-free evaluation. Missing evidence is unknown, not a failed score.
2. Replicate promising findings before explaining them. Sleep51 is a selected
   promising checkpoint; it does not establish an optimal developmental age.
3. Refine parenting separately from evaluating it. Ground guidance in actual
   child-visible evidence, vary subjects and checks, and preserve checkpoint
   and treatment boundaries. Do not train on sealed evaluation answers.
4. Use intentional withdrawal only as a bounded, announced probe, with the
   environment's ordinary feedback still available. Tapering is a hypothesis,
   not assumed necessary and not a substitute for working feedback delivery.
5. Preserve ongoing lives, checkpoints, journals, pending rows, and failed
   artifacts. All authentic child-authored rows remain eligible under the
   existing learning policy; frozen controls remain frozen. No new leases,
   access bypasses, or silent resets.
