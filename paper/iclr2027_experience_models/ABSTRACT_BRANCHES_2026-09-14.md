### Abstract (179 words)

Can one acting agent convert the consequences of its own actions into a
persistent parametric memory that changes later action after context reset?
We decompose this claim into three noncompensatory tests: repeated-write
retention, acquisition of a typed seek--predict--check--continue controller,
and an authentic two-sleep junction. A frozen language model acts through a
typed interface while only a per-life rank-8 adapter changes; admitted public
action--outcome records are replayed during sleep, and every write is checked
for provenance, interface preservation, and controller retention. On one
exposed eight-record development bank, new-only writing learned the new bank
but erased the old bank in all three optimizer seeds and two read views;
scheduled replay retained both banks at 4/4. `[STAGE2A RESULT]` qualified one
target-disjoint controller. In two complementary development worlds,
`[TSJ RESULT]`: the continued adapter stored child-authored records across two
sleeps, issued its own single-record reads after clean resets, and selected
first an old-dependent and then an old-plus-new-dependent held action while
preserving the controller. This is a finite conditional mechanism
demonstration, not evidence of population reliability, parenting, improving
lifetimes, compression, or superiority to textual memory.

### Abstract (174 words)

Low-rank updates can store supplied text, but an acting agent must also turn
its own outcomes into memory, retrieve the right content under a later goal,
and preserve the policy that uses it. We introduce a causal ladder separating
these requirements. A frozen language model acts through a typed
seek--predict--check--continue interface; a per-life rank-8 adapter is updated
only from provenance-bound public action--outcome records, with replay and
write-time interface checks. On one exposed eight-record development bank,
new-only continuation acquired the new bank but forgot the old bank at both
read views in all three optimizer seeds, whereas scheduled replay retained
both at 4/4. A target-disjoint Stage2A screen then qualified a controller at
`[STAGE2A RESULT]`. We next tested one continued child across a precommitted,
paired two-sleep junction with clean context resets, atom-only, foreign-life,
no-new-content, and controller-preservation controls. `[TSJ TERMINAL RESULT]`.
The result localizes the first unsupported transition between executable
controller acquisition and autobiographical action--outcome use. It does not
establish a general failure of continual learning, parenting, connected
memory, lifetime improvement, compression, or comparison with external
memory.

### Abstract (172 words)

Retained records are useful only if a model can selectively invoke them
through an executable policy without damaging its interface. We separate
storage, prompt robustness, and controller acquisition in a frozen-base,
rank-8 agent. In a sequential-write assay on one exposed development bank,
three optimizer seeds learned four A records, then learned four B records
while forgetting A under both new-dose- and work-matched new-only writes.
Scheduled replay and clean cumulative retraining each ended at A 4/4 and B
4/4 in both cold read views; this establishes bounded same-bank retention,
not generalization. A separate prediction assay failed its predeclared
minimal-view rule in two of three learners, separating content from robust
extraction. Finally, a
target-disjoint Stage2A test asked whether training could install the joint
SEEK, PROSPECT, CHECK, and CONTINUE/STOP behavior needed to use experience
autonomously. `[STAGE2A TERMINAL RESULT]`. Thus the tested system can retain
small repeated record sets, yet does not qualify the controller required for
an authentic action--outcome learning loop. Downstream claims about connected
experience, parenting, lifetime improvement, compression, and memory-baseline
superiority remain untested.
